Listening Part 4

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 2 — Listening Part 4

Developing Food Trends · Notes Completion (Q31–40)

📋 Quick Review

This is a single-speaker academic talk about how food fashions are created and sold. The lecturer first explains the machinery behind a food trend — smartphones, social media influencers, supermarkets and celebrity chefs — and then walks through four case studies: the avocado, oat milk, Norwegian skrei and quinoa. The talk ends on the ethical cost of a sudden surge in demand. You complete a set of bullet-point notes with ONE WORD ONLY for each answer.

Who is speaking? One lecturer giving a talk on food marketing — probably to business or nutrition students. There are no interruptions and no self-correction, so you cannot rely on a speaker repeating an answer.

The situation 情境: 一场关于“食物潮流是如何被制造出来的”的讲座。讲者先说明智能手机、社交媒体网红、超市和名厨如何共同推动潮流，再依次分析四个案例：牛油果（1990年代英国公关活动）、燕麦奶（瑞典品牌 Oatley）、挪威 skrei 鳕鱼，最后以藜麦为例谈潮流带来的伦理问题。答案严格按照题纸小标题的顺序出现。

Task type: Notes completion under four headings. Instruction: ONE WORD ONLY — no numbers are needed anywhere in this part, and writing two words scores zero even if both words are correct.

💡 Listening strategy tip: Part 4 has no second speaker to slow the talk down, so your only anchors are the headings on the question paper: Developing food trends → Marketing campaigns (avocado / oat milk / skrei) → Ethical concerns (quinoa). Those headings arrive in exactly this order in the recording, so if you lose a gap, jump forward to the next heading word (‘avocado’, ‘oat milk’, ‘skrei’, ‘quinoa’) rather than trying to catch up. Because the limit is ONE WORD ONLY, every answer here is a single noun or adjective — if you are tempted to write ‘coffee shops’ or ‘soil fertility’, you have written two words and scored zero. Decide the word type for each gap in the preparation time: Q32 and Q35 need adjectives/noun-modifiers, Q31, Q33 and Q34 need plural people or things, Q37–Q40 need abstract nouns.
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • Q31 “photos” must be plural — the note says ‘(31) of food being shared’. The audio says ‘taking photos of their food and instantly sharing them’. Photographs and pictures are also accepted, but a singular ‘photo’ is risky.
  • Q33 chefs, not influencers — influencers are discussed for a long time just before this gap, but the note is under supermarkets: they watch what ‘well-known chefs are putting on their menus’. The nearest loud word is not the answer.
  • Q34 journalists, not farmers or a PR company — three groups of people appear in one sentence: a British PR company (who paid), journalists (who travelled) and avocado farmers (who were visited). The note says ‘were invited to visit growers’, so you need the travellers.
  • Q36 coffee, not supermarkets — the audio explicitly rejects supermarkets: ‘This proved far more effective than offering samples in supermarkets.’ A comparison sentence often contains the wrong answer and the right one.
  • Q39 price, not demand — ‘when demand peaked… the price soared’. ‘Demand’ is the cause, ‘price’ is the increase the note asks about. (cost is also accepted.)
  • Q40 soil, not fertility — the note reads ‘poor quality (40)’, so the gap needs the thing, not the quality of it. Audio: ‘the fertility of the soil decreased dramatically’.

trendn.a general direction in which things are changing or becoming popular潮流；趋势“The food industry is obsessed with finding the next big food trend.”The title word of the whole talk. Collocations to know: set / follow / spot / exploit a trend.
fashionn.a style or activity that is popular for a period of time流行；时尚“Interest in food fashions has risen rapidly.”Here it means a passing craze, not clothes. ‘Food fashions’ = ‘food trends’.

ambassadorn.someone chosen to represent and promote a brand品牌大使；代言人“…as brand ambassadors in return for free samples.”Already printed on the question paper, so use it to locate the influencer section, not as an answer.
veganadj./n.containing no animal products at all; a person who eats no animal products纯素的；纯素食者“Companies which sell vegan produce were pioneers…”Answer to Q32. Note the paraphrase: audio produce → note food brands. Spelling: v-e-g-a-n, not ‘vegen’.
producen.food that is grown or made to be sold农产品；食品“Companies which sell vegan produce…”As a NOUN the stress moves to the front (PRO-duce) and it means goods, not the verb ‘to produce’.
pioneern./v.the first person or group to do something new先驱；开创者“…were pioneers in being able to increase sales really quickly.”Signals ‘first’ or ‘earliest’. It flags that vegan brands are the example being named.
chefn.a professional cook, especially the head cook in a restaurant厨师；主厨“…interested in what well-known chefs are putting on their menus.”Answer to Q33. ‘Well-known’ = ‘Famous’ on the question paper. cooks is also accepted; keep the plural.
dedicatedadj.existing for one purpose only专门的；专职的“They have dedicated teams closely following which new products are trending.”Common academic adjective: a dedicated team / fund / website. Do not confuse with ‘devoted to a person’.
journalistn.a person who writes for newspapers or magazines记者“They paid for a group of journalists to travel out to South Africa.”Answer to Q34. The paraphrase chain is: paid for them to travel → ‘were invited to visit’. reporters is also accepted.
growern.a person or company that grows crops commercially种植者；种植园主“…to meet avocado farmers.” (question paper: growers)This is the note-paper word for the audio’s farmers. Recognising it stops you writing ‘farmers’ in gap 34 by mistake.
profilen.the amount of public attention something receives知名度；曝光度“A British PR company was hired to raise its profile.”Fixed phrase: raise / boost the profile of something = make it better known.
staplen.a basic food that people eat regularly主食；日常必需食品“…which at this time was certainly not the daily staple it’s since become.”Useful for describing how a luxury becomes normal. ‘It’s since become’ = it has become since then.
nutrientn.a substance in food that living things need to grow and stay healthy营养素；养分“…a superfood rich in nutrients and therefore beneficial for health.”Leads directly into Q35: ‘beneficial for health’ = ‘health benefits’.
beneficialadj.having a good effect有益的“…therefore beneficial for health.”The question paper uses the noun benefits; the audio uses the adjective. Same family, different form.
provocationn.deliberately doing something to annoy or challenge people挑衅；激怒“…a media campaign which used provocation as a way of getting their message across.”Explains the note ‘received publicity by upsetting competitors’ — the annoyance was intentional.
aggravatev.to make someone angry or annoyed激怒；使恼火“The fact that this campaign aggravated competitors… was seen as a plus.”‘Aggravated’ = ‘upset’ on the question paper. It can also mean ‘make worse’ — context decides.
retailn./adj.the selling of goods directly to the public in shops零售“The brand decided against a big retail launch.”‘Decided against’ = chose not to. This is the sentence just before Q36.
chainn.a group of shops or restaurants owned by the same company连锁店“…getting the product into coffee chains.”The note says coffee shops, the audio says coffee chains — the answer word coffee survives both.
alternativeadj.different from the usual or traditional kind替代的；另类的“…other alternative milk products such as almond milk.”Collocation: alternative milk / energy / medicine. Almond milk is the comparison item, not an answer.
carbon footprintn. phr.the total amount of greenhouse gas an activity produces碳足迹“…it also has a relatively low carbon footprint.”One of the two clues for Q37 environment, together with ‘requires less water’.
delicacyn.a rare or expensive food that people consider special珍馐；美味佳肴“Norwegian skrei, a rarely available seasonal fish delicacy…”The commas around this phrase are a free definition of ‘skrei’ — you do not need to know the word itself.
reputationn.the opinion people generally have about something声誉；口碑“…used to build the reputation of Norway’s fisheries in general.”Answer to Q38. Note-paper ‘strengthen’ = audio ‘build’; ‘Norwegian seafood’ = ‘Norway’s fisheries’.
sustainablyadv.in a way that does not damage the environment long term可持续地“The public can be encouraged to buy things which are more sustainably produced.”Belongs to the positive side of the argument. Do not mix it up with the environment clue for Q37, which is about oat milk.
surgen.a sudden large increase激增；猛涨“…the effects a surge in demand can cause.”Synonym family for Q39: surge / soar / peak / grow. All four appear in this final section.
soarv.to rise very quickly to a high level飙升“When demand peaked… the price soared.”Key to Q39. ‘The price soared’ = ‘an increase in its price’.
unaffordableadj.too expensive for people to be able to buy买不起的；负担不起的“…making it unaffordable for local people.”Prefix un- + afford + -able. This is the ethical problem the heading points to.
fertilityn.the ability of soil to produce good crops肥力；肥沃程度“…the fertility of the soil decreased dramatically.”Careful: ‘fertility’ is the quality, soil is the answer to Q40 because the note says ‘poor quality ___’.
desertificationn.the process by which fertile land turns into desert荒漠化“…which could potentially lead to desertification in some areas.”A long word, but you only need to recognise it — it is the consequence, not the answer.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening the audio never uses the exact words printed on the question paper. Recognising paraphrase is the whole skill. 雅思听力中，录音几乎不会重复题干原词——识别同义替换才是关键。
Paraphrase Example 1
Question wording“The growth in interest started with (31) of food being shared on social media”
What you hear“…since the birth of the smartphone, when people first began taking photos of their food and instantly sharing them with their friends.”
Key paraphrasebeing shared (passive on the paper) = sharing them (active in the audio). The gap needs the object of ‘taking’: photos.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question wording“Sales of (32) food brands have grown this way”
What you hear“Companies which sell vegan produce were pioneers in being able to increase sales really quickly in this way.”
Key paraphraseproduce (audio) = food brands (paper); increase sales quickly = sales have grown
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question wording“Famous (33) are influential”
What you hear“They have dedicated teams… particularly interested in what well-known chefs are putting on their menus.”
Key paraphrasefamous = well-known. ‘Are influential’ = supermarkets pay attention to them.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question wording“(34) were invited to visit growers in South Africa”
What you hear“A British PR company… paid for a group of journalists to travel out to South Africa to meet avocado farmers.”
Key paraphrasepaid for X to travel = X were invited; farmers = growers. Three groups of people, only one travelled.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 5
Question wording“Promotion through (36) shops reduced the need for advertising”
What you hear“The brand decided against a big retail launch in favour of getting the product into coffee chains, which removed the need for a big advertising budget.”
Key paraphrasechains = shops; removed the need for = reduced the need for. ‘Supermarkets’ is rejected in the very next sentence.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 6
Question wording“It appealed to consumers who are concerned about the (37)”
What you hear“Many consumers prefer it because it has less of an impact on the environment. It requires significantly less water… and a relatively low carbon footprint.”
Key paraphraseappealed to = consumers prefer it; concerned about the environment = less impact on the environment
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 7
Question wording“has helped strengthen the (38) of Norwegian seafood”
What you hear“The demand for skrei has been used by a food marketing agency to build the reputation of Norway’s fisheries in general.”
Key paraphrasestrengthen = build; Norwegian seafood = Norway’s fisheries
