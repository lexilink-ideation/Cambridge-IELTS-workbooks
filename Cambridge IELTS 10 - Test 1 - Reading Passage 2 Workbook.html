Reading Passage 2

Cambridge IELTS 10 — Test 1 — Reading Passage 2: European Transport Systems 1990–2010

Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

A policy report on European transport. Paragraphs A–C explain why transport demand keeps rising (more cars, a 'flow' economy, growth in EU candidate countries). Paragraphs D–E introduce sustainable development and the CO₂ problem. Paragraphs F–I then set out three possible policy options for shifting the balance away from road.

这是一篇“政策报告”类文章,九个段落 A–I。前半部分讲原因,后半部分讲三种方案。段落标题题的关键在于抓住每段的“唯一主题”。

💡 Reading tip: For Matching Headings (Q14–21), read the first and last sentence of each paragraph first. Paragraph F is given as the example (vii), which tells you the writer's central idea is rebalancing — use that to eliminate. Paragraphs G, H and I are the three options, so their headings must be x, ii and iv in that order: pricing alone → pricing plus efficiency → pricing plus everything.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Although modern information technologies can reduce the demand for physical transport by facilitating teleworking and teleservices, the requirement for transport continues to increase.
Main Clausethe requirement for transport continues to increase
Subordinate / Relative'Although modern information technologies can reduce the demand for physical transport' = concession clause; 'by facilitating teleworking and teleservices' = prepositional phrase of means
Grammar Note'Although… , main clause' — the writer admits a counter-argument first, then states the real point. The main clause is always the point.
Simple MeaningTechnology could cut travel, but transport demand is still going up.
中文尽管现代信息技术可以通过促进远程办公和远程服务来减少对实体运输的需求,但运输需求仍在持续增长。
This phenomenon has been emphasised by the relocation of some industries, particularly those which are labour intensive, to reduce production costs, even though the production site is hundreds or even thousands of kilometres away from the final assembly plant or away from users.
Main ClauseThis phenomenon has been emphasised by the relocation of some industries
Subordinate / Relative'particularly those which are labour intensive' = relative clause narrowing the noun; 'to reduce production costs' = purpose infinitive; 'even though the production site is … away from users' = concession clause
Grammar NotePassive + three stacked modifiers. The 'even though' clause is what makes Q23 FALSE — factories move FAR FROM users, not closer.
Simple MeaningFactories, especially those needing many workers, moved to cut costs — even though they ended up thousands of kilometres from where the goods are used.
中文这一现象因某些产业(尤其是劳动密集型产业)为降低生产成本而迁移而更加突出,尽管生产地点距离最终组装厂或用户有数百甚至数千公里之遥。
This objective, agreed by the Gothenburg European Council, has to be achieved by integrating environmental considerations into Community policies, and shifting the balance between modes of transport lies at the heart of its strategy.
Main ClauseThis objective has to be achieved by integrating environmental considerations into Community policies (+ and) shifting the balance … lies at the heart of its strategy
Subordinate / Relative'agreed by the Gothenburg European Council' = reduced relative clause ('which was agreed by…')
Grammar NoteTwo independent clauses joined by 'and'. The second has an -ing phrase as its SUBJECT ('shifting the balance… lies…').
Simple MeaningThe Gothenburg Council agreed this goal; it must be met by putting the environment into EU policy, and its central idea is moving traffic from road to other modes.
中文这一由哥德堡欧洲理事会商定的目标,必须通过将环境因素纳入共同体政策来实现,而调整各运输方式之间的平衡正是其战略的核心。
According to the latest estimates, if nothing is done to reverse the traffic growth trend, CO₂ emissions from transport can be expected to increase by around 50% to 1,113 billion tonnes by 2020, compared with the 739 billion tonnes recorded in 1990.
Main ClauseCO₂ emissions from transport can be expected to increase by around 50% to 1,113 billion tonnes by 2020
Subordinate / Relative'if nothing is done to reverse the traffic growth trend' = conditional clause; 'compared with the 739 billion tonnes recorded in 1990' = comparison phrase with reduced relative
Grammar NoteConditional + passive + 'increase BY 50%' vs 'increase TO 1,113 billion'. BY = the size of the change; TO = the final figure. This distinction decides Q26.
Simple MeaningUnless traffic growth is stopped, transport CO₂ will rise about 50% — from 739 billion tonnes in 1990 to 1,113 billion by 2020.
中文根据最新估算,如果不采取措施扭转交通增长趋势,到2020年运输业二氧化碳排放预计将增长约50%,达到11130亿吨,而1990年记录的是7390亿吨。
Such a change cannot be achieved overnight, all the less so after over half a century of constant deterioration in favour of road.
Main ClauseSuch a change cannot be achieved overnight
Subordinate / Relative'all the less so after over half a century of constant deterioration in favour of road' = elliptical comparative phrase (= 'and it is even less possible after…')
Grammar Note'All the less so' is a compressed comparative. Learn it as a fixed chunk.
Simple MeaningThis change will take a long time, especially after 50+ years of things getting worse in road's favour.
中文这样的改变不可能一蹴而就,在经历了半个多世纪不断向公路倾斜的恶化之后就更是如此。
It is far more ambitious than it looks, bearing in mind the historical imbalance in favour of roads for the last fifty years, but would achieve a marked break in the link between road transport growth and economic growth, without placing restrictions on the mobility of people and goods.
Main ClauseIt is far more ambitious than it looks … but would achieve a marked break in the link
Subordinate / Relative'bearing in mind the historical imbalance…' = participle phrase meaning 'if you consider'; 'without placing restrictions on the mobility of people and goods' = prepositional phrase of exclusion
Grammar NoteComparative + participial aside + contrast 'but' + negative prepositional phrase. Note the subject 'It' is shared by 'is' and 'would achieve'.
Simple MeaningThe third approach is harder than it seems, but it would separate road growth from economic growth without limiting people's freedom to travel.
中文考虑到过去五十年历史上向公路倾斜的失衡,这一方案远比看上去更有雄心,但它能显著切断公路运输增长与经济增长之间的联系,同时不限制人员和货物的流动性。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

1. Cause & effect (原因与结果). Paragraphs A–C are a causal chain: rising demand ← two key factors (car growth for passengers; economic change for goods) ← abolished frontiers and industrial relocation. Signal words: behind this trend, due to, this phenomenon has been emphasised by, will also increase.

2. Contrast & concession (对比与让步). The writer constantly admits a point before rejecting it: 'Although modern information technologies can reduce demand… the requirement continues to increase'; 'even though the production site is thousands of kilometres away'; 'However, a new imperative…'; 'However, the lack of measures…'; 'However, this approach does not include investment'. Every 'However' in G, H and I introduces the weakness of an option — and that weakness is what the headings test.

3. Comparison (比较). Numbers are used comparatively, not absolutely: exports 'more than twice their 1990 volumes', imports 'more than five times'; road haulage +19.4% while rail haulage −43.5%; 84% of transport CO₂ from road; 8% market share for rail freight. Whenever you see two figures side by side, expect a TRUE/FALSE item.

4. Problem & solution (问题与方案). This is the master structure of the passage. Problem: transport demand and CO₂ are rising (A–E). Goal: modal rebalance (F). Solutions: option 1 = pricing only (G); option 2 = pricing + efficiency of other modes (H); option 3 = pricing + revitalisation + investment (I). The options get progressively more complete — and only the third works.

5. Chronology (时间顺序). 1990 (baseline) → 1990–1998 (candidate-country haulage figures) → 1998 (emissions and market shares) → 2010 (car fleet growth) → 2020 (objective 'can only be fully achieved by 2020') → 2040 ('a sustainable transport system … in 30 years' time'). Q25 tests whether you notice that the Gothenburg Council is recent, not 30 years old — the '30 years' refers to the future.

6. Author's attitude (作者态度). The writer is a policy analyst, not a neutral reporter. Loaded words: the main culprit, to blame for, constant deterioration, struggling along, saturated arteries, the most polluting. The preferred solution is clearly the third: 'far more ambitious than it looks' and 'would achieve a marked break'. Options 1 and 2 are dismissed with 'impossible', 'not enough to guarantee'.
