"Making Every Drop Count" — Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

"Making Every Drop Count" traces the history of human water management — from Roman aqueducts, through the massive dam-building of the 19th–20th centuries, to today's global water crisis, where over a billion people still lack clean drinking water. The passage then turns to a hopeful, surprising trend: demand for water in developed nations is falling, thanks to smarter technology and changing priorities, not just new infrastructure.

The passage has eight paragraphs (A–H) and is structured as a "history → problem → surprising solution" argument, ending with a balanced view: new infrastructure is still needed in poorer regions, but it must be built to higher standards.

💡 Reading strategy tip: This passage uses a classic "problem-solution" essay structure. Before reading the questions, identify which paragraphs describe problems (C, D) and which describe solutions/trends (E, F, G) — this will help you match headings quickly in Q14–20.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"As the United Nations report on access to water reiterated in November 2001, more than one billion people lack access to clean drinking water; some two and a half billion do not have adequate sanitation services."
Main Clausemore than one billion people lack access to clean drinking water; some two and a half billion do not have adequate sanitation services (two independent clauses joined by a semicolon)
Subordinate / Relative Clause"As the United Nations report... reiterated in November 2001" (introductory adverbial clause citing a source)
Grammar Note"As" here means "just as / in line with what," introducing evidence. The semicolon joins two closely related independent clauses without a conjunction.
Simple MeaningA UN report confirmed that over a billion people don't have clean water, and 2.5 billion lack proper sanitation.
中文正如联合国在2001年11月重申的报告所说，超过十亿人无法获得清洁饮用水；约二十五亿人缺乏充分的卫生设施。
"Tens of millions of people have been forced to move from their homes – often with little warning or compensation – to make way for the reservoirs behind dams."
Main ClauseTens of millions of people have been forced to move from their homes
Subordinate / Relative Clause"often with little warning or compensation" (inserted adverbial phrase, set off by dashes); "to make way for the reservoirs behind dams" (infinitive purpose clause)
Grammar NotePassive voice ("have been forced") emphasises the people as victims of the action; the dash-enclosed phrase adds detail without breaking the main clause's flow.
Simple MeaningTens of millions of people had to leave their homes — often suddenly and without payment — so that dam reservoirs could be built.
中文数千万人被迫离开家园——往往几乎没有任何预警或补偿——只为给水坝后面的水库腾出空间。
"Some water experts are now demanding that existing infrastructure be used in smarter ways rather than building new facilities, which is increasingly considered the option of last, not first, resort."
Main ClauseSome water experts are now demanding that existing infrastructure be used in smarter ways
Subordinate / Relative Clause"rather than building new facilities" (comparative gerund phrase); "which is increasingly considered the option of last, not first, resort" (non-defining relative clause referring back to "building new facilities")
Grammar Note"demand that + subjunctive (be used)" is a formal structure common in academic writing; "which" clauses after a comma add extra, non-essential information.
Simple MeaningExperts want current water systems used more cleverly instead of building new ones — building new facilities is now seen as a last resort.
中文一些水资源专家现在要求更明智地利用现有基础设施，而不是新建设施，新建设施如今越来越被视为最后而非首选的手段。
"Although population, industrial output and economic productivity have continued to soar in developed nations, the rate at which people withdraw water from aquifers, rivers and lakes has slowed."
Main Clause...the rate... has slowed
Subordinate / Relative Clause"Although population... have continued to soar" (concession clause); "at which people withdraw water from aquifers, rivers and lakes" (relative clause modifying "the rate")
Grammar Note"Although" introduces a contrast; "at which" is a relative pronoun structure used with prepositions describing measurable rates.
Simple MeaningEven though population and economic activity have kept growing, the speed at which water is being taken from natural sources has actually decreased.
中文尽管发达国家的人口、工业产出和经济生产力持续飙升，但人们从含水层、河流和湖泊中取水的速度却放缓了。
"And even in regions where new projects seem warranted, we must find ways to meet demands with fewer resources, respecting ecological criteria and to a smaller budget."
Main Clausewe must find ways to meet demands with fewer resources
Subordinate / Relative Clause"where new projects seem warranted" (relative clause modifying "regions"); "respecting ecological criteria" (participle phrase adding a condition)
Grammar Note"where" introduces a relative clause referring to a place/situation; "warranted" here is a formal adjective meaning "justified/necessary."
Simple MeaningEven in places that genuinely need new water projects, they must be built using fewer resources, respecting the environment, and on a smaller budget.
中文即使在确实需要新项目的地区，我们也必须找到方法，用更少的资源、尊重生态标准并在更小的预算内满足需求。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect

Cause: Dams and water withdrawals destroy free-flowing river ecosystems. → Effect: More than 20% of freshwater fish species are threatened or endangered.

Contrast

The passage contrasts the 20th-century philosophy ("more for some") with the emerging 21st-century philosophy ("some for all") — a shift from maximum extraction to equitable, efficient use.

Comparison

Water access is compared across time: Roman citizens received "as much water per person as is provided in many parts of the industrial world today," while half the world's population today has services inferior to the ancient Greeks and Romans.

Examples

Japan's water productivity is used as a concrete example: it used 13 million gallons of water per $1 million of output in 1965, dropping to 3.5 million gallons by 1989 — illustrating "a quadrupling of water productivity."

Definition

A footnote defines "aquifers" as "underground stores of water" — a small but important definition for understanding paragraph D.

Problem / Solution

Problem: Over a billion people lack clean water; water infrastructure has caused displacement and environmental damage. Solution proposed: use existing infrastructure more efficiently, prioritise "some for all," and build any new projects to higher ecological standards.

Chronology

The passage moves through time: Roman Empire → industrial revolution (19th–20th centuries) → UN report (November 2001) → 1965 and 1989 Japan data → new millennium's shift in philosophy.

Author's Attitude

The author is cautiously optimistic: acknowledging real progress ("fortunately — and unexpectedly") while still stressing serious unresolved problems, and calling for continued reform rather than complacency.
