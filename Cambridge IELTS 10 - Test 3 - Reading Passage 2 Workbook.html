Reading Passage 2

Cambridge IELTS 10 — Test 3 — Reading Passage 2: Autumn Leaves

Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

Why do some leaves turn red in autumn? Yellow and orange are easy — they are simply revealed when chlorophyll breaks down. But red requires the tree to manufacture new pigments (anthocyanins) just as it is shutting down. The writer reviews several theories and concludes that the 'light screen' hypothesis is the most plausible.

这是一篇科普说明文。结构非常清晰:现象 → 已知的部分 → 谜题 → 几种理论(逐一否定) → 最可信的假说 → 证据 → 仍未解决的问题。

💡 Reading tip: Paragraphs D and E each present a theory and then knock it down ('However there are problems with each of these theories'; 'The flaw in this theory lies in the lack of proof'). Paragraph F introduces the theory the writer accepts, and paragraph H gives the evidence. Knowing which paragraph does what answers Q14–18 almost instantly.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

So rather than maintaining the now redundant leaves throughout the winter, the tree saves its precious resources and discards them.
Main Clausethe tree saves its precious resources and discards them
Subordinate / Relative'rather than maintaining the now redundant leaves throughout the winter' = prepositional phrase with a gerund, expressing rejected alternative
Grammar Note'Rather than + -ing' at the front of a sentence sets up a contrast before the main clause arrives. This is the direct answer to Q15.
Simple MeaningInstead of keeping useless leaves all winter, the tree keeps its resources and drops the leaves.
中文因此,与其整个冬天维持这些已然多余的叶子,树木不如保存自己宝贵的资源,把叶子舍弃掉。
But it is hard to make sense of the manufacture of anthocyanins — why should a tree bother making new chemicals in its leaves when it's already scrambling to withdraw and preserve the ones already there?
Main Clauseit is hard to make sense of the manufacture of anthocyanins
Subordinate / Relativeafter the dash, a rhetorical question containing a 'when'-clause ('when it's already scrambling to withdraw and preserve the ones already there')
Grammar NoteA dash followed by a rhetorical question is a common way of stating a research puzzle. 'The ones' = the chemicals already in the leaf.
Simple MeaningIt makes no sense: why build new chemicals in leaves you are already emptying out?
中文但花青素的合成很难说得通——当树木已经在手忙脚乱地回收并保存叶中原有的化学物质时,它为何还要费力在叶子里制造新的化学物质呢?
However there are problems with each of these theories, including the fact that leaves are red for such a relatively short period that the expense of energy needed to manufacture the anthocyanins would outweigh any anti-fungal or anti-herbivore activity achieved.
Main Clausethere are problems with each of these theories
Subordinate / Relative'including the fact that…' = appositive noun clause; inside it, 'such … that …' = result structure; 'needed to manufacture the anthocyanins' = reduced relative
Grammar Note'Such a relatively short period THAT the expense … would outweigh…' — the 'such … that' pattern expresses cause and result. This kills the theories in Q23.
Simple MeaningAll these theories fail: leaves stay red so briefly that making the pigment costs more energy than it saves.
中文然而,这些理论各有问题,其中包括:叶片变红的时间如此之短,以至于制造花青素所需的能量消耗,会超过由此获得的任何抗真菌或抗食草动物的效益。
It sounds paradoxical, because the idea behind this hypothesis is that the red pigment is made in autumn leaves to protect chlorophyll, the light-absorbing chemical, from too much light.
Main ClauseIt sounds paradoxical
Subordinate / Relative'because the idea behind this hypothesis is that…' = reason clause containing a that-clause; 'the light-absorbing chemical' = appositive defining chlorophyll; 'to protect … from too much light' = purpose infinitive
Grammar NoteThe appositive is the source of the paradox: the light-absorbing chemical needs protection from light. Q24 tests exactly this.
Simple MeaningIt seems contradictory: the red pigment protects chlorophyll — the chemical that absorbs light — from too much light.
中文这听起来自相矛盾:因为这一假说背后的观点是,秋叶中制造红色色素,是为了保护叶绿素——这种吸光化学物质——免受过强光照的伤害。
The energy absorbed by the chlorophyll molecules of the unstable autumn leaf is not immediately channelled into useful products and processes, as it would be in an intact summer leaf.
Main ClauseThe energy … is not immediately channelled into useful products and processes
Subordinate / Relative'absorbed by the chlorophyll molecules of the unstable autumn leaf' = reduced relative; 'as it would be in an intact summer leaf' = comparison clause with ellipsis
Grammar NoteThe subject is separated from its verb by 13 words. 'As it would be' = 'as it would be channelled' — the verb is left out.
Simple MeaningIn an unstable autumn leaf the absorbed energy is not used straight away, unlike in a healthy summer leaf.
中文不稳定的秋叶中叶绿素分子所吸收的能量,不会像在完好的夏叶中那样被立即引导进入有用的产物和过程。
It has also been recognised for decades that the best conditions for intense red colours are dry, sunny days and cool nights, conditions that nicely match those that make leaves susceptible to excess light.
Main ClauseIt has also been recognised for decades that the best conditions … are dry, sunny days and cool nights
Subordinate / Relative'conditions that nicely match those that make leaves susceptible to excess light' = appositive with two nested relative clauses
Grammar Note'It has been recognised that…' = an impersonal passive used to report accepted knowledge. Note 'those' = 'the conditions'. This sentence supplies Q21.
Simple MeaningPeople have long known that dry sunny days and cool nights give the reddest leaves — the same conditions that make leaves vulnerable to too much light.
中文几十年来人们也已认识到,产生浓烈红色的最佳条件是干燥晴朗的白天和凉爽的夜晚——这些条件恰好与使叶片易受过量光照伤害的条件相吻合。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

1. Problem & solution (问题与方案). This is the clearest structure in the passage. Observation (A): leaves turn different colours. Partial explanation (B): yellow and orange are simply unmasked as chlorophyll is withdrawn. Remaining puzzle (C): red requires new chemicals to be manufactured at exactly the wrong moment. Candidate solutions (D, E) — both rejected. Preferred solution (F–G): the light-screen hypothesis. Evidence (H). Unsolved remainder (I).

2. Theory and rebuttal (提出与反驳). Learn the rebuttal signals: 'However there are problems with each of these theories, including the fact that…' (D); 'The flaw in this theory lies in the lack of proof… No one has as yet ascertained whether…' (E). Both rebuttals use the same logic — the theory is unsupported or the cost exceeds the benefit. Where a paragraph both states and destroys a theory, questions will ask which paragraph contains the idea, not whether it is true.

3. Cause & effect (因果). The light-screen chain: less sunlight in autumn → tree abandons photosynthesis → chlorophyll is dismantled → the leaf becomes unstable → absorbed energy is not channelled into useful processes → excited chlorophyll produces destructive oxygen → anthocyanin acts as a 'sunblock'. Being able to recite this chain answers Q16, Q24 and Q26 together.

4. Evidence (证据). Paragraph H is a list of four observable clues, each introduced plainly: 'One is straightforward' (the sunniest side is reddest); 'Not only that' (the upper surface is brighter); 'It has also been recognised for decades' (dry sunny days and cool nights); 'And finally' (redder further north). These four clues are exactly the four gaps in Q19–22 — the note-completion task is a compressed version of one paragraph.

5. Concession (让步). The writer keeps admitting difficulties: 'It sounds paradoxical'; 'Why does chlorophyll need protection when it is the natural world's supreme light absorber?'; 'Chlorophyll, although exquisitely evolved to capture the energy of sunlight, can sometimes be overwhelmed by it'. Rhetorical questions are the writer's way of anticipating the reader's objection before answering it.

6. What remains unexplained (未解之谜). Paragraph I is short but important for Q26: the writer explicitly says 'What is still not fully understood … is why some trees resort to producing red pigments while others don't bother.' So option D of Q26 is what the passage says it cannot explain. Similarly conifers (A) are mentioned only as 'an exception' with no explanation, and insect egg-laying behaviour (C) is explicitly unproven. Only the yellow/orange mechanism (B) is fully explained.
