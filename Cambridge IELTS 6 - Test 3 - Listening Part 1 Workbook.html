Cambridge IELTS 6 — Test 3 — Listening Part 1

Opening a Bank Account · Practice Workbook

📋 Quick Review

Pieter Henes, a Dutch engineer newly arrived in the UK, visits a bank to open a current account. A woman (bank clerk) asks him a series of standard questions to fill in the application form: his name, date of birth, address, phone numbers, occupation, a security question, how much money he wants to deposit, and how often he wants statements. At the end she offers him extra services, like internet banking.

This is a classic Part 1 form-completion task: two speakers, everyday transactional English, and answers that come in the same order as the questions.

💡 Part 1 strategy: Before listening, read every label in the form and guess what type of answer is needed — a name, a number, a date, an amount of money. Watch for the clerk asking Pieter to repeat or correct information (his phone number changes mid-sentence!) — the corrected version is always the answer.

Unit at a glance

📅 Book: Cambridge IELTS 6  |  🎧 Test: 3, Section 1  |  ❓ Questions: 1–10

📝 Task type: Form completion — ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER for each answer

🗣️ Setting: A bank branch — Pieter is opening a current account

👥 Speakers: Pieter Henes (customer, Dutch, an engineer) · Woman (bank clerk)

Task breakdown

Q1–9 (Form completion): Personal and account details — account type, birth date, address, phone, occupation, security question, opening deposit, statement frequency.

Q10 (Form completion): An extra request Pieter makes at the end of the call — about a bank service he wants information on.

🔄 Paraphrase Training

IELTS Listening questions never use the exact same words as the audio. Study each script excerpt, note the key paraphrase, then practise.
Q2 — Date of Birth (Spoken Date Format)
Question wordingDate of birth: ______
Exact script line
PIETER: The twenty-seventh of the first, nineteen seventy-three.
Key paraphrase"the first" = January (month 1)  |  Spoken day-month-year → written as 27/01/1973

Q4 — Time at Address (Correcting an Assumption)
Question wordingTime at current address: ______
Exact script line
WOMAN: Is it more than two years?PIETER: Ah, just two weeks actually. I only arrived in the country a month ago.
Key paraphrase⚠️ Distractor: the woman guesses "two years" — Pieter corrects her to "two weeks". Always trust the correction, not the guess.
Your practice
Q5 — Phone Number (Self-Correction)
Question wordingTelephone: work ______
Exact script line
PIETER: The number at my office is six-oh-six-two-nine-five. Um, just a minute, I'd better check. Oh, no sorry, six-one-six.
Key paraphrase"I'd better check" / "no, sorry" = a self-correction is coming — final number is 616295, not 606295
Your practice
Q6 — Occupation (Two Roles Mentioned)
Question wordingOccupation: ______
Exact script line
PIETER: Well, I'm in Britain as a project manager, but that's not my main job. I'm an engineer by profession.
Key paraphrase"by profession" = the true, official occupation  |  "project manager" is only his current role, not his profession

Q7 — Security Question (Reason Given)
Question wordingIdentity (security): name of his ______ : Siti

PIETER: Like, erm, my wife's first name?WOMAN: Mother's might be better. It's less likely to be known.
Key paraphraseThe clerk rejects Pieter's suggestion and proposes "mother's" name instead — Siti is actually his wife's name, but it is recorded under "mother" for the form
