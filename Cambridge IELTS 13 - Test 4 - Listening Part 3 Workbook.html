Listening Part 3

Cambridge IELTS 13 — Test 4 — Listening Part 3

Practice Workbook

📋 Quick Review

Jack and Alice review their project on nutritional food labels. They discuss how the project changed their own shopping habits, pizza labels, the daily value system, chicken-flavour crisps and the research evidence (Q21–26). They then criticise the traffic-light labelling system and the study behind it (Q27–30, choose TWO twice).

💡 Listening tip: This conversation is full of agreement and disagreement. When a question says 'Jack agrees with Alice', the answer must be something BOTH say. Track who says what — Alice usually raises the point and Jack confirms it.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

Agreement questions require you to hear the confirmation, not just the claim. Practise spotting it.
Q23 — the pizza label
Question wording
Jack agrees with Alice that the nutritional label is misleading.
What you hear
Alice: 'the nutritional values given are for half a pizza' → Jack: 'When most people eat the whole pizza. Not exactly transparent, is it?'
Key paraphrase
not transparent = misleading; Jack's reply is the agreement → C
Your turn
Q24 — the daily value system
Question wording
because it is more comprehensive
What you hear
'I'm not sure it's the easiest for people to use but at least you get the full story… not just fat, salt and sugar'
Key paraphrase
the full story / all the ingredients = comprehensive → C (and 'not the easiest' rules out A)
Your turn
Q26 — the research
Question wording
It did not produce clear results.
What you hear
'those findings weren't that conclusive and it was quite a small-scale study'
Key paraphrase
not conclusive = not clear → A
Your turn
Q29&30 — the participants
Question wording
Which TWO things are true about the participants?
What you hear
'it's good to get feedback from all socio-economic groups' … 'if they hadn't interviewed participants face-to-face, they could have used a bigger sample'
Key paraphrase
the counterfactual 'if they hadn't' proves they DID → D and E
Your turn
