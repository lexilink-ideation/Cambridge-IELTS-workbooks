Cambridge IELTS 10 — Test 1 — Reading Passage 1: Stepwells

Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

Stepwells are stone wells with steps leading down to the water, built in the dry north-western Indian states of Gujarat and Rajasthan from the sixth century onwards. They gave people clean groundwater in the dry season, but they were also social, religious and architectural spaces. Most later fell into disuse; today the Archaeological Survey of India is restoring the most important ones.

这是一篇“历史建筑 + 保护”类文章。结构是:发明背景 → 建筑特点 → 衰落 → 具体例子 → 现状保护。后半段的四个具体例子是表格题的答案来源。

💡 Reading tip: Questions 9–13 are a table about four named stepwells (Rani Ki Vav, Surya Kund, Raniji Ki Baori, Chand Baori, Neemrana Ki Baori). Each well has its own paragraph, and the paragraphs come in the same order as the table rows. Scan for the italicised names and the dates — they are the fastest locators in the whole passage.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

During the sixth and seventh centuries, the inhabitants of the modern-day states of Gujarat and Rajasthan in north-western India developed a method of gaining access to clean, fresh groundwater during the dry season for drinking, bathing, watering animals and irrigation.
Main Clausethe inhabitants … developed a method
Subordinate / Relative'of gaining access to clean, fresh groundwater' = prepositional -ing phrase defining 'method'; 'during the dry season' + 'for drinking, bathing, watering animals and irrigation' = adverbials of time and purpose
Grammar NoteOne short main clause buried under three long modifiers. Find the verb 'developed' first — everything else is detail.
Simple Meaning1,400 years ago, people in north-west India invented a way to reach clean underground water in the dry season.
中文在六、七世纪,印度西北部现今古吉拉特邦和拉贾斯坦邦的居民,发明了一种在旱季获取干净淡水地下水的方法,用于饮用、洗浴、饮畜和灌溉。
However, the significance of this invention — the stepwell — goes beyond its utilitarian application.
Main Clausethe significance of this invention goes beyond its utilitarian application
Subordinate / Relative'— the stepwell —' = appositive between dashes, renaming 'this invention'
Grammar NoteDashes give a definition for free. 'Goes beyond X' = 'is more than just X' — this is the sentence that makes Q2 TRUE.
Simple MeaningThe stepwell mattered for more than just practical reasons.
中文然而,这项发明——阶梯井——的意义远不止于其实用功能。
As their name suggests, stepwells comprise a series of stone steps descending from ground level to the water source (normally an underground aquifer) as it recedes following the rains.
Main Clausestepwells comprise a series of stone steps
Subordinate / Relative'descending from ground level to the water source' = reduced relative ('which descend'); 'as it recedes following the rains' = time/manner clause; '(normally an underground aquifer)' = bracketed gloss
Grammar Note'As their name suggests' is a discourse marker, not a clause you need. 'As' in the second half means 'while / at the same rate that'.
Simple MeaningStepwells are stone steps that go down to the water, which sinks lower after the rainy season ends.
中文顾名思义,阶梯井由一系列石阶组成,从地面向下延伸至水源(通常是地下含水层),而水位在雨季之后会逐渐下降。
When the water level was high, the user needed only to descend a few steps to reach it; when it was low, several levels would have to be negotiated.
Main Clausethe user needed only to descend a few steps / several levels would have to be negotiated
Subordinate / Relativetwo parallel 'when'-clauses of time, joined by a semicolon
Grammar NoteBalanced contrast: high water → few steps; low water → many levels. This is the exact evidence for Q5 (TRUE).
Simple MeaningIf the water was high you walked down a few steps; if it was low you had to go much further down.
中文水位高时,使用者只需走下几级台阶就能取水;水位低时,则必须走过好几层。
Down the centuries, thousands of wells were constructed throughout north-western India, but the majority have now fallen into disuse; many are derelict and dry, as groundwater has been diverted for industrial use and the wells no longer reach the water table.
Main Clausethousands of wells were constructed … but the majority have now fallen into disuse
Subordinate / Relative'as groundwater has been diverted for industrial use and the wells no longer reach the water table' = double reason clause introduced by 'as'
Grammar NoteContrast ('but') + semicolon expansion + causal 'as'. Three ideas in one sentence: built → abandoned → why.
Simple MeaningThousands were built, but most are now unused and dry because the groundwater is taken for industry.
中文几个世纪以来,印度西北部建造了数千口井,但大多数如今已废弃;许多已破败干涸,因为地下水被引作工业用途,井已无法到达地下水位。
Incredibly, in January 2001, this ancient structure survived an earthquake that measured 7.6 on the Richter scale.
Main Clausethis ancient structure survived an earthquake
Subordinate / Relative'that measured 7.6 on the Richter scale' = defining relative clause describing the earthquake
Grammar NoteSimple past + relative clause. Note 'Incredibly' — an evaluative adverb showing the writer's attitude.
Simple MeaningAmazingly, the old building was not destroyed by the big 2001 earthquake.
中文令人惊叹的是,2001年1月,这座古建筑经受住了里氏7.6级的地震。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

1. Cause & effect (原因与结果). The passage repeatedly links a physical cause to a human result. Groundwater falls in the dry season → people build steps down to it. Groundwater is diverted for industrial use → wells no longer reach the water table → they become derelict. Signal words: as, because, due to, so that.

2. Contrast (对比). Two contrasts run through the text. First, utilitarian versus social/religious/artistic function ('goes beyond its utilitarian application'). Second, decline versus restoration ('the majority have now fallen into disuse' … However, 'some important sites … have recently undergone major restoration'). The word However at the start of a paragraph almost always marks a turn.

3. Definition (定义). Difficult words are explained in place, usually inside brackets or dashes: 'a water source (normally an underground aquifer)', 'a tank (kund means reservoir or pond)', '— the stepwell —'. Never panic at a technical noun; the definition is usually within five words.

4. Exemplification (举例). The second half of the passage is a chain of examples, each opened by a signpost: 'the finest current example', 'Another example is…', 'Rajasthan also has a wealth of wells', 'One of the larger examples is…', 'Still in public use is…'. Use these five signposts to jump straight to the table answers instead of reading every line.

5. Chronology (时间顺序). Dates order the whole article: 6th–7th centuries (invention) → late 11th century (Rani Ki Vav) → 1026 (Surya Kund) → 850 AD (Chand Baori) → 1699 (Raniji Ki Baori) → c.1700 (Neemrana) → 1960s (restoration begins) → 1996–2004 (drought) → 2001 (earthquake) → 'last year' (state announcement). Notice the examples are not in date order — they are grouped by state, so do not assume chronology in the table.

6. Author's attitude (作者态度). The writer is clearly admiring: spectacular, impressive, incredibly, stunning, aesthetically … most dramatic, architectural marvels, gaze in wonder. There is no criticism of the wells, only regret at 'years of neglect'. Knowing this helps with any YES/NO/NOT GIVEN-style reasoning.
