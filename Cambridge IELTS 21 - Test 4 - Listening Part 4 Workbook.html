📋 Quick Review

This Part 4 lecture is about how music therapy can help patients recover from surgery. It explains why patients feel uncomfortable after operations, reviews a large study of recorded music in post-operative care, and reports the findings — including shorter hospital stays and less need for pain medication.

💡 Listening strategy tip: Part 4 note-completion needs ONE word, usually a noun or adjective. Listen for the results the researchers "discovered" or "found", and for the exact describing word (e.g. the music had a calming quality).

Derived Test Answers (Q31–40)

Key facts
~100 trials, 7,000 patients played recorded music
the study reviewed all available evidence.
Result 1
patients felt happier and more satisfied
post-operative phase.
Result 2
shorter length of hospital stay
vs. patients with no music.
Music type
a wide variety, but with a calming quality
the common factor.
Delivery
headphones or music pillows (audible to one person)
tested before/during/after surgery.
Result 3
no or only slight anxiety
non-music patients reported more dissatisfaction.
Result 4
needed less pain medication
cross-checked against medical notes.
Remarkable
worked even under general anaesthetic
type, choice and timing made little difference.
