📋 Quick Review

A lecture on conflict at work for human resources students. The speaker defines workplace conflict, gives its causes (the need to show superiority, personality clashes, 'structural' loyalty to one's own team), then looks separately at CEOs and other managers, and finishes with ways to minimise conflict. Note completion, ONE WORD ONLY.

💡 Listening tip: 'ONE WORD ONLY' means a compound answer like 'side effects' is impossible here — if you hear two words, one of them is the answer. Also note Q34 sits inside quotation marks in the notes ('___' conflicts), which tells you the speaker will name the term explicitly.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

Lecture notes rename ideas. Practise going from the speaker's full clause to a single note word.
Q32 — the motive
Question note
Often a result of people wanting to prove their ___
What you hear
'someone who feels the need to show their superiority over someone else'
Key paraphrase
wanting to prove = feels the need to show → superiority
Your turn
Q35 — the consequence
Question note
Conflict-related stress can cause ___ that may last for months
What you hear
'very stressful for victims, resulting in their absence from work, possibly for months'
Key paraphrase
cause ___ = resulting in ___; may last for months = possibly for months → absence
Your turn
Q38 — the surprising finding
Question note
A structure that is more ___ may create a feeling of uncertainty about who staff should report to
What you hear
'Interestingly, though, a company with a more democratic business model can suffer more, when uncertainty about who to report to leads to conflicting demands'
Key paraphrase
'Interestingly, though' warns you the obvious answer (autocratic) is wrong → democratic
Your turn
Q40 — the outsider's role
Question note
Someone from outside the company may be given the role of ___
What you hear
'an external mediator might be able to help… precisely because they're independent'
Key paraphrase
someone from outside = external → mediator
Your turn

🧠 Mini Dictation

HR-lecture chunks. Note the definition frames: 'by which I mean…', 'These conflicts are called…'.
1.a whole range of behaviours that the victim finds unacceptable
2.one or more people behaving abusively or aggressively against another
3.the need to show their superiority over someone else
4.the personality of one is in some way incompatible with that of the other
5.resulting in their absence from work, possibly for months
6.confidence, that is, the belief that they're capable of great achievements
7.uncertainty about who to report to leads to conflicting demands
8.give feedback that both sides will accept, precisely because they're independent
