📋 Quick Review

A lecturer explains why groups of whales and dolphins sometimes swim onto beaches and die — events called mass strandings. He reviews five competing theories: parasites, toxins, accidental strandings, human activity and group behaviour, ending with a reading recommendation. Note completion — NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS.

💡 Listening tip: The question paper gives you the theory headings (Parasites, Toxins, Accidental Strandings, Human Activity, Group Behaviour). Use them as anchors — each new heading in the talk is announced ('The first is…', 'Another theory is…', 'Alternatively…', 'A final theory…'). If you lose one answer, jump to the next heading rather than freezing.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

Part 4 notes compress long academic sentences. Practise going from the sentence to the two-word note.
Q32 — What parasites damage
Question note
some parasites can affect marine animals' ______, which they depend on for navigation
What you hear
'a type of worm is commonly found in the ears of dead whales. Since marine animals rely heavily on their hearing to navigate…'
Key paraphrase
'depend on for navigation' = 'rely heavily on… to navigate' → hearing / ears
Your turn
Q34 — Why the chase theory fails
Question note
the majority of animals were not ______ when they stranded
What you hear
'examination of the animals' stomach contents reveal that most had not been feeding as they stranded'
Key paraphrase
'the majority' = 'most'; 'as they stranded' = 'when they stranded' → feeding
Your turn
Q37 — Why the Bahamas case was unusual
Question note
the whales were not in a ______
What you hear
'the animals were spread out along 38 kilometres of coast, whereas it's more common for the animals to be found in a group'
Key paraphrase
'spread out along 38 km' = the opposite of being in a group → group (38 is a distractor number)
Your turn
Q38 — Which species strand most
Question note
More strandings in the most ______ species of whales
What you hear
'the whales that are thought to be most social — the toothed whales — are the group that strand the most frequently'
Key paraphrase
'thought to be most social' → social (NOT 'toothed', which names the species, not the quality)
Your turn
