📋 Quick Review

This passage reports research led by Professor Ianthi Tsimpli into why multilingual children in India do not gain the benefits seen elsewhere. It links low learning outcomes to being taught in an unfamiliar language (often English), and argues children learn best in a language they know — while noting a surprising finding about children from slum backgrounds.

💡 Reading strategy tip: Passage 2 uses summary completion from a word list, YES/NO/NOT GIVEN, and MCQ. For the word list, match meaning (a paraphrase), not just a similar word. For Y/N/NG, choose NO only when the writer clearly disagrees.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"Stop to listen and, above the din of rickshaws and buses, you’ll hear a multitude of languages, as more than 20 million people go about their daily lives."
Main Clauseyou’ll hear a multitude of languages
Subordinate / Relative Clauseas more than 20 million people go about their daily lives (reason/time)
Grammar Note"Stop to listen and …" is an imperative + result. "above the din of …" = louder than the noise of. "as" = while.
Simple MeaningIf you stop and listen over the traffic noise, you’ll hear many languages while millions go about their day.
中文停下来倾听，在人力车和公交车的喧嚣之上，你会听到多种语言，与此同时两千多万人正过着他们的日常生活。
"She explains that low educational achievement can lead to many of these students dropping out of school — a problem disproportionately affecting female students."
Main Clauselow educational achievement can lead to many of these students dropping out of school
Subordinate / Relative Clausea problem disproportionately affecting female students (appositive)
Grammar Note"lead to somebody doing" = cause them to do. The dash adds an appositive commenting on the problem.
Simple MeaningPoor results can make many students leave school early — and this happens far more to girls.
中文她解释说，学业成绩差会导致许多学生辍学——这一问题对女生的影响尤为严重。
"Tsimpli and her team have found that the medium of instruction used in schools, especially English, may hold back those children who have little familiarity with, or exposure to, the language before starting school."
Main ClauseTsimpli and her team have found that the medium of instruction … may hold back those children
Subordinate / Relative Clausewho have little familiarity with, or exposure to, the language before starting school (relative clause)
Grammar Note"found that …" reports a finding. "who …" describes the children. "familiarity with / exposure to" both take objects.
Simple MeaningThe team found that teaching in English can hold back children who barely knew the language before school.
中文茨姆普利及其团队发现，学校采用的教学语言（尤其是英语）可能会拖累那些入学前对该语言几乎不熟悉、也很少接触的孩子。
"An unanticipated finding has been that children from slum backgrounds do not seem to lag behind children from other urban poor backgrounds — and in some cases outperform them."
Main ClauseAn unanticipated finding has been that children from slum backgrounds do not seem to lag behind …
Subordinate / Relative Clauseand in some cases outperform them (coordinated clause)
Grammar Note"finding has been that …" reports the result. "do not lag behind" = are not worse; "outperform" = do better.
Simple MeaningSurprisingly, slum children are not behind other poor urban children — and sometimes do better.
中文一个未曾预料的发现是：来自贫民窟背景的孩子似乎并不落后于其他城市贫困背景的孩子——有时甚至表现更好。
"The trick may be to bridge school skills with life skills and make use of the richness of a child’s life experience to help them learn in the most effective ways possible."
Main ClauseThe trick may be to bridge school skills with life skills and make use of the richness of a child’s life experience
Subordinate / Relative Clauseto help them learn in the most effective ways possible (infinitive of purpose)
Grammar Note"The trick may be to …" proposes a solution. "bridge A with B" = connect them. "to help …" states the purpose.
Simple MeaningThe key may be to connect school skills with life skills, using a child’s real experience to help them learn best.
中文诀窍或许在于把学校技能与生活技能衔接起来，并利用孩子丰富的生活经验，帮助他们以最有效的方式学习。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Follow the research argument from problem to recommendation.

Cause & Effect

Being taught in an unfamiliar language (often English) → low learning outcomes → dropping out (especially girls). The proposed cause is the language of instruction, not the children’s ability.

Contrast

Europe vs India: multilingual children in Europe gain benefits that many Indian children do not. The passage asks why this difference exists.

Unexpected Finding

Against expectations, slum children do NOT lag behind other urban poor children and sometimes outperform them — possibly because they mature faster and use numeracy for daily survival.

Recommendation

Tsimpli recommends starting in a language children are comfortable in; English can be taught as a subject rather than as the medium of instruction. Note the stance for Y/N/NG questions.
