Listening Part 3

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 4 — Listening Part 3

Teaching Handwriting · Multiple Choice, two answers + single answer (Q21–30)

📋 Quick Review

Two education students — a man and a woman — are discussing an assignment on teaching handwriting to children. They begin with the benefits of learning to write by hand (memory, concentration, imagination, spatial awareness, fine motor skills), then talk about how to help children with dyspraxia and dyslexia, then compare cursive and print writing, and finally give their views on exams, the future of handwriting and their own writing. Questions 21–24 are pick TWO letters from A–E; questions 25–30 are 3-option multiple choice.

Who is speaking? Two education students. In this recording the man speaks first (“How are you getting on with the assignment?”) and the woman replies. She is the one who mentions the pressure-sensitive pen, grid paper, keyboards for dyslexic children, cursive versus print, and her own spelling and punctuation. He is the one who talks about the study on imagination, exam results, the future of handwriting, and missing writing by hand.

The situation 情境: 两名教育专业的学生在讨论一份关于「教孩子写字」的作业。他们先谈手写的好处（记忆、专注力、想象力、空间意识、精细动作），再谈如何帮助有书写障碍（dyspraxia）和阅读障碍（dyslexia）的孩子，接着比较连笔体和印刷体，最后谈考试评分、手写的未来，以及他们自己的字（Q21–30）。

Task type: Q21–22 and Q23–24: choose TWO letters, A–E — one mark per letter, either order. Q25–30: choose the correct letter A, B or C. In the 3-option questions all three ideas are normally said out loud; only one of them answers the exact question asked.

💡 Listening strategy tip: Almost every option in this section is spoken aloud, so “I heard it” is worthless here. Two habits decide your score. First, for Q21–22 the stem says both students found it surprising, so you need two reactions, not one — listen for agreement markers (“Same here”, “I thought that too”) and reject anything one of them says was obvious or expected. Second, for Q25–30 check who the stem names: three questions belong to the woman, one to the man, and two to the students together. Write M and W beside the questions before the audio starts, and ignore the other voice when the stem is speaker-specific. 先在题号旁标出「男/女/两人」，再听——说话人错了，选项再像也是零分。
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • Q21–22 needs TWO surprised people. Memory (B) is dismissed with “that seems pretty obvious” and fine motor skills (A) with “as I’d always assumed”. Imagination (D) is the cruellest option: the woman says “I’m not sure I understand how it improves… imagination”, but the man replies “I would have guessed that would be the case” — so only one of them is surprised, and D is out.
  • Q23–24 asks what is EASIEST to correct, not what is discussed. Letter shapes (D) needs “lots of games… a lot of patience”; writing slowly (E) gets “I’m not sure there’s much you can do about”. The two answers are flagged with easiness words: “a really simple solution” and “one quite simple thing you can do”.
  • Q23–24, option B (a straight line). It is mentioned in the very same sentence as the spacing answer — but as the thing that matters less: “that’s more important for legibility than trying to get them to write in a straight line”.
  • Q25, option B. “Fluent” really is said — inside a negative: “I read that developing fluency isn’t any faster”. Option A is the exact opposite of “they can be more willing to have a go”.
  • Q26, option C. Teachers are mentioned, but the woman says “teachers’ attitudes have changed”, and the stylish-and-educated view of cursive belongs to the past: “but not anymore”.
  • Q29: two decoys around the real answer. Note-taking (A) is raised and then dismissed (“there are lots of apps for that”), and historical documents (C) are said to be a problem in general, not her problem. Her own difficulty is spelling and punctuation.
  • Q30, option B. Someone really is shocked by a comment — but her mother is shocked by her spelling, not her handwriting. Both students end by saying they miss writing by hand, which is regret (C).

strugglev.to find something very difficult费力；挣扎“They often really struggle with handwriting, and some just give up.”Common in Part 3 discussions. Collocation: struggle with something.
frustratingadj.making you annoyed because you cannot succeed令人沮丧的“It’s not as frustrating for them if they get things wrong.”Key to Q25 (C). less frustrating when you make a mistake = children react more positively to mistakes.

learning difficultiesn.conditions that make learning harder than usual学习障碍“Especially for children with learning difficulties who find joining up letters really challenging.”Key to Q26 (A). ‘a certain group of children’ on the paper = this phrase in the audio.
scriptn.a piece of written work produced in an exam答卷；卷面“Marks are definitely affected if examiners can’t read the script.”Exam-specific meaning — not a film script. Supports Q27 (A): grades are affected.
touch typingn.typing without looking at the keyboard盲打“Touch typing still isn’t taught in most schools.”Q28 option A says typing will be taught before writing — the man actually says it isn’t taught at all, and may not need to be.
punctuationn.the marks used in writing, such as commas and full stops标点符号“My punctuation is really inconsistent.”Answer to Q29 (B), together with spelling. Spelling: p-u-n-c-t-u-a-t-i-o-n.
inconsistentadj.not always the same; not regular不一致的；不稳定的“The fact that my punctuation is really inconsistent.”Negative prefix in-. A soft way of admitting a weakness — common in academic speech.
put something down tophr.to explain something as being caused by把……归因于“I think you can put that down to lack of practice.”A causal expression. Paraphrase family: blame on / attribute to / it’s because of.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening the audio never uses the exact words printed on the question paper. Recognising paraphrase is the whole skill. 雅思听力中，录音几乎不会重复题干原词——识别同义替换才是关键。
Paraphrase Example 1
Question wording“improved concentration” — a surprise for BOTH (Q21–22, C)
What you hearW: “What’s less obvious is how it helps develop their concentration. They have to sit still and focus on one thing.” M: “That aspect had never occurred to me before.” W: “Same here.”
Key paraphrasesurprising = less obvious + never occurred to me + ‘Same here’ (the second speaker’s agreement is what makes it valid)
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question wording“improved spatial awareness” (Q21–22, E)
What you hearM: “I’d never associated spatial awareness with handwriting either.” W: “I thought that too. But good spatial awareness is essential for writing because you have to space words correctly.”
Key paraphrase‘I’d never associated X with Y’ = I was surprised that X and Y are connected
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question wording“improved imagination” (Q21–22, D — WRONG)
What you hearW: “I’m not sure I understand how it improves children’s imagination.” M: “There was that study… I would have guessed that would be the case.”
Key paraphraseOnly ONE speaker is surprised. The stem says both — one reaction is not enough.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question wording“applying too much pressure when writing” (Q23–24, C)
What you hearW: “I like the idea of using one of those pens that light up if you press too hard. That seems like a really simple solution.”
Key paraphrasepress too hard = apply too much pressure; ‘a really simple solution’ = easiest to correct
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 5
Question wording“Children become fluent relatively quickly” (Q25, B — WRONG)
What you hearW: “On a keyboard, they can be more willing to have a go. But I read that developing fluency isn’t any faster.”
Key paraphraseThe keyword (fluency) is inside a negative. Always listen to the end of the clause before you tick a box.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 6
Question wording“cursive writing disadvantages a certain group of children” (Q26, A)
What you hearW: “It’s been proved that cursive is more difficult to learn, especially for children with learning difficulties who find joining up letters really challenging.”
Key paraphraseharder for children with learning difficulties = disadvantages a certain group of children
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 7
Question wording“regretful that they have lost the habit” (Q30, C)
What you hearM: “Personally, I miss writing by hand. I hardly ever write anything now… It’s a shame.” W: “I feel the same way. I used to write a diary by hand and now I do that digitally.”
Key paraphraseI miss… + it’s a shame + I used to… and now = regret about a lost habit

Q28

Only future or ‘will continue’ ideas count. “I can’t see that changing much… teachers understand the value… a basic life skill.”
Q29
B — LETTER A / B / C — a personal weakness
Look for a first-person admission (“my…”). Anything solved by an app, or described as a general problem, is a decoy.
Q30
C — LETTER A / B / C — a shared feeling
Listen to the last exchange only. Feeling words: “I miss…”, “It’s a shame”, “I feel the same way”.

Review Quiz

Q1
C. ‘That seems pretty obvious when you think about it.’
‘Obvious’ is a rejection signal. It is used about memory, which is why option B in the exam question is wrong.
Q2
B. Only the woman is surprised; the man says he would have guessed it.
The stem requires both students to be surprised. ‘I would have guessed that would be the case’ cancels the option.
Q3
A. Children have to space words correctly.
She corrects her earlier idea that spatial awareness is only about ‘where you are in relation to objects or other people’.
Q4
C. confusing letter shapes
‘You need to play lots of games to help them distinguish letter shapes. It takes a lot of patience.’ Writing very slowly is also hard to fix.
Q5
B. Spacing matters more than straightness, so the straight line is not the answer.
Comparative structures rank ideas. The thing on the ‘more important’ side is the one the exam wants.
Q6
C. They find it less frustrating and are more willing to try.
This is Q25 (C). Note that giving up is what happens with handwriting, not with the keyboard.
Q7
B. It was the view in the past, but not any more.
‘In the past, cursive writing was certainly considered more stylish and educated, but not anymore.’ Tense is the trap in Q26 option C.
Q8
C. Research confirms the man’s worry, and marks suffer if examiners cannot read the script.
Two separate statements point the same way, which is why Q27 is A: there is evidence that grades are affected.
Q9
B. It is still not taught in most schools, and may not need to be.
Option A of Q28 reverses this. His actual prediction is that children will go on learning to write by hand because it is ‘a basic life skill’.
Q10
B. spelling and punctuation
Note-taking is dismissed (‘lots of apps for that’) and historical documents are a general point. Her personal admission is the answer to Q29.
Q11
B. Her mother’s comment is about her spelling, not her handwriting.
The comment exists, but it is attached to the wrong thing. Both students end by saying they miss writing by hand — that is regret, answer C.
Q12
B. Mark who each stem is about (man / woman / both) and reject options said by the wrong speaker or in a negative.
Nearly every option is spoken aloud somewhere. Speaker identity and negatives (‘isn’t any faster’, ‘not anymore’) are what separate the answer from the decoys.
