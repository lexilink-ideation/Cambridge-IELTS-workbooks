📋 Passage Overview

Why fairy tales are really scary tales. Anthropologist Jamie Tehrani used techniques from evolutionary biology (phylogenetic analysis) on 58 versions of Little Red Riding Hood. Surprisingly, the 'moral' or cautionary parts changed freely, and the central section was no more stable than the rest — but the frightening, gruesome details survived best. The passage also gives other scholars' reactions. Question types: sentence endings (Q27–31), summary with a word list (Q32–36) and multiple choice (Q37–40).

💡 Reading tip: For 'complete the sentence with the correct ending', match grammar as well as meaning — the ending must fit the start. For the word-list summary, decide the part of speech first, then pick from the box; letters are not reused across question sets.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

People of every culture tell each other fairy tales, but the same story often takes a variety of forms in different parts of the world.
Main ClausePeople of every culture tell each other fairy tales, but the same story often takes a variety of forms
Subordinate / Relativetwo independent clauses joined by 'but'; 'in different parts of the world' = place phrase
Grammar NoteContrast with 'but'; the second clause states the variation.
Simple MeaningEveryone tells fairy tales, but the same tale appears in many different versions around the world.
中文每一种文化的人都会讲童话,但同一个故事在世界不同地区常常有多种版本。
Neither did his analysis support the theory that the central section of a story is the most conserved part.
Main Clausehis analysis (did not) support the theory
Subordinate / Relative'that the central section of a story is the most conserved part' = noun clause naming the theory; 'Neither did' = negative inversion linking to the previous sentence
Grammar NoteNegative inversion ('Neither did his analysis support…') + a that-clause.
Simple MeaningHis analysis also failed to support the idea that a story's middle is its most stable part.
中文他的分析也不支持“故事中段是最稳定部分”这一理论。
Yet in his analysis such elements were just as flexible as seemingly trivial details.
Main Clausesuch elements were just as flexible as seemingly trivial details
Subordinate / Relative'Yet' = contrast marker; 'just as flexible as …' = equal comparison
Grammar NoteContrast marker + an 'as … as' equality comparison.
Simple MeaningYet those 'important' warning parts changed just as easily as unimportant little details.
中文然而在他的分析中,这些(警示性)成分与看似琐碎的细节一样容易变化。
Maybe being swallowed whole by a wolf, then cut out of its stomach alive, is so gripping that it helps the story remain popular, no matter how badly it's told.
Main ClauseMaybe being swallowed whole by a wolf … is so gripping that it helps the story remain popular
Subordinate / Relative'so gripping that it helps the story remain popular' = result clause ('so … that …'); 'no matter how badly it's told' = concession clause
Grammar NoteGerund subject ('being swallowed…') + 'so … that …' result + a 'no matter how' concession.
Simple MeaningPerhaps being eaten whole and then cut out alive is so gripping that the story stays popular even when told badly.
中文也许被狼整个吞下、再被活着剖出这一情节极其扣人心弦,以至于无论讲得多糟,故事都能保持流行。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Expectation vs finding. The passage repeatedly sets up what Tehrani (or others) expected, then reveals a surprise ('Yet…', 'the really big surprise'). MCQs test these reversals, so read for 'surprisingly', 'contrary to'.

Opinion mapping. Several scholars respond — Tehrani, Jack Zipes, Mathias Clasen. For questions about who thinks what, read the sentences around each name and note whether they agree or disagree.

Cause of survival. The central argument: fear/horror, not moral lessons, keeps tales alive because gripping content survives even poor telling. This drives the summary (Q32–36) and the final MCQs.
