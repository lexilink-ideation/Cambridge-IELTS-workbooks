📋 Passage Overview

'The Intersection of Health Sciences and Geography' — an introduction to health geography, the hybrid field that combines geographical analysis with the study of health. Eight sections (A–H) cover globalised infection, malaria and climate, urban pollution, the definition of the field, and its two subcategories: the geography of disease and the geography of healthcare provision. Question types: matching information (Q14–19) and sentence completion (Q20–26).

💡 Reading tip: 'NB You may use any letter more than once' is a warning, not decoration — here paragraph D is the answer twice (Q14 and Q18). Never cross a paragraph off just because you have already used it.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

While many diseases that affect humans have been eradicated due to improvements in vaccinations and the availability of healthcare, there are still areas around the world where certain health issues are more prevalent.
Main Clausethere are still areas around the world where certain health issues are more prevalent
Subordinate / Relative'While many diseases… have been eradicated' = concession clause; 'that affect humans' = defining relative clause; 'due to improvements in…' = reason phrase; 'where certain health issues are more prevalent' = relative clause of place.
Grammar Note'While' here means 'although'. Four layers of subordination around one short main clause.
Simple MeaningAlthough vaccines and healthcare have wiped out many diseases, some health problems are still common in certain places.
中文尽管由于疫苗接种的改进和医疗保健的普及,许多影响人类的疾病已被根除,但世界上仍有一些地区某些健康问题更为普遍。
Perhaps one of the most obvious examples of this idea is malaria-prone areas, which are usually tropical regions that foster a warm and damp environment in which the mosquitos that can give people this disease can grow.
Main Clauseone of the most obvious examples of this idea is malaria-prone areas
Subordinate / Relative'which are usually tropical regions' = non-defining relative clause; 'that foster a warm and damp environment' = defining relative clause; 'in which the mosquitos… can grow' = relative clause of place; 'that can give people this disease' = a further relative clause defining the mosquitos.
Grammar NoteFour stacked relative clauses — a chain. Read them as a ladder: areas → regions → environment → mosquitos.
Simple MeaningThe clearest example is malaria areas: tropical places that are warm and damp, where the mosquitos carrying malaria can breed.
中文或许最明显的例子就是疟疾高发地区,这些地区通常是热带区域,营造出温暖潮湿的环境,而传播这种疾病的蚊子正是在其中滋生的。
In many large cities, the wind is not strong enough to clear the air of the massive amounts of smog and pollution that cause asthma, lung problems, eyesight issues and more in the people who live there.
Main Clausethe wind is not strong enough to clear the air
Subordinate / Relative'of the massive amounts of smog and pollution' = what is cleared away; 'that cause asthma, lung problems, eyesight issues and more' = defining relative clause; 'who live there' = a further relative clause.
Grammar Note'not + adjective + enough + to-infinitive' = insufficient to do something; 'clear X of Y' = remove Y from X.
Simple MeaningIn big cities the wind cannot blow away the smog, which gives residents asthma, lung disease and eye problems.
中文在许多大城市,风力不足以吹散大量的雾霾和污染,而这些污染会使当地居民患上哮喘、肺部疾病、视力问题等。
Health geography is the combination of, on the one hand, knowledge regarding geography and methods used to analyse and interpret geographical information, and on the other, the study of health, diseases and healthcare practices around the world.
Main ClauseHealth geography is the combination of … and …
Subordinate / Relative'on the one hand… and on the other' = a balanced pair; 'used to analyse and interpret geographical information' = reduced passive relative clause modifying 'methods'.
Grammar NoteThe definition sentence of the whole passage. The inserted 'on the one hand / on the other' separates the two halves of 'the combination of A and B'.
Simple MeaningHealth geography joins two things: geographical knowledge and methods, and the study of health and healthcare worldwide.
中文健康地理学是两方面的结合:一方面是地理知识以及用于分析和解读地理信息的方法,另一方面是对全球健康、疾病与医疗实践的研究。
The geography of disease and ill health analyses the frequency with which certain diseases appear in different parts of the world, and overlays the data with the geography of the region, to see if there could be a correlation between the two.
Main ClauseThe geography of disease and ill health analyses the frequency … and overlays the data with the geography of the region
Subordinate / Relative'with which certain diseases appear in different parts of the world' = relative clause with a preposition moved to the front; 'to see if there could be a correlation' = infinitive of purpose containing an if-clause.
Grammar Note'the frequency with which X happens' = how often X happens. Two main verbs share one subject.
Simple MeaningThis branch measures how often diseases occur in different places, then maps that against the region's geography to look for a link.
中文疾病与不良健康的地理学分析某些疾病在世界不同地区出现的频率,并把这些数据与该地区的地理情况叠加,以考察两者之间是否存在相关性。
Individuals working in the area of the geography of healthcare provision attempt to assess the levels of healthcare in the area (for instance, it may be very difficult for people to get medical attention because there is a mountain between their village and the nearest hospital).
Main ClauseIndividuals … attempt to assess the levels of healthcare in the area
Subordinate / Relative'working in the area of the geography of healthcare provision' = reduced relative clause (= 'who work in…'); the bracket contains a full example clause with its own 'because' reason.
Grammar NoteReduced relative + a parenthetical illustration. Brackets in IELTS passages almost always contain an example — and often an answer (Q26 = mountain).
Simple MeaningResearchers in this branch measure how good healthcare is in an area — for example, a mountain between a village and the hospital can make treatment hard to reach.
中文从事医疗服务地理学研究的人员会评估该地区的医疗水平(例如,由于村庄与最近的医院之间隔着一座山,人们可能很难获得医疗救治)。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

General → specific → definition → subcategories. A states the global problem, B and C give two concrete illustrations (malaria, urban pollution), D defines the field, E–G split it into its two branches, H concludes. Recognising that D is the hinge paragraph explains why it answers two questions.

Cause and effect chains. Globalisation → more contact → antibiotic-resistant super-viruses. Cars + coal factories + weak wind → smog → asthma and eye problems. Industrialisation → deforestation → less fresh air → worse pollution. Each chain is one sentence long, so the answer word is always inside the chain.

Contrast by location. Section B works by opposition: warm damp tropics (malaria common) vs high-altitude deserts (malaria rare). Whenever a passage names a place where something is less of a problem, expect a question about it — here Q19.

Concession inside a claim. 'While people will always be prone to illness, the study of how geography affects our health could lead to the eradication of certain illnesses.' The 'while' clause is an admission that not everything can be cured — the exact target of Q14.

Definition by classification. Section F is about sorting: 'work to create a clear way of categorising illnesses, diseases and epidemics into local and global scales'. Classification language ('categorise', 'scales', 'subcategory') is what Q16 asks for.
