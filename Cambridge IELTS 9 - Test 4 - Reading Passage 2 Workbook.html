Cambridge IELTS 9 — Test 4 — Reading Passage 2

Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

Young children's sense of identity. Eight paragraphs (A–H) trace how a sense of self develops, using William James's distinction between the self-as-subject (awareness that one exists and can act) and the self-as-object (placing oneself in social categories). It covers Cooley's 'looking-glass self', Mead's claim that the self is inherently social, the Lewis and Brooks-Gunn mirror experiment, and Bronson's study of toddler rage. Question types: matching information to paragraphs (Q14–19), matching researchers (Q20–23) and summary completion (Q24–26, ONE WORD ONLY).

💡 Reading tip: Five researchers appear (James, Cooley, Lewis & Brooks-Gunn, Mead, Bronson) — build a name-to-idea table as you read and Q20–23 becomes mechanical. Note that Lewis and Brooks-Gunn appear in two paragraphs (C and G), which is why the instructions say a letter may be used more than once for Q14–19.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

The process can usefully be thought of in terms of the gradual emergence of two somewhat separate features: the self as a subject, and the self as an object.
Main ClauseThe process can usefully be thought of in terms of the gradual emergence of two somewhat separate features.
Subordinate / RelativeThe colon introduces the two features in apposition.
Grammar Note'be thought of in terms of' is a formal framing phrase; the colon then names the framework. This sentence sets up the entire passage structure.
Simple MeaningIt helps to see the process as two separate things gradually appearing: the self as subject and the self as object.
中文这一过程可以有益地理解为两个略有区别的特征逐渐显现：作为主体的自我，以及作为客体的自我。
However, Lewis and Brooks-Gunn (1979) suggest that infants' developing understanding that the movements they see in the mirror are contingent on their own, leads to a growing awareness that they are distinct from other people.
Main ClauseLewis and Brooks-Gunn suggest that… understanding… leads to a growing awareness.
Subordinate / Relative'that the movements they see in the mirror are contingent on their own' (appositive that-clause inside the subject) + 'that they are distinct from other people' (appositive on 'awareness').
Grammar NoteA very long subject ('infants' developing understanding that…') delays the verb 'leads to'. Q23 rests here.
Simple MeaningLewis and Brooks-Gunn think that when infants realise the mirror movements depend on their own, they start to see themselves as separate people.
中文然而，刘易斯与布鲁克斯-冈恩（1979）认为，婴儿逐渐认识到镜中所见的动作取决于自身的动作，这会促使他们日益意识到自己与他人是不同的。
Empirical investigations of the self-as-subject in young children are, however, rather scarce because of difficulties of communication: even if young infants can reflect on their experience, they certainly cannot express this aspect of the self directly.
Main ClauseEmpirical investigations… are rather scarce because of difficulties of communication.
Subordinate / RelativeThe colon introduces the explanation + 'even if young infants can reflect on their experience' (concession clause).
Grammar Note'even if' concedes a possibility in order to dismiss its usefulness. This sentence is the whole answer to Q17.
Simple MeaningThere is little hard research on the self-as-subject because infants cannot communicate — even if they can think about their experience, they cannot describe it.
中文然而，对幼儿「作为主体的自我」的实证研究相当稀少，原因在于交流困难：即便婴幼儿能够反思自身经验，他们也肯定无法直接表达自我的这一方面。
This has been seen by many to be the aspect of the self which is most influenced by social elements, since it is made up of social roles and characteristics which derive their meaning from comparison or interaction with other people.
Main ClauseThis has been seen by many to be the aspect of the self which is most influenced by social elements.
Subordinate / Relative'which is most influenced by social elements' (relative) + 'since it is made up of…' (reason) + 'which derive their meaning from comparison' (second relative).
Grammar NoteThree subordinate layers. 'since' = because. Q19 asks for examples of the wide range of features — the roles and characteristics listed here.
Simple MeaningMany see the self-as-object as the most socially shaped part of the self, because it consists of social roles and traits that only mean something in comparison with others.
中文许多人认为这是自我中受社会因素影响最大的方面，因为它由社会角色以及那些只有在与他人比较或互动中才具意义的特质所构成。
He called the self-as-object the 'looking-glass self', since people come to see themselves as they are reflected in others.
Main ClauseHe called the self-as-object the 'looking-glass self'.
Subordinate / Relative'since people come to see themselves as they are reflected in others' = reason clause.
Grammar Note'call + object + name' is the naming pattern. The metaphor works because other people function as a mirror.
Simple MeaningCooley named the self-as-object the 'looking-glass self' because we see ourselves as reflected in other people.
中文他把「作为客体的自我」称为「镜中自我」，因为人们是通过他人对自己的映照来认识自己的。
Often, the children's disagreements involved a struggle over a toy that none of them had played with before or after the tug-of-war: the children seemed to be disputing ownership rather than wanting to play with it.
Main ClauseThe children's disagreements involved a struggle over a toy.
Subordinate / Relative'that none of them had played with before or after the tug-of-war' (defining relative with past perfect) + the colon introduces the interpretation.
Grammar NoteThe past perfect ('had played') establishes that the toy was of no interest either side of the fight — which is the evidence for the ownership claim. Q26 comes from here.
Simple MeaningThe children fought over a toy none of them played with before or afterwards — they were arguing about ownership, not wanting to play.
中文孩子们的争执常常是围绕一件在争夺前后都无人玩耍的玩具展开：他们似乎是在争夺所有权，而非真想玩它。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Two-part framework: the whole passage is organised by James's distinction — paragraphs B–D develop the self-as-subject, paragraphs E–H develop the self-as-object. Knowing which half you are in halves your search time.

Chronology of scholarship: James (1892) → Cooley (1902) → Mead (1934) → Bronson (1975) → Lewis and Brooks-Gunn (1979) → Dunn (1988). Dates in brackets are the fastest way to locate a named researcher.

Cause & effect: parents mimic infants and mirrors move contingently (cause) → infants realise they alone control the reflection (effect) → they become aware of being distinct from others (effect). Q23 sits at the end of this chain.

Definition and re-labelling: the same idea is named twice — 'self-as-object' (James) and 'looking-glass self' (Cooley). Passages often rename a concept; matching tasks exploit exactly that.

Problem in the research: 'Empirical investigations… are, however, rather scarce because of difficulties of communication.' The writer flags a limitation rather than a finding — Q17 tests whether you can tell the two apart.

Escalation of claims: Cooley suggests a close connection between self and others' views; 'Mead (1934) went even further, and saw the self and the social world as inextricably bound together.' 'Went even further' ranks the two positions — that ranking is what makes Q20 Mead, not Cooley.

Cultural qualification: 'Although it may be less marked in other societies, the link between the sense of self and of ownership is a notable feature of childhood in Western societies.' The writer limits the claim's scope — a hallmark of careful academic writing.
