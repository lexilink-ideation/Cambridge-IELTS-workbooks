📋 Passage Overview

The growth mindset. The passage traces the idea that intelligence can be developed, centres on Carol Dweck's research, then reviews strong criticism (Gelman, Bates) and the difficulty of turning the growth mindset into classroom practice. The writer concludes it may work better as a philosophy than as a direct intervention. Question types: multiple choice, matching statements to people and Yes/No/Not Given.

💡 Reading tip: For 'match the statement to the person', anchor on each named researcher and read their quotation. A letter may be used more than once. For YES/NO/NOT GIVEN, judge the writer's own view, which is clearest in the final paragraphs.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

The concept of intelligence as something innate has been supplanted by the idea that intelligence is not fixed, and that, with the right training, we can be the authors of our own cognitive capabilities.
Main ClauseThe concept of intelligence as something innate has been supplanted by the idea that intelligence is not fixed.
Subordinate / Relative'that intelligence is not fixed' + 'that… we can be the authors of our own cognitive capabilities' (two noun clauses).
Grammar NotePassive 'has been supplanted by'; two 'that'-clauses define 'the idea'.
Simple MeaningThe old view that intelligence is inborn has been replaced by the belief that we can develop it with training.
中文认为智力与生俱来的观念，已被这样一种观点取代：智力并非固定不变，通过恰当的训练，我们可以成为自己认知能力的塑造者。
A major focus of the growth mindset in schools is coaxing students away from seeing failure as an indication of their ability, and towards seeing it as a chance to improve that ability.
Main ClauseA major focus… is coaxing students away from seeing failure as an indication of their ability, and towards seeing it as a chance to improve.
Subordinate / Relative'away from seeing… and towards seeing…' (balanced prepositional contrast).
Grammar Note'coax away from X and towards Y' contrasts two ways of seeing failure.
Simple MeaningSchools try to get students to view failure not as proof of poor ability but as a chance to improve.
中文成长型思维在学校的一个重点，是引导学生不再把失败视为能力的标志，而是把它看作提升能力的机会。
This group had inferred that success or failure is due to innate ability, and this 'fixed mindset' had led them to fear of failure and lack of effort.
Main ClauseThis 'fixed mindset' had led them to fear of failure and lack of effort.
Subordinate / Relative'This group had inferred that success or failure is due to innate ability' (leading clause with a noun clause).
Grammar NotePast perfect 'had inferred / had led' shows the earlier cause.
Simple MeaningBelieving success comes from inborn ability made this group fear failure and try less.
中文这组学生推断成功或失败源于天生能力，这种"固定型思维"使他们害怕失败、缺乏努力。
Paradoxically, however, that aspiration is not well served by direct interventions that try to instil it.
Main ClauseThat aspiration is not well served by direct interventions.
Subordinate / Relative'that try to instil it' (relative clause modifying 'interventions').
Grammar Note'Paradoxically' flags a surprising contrast; passive 'is not well served by'.
Simple MeaningOddly, trying to force the growth mindset on students doesn't really help it grow.
中文然而，矛盾的是，试图灌输这种志向的直接干预，反而并不能很好地促成它。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Historical shift: paragraph 1 contrasts the old 'innate' view (Binet) with the newer 'developable' view (Dewey) — key to Q14 and Q19.

Experiment & result: Dweck's study — praising effort vs ability — shows praising ability made students perform worse (cause → effect), central to the third paragraph (Q16).

Criticism & response: Gelman and Bates attack the research; Dweck responds and clarifies. Tracking who says what answers Q17–22.

Evaluation: the writer's own view emerges late — interventions 'don't seem to work', and the mindset 'works best as a philosophy and not an intervention' (Q23–26).
