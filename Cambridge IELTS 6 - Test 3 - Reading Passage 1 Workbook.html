Cambridge IELTS 6 — Test 3 — Reading Passage 1

The Power of the Big Screen · Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

This passage traces the history and cultural impact of cinema, from the Lumière Brothers' first public screening in Paris in 1895 to the present day. It explains why early audiences found moving pictures so shocking, how cinema evolved into a storytelling medium dominated by Hollywood, and how film has shaped the way the world understands itself — through the power of the "star" and the speed of cultural change.

The passage has ten paragraphs (A–J), each making one main point, which makes it ideal practice for the "matching information to paragraphs" question type.

💡 Reading strategy tip: This passage is an unusually good example of paragraphs each carrying a single clear idea. Before answering Q1–5, skim each paragraph and write a 3–5 word mental "label" for it (e.g., Para C = "the train film shocks audience"). This makes matching information much faster.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"As the train approached, panic started in the theatre: people jumped and ran away. That was the moment when cinema was born."
Main Clausepanic started in the theatre
Subordinate Clause"As the train approached" (time clause)  +  "when cinema was born" (relative clause modifying "the moment")
Grammar Note"As" introduces simultaneous time; "when" introduces a relative clause describing "the moment" — a common IELTS structure for defining a specific point in time
Simple MeaningWhen the train got closer on screen, the audience panicked — this reaction marked the true beginning of cinema.
中文当火车驶近时，剧院里一片恐慌：人们跳起来逃跑。那一刻，电影诞生了。
"A still picture could only imply the existence of time, while time in a novel passed at the whim of the reader."
Main ClauseA still picture could only imply the existence of time
Subordinate Clause"while time in a novel passed at the whim of the reader" (contrast clause)
Grammar Note"while" here signals contrast (= "whereas"), not simultaneous time — a two-way comparison structure common in academic writing
Simple MeaningPhotos can only suggest that time exists; in novels, the reader controls how fast time seems to pass.
中文静止的照片只能暗示时间的存在，而小说中时间的流逝则取决于读者的意愿。
"Long before people travelled to America or anywhere else, they knew what other places looked like; they knew how other people worked and lived."
Main Clausethey knew what other places looked like; they knew how other people worked and lived
Subordinate Clause"Long before people travelled to America or anywhere else" (time clause)  +  two embedded noun clauses ("what other places looked like," "how other people worked and lived")
Grammar NoteSemicolon joins two parallel independent clauses; embedded wh-clauses act as objects of "knew" — common in descriptive academic prose
Simple MeaningCinema let people learn about foreign places and lifestyles before they ever travelled themselves.
中文早在人们去美国或其他地方旅行之前，他们就已经知道其他地方是什么样子，知道其他人是如何工作和生活的。
"Film personalities have such an immediate presence that, inevitably, they become super-real."
Main ClauseFilm personalities have such an immediate presence
Subordinate Clause"that...they become super-real" (result clause, using the such...that structure)
Grammar Note"such...that" expresses a degree leading to a result — a very common IELTS sentence pattern for cause-and-effect
Simple MeaningFilm stars feel so present and familiar to audiences that they seem more real than ordinary people.
中文电影明星具有如此强烈的存在感，以至于他们不可避免地变得“超现实”。
"When the Lumière Brothers and other pioneers began showing off this new invention, it was by no means obvious how it would be used."
Main Clauseit was by no means obvious how it would be used
Subordinate Clause"When the Lumière Brothers and other pioneers began showing off this new invention" (time clause)  +  "how it would be used" (embedded question as subject complement)
Grammar Note"it" is a dummy subject anticipating the real subject clause "how it would be used" — extraposition, a common academic structure
Simple MeaningWhen cinema was first shown to the public, nobody knew yet what purpose it would eventually serve.
中文当卢米埃尔兄弟和其他先驱开始展示这项新发明时，人们完全不清楚它将如何被使用。
"And it has all happened so quickly. Almost unbelievably, it is a mere 100 years since that train arrived and the audience screamed and fled."
Main Clauseit is a mere 100 years
Subordinate Clause"since that train arrived and the audience screamed and fled" (time clause modifying "100 years")
Grammar Note"since" + past events measures elapsed time; "mere" emphasises surprise at how short the period is
Simple MeaningCinema has changed enormously in only about a century since its shocking first screening.
中文而这一切发生得如此之快。几乎令人难以置信，距离那列火车出现、观众尖叫逃跑，仅仅过去了一百年。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect

Cause: George saw the train film's realistic movement. → Effect: audiences panicked, believing a real train would crush them. This cause-effect link explains why paragraph C is so often referenced as evidence of cinema's early "impact" (Q10).

Contrast

The passage contrasts a "still picture" (which can only imply time) with cinema (which captures the "real, objective flow of time"). Recognising this contrast is essential for understanding Tarkovsky's argument in Paragraph D (Q11).

Comparison

Paragraph F compares knowledge of the 20th century (very detailed, because of film) with knowledge of the 14th century or classical Greece (much less detailed, no film record) — this comparison supports the idea that film builds a uniquely thorough historical record.

Examples

Paragraph I gives examples of what cinema "might have become" (a documentary form, or like television) — these are hypothetical examples used to show that its storytelling role was not inevitable.

Definition

"The star" is defined in Paragraph G as "the magnified human self" — a person whose immediate, repeated on-screen presence makes them seem more real than everyday people.

Problem / Solution

Problem: early producers doubted audiences could concentrate for more than one reel. Solution: the success of a 1912 Italian 2-hour film proved longer films could work, leading Hollywood to adopt the novel-length narrative.

Chronology

The passage moves chronologically: 1895 (first screening) → early "wonder of movement" era → storytelling develops → 1912 (feature-length film succeeds) → present day (100 years later). Tracking these dates helps locate specific details quickly.

Author's Attitude

The writer's tone is admiring and enthusiastic toward cinema ("the most dynamic, effective art form of the 20th century"), which is useful background for the YES/NO/NOT GIVEN questions about the writer's views (Q6–9).
