📋 Passage Overview

'Collecting as a Hobby' — a psychologist's survey of why people collect. The writer works through several motives in turn: money (instrumental), social life, the hunt for something special, education, and a psychological need for control and individualism, before concluding that the common factor is passion. Question types: sentence completion (Q14–21) and TRUE / FALSE / NOT GIVEN (Q22–26).

💡 Reading tip: This passage is organised by reason, one per paragraph. Before you read the questions, label each paragraph with a one-word motive (money / social / hunt / education / control / individualism). Sentence-completion answers then fall out of the paragraph you have already labelled.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Amassing hundreds or even thousands of postcards, chocolate wrappers or whatever, takes time, energy and money that could surely be put to much more productive use.
Main ClauseAmassing … takes time, energy and money
Subordinate / Relative'that could surely be put to much more productive use' = defining relative clause modifying 'time, energy and money'.
Grammar NoteGerund subject ('Amassing…') + passive relative clause; 'surely' carries the writer's ironic tone.
Simple MeaningCollecting thousands of small items uses up resources that you could spend on something more useful.
中文收集成百上千张明信片、巧克力包装纸之类的东西,耗费的时间、精力和金钱本可以用在更有益的事情上。
But there may well be a psychological element, too — buying cheap and selling dear can give the collector a sense of triumph.
Main Clausethere may well be a psychological element, too
Subordinate / RelativeThe material after the dash is a second independent clause explaining the first.
Grammar Note'may well be' = a hedge meaning 'is quite likely to be'; a dash introduces the explanation.
Simple MeaningThere is probably also a mental reason: making a good deal feels like winning.
中文但很可能也存在心理因素——低买高卖能给收藏者一种胜利的感觉。
There is a danger, though, that if the individual is ever lucky enough to find what they're looking for, rather than celebrating their success, they may feel empty, now that the goal that drove them on has gone.
Main ClauseThere is a danger
Subordinate / Relative'that … they may feel empty' = noun clause naming the danger; inside it, 'if the individual is ever lucky enough to find…' = conditional clause; 'rather than celebrating their success' = contrast phrase; 'now that the goal that drove them on has gone' = reason clause containing its own relative clause.
Grammar NoteExistential 'There is' + that-clause, with three embedded clauses. Read the skeleton first: There is a danger that they may feel empty.
Simple MeaningThe risk is that when collectors finally find the item, they feel empty instead of happy, because the thing motivating them has disappeared.
中文不过存在一种危险:如果这个人真的幸运地找到了他们苦苦寻找的东西,他们可能不会庆祝成功,反而会感到空虚,因为驱使他们前进的目标已经消失了。
Without those collections, our understanding would be greatly inferior to what it is.
Main Clauseour understanding would be greatly inferior to what it is
Subordinate / Relative'Without those collections' = an implied conditional (= 'If we did not have those collections'); 'what it is' = nominal relative clause.
Grammar NoteSecond conditional expressed by a prepositional phrase rather than an 'if' clause.
Simple MeaningIf those 19th-century collections had never existed, we would know much less than we do now.
中文如果没有那些收藏,我们的认识水平会远远不及现在。
Someone who decides to collect something as unexpected as dog collars, for instance, may be conveying their belief that they must be interesting themselves.
Main ClauseSomeone … may be conveying their belief
Subordinate / Relative'who decides to collect something as unexpected as dog collars' = defining relative clause; 'that they must be interesting themselves' = noun clause naming the belief.
Grammar Note'as + adjective + as' comparison inside a relative clause; 'must be' = deduction, not obligation.
Simple MeaningIf you collect something odd like dog collars, you may be signalling that you yourself are an interesting person.
中文例如,一个决定收集狗项圈这种出人意料之物的人,可能是在表达他们认为自己也一定很有趣。
To non-collectors it may appear an eccentric, if harmless, way of spending time, but potentially, collecting has a lot going for it.
Main Clauseit may appear an eccentric … way of spending time / collecting has a lot going for it
Subordinate / Relative'if harmless' = a concessive insertion (= 'although harmless'); 'but' joins the two clauses.
Grammar Note'if + adjective' between commas is a compact concession: eccentric, if harmless = odd, though it does no harm.
Simple MeaningPeople who don't collect may think it strange but harmless; the writer thinks it actually has many benefits.
中文在不收藏的人看来,这或许是一种古怪但无害的消磨时间方式,但收藏其实有很多可取之处。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Question → list of answers. Paragraph 1 ends with the question 'Why do they do it?' Everything after that is a list of answers. Every time you meet 'Another motive…', 'a popular form…', 'One reason…', you have arrived at a new item on the list — and probably a new question.

Definition and exemplification. 'Instrumental' is defined immediately ('that is, collecting as a means to an end') and then illustrated ('They'll look for, say, antiques…'). Watch for 'that is', 'such as', 'for instance', 'say' — the answer word is usually inside the example.

Contrast within a paragraph. The 'hunt' paragraph contains a hidden reversal: the search gives purpose to an otherwise aimless life, but success may leave the collector feeling empty. Signals: 'There is a danger, though', 'rather than'.

Comparison across time. Doll materials move from wood (16th century Europe) → wax and porcelain (later centuries) → plastic (today). Time-ordered comparison paragraphs are where NOT GIVEN traps sit: the text says what dolls were made of, not how many were bought (Q22).

Author's attitude. The last paragraph is evaluative: 'pleasure is putting it far too mildly', 'totally engrossing', 'a strong sense of personal fulfilment', 'has a lot going for it'. This is where Q26 is answered — the writer states that collecting gives a feeling other hobbies are unlikely to inspire.
