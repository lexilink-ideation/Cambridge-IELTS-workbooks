📋 Passage Overview

'Great Migrations' asks how biologists define animal migration. Hugh Dingle lists five characteristics; Joel Berger prefers a simpler, practical definition. The passage illustrates both with the arctic tern, zooplankton and aphids, then turns to the pronghorn and the human threat to its migration corridor.

题型:T/F/NG Q14–18 + 句子配对 Q19–22 + 摘要填空 Q23–26。

💡 Reading tip: For Q19–22 (sentence endings), first decide the grammar of each ending — all five begin with a bare verb, so they must complete 'are likely to…' / 'are unlikely to…'. Then match the meaning. Wrong endings are usually true statements taken from the wrong part of the passage.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

It can loosely be described as travel that takes place at regular intervals — often in an annual cycle — that may involve many members of a species, and is rewarded only after a long journey.
Main ClauseIt can loosely be described as travel
Subordinate / Relativethree relative clauses describing 'travel': 'that takes place at regular intervals', 'that may involve many members of a species', 'and is rewarded only after a long journey'; '— often in an annual cycle —' = parenthetical
Grammar NoteA chain of relative clauses hanging off one noun. Read to the end before deciding the meaning.
Simple MeaningMigration is roughly travel that happens regularly, often yearly, may involve many animals, and pays off only after a long trip.
中文它可以宽泛地描述为定期(常为年度周期)发生的旅行,可能涉及一个物种的许多成员,而且只有在长途跋涉之后才会得到回报。
The arctic tern resists distraction because it is driven at that moment by an instinctive sense of something we humans find admirable: larger purpose.
Main ClauseThe arctic tern resists distraction
Subordinate / Relative'because it is driven … by an instinctive sense of something' = reason clause; 'we humans find admirable' = contact relative clause (no 'that'); ': larger purpose' = colon explanation
Grammar NoteNote the missing relative pronoun: 'something (that) we humans find admirable'. The colon then names it.
Simple MeaningThe tern ignores food because instinct gives it a sense of a bigger goal.
中文北极燕鸥之所以能抵御干扰,是因为此刻它被一种我们人类觉得可敬的本能所驱动:更宏大的目的。
Generally the reason for such seasonal back-and-forth movement is to seek resources that aren't available within a single area year-round.
Main Clausethe reason … is to seek resources
Subordinate / Relative'that aren't available within a single area year-round' = defining relative clause
Grammar Note'The reason for X is to + verb' — a clean cause-and-purpose pattern worth copying in Writing Task 2.
Simple MeaningAnimals move back and forth each season to find things they cannot get in one place all year.
中文一般来说,这种季节性往返移动的原因,是为了寻找在单一区域内无法全年获得的资源。
They allow for the fact that, for example, aphids will become sensitive to blue light (from the sky) when it's time for takeoff on their big journey, and sensitive to yellow light (reflected from tender young leaves) when it's appropriate to land.
Main ClauseThey allow for the fact
Subordinate / Relative'that … aphids will become sensitive to blue light … and sensitive to yellow light …' = noun clause; two 'when'-clauses; two bracketed explanations
Grammar Note'allow for the fact that' = 'take into account that'. Note the parallel 'sensitive to X when… and sensitive to Y when…'.
Simple MeaningHis five features explain why aphids react to blue light when taking off and to yellow light when landing.
中文这些特征考虑到了这样的事实:例如,当到了启程的时候,蚜虫会对(来自天空的)蓝光敏感;而当适合降落时,则对(从鲜嫩幼叶反射的)黄光敏感。
At one of the bottlenecks, forested hills rise to form a V, leaving a corridor of open ground only about 150 metres wide, filled with private homes.
Main Clauseforested hills rise to form a V
Subordinate / Relative'leaving a corridor of open ground only about 150 metres wide' = result participle; 'filled with private homes' = reduced passive relative
Grammar NoteA short main clause followed by two stacked participle phrases — the sentence pattern that carries Q26.
Simple MeaningAt one bottleneck the wooded hills form a V, so only a 150-metre strip of open ground remains, and it is full of houses.
中文在其中一个瓶颈处,林木覆盖的山丘隆起形成一个V字形,只留下一条约150米宽的开阔通道,而那里布满了私人住宅。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Definition and refinement. The passage opens with a loose definition, refines it with Dingle's five characteristics, then contrasts it with Berger's simpler working definition. Q15 turns on the observation that definitions differ by field of study.

Illustration by contrast. The arctic tern ignores the herring; the local gulls dive for it. That single contrast demonstrates 'attentiveness to the greater mission' — and makes Q14 FALSE.

Example → principle. Zooplankton (daily, vertical) and aphids (one-way, never returning) both stretch the everyday idea of migration. The writer uses them to show why a broader definition is needed.

Problem → threat → response. The pronghorn section moves from description (route, bottlenecks) to threat (development choking the corridor) to response (protected migration corridor; limits of what agencies can do on private land).

Cause and effect with conditions. 'If they can't pass through each of the three during their spring migration, they can't reach their bounty of summer grazing; if they can't pass through again in autumn … they are likely to die.' Two parallel conditionals show how fragile the route is.
