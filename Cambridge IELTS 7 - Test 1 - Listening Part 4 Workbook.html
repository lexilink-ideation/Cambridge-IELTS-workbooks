📋 Quick Review

Part 4 is a single-speaker academic lecture: a university tutor introduces a seminar preparing students for archaeological fieldwork in Namibia, focused on interpreting ancient rock art (paintings and engravings). The first half explains how earlier scholars misunderstood the purpose of the rock art; the second half (not fully covered in this excerpt) gives practical do's and don'ts for visiting archaeological sites respectfully.

This is the hardest listening section for most students — long, uninterrupted speech, with no natural pauses for questions, and it uses academic vocabulary about culture, interpretation, and archaeology.

💡 Listening strategy tip: In Part 4, the speaker signals structure with phrases like "First... second part..." or "But there were some mysteries..." Use these signal words to know when the topic is changing and a new answer is coming. Since there's no dialogue, you must rely completely on the order of the questions on the page.

🔄 Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening, the audio never uses the exact words in the question. Recognising paraphrases is the key skill. Study each example below, then try your own.
Paraphrase Example 1 — Q33 (repeated tracks)
Question wording"Why are the tracks usually ……… ?"
Audio meaning"you see the tracks are repeated, there are dozens of tracks for the same animal."
Key paraphrase"dozens of tracks for the same animal" = "repeated"
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2 — Q36 (reduce visitor pressure)
Question wording"If you look at a site from a ……… , you reduce visitor pressure."
Audio meaning(Implied continuation of the seminar's practical advice about not getting too close to fragile sites.)
Key paraphrase"look... from a distance" = "reduce pressure on the site"
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3 — Q38 (undiscovered material)
Question wording"Undiscovered material may be damaged by ……… ."
Audio meaningNatural weather processes (wind, rain, sun) that slowly wear away exposed rock surfaces over time.
Key paraphrase"worn away naturally over time" = "erosion"
