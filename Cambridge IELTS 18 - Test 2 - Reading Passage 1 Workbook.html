📋 Passage Overview

Stonehenge. This passage surveys the mysteries of the prehistoric monument on Salisbury Plain — how it was built in several stages, competing theories about how the bluestones were moved, who the builders were, and what the site was for (a burial ground, a calendar or a place of healing). Question types: note completion and True/False/Not Given.

💡 Reading tip: For note completion, follow the passage in order and copy words exactly (spelling counts). For TRUE/FALSE/NOT GIVEN, keep to what the text actually states — Q13 is NOT GIVEN because the passage never says who Hawkins's critics were.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

First, Neolithic Britons used primitive tools, which may have been fashioned out of deer antlers, to dig a massive circular ditch and bank, or henge.
Main ClauseNeolithic Britons used primitive tools to dig a massive circular ditch and bank.
Subordinate / Relative'which may have been fashioned out of deer antlers' (non-defining relative).
Grammar Note'may have been + past participle' shows past possibility; 'or henge' renames the ditch and bank.
Simple MeaningEarly Britons dug a big circular ditch using simple tools, perhaps made from deer antlers.
中文首先，新石器时代的不列颠人用原始工具（可能由鹿角制成）挖出一条巨大的环形沟和土堤，即所谓的"henge"。
How, then, did prehistoric builders without sophisticated tools or engineering haul these boulders, which weigh up to four tons, over such a great distance?
Main ClauseHow did prehistoric builders haul these boulders over such a great distance?
Subordinate / Relative'without sophisticated tools or engineering' (prepositional phrase) + 'which weigh up to four tons' (relative).
Grammar NoteA rhetorical question; the relative clause adds the weight detail.
Simple MeaningHow could early people, with no advanced tools, move four-ton stones so far?
中文那么，没有先进工具或工程技术的史前建造者，是如何将这些重达四吨的巨石搬运如此远的距离的呢？
Challenging the classic image of industrious builders pushing, carting, rolling or hauling giant stones from faraway Wales, some scientists have suggested that it was glaciers, not humans, that carried the bluestones to Salisbury Plain.
Main ClauseSome scientists have suggested that it was glaciers that carried the bluestones.
Subordinate / Relative'Challenging the classic image of industrious builders…' (participial phrase) + 'that carried…' (cleft structure).
Grammar Note'it was glaciers, not humans, that…' is a cleft sentence for emphasis.
Simple MeaningSome scientists argue glaciers, not people, moved the bluestones to the plain.
中文一些科学家对勤劳建造者从遥远的威尔士推、拉、滚运巨石的经典形象提出质疑，认为是冰川而非人类把蓝石带到了索尔兹伯里平原。
While there is consensus among the majority of modern scholars that Stonehenge once served the function of burial ground, they have yet to determine what other purposes it had.
Main ClauseThey have yet to determine what other purposes it had.
Subordinate / Relative'While there is consensus… that Stonehenge once served the function of burial ground' (concession) + 'what other purposes it had' (noun clause).
Grammar Note'While' concedes one agreed point before stating what is still unknown.
Simple MeaningScholars agree it was a burial ground but still don't know its other uses.
中文尽管大多数现代学者一致认为巨石阵曾用作墓地，但他们尚未确定它还有哪些其他用途。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Chronology: the passage is organised by construction stages (ditch/henge → bluestones → sarsens), which is exactly how the Q1–8 notes are laid out.

Competing theories: for the bluestones' transport, the writer lists an archaeological view (sledges, rollers, rafts, oxen) and a geological one (glaciers). Sorting these apart answers Q3–5.

Concession & refutation: Aubrey's 'druids' theory was popular, 'However… radiocarbon dating demonstrated that Stonehenge stood more than 1,000 years before the Celts.' The 'however' overturns the claim (Q11).

Speculation signalling: words like 'may have', 'it is thought', 'some believe', 'suggested' mark ideas that are not certain — useful for spotting NOT GIVEN and FALSE.
