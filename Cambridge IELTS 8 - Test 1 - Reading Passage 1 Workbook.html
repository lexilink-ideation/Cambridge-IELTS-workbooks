📋 Passage Overview

This passage, 'The measurement of time', traces how humans have measured time from ancient calendars to modern clocks. It explains the three natural cycles (day, lunar month, solar year), Babylonian and Egyptian calendars, temporal hours, sundials and water clocks, and the mechanical clock — the escapement, the coiled spring, the pendulum, the 1670 anchor escapement and the grandfather clock — ending with today's quartz and satellite timekeeping.

💡 Reading strategy tip: With paragraph-matching and matching-features questions, do NOT read top to bottom first. Scan for names, dates and keywords (Babylonians, 1670, grandfather clock) and jump to that spot. The answers are NOT in passage order for these task types.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"Before the invention of artificial light, the moon had greater social impact."
Main Clausethe moon had greater social impact
Subordinate / RelativeBefore the invention of artificial light (adverbial phrase of time)
Grammar NoteFronted time phrase sets the context; comma separates it from the main clause.
Simple MeaningLong ago, when there were no electric lights, the moon mattered more to people.
中文在人造光发明之前，月亮对社会的影响更大。
"As the Roman Empire expanded northward, it organised its activity chart for the most part around the solar year."
Main Clauseit organised its activity chart … around the solar year
Subordinate / RelativeAs the Roman Empire expanded northward (adverbial clause, 'as' = when/because)
Grammar Note'As' shows time + reason; 'for the most part' = mostly.
Simple MeaningWhen Rome grew towards the north, it mainly planned life by the solar year.
中文随着罗马帝国向北扩张，它主要按太阳年来安排活动。
"The revolutionary aspect of this new timekeeper was neither the descending weight … nor the gear wheels …; it was the part called the escapement."
Main Clauseit was the part called the escapement
Subordinate / Relativeneither the descending weight … nor the gear wheels … (paired negative)
Grammar Note'neither … nor …' rules out two things; the semicolon introduces the real answer.
Simple MeaningWhat was truly new was not the weight or gears, but the escapement.
中文真正革命性的不是下垂的重锤，也不是齿轮，而是叫做“擒纵机构”的部件。
"So integral have these time-based technologies become to day-to-day existence that our dependency on them is recognised only when they fail to work."
Main Clauseour dependency on them is recognised only when they fail to work
Subordinate / RelativeSo integral have these technologies become … that … (so + adj + that, inverted)
Grammar NoteInversion after 'So integral' for emphasis; 'so … that …' = result.
Simple MeaningThese technologies are so essential that we notice our reliance only when they stop working.
中文这些技术已如此不可或缺，以至于只有在它们失灵时我们才意识到自己有多依赖它们。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect — The 1670 anchor escapement allowed a long pendulum that beats once a second, which led to the grandfather clock. Signal words: led to, allowed, meant that, resulted in.

Contrast — Water clocks worked well around the Mediterranean, but could not be relied on in the freezing weather of northern Europe. Signal: but, however, although.

Comparison / Latitude — Lower-latitude calendars followed the lunar cycle; northern, farming regions relied more on the solar year. Signal: whereas, more … than, however.

Chronology — The passage moves through time: Babylonians → Egyptians → Greeks/Romans → 13th-c. mechanical clock → 1400s spring → 1600s pendulum → today. Dates anchor paragraph-matching answers.
