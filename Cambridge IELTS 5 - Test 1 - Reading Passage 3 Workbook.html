📋 Passage Overview

This passage, 'The Truth about the Environment', argues against widespread environmental pessimism. The writer claims natural resources have become more abundant, more food is produced per person, extinction rates are lower than predicted, and much pollution is exaggerated or temporary. He identifies four reasons why public perception is worse than reality — biased research funding, campaigning environmental groups, the media's appetite for bad news, and poor individual perception — and argues that adapting to global warming may be cheaper than drastically cutting emissions.

💡 Reading strategy tip: This is an argument passage, so 'YES / NO / NOT GIVEN' asks about the writer's claims, not general facts. Ask: does the writer actually make this claim? Beware statements that sound reasonable but that the writer never argues.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"For many environmentalists, the world seems to be getting worse … that natural resources are running out; that the population is ever growing, leaving less to eat."
Main Clausethe world seems to be getting worse
Subordinate / Relativethat natural resources are running out; that the population is ever growing … (list of 'that'-clauses = the fears)
Grammar NoteA colon/semicolon list of noun clauses spells out the 'hit-list of fears'.
Simple MeaningEnvironmentalists list several fears: resources running out, population rising, less food.
中文在许多环保主义者看来，世界似乎在变糟：自然资源正在枯竭，人口不断增长，可供食用的越来越少。
"Most forms of environmental pollution either appear to have been exaggerated, or are transient — associated with the early phases of industrialisation."
Main ClauseMost forms of environmental pollution … appear to have been exaggerated, or are transient
Subordinate / Relativeassociated with the early phases of industrialisation (reduced relative explaining 'transient')
Grammar Note'either … or …' offers two claims; the dash adds explanation.
Simple MeaningMost pollution is either overstated or only temporary, tied to early industrial stages.
中文大多数环境污染要么似乎被夸大了，要么是短暂的——与工业化早期阶段相关。
"Understandably, perhaps, they sometimes overstate their case … seen as altruistic, even if an impartial view might suggest they are doing more harm than good."
Main Clausethey sometimes overstate their case
Subordinate / Relativeeven if an impartial view … might suggest they are doing more harm than good (concessive 'even if' clause)
Grammar Note'even if' concedes a point; 'more harm than good' is a fixed comparison.
Simple MeaningGreen groups may exaggerate; a neutral look might show their actions can do harm.
中文或许可以理解，他们有时会夸大其词——即使客观来看，他们的做法弊大于利。
"Economic analyses clearly show it will be far more expensive to cut carbon dioxide emissions radically than to pay the costs of adaptation to the increased temperatures."
Main ClauseEconomic analyses clearly show (that) it will be far more expensive to cut emissions … than to pay the costs of adaptation
Subordinate / Relativeto the increased temperatures (prepositional phrase modifying 'adaptation')
Grammar Note'it … to cut … than to pay …' compares two costed options with 'more … than'.
Simple MeaningStudies show that sharply cutting CO2 costs much more than simply adapting to warmer temperatures.
中文经济分析清楚地表明，大幅削减二氧化碳排放的代价，远高于为适应升温所付出的代价。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Claim + Evidence — The writer states the world is not getting worse, then gives four supporting facts (resources, food, extinction ~0.7%, pollution). Each fact answers a specific fear.

Contrast (perception vs reality) — Opinion polls say standards are declining, but the facts suggest otherwise. This gap is explained by four factors. Signal: Yet, however, but.

Cost–benefit reasoning — El Niño: $4bn damage but $19bn benefit; cutting CO2 is far more expensive than adapting. Numbers drive the argument.

Author's attitude — Sceptical of environmental alarm; he thinks the 'response' to warming may be a bigger problem than the warming itself.
