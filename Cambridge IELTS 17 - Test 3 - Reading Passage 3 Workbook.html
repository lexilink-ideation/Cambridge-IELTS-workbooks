📋 Passage Overview

Building the Skyline — Katharine Shester reviews Jason Barr's book on the history of Manhattan's skyscrapers. The review walks through the book's two parts and chapters (settlement, the grid, immigration, skyscraper economics, the 'bedrock myth', the 1920s boom, land values) and offers praise and mild criticism. Question types: multiple choice (Q27–31), YES / NO / NOT GIVEN (Q32–35) and summary from a phrase list (Q36–40).

💡 Reading tip: This is a review, so separate what Barr says (the book's content) from what Shester thinks (her evaluation). YES/NO/NG and the MC questions test Shester's opinion — look for 'I would have liked', 'what I enjoyed most', 'more technical than would be preferred'.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

The book combines geology, history, economics, and a lot of data to explain why business clusters developed where they did and how the early decisions of workers and firms shaped the skyline we see today.
Main ClauseThe book combines geology, history, economics, and a lot of data
Subordinate / Relativeto explain why business clusters developed where they did and how the early decisions… shaped the skyline (infinitive of purpose + two embedded questions); (that) we see today (contact relative clause)
Grammar NoteInfinitive of purpose 'to explain' controls two indirect questions ('why…' and 'how…').
Simple MeaningUsing many fields and data, the book explains why businesses clustered and how early choices shaped today's skyline.
中文这本书结合地质、历史、经济和大量数据,解释了商业集群为何在特定地点形成,以及工人和企业的早期决策如何塑造了我们今天所见的天际线。
I would have liked Barr to expand upon his claim that existing tenements prevented skyscrapers in certain neighborhoods.
Main ClauseI would have liked Barr to expand upon his claim
Subordinate / Relativethat existing tenements prevented skyscrapers in certain neighborhoods (noun clause defining 'claim')
Grammar Note'would have liked + object + to-infinitive' expresses a wish about the past; 'that…' explains the claim.
Simple MeaningI wish Barr had said more about his idea that tenements stopped skyscrapers in some areas.
中文我本希望巴尔能更详细地阐述他的说法——即现有的廉租公寓阻止了某些街区建造摩天大楼。
For example, the introduction of iron and steel skeletal frames made thick, load-bearing walls unnecessary, expanding the usable square footage of buildings.
Main Clausethe introduction of iron and steel skeletal frames made thick, load-bearing walls unnecessary
Subordinate / Relativeexpanding the usable square footage of buildings (participle clause of result)
Grammar Note'made + object + adjective' (causative); '-ing' clause shows the consequence.
Simple MeaningIron and steel frames removed the need for thick walls, so buildings had more usable space.
中文例如,铁和钢骨架的出现使厚重的承重墙变得不必要,从而扩大了建筑的可用面积。
He finds that changes in land values predict future building height, but the reverse is not true.
Main ClauseHe finds that changes in land values predict future building height, but the reverse is not true
Subordinate / Relativethat changes in land values predict future building height (noun clause = what he finds)
Grammar NoteReporting verb 'finds' + that-clause; 'but' introduces the contrasting clause 'the reverse is not true'.
Simple MeaningHe shows that land values predict building height, but height does not predict land values.
中文他发现地价的变化能预测未来的建筑高度,但反过来并不成立。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Review structure. The review follows the book chapter by chapter, alternating summary with judgement. Track the shift markers ('What I enjoyed most', 'I would have liked') to separate opinion from content.

Concession + criticism. Shester praises the research but flags a possible inconsistency (tenements vs bedrock costs) and warns some chapters are too technical or of limited appeal — the source of Q28, Q29 and Q30.

Cause vs effect. The final chapter asks whether skyscrapers cause or result from high land values; Barr concludes values predict height, not the reverse. Recognising this directional claim answers Q31 and Q35.
