Practice Workbook — "The True Cost of Food"

📋 Passage Overview

This passage argues that although food in the West has become cheaper to buy since the 1960s, its real cost is much higher once environmental and social damage is taken into account. Intensive farming methods (mechanisation, chemical fertilisers, pesticides, monocultures, battery rearing, and genetic engineering) have caused pollution, wildlife loss, and health risks — costs economists call "externalities" because nobody pays for them directly at the checkout.

The writer presents research by Professor Jules Pretty, who calculated a total figure for these hidden costs in British agriculture, then discusses possible solutions, including a proposed "Greener Food Standard" as a realistic middle ground between intensive and fully organic farming.

💡 Reading strategy tip: This passage is organised in labelled paragraphs (A–G), which is a strong signal that you'll face "which paragraph contains…" matching questions. Note the function of each paragraph as you read (introduction, historical stages, definition of a term, data/statistics, and proposed solution) so you can locate information fast.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"It has now reached a point where a growing number of people believe that it is far too high, and that bringing it down will be one of the great challenges of the twenty first century."
Main ClauseIt has now reached a point
Subordinate / Relative Clause"where a growing number of people believe that..." — relative clause modifying "point", containing two parallel "that" noun clauses as objects of "believe"
Grammar Note"where" introduces a relative clause referring to an abstract noun ("a point"); the two "that" clauses are joined by "and" as parallel objects.
Simple MeaningFood costs have risen so much that many people now think it's too expensive and that lowering it is a major 21st-century challenge.
中文如今已经达到这样一个地步：越来越多的人认为食品成本过高，而降低这一成本将成为21世纪的一大挑战。
"The cost is in the collateral damage of the very methods of food production that have made the food cheaper: in the pollution of water, the enervation of soil, the destruction of wildlife, the harm to animal welfare and the threat to human health caused by modern industrial agriculture."
Main ClauseThe cost is in the collateral damage of the very methods of food production
Subordinate / Relative Clause"that have made the food cheaper" — relative clause modifying "methods"; the colon introduces an explanatory list
Grammar NoteRelative pronoun "that" refers to "methods"; the colon signals five parallel noun phrases elaborating "collateral damage".
Simple MeaningThe hidden cost is damage caused by the very farming methods that made food cheap: water pollution, soil damage, wildlife loss, animal cruelty, and health risks.
中文这种代价体现在使食品变得廉价的那些生产方式所造成的附带损害中：水污染、土壤退化、野生动物遭到破坏、动物福利受损，以及现代工业化农业对人类健康构成的威胁。
"That is mainly because the costs of all this damage are what economists refer to as externalities: they are outside the main transaction, which is for example producing and selling a field of wheat, and are borne directly by neither producers nor consumers."
Main Clausethe costs of all this damage are what economists refer to as externalities
Subordinate / Relative Clause"which is for example producing and selling a field of wheat" (non-restrictive relative clause on "the main transaction"); "and are borne directly by neither producers nor consumers" (coordinated predicate)
Grammar Note"what economists refer to as X" is a nominal relative clause as subject complement; "neither... nor..." expresses double negation.
Simple MeaningThese are called "externalities" — costs outside the main sale/purchase, which neither buyers nor sellers pay directly.
中文这主要是因为这些损害成本正是经济学家所称的"外部性"：它们游离于主要交易（例如种植和出售一片小麦地）之外，既不由生产者也不由消费者直接承担。
"Professor Pretty and his colleagues calculated the externalities of British agriculture for one particular year."
Main ClauseProfessor Pretty and his colleagues calculated the externalities of British agriculture for one particular year (whole sentence)
Subordinate / Relative ClauseNone — a simple sentence, useful as a base for combining exercises
Grammar NoteStraightforward Subject–Verb–Object structure with a time adverbial ("for one particular year").
Simple MeaningProfessor Pretty's team worked out the hidden costs of British farming for one year.
中文普雷蒂教授及其同事计算了英国农业在某一特定年份中产生的外部性成本。
"We are paying for our supposedly cheaper food in three separate ways: once over the counter, secondly through our taxes, which provide the enormous subsidies propping up modern intensive farming, and thirdly to clean up the mess that modern farming leaves behind."
Main ClauseWe are paying for our supposedly cheaper food in three separate ways
Subordinate / Relative Clause"which provide the enormous subsidies propping up modern intensive farming" (relative clause on "taxes"); "that modern farming leaves behind" (relative clause on "the mess")
Grammar NoteThree parallel adverbial phrases ("once...", "secondly...", "thirdly...") explain "three separate ways", with two relative clauses nested inside.
Simple MeaningWe pay for food three times: at the checkout, through taxes funding farm subsidies, and through cleaning up farming's damage.
中文我们以三种不同的方式为所谓"更便宜"的食品买单：一是在收银台直接付款，二是通过为现代集约化农业提供巨额补贴的税收，三是为清理现代农业遗留的烂摊子买单。
"He is recommending the immediate introduction of a 'Greener Food Standard', which would push the market towards more sustainable environmental practices than the current norm, while not requiring the full commitment to organic production."
Main ClauseHe is recommending the immediate introduction of a 'Greener Food Standard'
Subordinate / Relative Clause"which would push the market towards more sustainable... practices" (non-restrictive relative clause); "while not requiring the full commitment to organic production" (concessive/contrast clause)
Grammar NoteNon-restrictive relative clause adds extra information about the standard; "while" here signals contrast, not time.
Simple MeaningHe recommends a "Greener Food Standard" that pushes farming to be more sustainable than now, without requiring full organic conversion.
中文他建议立即推行一项"绿色食品标准"，该标准将推动市场朝着比目前更可持续的环保实践方向发展，同时无需完全承诺转向有机生产。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect

Intensive farming methods (chemical fertilisers, pesticides, monocultures) → cause the decline of farmland birds, disappearance of hedgerows/ponds, and pollution of rivers and lakes. The passage builds a chain: cheap production methods → environmental damage → hidden financial costs.

Contrast

The passage contrasts the immediate cash price of food (which has fallen since 1960) with its true, hidden cost (which has risen due to externalities). It also contrasts full organic farming (rejected as too big a jump) with the proposed middle-ground "Greener Food Standard".

Comparison

The total externality figure (£2,343m) is compared to government/EU farming subsidies for the same year ("almost as much again"), emphasising the huge scale of hidden costs relative to visible public spending.

Examples

Paragraph E gives concrete examples of externality costs: £120m for pesticide removal, £16m for nitrates, £169m from food poisoning, £607m from cattle disease — supporting the claim that damage "can actually be quantified".

Definitions

Key term: externalities = costs of an economic activity that fall outside the main transaction and are not paid directly by either producer or consumer (defined in paragraph C).

Problem / Solution

Problem: cheap food hides massive environmental/social costs paid indirectly through taxes and clean-up. Solution proposed: a government-led "Greener Food Standard" — a realistic step toward sustainability without requiring full organic conversion.

Author's Attitude

The writer is critical of intensive farming's hidden damage but pragmatic about solutions — acknowledging that full organic farming "would be too big a jump" for many farmers and would raise prices beyond poorer consumers' reach.
