Listening Part 2

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 4 — Listening Part 2

Football Stadium Tour · Multiple Choice (two answers) + Matching (Q11–20)

📋 Quick Review

A stadium guide is welcoming a group of visitors — many of them families with children — before taking them round a football stadium. In the first half he gives practical advice for parents (stairs, a dark players’ tunnel, photographs, the cafe, a drawing competition) and then lists the features of the tour, saying which ones are brand new. In the second half he tells the story of the club’s site and of English football in the 1870s, giving one key event for each year. Questions 11–14 are pick TWO letters from A–E; questions 15–20 are matching six years to six events from a box of eight.

Who is speaking? One speaker only: a stadium guide giving a welcome talk to visitors before a tour. Because it is a monologue there is no second voice to confirm anything — the guide corrects and qualifies himself (“which has been very popular over the years… we’ve only just introduced this”), and those qualifications are where the answers hide.

The situation 情境: 一位讲解员在带团参观足球场之前先做介绍：先是给带小孩的家长的注意事项（楼梯多、球员通道很暗、可以拍照、咖啡厅、儿童画画比赛），再介绍这次导览有哪些项目、其中哪两项是今年新推出的；最后讲这家俱乐部所在地的历史，以及1870–1880年间英国足球每一年发生的大事（Q15–20）。

Task type: Q11–12 and Q13–14: choose TWO letters, A–E. The two letters score one mark each and may be written in either order. Q15–20: matching — choose six answers from a box of eight (A–H) and write one letter next to each year. Two letters are never used and no letter is used twice.

💡 Listening strategy tip: The two halves need two completely different techniques. For Q11–14 the guide touches on every option, so you cannot choose an answer just because you heard the words. Tick an option only when you can point to the exact idea in the stem — “a competition”, “must stay”, “new this year”. For Q15–20 write the six years down the side of your page before the audio starts, then track the years as he moves forward in time. He goes in date order (1870 → 1880) but he also mentions extra years that are not in the question list (1989, 1872, 1873), so keep your pen on the year you are waiting for and let the others pass. 配对题一定要按时间顺序跟读，多余年份直接跳过。
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • Q11–12, option A (photo with a player). You really do hear “take pictures”, but what you photograph is “your child shooting a penalty” — no player is involved. Hearing the topic is not the same as matching the detail.
  • Q11–12, options D and E. The guide says it is “very hot and sunny” and recommends the “healthy lunch boxes”, but he never says children need sunhats and drinks, and a recommendation is not a special offer. Watch out for stems that upgrade a comment into an instruction or a discount.
  • Q13–14, option B (360 cinema). It is the very first feature mentioned, so it feels like the answer — but “which has been very popular over the years” tells you it is old. Option E (other languages) is only something they are “thinking of offering… in future”. The two new ones are both flagged with “we’ve only just”.
  • New clubs are mentioned twice (1872–73 and 1877), but “new clubs” is not in the box. 1872–73 is not even a question. For 1877 you must ignore the founding of clubs and wait for “in the same year… a limit of 90 minutes” (H).
  • 1874 contains TWO rule changes. Sending players off and swapping ends at half-time — that is why the answer is F (two changes to the rules), not a single-change option. Do not stop listening after the first fact.
  • Option A (pay for players) is actively denied. In 1880 clubs began charging fans, “even though players were still amateurs”. A and E are the two letters you never use.

offencen.an action that breaks a rule or law犯规；违例“If they committed certain offences.”British spelling offence, American offense (the transcript uses the American form). Both are accepted in IELTS.
swapv.to exchange one thing for another交换“Teams started having to swap ends at half-time.”This is the SECOND rule change in 1874 — the one that makes the answer F (two changes) rather than a single change.
crossbarn.the horizontal bar across the top of a goal球门横梁“That tape was replaced with the solid crossbar.”Answer to Q17 (B). tape → solid crossbar = a change to the design of the goal.
foundingn.the act of starting an organisation创立；成立“1877 saw the founding of further new clubs.”A deliberate distractor: new clubs are not in the box. Keep listening to the end of the 1877 sentence.
whistlen.a small instrument blown to make a loud sound哨子“Referees started using whistles.”Silent ‘t’: /‘wis–l/. Another 1878 fact that is not in the box — the box wants the lights.
installv.to put equipment somewhere so it can be used安装“Electric lamps were installed on certain pitches.”Answer to Q19 (C). electric lamps = lights; ‘games could be played in the evenings’ confirms it.
admissionn.the money you pay to enter a place入场费；准入“Clubs began to charge fans for admission to games.”Answer to Q20 (G). charge fans for admission = a fee for spectators.
amateurn./adj.doing something for pleasure, not for money业余的；业余爱好者“Even though players were still amateurs.”This one word kills option A (pay for players) for 1880. Spelling: a-m-a-t-e-u-r.
spectatorn.someone who watches a sports event观众“A fee for spectators” (question paper) = “charge fans” (audio).fans / crowd / supporters / spectators are one paraphrase family. The paper uses the formal word, the audio the informal one.
significantadj.important; large enough to matter重大的；显著的“This was a significant change as games could be played in the evenings.”A strong evaluation word. When a speaker says something was significant, an answer is usually right there.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening the audio never uses the exact words printed on the question paper. Recognising paraphrase is the whole skill. 雅思听力中，录音几乎不会重复题干原词——识别同义替换才是关键。
Paraphrase Example 1
Question wording“Parents must stay with their children at all times.” (option C)
What you hear“The stadium has lots of stairs and the players’ tunnel is very dark. Please don’t let your children wander off on their own, even for a minute. We don’t want any accidents.”
Key paraphrasea negative instruction (don’t let them go off alone) = a positive obligation (parents must stay with them)
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question wording“There is a competition for children today.” (option B)
What you hear“Children are invited to do a football themed drawing. We’ll pick the best one at the end of the afternoon… we’ll send your prize.”
Key paraphrasepick the best one + a prize = a competition. The word ‘competition’ is never said.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question wording“Children can get their photo taken with a football player.” (option A — WRONG)
What you hear“Cameras are permitted everywhere and you can take pictures of your child shooting a penalty.”
Key paraphraseThe topic (photos) matches, but the detail (with a player) does not. Topic match ≠ answer.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question wording“audio guide” — new this year (option C)
What you hear“If you’d prefer your visit to be self-guided, please collect headphones from the reception, and then you can listen to the pre-recorded information at your own speed. We’ve only just introduced this feature.”
Key paraphraseheadphones + pre-recorded information = audio guide; ‘only just introduced’ = new this year
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 5
Question wording“360 cinema experience” — new this year? (option B — WRONG)
What you hear“We’ll start with the 360 cinema experience, which has been very popular over the years.”
Key paraphrase‘over the years’ = it has existed for a long time = NOT new. One relative clause cancels the whole option.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 6
Question wording“the introduction of goalkeepers” (Q15, letter D)
What you hear“1870 was also the year that football teams started to include a player whose role it was to guard the goal.”
Key paraphraseThe box names the job (goalkeeper); the audio describes the job (guard the goal).
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 7
Question wording“the introduction of a fee for spectators” (Q20, letter G)
What you hear“In 1880, clubs began to charge fans for admission to games, even though players were still amateurs.”
Key paraphrasecharge = a fee; fans = spectators; admission = getting in. ‘Still amateurs’ rules out option A (pay for players).

A recommendation (‘I really recommend the healthy lunch boxes’) is not a special offer. Watch for stems that add a price idea that was never said.

‘Only just’ = very recently. A means it is old, and C means it does not exist yet.
