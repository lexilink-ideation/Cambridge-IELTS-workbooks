📋 Passage Overview

Information theory. Six paragraphs (A–F) explain Claude Shannon's science of communication. It opens with the 2002 repair of the Voyager I probe 12 billion km from Earth, then covers Shannon's life and modesty, his definition of information as the bit, his theorems about noise and bandwidth, the coding methods that followed (Voyager codes, bar codes, turbo codes), and finally data compression. Question types: matching information to paragraphs (Q27–32), note completion (Q33–37) and True/False/Not Given (Q38–40).

💡 Reading tip: Q33–37 retells paragraph A in order, so once you find 'In April 2002 an event took place…' you can read straight down for all five answers. Two of them need two words joined by 'and' (Q33 and Q35) — the instruction says both are required for one mark, so never write just one.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

After 25 years of exposure to the freezing temperatures of deep space, the probe was beginning to show its age.
Main ClauseThe probe was beginning to show its age.
Subordinate / Relative'After 25 years of exposure to the freezing temperatures of deep space' = fronted time/cause phrase.
Grammar NoteA long prepositional phrase supplies both duration and cause before the short main clause — a common way to open a narrative paragraph.
Simple MeaningTwenty-five years in the extreme cold of space had started to wear the probe out.
中文在深空的极寒中暴露了 25 年之后，这个探测器开始显出老态。
Even travelling at the speed of light, it took over 11 hours to reach its target, far beyond the orbit of Pluto.
Main ClauseIt took over 11 hours to reach its target.
Subordinate / Relative'Even travelling at the speed of light' (concessive participial phrase) + 'far beyond the orbit of Pluto' (appositive describing 'target').
Grammar Note'Even + -ing' concedes the fastest possible speed to emphasise the distance. Note Q37 is about the radio dish, not this sentence.
Simple MeaningDespite moving at light speed, the signal needed more than 11 hours to arrive, far past Pluto's orbit.
中文即便以光速传播，信号也用了 11 个多小时才抵达目标——那里远在冥王星轨道之外。
While at Bell Laboratories, Shannon developed information theory, but shunned the resulting acclaim.
Main ClauseShannon developed information theory, but shunned the resulting acclaim.
Subordinate / Relative'While at Bell Laboratories' = reduced time clause (= while he was at…).
Grammar NoteA reduced clause with the subject and verb omitted. The short 'but' clause is the whole answer to Q29 — attitude to fame.
Simple MeaningAt Bell Labs, Shannon created information theory but avoided the fame it brought.
中文在贝尔实验室期间，香农创立了信息论，却回避了随之而来的赞誉。
In particular, Shannon showed that noise sets a limit on the rate at which information can pass along communication channels while remaining error-free.
Main ClauseShannon showed that noise sets a limit on the rate.
Subordinate / Relative'at which information can pass along communication channels' (relative clause on 'rate') + 'while remaining error-free' (reduced concessive).
Grammar Note'the rate at which…' is a formal alternative to 'the rate that'. This sentence and the next together answer Q39.
Simple MeaningShannon proved that noise limits how fast information can travel without errors.
中文香农特别指出，噪声为信息在通信信道中保持无差错传输的速率设定了上限。
This rate depends on the relative strengths of the signal and noise travelling down the communication channel, and on its capacity (its 'bandwidth').
Main ClauseThis rate depends on the relative strengths of the signal and noise, and on its capacity.
Subordinate / Relative'travelling down the communication channel' (participial phrase) + the bracketed gloss '(its bandwidth)'.
Grammar Note'depends on A… and on B' coordinates two factors. Q39 asks whether signal strength and noise level determine the rate — this sentence says yes.
Simple MeaningThat speed limit depends on how strong the signal is compared with the noise, and on the channel's capacity.
中文该速率取决于沿通信信道传输的信号与噪声的相对强度，以及信道的容量（即「带宽」）。
Other codes have become part of everyday life - such as the Universal Product Code, or bar code, which uses a simple error-detecting system that ensures supermarket check-out lasers can read the price even on, say, a crumpled bag of crisps.
Main ClauseOther codes have become part of everyday life.
Subordinate / Relative'such as the Universal Product Code, or bar code' (exemplification) + 'which uses a simple error-detecting system' (relative) + 'that ensures supermarket check-out lasers can read the price' (second relative).
Grammar NoteTwo stacked relative clauses build a chain of explanation. Q30 asks for 'a machine capable of interpreting incomplete information' — the check-out laser reading a crumpled bag.
Simple MeaningBar codes are now everyday: their error-detecting system lets supermarket scanners read prices even on a crushed crisp packet.
中文其他编码已成为日常生活的一部分——例如通用产品码（即条形码），它采用一种简单的检错系统，使超市收银激光扫描器即便在皱巴巴的薯片袋上也能读出价格。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Hook → biography → theory → applications: the passage opens with a dramatic story (Voyager), moves back to the man (Shannon), then to the theory (bits, noise, bandwidth), and finally to what the theory built (codes, compression). Recognising this shape places every question type instantly.

Chronology: 1916 born → 1939 graduate student at MIT → 1940s creates information theory at Bell Labs → 1977 Voyager launched → 1993 turbo codes → 2001 Shannon dies → April 2002 the Voyager repair. Note that the passage does NOT tell this in order, which is exactly why Q27–32 is a matching task.

Problem → solution: sensors and circuits failing 12 billion km away (problem) → send a message telling the probe to switch to spares (solution) → success. Paragraph A is one complete problem-solution cycle and supplies all of Q33–37.

Definition: 'the binary unit, or bit, of the form 1 or 0'; 'random interference - noise'; 'its capacity (its bandwidth)'. Every technical term is glossed inline with a comma, dash or bracket — that punctuation is your signal.

Cause & effect: noise exists (cause) → it limits error-free transmission rate (effect) → coding is needed to cope (effect) → codes enable clear planet images, working bar codes and mobile video (effects). Q27 and Q39 both sit on this chain.

Exemplification: 'such as the Universal Product Code'; 'As mobile phone text messages like I CN C U show'. Examples always follow the general claim they illustrate — so scan backwards from an example to find the principle.

Author's attitude — admiring: 'a triumph for the NASA engineers', 'the astonishing power of the techniques', 'incredibly, the little probe managed to hear'. The writer celebrates both the engineering and Shannon's modesty.
