Cambridge IELTS 7 — Test 3 — Reading Passage 1

"Ant Intelligence" — Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

"Ant Intelligence" asks whether ants — not just apes and monkeys — show real signs of intelligence. The passage looks at how ants communicate with chemical signals, farm fungi as a food crop, navigate using mental "maps" of bearings and distance, and even pass complex messages to each other about the location of food.

The writer compares ant achievements (underground "megacities," sustainable farming, group problem-solving) to human history, arguing that although ants lack cultural transmission (learning passed between generations), their instinctive abilities are still remarkably sophisticated.

💡 Reading strategy tip: This is a discursive/argument-style passage (it poses a question and explores evidence for and against). Notice the recurring structure: a claim, then supporting research (a named scientist or university), then a qualifying comment. Track which paragraph belongs to which sub-topic (communication → farming → cities → navigation → messaging) before you start the questions.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"But in fact the social lives of some members of the insect kingdom are sufficiently complex to suggest more than a hint of intelligence."
Main Clause"the social lives... are sufficiently complex"
Subordinate Element"to suggest more than a hint of intelligence" (infinitive clause of result)
Grammar Note"sufficiently... to..." shows a result structure: complex ENOUGH TO cause a certain conclusion.
Simple MeaningSome insects' social behaviour is complex enough to suggest they may be intelligent.
中文实际上，一些昆虫的社会生活足够复杂，足以表明它们具有一定的智能。
"Whereas prehistoric man had no exposure to urban lifestyles — the forcing house of intelligence — the evidence suggests that ants have lived in urban settings for close on a hundred million years."
Main Clause"the evidence suggests that..."
Subordinate/Relative Clause"Whereas..." (contrast clause) + "that ants have lived..." (noun clause, object of "suggests")
Grammar Note"Whereas" introduces a contrast between two subjects (prehistoric man vs. ants); the dash phrase is an appositive adding extra description.
Simple MeaningEarly humans never lived in cities, but ants have — for almost 100 million years.
中文史前人类从未接触过城市生活——即所谓"智力的催化场所"——而证据表明蚂蚁在城市环境中已经生活了近一亿年。
"Research... has shown that when desert ants return from a foraging trip, they navigate by integrating bearings and distances, which they continuously update in their heads."
Main Clause"Research has shown that..."
Subordinate/Relative Clause"that... they navigate..." (noun clause); "when... return" (time clause); "which they... update" (relative clause modifying "bearings and distances")
Grammar NoteThree clause layers nested together — a very common IELTS academic sentence pattern.
Simple MeaningStudies show desert ants find their way home by constantly recalculating direction and distance in their minds.
中文研究表明，沙漠蚂蚁在觅食归来时，通过整合方向和距离进行导航，并在脑中不断更新这些信息。
"It was once thought that the fungus that ants cultivate was a single type that they had propagated, essentially unchanged from the distant past."
Main Clause"It was once thought that..."
Subordinate/Relative Clause"that the fungus... was a single type" (noun clause); "that ants cultivate" and "that they had propagated" (relative clauses modifying "fungus"/"type")
Grammar Note"It was once thought that..." signals an OLD belief the text is about to correct — watch for a following contrast ("Not so").
Simple MeaningPeople used to believe ants farmed only one unchanging fungus species — this turned out to be wrong.
中文人们曾经认为蚂蚁栽培的真菌是单一品种，是它们自古以来一直繁殖、基本未变的品种。
"Scouts who had located food in a maze returned to mobilise their foraging teams."
Main Clause"Scouts... returned to mobilise their foraging teams"
Subordinate/Relative Clause"who had located food in a maze" (relative clause modifying "Scouts")
Grammar NotePast perfect ("had located") shows the scouting happened BEFORE the returning — sequence of past events.
Simple MeaningAnts that had already found food went back to gather their team to collect it.
中文已经在迷宫中找到食物的侦察蚁返回，召集它们的觅食队伍。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect

Because ants cannot digest cellulose directly, they cultivate fungi that CAN break it down — the fungi convert leaves into a digestible food source. Cause: inability to digest cellulose → Effect: developing fungus farming.

Contrast

The passage repeatedly contrasts ants and humans: ants rely entirely on genetics (no cultural transmission) while humans pass down learned skills; prehistoric humans had no urban life, while ants have lived in "cities" for ~100 million years.

Comparison

Ant farming methods are compared favourably to human agribusiness: both grow crops and manage pests, but the text argues ant farming may be more sustainable because it "does not ruin environments or use enormous amounts of energy."

Examples

The writer uses specific named examples as evidence: Lewis Thomas's quotation, Ulrich Mueller's screening of 862 fungi types, the Formica yessensis supercolony on the Ishikari Coast, and the 12-year Ryabko/Reznikova maze study.

Definitions

Technical or unfamiliar terms are defined via footnotes (e.g. "aphids: small insects of a different species from ants") — always check footnotes, as IELTS questions sometimes rely on this information.

Chronology

The passage moves through time markers: "ants were farmers fifty million years before humans," fungi diversity going back to "the distant past," ant species existing "more than seventy million years ago," cave paintings "some 20,000 years" old.

Author's Attitude

The tone is admiring and slightly playful — quoting Lewis Thomas's ironic line "They do everything but watch television" and ending with Wilson's warm advice "Watch where you step. Be careful of little lives." The author clearly respects ant "intelligence" without overstating the claim (note qualifiers like "albeit of a different kind").
