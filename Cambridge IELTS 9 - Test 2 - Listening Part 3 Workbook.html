Listening Part 3

Cambridge IELTS 9 — Test 2 — Listening Part 3

Practice Workbook

📋 Quick Review

Pam (the Director of Studies) and Jun (a student representative) discuss the future of the university's Self-Access Centre — whether to keep it, whether to move it into the library, and what needs improving. Question types: multiple choice (Q21–24) and note completion (Q25–30, NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS).

💡 Listening tip: Two speakers with different roles means the exam will test who thinks what. Q21 is the students' view, Q22 the teachers' view, Q23 the students' worry, Q24 the Director's worry. Mark the speaker beside each question before you listen. Also listen for the classic 'It's not so much X… but it's Y' structure in Q24 — the real answer follows 'but'.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

Options are formal; the speakers are conversational. Practise the swap both ways.
Q21 — Why students want to keep it
Option C
it is an important part of their studies
What you hear
'they see it as a pretty major component of their course'
Key paraphrase
'a major component of their course' = an important part of their studies
Your turn
Q24 — The Director's concern
Option C
the difficulty in supervising the centre
What you hear
'It's not so much the expense that I'm worried about, and we've certainly got room to do it, but it's the problem of timetabling a teacher to be in there outside class hours'
Key paraphrase
'not so much X… but Y' eliminates A and B; timetabling a teacher = supervising → C
Your turn
Q25 — Reading materials
Note line
The level of the ______ materials should be more clearly shown
What you hear
'they find it difficult to find materials appropriate for their level, especially reading resources, so I think we need to label them more clearly'
Key paraphrase
'label more clearly' = shown more clearly; the word needed is reading
Your turn
Q30 — Email restriction
Note line
Restrict personal use of ______ on computers
What you hear
'see whether we could somehow limit the access to email… I don't want to see that resource misused'
Key paraphrase
'limit the access to' = restrict → email(s)
Your turn
