Reading Passage 2

Cambridge IELTS 10 — Test 2 — Reading Passage 2: Gifted Children and Learning

Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

An academic review of research on gifted children. It argues that IQ tests measure current achievement rather than learning processes, that excellence needs the right help, and that the crucial skill is metacognition — children understanding and regulating their own learning. It ends with the role of knowledge, motivation and emotion.

这是一篇教育心理学综述,六段 A–F,大量“作者(年份)”式引用。人名配对题是本篇的核心考点。

💡 Reading tip: This passage names researchers in brackets — Freeman (2010), Shore and Kanevsky (1993), Elshout (1995), Simonton (1988), Weisberg (1993), Boekaerts (1991). For matching-features questions (Q18–22), scan for the names first; they stand out visually, and the claim is always in the same sentence.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Internationally, 'giftedness' is most frequently determined by a score on a general intelligence test, known as an IQ test, which is above a chosen cut-off point, usually at around the top 2-5%.
Main Clause'giftedness' is most frequently determined by a score on a general intelligence test
Subordinate / Relative'known as an IQ test' = reduced relative in apposition; 'which is above a chosen cut-off point' = relative clause modifying 'a score'; 'usually at around the top 2-5%' = adverbial phrase
Grammar NoteNote that 'which is above…' refers back to a score, not to the test. Tracking what a relative pronoun points to is a Band 7+ skill.
Simple MeaningAround the world, a child is called gifted if their IQ score is in roughly the top 2–5%.
中文在国际上,“天赋”最常见的判定方式,是通用智力测验(即IQ测验)得分高于某个选定的分界线,通常在前2%–5%左右。
Because IQ tests are decidedly influenced by what the child has learned, they are to some extent measures of current achievement based on age-norms; that is, how well the children have learned to manipulate their knowledge and know-how within the terms of the test.
Main Clausethey are to some extent measures of current achievement based on age-norms
Subordinate / Relative'Because IQ tests are decidedly influenced by what the child has learned' = reason clause containing a noun clause ('what the child has learned'); after the semicolon, 'that is, how well the children have learned…' = restatement
Grammar Note'That is' introduces a rephrasing — free paraphrase practice built into the text. The hedge 'to some extent' is important: the writer does not say IQ tests are only achievement tests.
Simple MeaningBecause IQ tests depend on what a child has already learned, they partly measure present achievement for that age.
中文由于IQ测验明显受到儿童已学内容的影响,它们在某种程度上是基于年龄常模的当前成就测量;也就是说,衡量儿童在测验范围内运用其知识和技能的熟练程度。
There appears to be a qualitative difference in the way the intellectually highly able think, compared with more average-ability or older pupils, for whom external regulation by the teacher often compensates for lack of internal regulation.
Main ClauseThere appears to be a qualitative difference in the way the intellectually highly able think
Subordinate / Relative'compared with more average-ability or older pupils' = comparison phrase; 'for whom external regulation by the teacher often compensates for lack of internal regulation' = relative clause with preposition + whom
Grammar Note'For whom' = 'for these pupils'. Formal written English puts the preposition before the relative pronoun. This clause is the answer to Q24.
Simple MeaningVery able children seem to think differently from average or older pupils, whose teachers' control makes up for their own lack of self-control in learning.
中文与能力中等或年龄更大的学生相比,智力超常者的思维方式似乎存在质的差异;对后者而言,教师的外部调控往往弥补了其内部调控的不足。
High achievers have been found to use self-regulatory learning strategies more often and more effectively than lower achievers, and are better able to transfer these strategies to deal with unfamiliar tasks.
Main ClauseHigh achievers have been found to use self-regulatory learning strategies … and are better able to transfer these strategies
Subordinate / Relative'more often and more effectively than lower achievers' = double comparative; 'to deal with unfamiliar tasks' = purpose infinitive
Grammar NotePassive reporting structure 'have been found to' = a hedged research claim. Two parallel predicates share one subject.
Simple MeaningTop learners use self-managed learning methods more, and better, and can apply them to new problems.
中文研究发现,高成就者比低成就者更频繁、更有效地使用自我调节的学习策略,并且更善于把这些策略迁移到陌生任务上。
Given that a fundamental goal of education is to transfer the control of learning from teachers to pupils, improving pupils' learning to learn techniques should be a major outcome of the school experience, especially for the highly competent.
Main Clauseimproving pupils' learning to learn techniques should be a major outcome of the school experience
Subordinate / Relative'Given that a fundamental goal of education is to transfer the control of learning from teachers to pupils' = reason clause; 'especially for the highly competent' = restrictive adverbial
Grammar Note'Given that' = 'because / since'. The subject of the main clause is a gerund phrase ('improving … techniques').
Simple MeaningSince education aims to hand control of learning to the pupils, schools should above all teach them how to learn.
中文鉴于教育的一个根本目标是把学习的控制权从教师转移给学生,提升学生“学会学习”的技能理应成为学校教育的主要成果,对高能力者尤其如此。
Fear, for example, can limit the development of curiosity, which is a strong force in scientific advance, because it motivates problem-solving behaviour.
Main ClauseFear can limit the development of curiosity
Subordinate / Relative'which is a strong force in scientific advance' = non-defining relative clause describing curiosity; 'because it motivates problem-solving behaviour' = reason clause explaining why curiosity matters
Grammar NoteCareful with 'it' — it refers to curiosity, not to fear. Pronoun tracking is a common trap.
Simple MeaningFear can stop curiosity growing, and curiosity drives science because it makes people want to solve problems.
中文例如,恐惧会限制好奇心的发展,而好奇心是科学进步的强大动力,因为它能激发解决问题的行为。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

1. Definition (定义). The passage defines its central terms as it goes: giftedness = a score above a cut-off point (top 2–5%); metacognition = 'identify their own ways of learning', including planning, monitoring, evaluation, choice and emotional awareness; creativity = 'expertise mixed with a high level of motivation'. Whenever you meet a dash or the word 'that is', a definition follows — and definitions are heavily tested in sentence-completion questions (Q23–26).

2. Cause & effect (因果). Two chains dominate. Positive: home educational provision → higher IQ scores; self-regulatory strategies → high achievement and transfer to new tasks; teachers helping pupils reflect → increased self-regulation. Negative: overdirecting → diminished autonomy; too much dependence → loss of motivation to discover; fear → limited curiosity → less problem-solving.

3. Contrast (对比). The passage is built on paired opposites, and knowing them answers most questions: internal vs external regulation; quantitative ('merely think more quickly') vs qualitative difference; examination results vs life successes; positive vs negative emotions (facilitate vs inhibit); knowing a great deal vs intellectual skills.

4. Concession (让步). The writer repeatedly grants a point before qualifying it: 'Although spoon-feeding can produce extremely high examination results, these are not always followed by…'; 'But of course, this is not entirely the case'; 'Yet in order to learn by themselves, the gifted do need some support'; 'Conversely, teachers who … overdirect can diminish…'. The word after the concession is where the writer's real view lives.

5. Citation as evidence (以引用为证据). Almost every claim carries a name and a year. This is not decoration — it is the exam's target. Freeman (2010) = home provision; Shore and Kanevsky (1993) = the instructor's problem, quoted directly; Elshout (1995) = domain knowledge; Simonton (1988) = independence beyond a certain level; Weisberg (1993) = creativity as expertise plus motivation; Boekaerts (1991) = emotional forces in harness. Build this list before you attempt Q18–22.

6. Author's stance (作者立场). The writer is sceptical of IQ testing ('can neither identify the processes of learning and thinking nor predict creativity') and firmly in favour of learner autonomy. Note the strong evaluative words: 'Excellence does not emerge without appropriate help'; 'a fundamental goal of education'; 'should be a major outcome'. Modal verbs like should mark recommendation rather than fact.
