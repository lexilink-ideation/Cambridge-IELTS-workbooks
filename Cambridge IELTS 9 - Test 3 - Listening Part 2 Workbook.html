📋 Quick Review

A radio interview with Simon Winridge, co-founder of Winridge Forest Railway Park. He explains how the family turned 80 acres of sheep-farming land into a popular visitor attraction, who now does what in the family business, and what new features are being developed. Question types: multiple choice (Q11–13), matching people to jobs (Q14–18) and table completion (Q19–20).

💡 Listening tip: Q14–18 gives you five people and eight job options — three are distractors. Write the five names down the margin BEFORE you listen, then catch the paraphrase for each: 'keeping the trains going' = engine maintenance, 'recruiting and supporting workers' = staffing, 'kept fed and watered' = food and drink. The jobs are never named directly.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

Job titles never appear in the recording — only descriptions. This is the core skill for Q14–18.
Q14 — Simon's job
Option E
engine maintenance
What you hear
'I only concern myself with looking after the mechanical side of things - keeping the trains going'
Key paraphrase
'looking after the mechanical side / keeping the trains going' = engine maintenance
Your turn
Q16 — Sarah's job
Option F
food and drink
What you hear
'Sarah has now returned to the park and makes sure the visitors are kept fed and watered'
Key paraphrase
'fed and watered' is an idiom = given food and drink → F (her teaching past is a trap for option D)
Your turn
Q12 — Why July
Option A
the weather was expected to be good
What you hear
'in July - our driest month - because our children said they didn't want our guests to have a miserable, wet visit'
Key paraphrase
'our driest month' = good weather expected → A
Your turn
Q13 — Visitor numbers
Question
Since opening, the park has had…
What you hear
'50,000 visitors a year - a million and a half people have been through our doors since we opened'
Key paraphrase
'since we opened' matches the question stem → 1,500,000
Your turn
