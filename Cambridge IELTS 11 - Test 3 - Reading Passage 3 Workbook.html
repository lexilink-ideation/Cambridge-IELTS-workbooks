📋 Passage Overview

This is the author's own preface to a mathematics book, 'How the other half thinks: Adventures in mathematical reasoning'. In seven short sections (A–G) he explains who the book is for, why it is different from other maths books, how it aims to bridge the gap between the humanities and the sciences, and how mathematical reasoning sharpens analytical skill.

题型:段落信息配对 Q27–34(可重复使用字母)+ 句子填空 Q35–40(ONE WORD ONLY)。

💡 Reading tip: 'Which section contains…' questions are NOT in passage order and letters may repeat (NB You may use any letter more than once). Read all eight statements first, then read each section once and tick off every statement it matches.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

There are some discoveries in advanced mathematics that do not depend on specialized knowledge, not even on algebra, geometry, or trigonometry.
Main ClauseThere are some discoveries in advanced mathematics
Subordinate / Relative'that do not depend on specialized knowledge' = defining relative clause; 'not even on algebra, geometry, or trigonometry' = emphatic addition
Grammar Note'not even' escalates the claim — the writer is saying the barrier to entry is very low.
Simple MeaningSome advanced maths results need no special knowledge at all — not even algebra or geometry.
中文高等数学中有一些发现并不依赖专业知识,甚至不依赖代数、几何或三角学。
I hope this book will help bridge that notorious gap that separates the two cultures: the humanities and the sciences, or should I say the right brain (intuitive) and the left brain (analytical, numerical).
Main ClauseI hope this book will help bridge that notorious gap
Subordinate / Relative'that separates the two cultures' = defining relative; ': the humanities and the sciences' = colon naming them; 'or should I say…' = self-correction
Grammar NoteA colon introduces the definition; 'or should I say' is a conversational aside typical of a preface.
Simple MeaningI hope the book connects the humanities and the sciences — the intuitive and the analytical mind.
中文我希望这本书能弥合那道众所周知的鸿沟——它把人文与科学两种文化分隔开来,或者说,把右脑(直觉的)与左脑(分析的、数字的)分隔开来。
Other scientists have written books to explain their fields to non-scientists, but have necessarily had to omit the mathematics, although it provides the foundation of their theories.
Main ClauseOther scientists have written books … but have necessarily had to omit the mathematics
Subordinate / Relative'to explain their fields to non-scientists' = purpose infinitive; 'although it provides the foundation of their theories' = concession clause
Grammar NoteTwo verbs share one subject across 'but'; the concession clause closes the sentence. Q38 comes straight from this.
Simple MeaningOther scientists wrote for general readers but had to leave out the maths, even though it underpins their theories.
中文其他科学家写书向非科学工作者解释自己的领域,但不得不略去数学,尽管数学正是他们理论的基础。
You will turn these pages much more slowly than when reading a novel or a newspaper.
Main ClauseYou will turn these pages much more slowly
Subordinate / Relative'than when reading a novel or a newspaper' = reduced comparative clause ('than when you read')
Grammar NoteComparative + reduced time clause. Q32 ('a contrast between reading this book and reading other kinds of publication') points here.
Simple MeaningYou will read this book far more slowly than a novel or a newspaper.
中文你翻阅这些书页的速度,会比读小说或报纸时慢得多。
Lawyers who have studied mathematics can master the legal principles in a way that most others cannot.
Main ClauseLawyers … can master the legal principles
Subordinate / Relative'who have studied mathematics' = defining relative clause; 'in a way that most others cannot' = manner clause with ellipsis ('cannot master them')
Grammar NoteThe final clause omits the repeated verb phrase — a common feature of natural English.
Simple MeaningLawyers with a maths background understand legal principles better than most.
中文学过数学的律师能以大多数人做不到的方式掌握法律原则。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Analogy as an opening move. The writer begins with music ('easy parts a beginner could play') and immediately transfers it to mathematics ('So it is with mathematics as well'). Q35 comes directly from this analogy.

Contrast with other books. Section B defines this book by what it is not: not biographies of mathematicians, not applications, not procedures requiring algebra. Q28 and Q31 both live here.

Bridging two cultures. Section C sets humanities against sciences, right brain against left brain, then argues the gap is 'alleged' and can be overcome — and proves it with four people who combine 'incompatible' abilities (Q30).

Reader guidance. Section E tells you how to read: slowly, with a pencil and paper. Section F names the two intended audiences. These 'instructions to the reader' are the answers to Q32 and Q34.

Evidence by testimonial. Section G supplies two first-person quotations (a physician and a lawyer) as proof that mathematics develops analytical skill — that is personal evidence of being helped by mathematics (Q29).
