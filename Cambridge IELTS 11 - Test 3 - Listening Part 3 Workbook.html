Listening Part 3

Cambridge IELTS 11 — Test 3 — Listening Part 3

Practice Workbook

📋 Quick Review

Helen and Jeremy discuss their creative-writing project. Jeremy has written a story called 'The Forest' and Helen has illustrated it. First they agree on changes to three drawings (Q21–26), then they divide up the written report between them (Q27–30).

前半是表格填空,后半是“谁来写”配对——注意 D 选项“两人都不写”。

💡 Listening tip: In Q27–30 the four options are A (Helen only), B (Jeremy only), C (both), D (neither). Option D is easy to miss — it appears when the pair realise a section is not part of the report at all. Listen for 'So it isn't going to be part of the report at all?'

✏️ Paraphrase Training

The table words are said almost directly — but the sentence around them is long. Practise picking out the noun.
Q22 — the tiger
Table note
Add Malcolm and a ___ noticing him
What you hear
'What about putting in a tiger … it could be sitting under a tree washing itself' — 'the tiger stops … when it sees Malcolm walking past'
Key paraphrase
'when it sees Malcolm' = 'noticing him' → tiger
Your turn
Q24 — crying
Table note
Add Malcolm sitting on a tree trunk and ___
What you hear
'he's been left out of all the fun, so I'd like him to be crying'
Key paraphrase
the reason comes first, the answer second — keep listening past 'so'
Your turn
Q25 — grass
Table note
Ice-skaters on ___ covered with ice
What you hear
'they're supposed to be skating on grass, aren't they?' — 'That's right, and it's frozen over'
Key paraphrase
'frozen over' = 'covered with ice' → grass
Your turn
Q27 — planning section
Question
how they planned the project
What you hear
'you've had most of the good ideas so far. How do you feel about drafting something, then we can go through it together?'
Key paraphrase
Helen drafts it alone; discussing it afterwards does not make it 'both' → A
Your turn
Q29 — interpretation
Question
an interpretation of their stories
What you hear
'everyone in the class is going to read each other's stories and come up with their own interpretations, which we're going to discuss'
Key paraphrase
it happens in class, not in the report → D (neither)
Your turn
