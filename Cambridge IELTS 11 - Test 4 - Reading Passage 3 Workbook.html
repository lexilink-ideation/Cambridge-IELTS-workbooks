📋 Passage Overview

'This Marvellous Invention' argues that language is humanity's greatest creation — except that it was never actually invented. Six paragraphs (A–F) explore this paradox: how thirty-odd sounds produce infinite meaning, why everybody can do it effortlessly, and how Turkish and Sumerian reveal the design that everyday use hides.

题型:段落小标题 Q27–32 + 词库摘要 Q33–36 + YES/NO/NOT GIVEN Q37–40。

💡 Reading tip: For matching headings, read the first sentence and the last sentence of each paragraph. Paragraph B ends on 'This apparent paradox', which points straight to heading iv ('Apparently incompatible characteristics of language').

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Compared to language, all other inventions pale in significance, since everything we have ever achieved depends on language and originates from it.
Main Clauseall other inventions pale in significance
Subordinate / Relative'Compared to language' = reduced passive clause; 'since everything we have ever achieved depends on language and originates from it' = reason clause
Grammar Note'Compared to X, Y…' + 'since' reason clause. A compact way to make a strong claim.
Simple MeaningEvery other invention matters less, because all our achievements come from language.
中文与语言相比,所有其他发明都黯然失色,因为我们所取得的一切都依赖语言并源自语言。
Language is mankind's greatest invention — except, of course, that it was never invented.
Main ClauseLanguage is mankind's greatest invention
Subordinate / Relative'except, of course, that it was never invented' = exception clause
Grammar NoteThe dash plus 'except that' delivers the paradox in a single stroke. This is the sentence heading iv describes.
Simple MeaningLanguage is our greatest invention — but nobody invented it.
中文语言是人类最伟大的发明——当然,只不过它从未被发明过。
But run them through the cogs and wheels of the language machine, let it arrange them in some very special orders, and there is nothing that these meaningless streams of air cannot do.
Main Clausethere is nothing that these meaningless streams of air cannot do
Subordinate / Relativetwo imperative clauses ('run them…', 'let it arrange them…') act as conditions joined by 'and'
Grammar NoteImperative + and + result is an informal conditional: 'If you run them through…, then there is nothing…'. Note the double negative for emphasis.
Simple MeaningPut those sounds through the language machine in the right order and they can express anything.
中文但只要让它们经过语言机器的齿轮与转轮,让它按某些非常特殊的顺序排列它们,这些原本无意义的气流就无所不能。
Yet it is precisely this deceptive ease which makes language a victim of its own success, since in everyday life its triumphs are usually taken for granted.
Main Clauseit is precisely this deceptive ease which makes language a victim of its own success
Subordinate / Relativecleft structure 'it is X which…'; 'since in everyday life its triumphs are usually taken for granted' = reason clause
Grammar NoteA cleft sentence for emphasis, plus a 'since' clause. Q36 comes from 'ease' here.
Simple MeaningBecause language feels so easy, we stop noticing how remarkable it is.
中文然而,正是这种具有欺骗性的轻松,使语言成为自身成功的受害者,因为在日常生活中,它的种种成就通常被视为理所当然。
If you were to ask which bit in the Sumerian word corresponds to the pronoun 'it' in the English translation, then the answer would have to be nothing.
Main Clausethen the answer would have to be nothing
Subordinate / Relative'If you were to ask which bit … corresponds to the pronoun it' = second conditional containing an embedded question
Grammar Note'If you were to…' is a formal second conditional. The embedded question keeps statement word order ('which bit corresponds', not 'which bit does correspond').
Simple MeaningIf you asked which part of the Sumerian word means 'it', the answer would be: no part at all.
中文如果你要问苏美尔语单词中哪一部分对应英语译文里的代词“它”,那么答案只能是:没有任何部分。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Claim → paradox → evidence. A makes the claim (language is the greatest invention); B introduces the paradox (it was never invented); C–D show the machine at work and how effortlessly everyone uses it; E–F give foreign-language evidence of its hidden design.

Quotation as authority. The 1660 Port-Royal grammarians are quoted at length to establish that people have long admired language. The writer then turns their praise against itself by pointing out the 'flaw' in it.

Scale contrast. Twenty-five to thirty sounds → infinite expressions; 'three dozen measly morsels of sound' → 'unravelling the fundamental order of the universe'. This small-to-vast contrast is the engine of paragraph C (heading ii).

Universality. Paragraph D stresses that everybody can do it — foragers and philosophers alike, 'without the slightest exertion' — which is exactly heading vii.

Two exotic examples. Turkish shows extreme length; Sumerian shows extreme compactness, including a meaningful absence of sound. The pairing lets E take heading i and F take heading v.
