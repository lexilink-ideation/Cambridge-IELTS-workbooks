📋 Passage Overview

Alternative Medicine in Australia traces how alternative (complementary) therapies — acupuncture, herbalism, naturopathy — have grown more accepted. Australia was traditionally conservative, but rising public disenchantment with orthodox medicine has increased demand, and many doctors now train in, or work alongside, alternative therapists. A survey ranks the complaints patients bring.

💡 Reading tip: Yes / No / Not Given asks about the writer's views/claims, not the outside world. Match numbers and dates carefully (1983, 1990, 1993) — several statements test whether you read the figures correctly.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Australia has been unusual in the Western world in having a very conservative attitude to natural or alternative therapies.
Main ClauseAustralia has been unusual in the Western world
Subordinate / Relativein having a very conservative attitude to natural or alternative therapies (prepositional gerund phrase)
Grammar Note'unusual … in doing' explains how it is unusual; present perfect for a continuing state.
Simple MeaningCompared with other Western countries, Australia has been unusually cautious about alternative therapies.
中文在西方世界中，澳大利亚对自然疗法或替代疗法持非常保守的态度，这一点很不寻常。
A better educated and less accepting public has become disillusioned with the experts in general, and increasingly sceptical about science.
Main ClauseA … public has become disillusioned with the experts … and increasingly sceptical about science
Subordinate / Relative(compound predicate: 'disillusioned … and … sceptical')
Grammar Notetwo adjective complements share one subject; 'less accepting' modifies 'public'.
Simple MeaningA better-educated, more questioning public trusts experts less and doubts science more.
中文受过更好教育、不那么轻信的公众，对专家普遍感到失望，并对科学越来越持怀疑态度。
An increasing exodus from their clinics, coupled with a number of other relevant surveys, have led mainstream doctors themselves to begin to admit they could learn from alternative therapists.
Main ClauseAn increasing exodus …, coupled with … surveys, have led mainstream doctors … to admit …
Subordinate / Relativecoupled with a number of other relevant surveys (participle phrase); they could learn from alternative therapists (noun clause)
Grammar Note'coupled with' adds a second cause; 'led … to admit' = caused them to admit.
Simple MeaningAs patients left and surveys showed shortcomings, mainstream doctors began to admit they could learn from alternative therapists.
中文越来越多的病人离开他们的诊所，再加上其他相关调查，促使主流医生自己开始承认，他们可以向替代疗法师学习。
Alternative medicine appears to be an adjunct, sought in times of disenchantment when conventional medicine seems not to offer the answer.
Main ClauseAlternative medicine appears to be an adjunct
Subordinate / Relativesought in times of disenchantment (reduced relative); when conventional medicine seems not to offer the answer (relative clause of time)
Grammar Note'sought…' = which is sought; 'when' modifies 'times of disenchantment'.
Simple MeaningAlternative medicine seems to be an add-on, used when people are disappointed and mainstream medicine hasn't helped.
中文替代医学似乎是一种补充，在人们感到失望、常规医学似乎无法提供答案时被求助。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Comparison: Australia vs Europe/Germany/USA — other Western countries integrated therapies earlier ('hand in glove').

Trend with data: rising figures (1.9% in 1983 → 2.6% in 1990) chart the growth of alternative-therapy use.

Cause & effect: public disenchantment + surveys of doctors' 'inadequacies' → doctors adopt or partner with alternative therapists.

Definition/qualification: the writer argues 'complementary' is a better term than 'alternative' — an add-on, not a replacement.
