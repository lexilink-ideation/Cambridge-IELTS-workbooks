📋 Quick Review

In a seminar, biologist Grant Freeman from the Australian Quarantine Service explains his research on the Asian Honey Bee and the risk it poses to Australia's native bees. He describes the mites the Asian bees carry, the value of native bees to agriculture, and a clever detection method using the diet of a bird — the Rainbow Bee Eater. There are multiple-choice questions and a summary to complete.

💡 Listening strategy tip: Part 3 discussions often move fast between speakers. For the summary completion (ONE WORD ONLY), predict the type of word first, then listen for the paraphrase. Watch for the speaker correcting or ranking ideas ('not as such … but …', 'much more reliable than …').

🔄 Paraphrase Training

In IELTS the question words are almost never the words you hear or read. Recognising paraphrase is the key skill. Study each example, then write your own sentence.
Paraphrase Example 1
Question / promptQ21: Where have Asian honey bees been found in the past?
What you hear / read"We discovered a few of them in Queensland once and eradicated them."
Key paraphrase'in the past' points to Queensland (A), not the states they now watch.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question / promptQ22: A problem with Asian honey bees is that they …
What you hear / read"they are infested with mites … which can seriously damage our own home-grown bees."
Key paraphrase'carry parasites' = infested with mites → answer B.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question / promptQ24: If Asian bees got into Australia …
What you hear / read"We could lose a lot of money because … native bees' pollination … is worth 1.2 billion dollars a year."
Key paraphrase'the country's economy would be affected' = lose a lot of money → answer A.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question / promptQ30: The benefit of this research is that the result is more …
What you hear / read"a good result and much more reliable than trying to find live ones."
Key paraphraseComparative signals the answer: reliable/accurate.
