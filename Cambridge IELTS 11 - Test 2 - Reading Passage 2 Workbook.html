📋 Passage Overview

'What destroyed the civilisation of Easter Island?' sets out two opposing explanations. Jared Diamond argues the Rapanui wrecked their own environment and that the moai statues accelerated the collapse. Terry Hunt and Carl Lipo argue the islanders were skilled, sustainable farmers, that the moai were walked upright, and that rats and European diseases did the damage.

题型:段落小标题 Q14–20 + 摘要填空 Q21–24 + 选两个 Q25–26。

💡 Reading tip: For matching headings, read the first and last sentence of each paragraph and ask 'what is this paragraph for?' Paragraph A presents rival theories (heading ii, an undisputed answer about who built them); paragraph C is about how the moai made things worse (heading viii).

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

All the energy and resources that went into the moai — some of which are ten metres tall and weigh over 7,000 kilos — came from the island itself.
Main ClauseAll the energy and resources … came from the island itself
Subordinate / Relative'that went into the moai' = defining relative clause; 'some of which are ten metres tall and weigh over 7,000 kilos' = non-defining relative with a quantifier
Grammar Note'some of which' is the formal way to say 'some of them'. Note the dashes isolate the extra detail.
Simple MeaningEverything used to make the statues — some 10 m tall and over 7,000 kg — came from the island.
中文制作摩埃像所耗费的全部能量与资源——其中一些高达十米、重逾7000公斤——都来自岛上本身。
When the islanders cleared the forests for firewood and farming, the forests didn't grow back.
Main Clausethe forests didn't grow back
Subordinate / RelativeWhen the islanders cleared the forests for firewood and farming (time clause functioning as a cause)
Grammar NoteA simple 'when'-clause carrying a causal meaning. Q21 ('farming') comes straight from it.
Simple MeaningOnce the islanders cut the forests for fuel and fields, the trees never returned.
中文当岛民为取柴和耕作而清除森林后,森林再也没有长回来。
Diamond thinks they laid the moai on wooden sledges, hauled over log rails, but that required both a lot of wood and a lot of people.
Main ClauseDiamond thinks (that) they laid the moai on wooden sledges … but that required both a lot of wood and a lot of people
Subordinate / Relative'hauled over log rails' = reduced passive relative ('which were hauled')
Grammar Note'both A and B' balances the two costs. This sentence gives the answer to Q24.
Simple MeaningDiamond believes they dragged the statues on wooden sledges over log rails, which needed lots of wood and workers.
中文戴蒙德认为,他们把摩埃像放在木橇上,沿着圆木轨道拖行,但这既需要大量木材,也需要大量人力。
Archaeological finds of nuts from the extinct Easter Island palm show tiny grooves, made by the teeth of Polynesian rats.
Main ClauseArchaeological finds of nuts … show tiny grooves
Subordinate / Relative'from the extinct Easter Island palm' = prepositional modifier; 'made by the teeth of Polynesian rats' = reduced passive relative
Grammar NoteTwo modifiers stack after the subject; the main verb 'show' is easy to miss.
Simple MeaningNuts from the extinct palm have small marks left by rats' teeth.
中文考古发现的已灭绝复活节岛棕榈的坚果上有细小凹槽,这是波利尼西亚鼠牙齿留下的。
They would have prevented the reseeding of the slow-growing palm trees and thereby doomed Rapa Nui's forest, even without the settlers' campaign of deforestation.
Main ClauseThey would have prevented the reseeding … and thereby doomed Rapa Nui's forest
Subordinate / Relative'even without the settlers' campaign of deforestation' = concessive prepositional phrase
Grammar Note'would have + past participle' = a hypothetical past result; 'thereby + -ing' shows consequence.
Simple MeaningThe rats alone would have stopped the palms reseeding and killed the forest, even if people had cut nothing.
中文即使没有定居者的砍伐运动,老鼠也会阻止生长缓慢的棕榈重新播种,从而注定了复活节岛森林的消亡。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Two opposing views. The whole passage is built on a contrast: paragraphs B–C give Diamond; paragraphs D–F give Hunt and Lipo; paragraph G sums up their vision. Paragraph A sets up the question and gives one point that is now settled (who built the moai) — hence heading ii.

Evidence types. Pollen in lake sediments (B), archaeological excavations of windbreaks (D), experiments with a replica (E), tooth grooves on palm nuts (F). Each paragraph is defined by the kind of evidence it uses — that is the fastest route to the right heading.

Concession and refutation. 'Hunt and Lipo agree that Easter Island lost its lush forests … but they believe the islanders themselves weren't to blame.' Recognising 'agree … but' tells you paragraph D is a counter-argument, not a summary.

External vs. internal causes. Diamond blames the islanders (internal); Hunt and Lipo blame rats, European disease and slave traders (external). Heading vii ('Destruction outside the inhabitants' control') therefore belongs to paragraph F.

Author's balance. The writer never decides: 'Whichever is the case, there are surely some valuable lessons…'. That neutrality is why Q25–26 asks only where the two sides disagree.
