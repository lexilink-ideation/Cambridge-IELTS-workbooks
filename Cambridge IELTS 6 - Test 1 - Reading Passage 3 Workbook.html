Cambridge IELTS 6 — Test 1 — Reading Passage 3

Practice Workbook — "Climate Change and the Inuit"

📋 Passage Overview

This passage examines how climate change is affecting the Arctic and Canada's Inuit people. Unusual events — early thaws, melting permafrost, shrinking sea ice — are already disrupting traditional hunting life, and the Inuit see the problem as urgent because it threatens their entire way of life.

The passage explains the harsh history of Arctic settlement (the Thule people, ancestors of today's Inuit), the health and social costs of losing traditional skills, and the growing role of Inuit traditional knowledge (called "IQ") alongside Western science in understanding what's happening.

💡 Reading strategy tip: This passage has seven lettered paragraphs (A–G) and uses a heading-matching task plus a summary-completion task drawn from two specific paragraphs (C and D). When a task restricts you to certain paragraphs, don't waste time searching the whole passage — go straight to those paragraphs.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"Nobody knows the Arctic as well as the locals, which is why they are not content simply to stand back and let outside experts tell them what's happening."
Main ClauseNobody knows the Arctic as well as the locals
Relative Clausewhich is why they are not content simply to stand back and let outside experts tell them what's happening
Grammar Note"which" refers back to the whole previous clause, not just one noun — a non-restrictive relative clause of result
Simple MeaningBecause locals understand the Arctic best, they insist on being involved rather than letting outsiders explain it to them.
中文没有人比当地人更了解北极，这就是为什么他们不满足于袖手旁观、任由外来专家告诉他们正在发生什么。
"In Nunavut's 'igloo and email' society, where adults who were born in igloos have children who may never have been out on the land, there's a high incidence of depression."
Main Clausethere's a high incidence of depression
Relative Clausewhere adults who were born in igloos have children who may never have been out on the land
Grammar NoteNested relative clauses ("where..." containing two "who..." clauses) show rapid generational change
Simple MeaningIn just one generation, Inuit life changed so fast that grandparents lived traditionally while grandchildren may never experience the land — causing psychological harm.
中文在努纳武特"冰屋与电邮"并存的社会里，出生在冰屋里的成年人，他们的孩子可能从未在野外生活过，抑郁症的发生率很高。
"IQ could help to bridge the gap and resolve the tremendous uncertainty about how much of what we're seeing is natural capriciousness and how much is the consequence of human activity."
Main ClauseIQ could help to bridge the gap and resolve the tremendous uncertainty
Noun Clauseabout how much of what we're seeing is natural capriciousness and how much is the consequence of human activity
Grammar NoteA parallel pair of indirect questions ("how much... and how much...") functioning as the object of "about"
Simple MeaningTraditional Inuit knowledge could help scientists work out whether changes are natural or human-caused.
中文IQ（因纽特传统知识）可以帮助弥合这一差距，解决关于我们所看到的现象有多少是自然的反复无常、又有多少是人类活动的后果这一巨大不确定性。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect: "Sea ice breaking up earlier than usual → seals carried beyond the reach of hunters." Small climate changes have direct, immediate effects on Inuit hunting.

Contrast: The passage contrasts "abstract" climate change for most people (paragraph A) with the very real, urgent problem it poses for the Inuit (paragraph B) — a shift from global to local perspective.

Problem → Solution: Problem — scientists once ignored Inuit knowledge ("these people don't know very much"). Solution — Inuit Qaujimajatuqangit (IQ) is now a required part of research permission, giving communities a say in what gets studied.

Comparison: The passage compares older generations (born in igloos, skilled hunters) with younger generations (may never have been "out on the land"), showing the speed of cultural change in a single lifetime.

Author's Attitude: The tone is balanced and evidence-based — the writer notes both scientists who doubt traditional knowledge's value and those who see it as essential, without taking an explicit side.
