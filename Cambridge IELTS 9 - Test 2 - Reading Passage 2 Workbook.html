📋 Passage Overview

Venus in transit. Seven paragraphs (A–G) explain why the rare passage of Venus across the Sun mattered to science. Edmond Halley realised that timing a transit from two distant places would give the parallax angle, and so the Earth–Sun distance (the 'astronomical unit'). Expeditions followed, with famous misfortunes; the 'black drop' effect spoiled accuracy; Encke eventually produced a figure close to today's. Question types: matching information to paragraphs (Q14–17), matching people (Q18–21) and True/False/Not Given (Q22–26).

💡 Reading tip: Q18–21 is a matching-people task with only four names — build a quick name–fact table as you read: Halley = the method; Kepler = orbital speeds; Le Gentil = the unlucky traveller; Encke = the final calculation. Then the questions answer themselves. Watch Q22 closely: Halley observed a transit of Mercury, not Venus.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

By timing the transit from two widely-separated locations, teams of astronomers could calculate the parallax angle - the apparent difference in position of an astronomical body due to a difference in the observer's position.
Main ClauseTeams of astronomers could calculate the parallax angle.
Subordinate / Relative'By timing the transit from two widely-separated locations' (means) + the dash introduces an appositive definition.
Grammar Note'By + -ing' expresses method. The dash defines a technical term without a relative clause — a favourite academic device.
Simple MeaningIf astronomers timed the transit from two far-apart places, they could work out the parallax angle — how much an object seems to shift when you move.
中文通过在相距甚远的两地为凌日现象计时，天文学家团队便可计算出视差角——即由于观测者位置不同而导致天体位置的表观差异。
Johannes Kepler, in the early 17th century, had shown that the distances of the planets from the Sun governed their orbital speeds, which were easily measurable.
Main ClauseJohannes Kepler had shown that…
Subordinate / Relative'that the distances… governed their orbital speeds' (reported clause) + 'which were easily measurable' (non-defining relative).
Grammar NotePast perfect ('had shown') places Kepler before Halley in time. This sentence is the whole answer to Q20.
Simple MeaningIn the early 1600s Kepler proved that how far a planet is from the Sun decides how fast it orbits.
中文开普勒在 17 世纪初已经证明，行星与太阳的距离决定了它们的轨道速度，而轨道速度是易于测量的。
However, Halley realised that Mercury was so far away that its parallax angle would be very difficult to determine.
Main ClauseHalley realised that Mercury was so far away…
Subordinate / Relative'that its parallax angle would be very difficult to determine' = result clause inside the reported clause.
Grammar Note'so + adj + that + result' nested inside 'realised that…' — two levels of that-clause.
Simple MeaningHalley saw that Mercury was too distant for its parallax angle to be measured easily.
中文然而，哈雷意识到水星距离太远，其视差角极难测定。
He was thwarted by the fact that the British were besieging his observation site at Pondicherry in India.
Main ClauseHe was thwarted.
Subordinate / Relative'by the fact that the British were besieging his observation site' = agent phrase containing a that-clause.
Grammar NotePassive + 'by the fact that' names the obstacle. This is Q15's 'event which prevented a transit observation'.
Simple MeaningHe failed because British forces were attacking the place where he planned to observe.
中文他因英军围攻其位于印度本地治里的观测点而受阻。
While this showed astronomers that Venus was surrounded by a thick layer of gases refracting sunlight around it, both effects made it impossible to obtain accurate timings.
Main ClauseBoth effects made it impossible to obtain accurate timings.
Subordinate / Relative'While this showed astronomers that Venus was surrounded by a thick layer of gases' (concession) + 'refracting sunlight around it' (participial phrase describing the gases).
Grammar Note'While' = although. Note carefully: the passage says gases exist, but never says astronomers thought them toxic — that is the Q25 trap.
Simple MeaningThe halo revealed that Venus has a thick gas layer, but both effects still ruined accurate timing.
中文虽然这向天文学家表明金星被一层厚厚的气体所包围，这层气体会使阳光在其周围发生折射，但这两种效应仍使得精确计时无法实现。
If we look at a star in January - when Earth is at one point in its orbit - it will seem to be in a different position from where it appears six months later.
Main ClauseIt will seem to be in a different position.
Subordinate / Relative'If we look at a star in January' (condition) + '- when Earth is at one point in its orbit -' (parenthetical relative) + 'from where it appears six months later' (comparison clause).
Grammar NoteDashes insert extra information without breaking the main structure. This sentence is the second application of parallax — key to Q14.
Simple MeaningA star seen in January looks like it is in a different place from where it looks six months later, because Earth has moved.
中文如果我们在一月观测一颗恒星——此时地球处于其轨道上的某一点——它看起来会与六个月后所处的位置不同。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Chronology: Halley observes Mercury (1677) → predicts Venus transits (1761, 1769) → expeditions set out → Le Gentil's failures → black drop problem → Encke's calculation → radar today → 2004 transit. Dates are your fastest navigation tool in this passage.

Problem → method → obstacle → result: the AU cannot be measured directly (problem) → time a transit from two latitudes (method) → war, weather and the black drop interfere (obstacle) → Encke still gets 153,340,000 km (result). Each paragraph occupies one slot in this frame.

Cause & effect: 'As Venus was closer to the Earth, its parallax angle would be larger' — proximity (cause) produces a bigger, more measurable angle (effect). This is why Venus replaced Mercury in the plan.

Contrast: 'transits of Venus, unlike those of Mercury, are rare' — the advantage of Venus comes with a disadvantage. Watch 'unlike' as a comparison signal.

Concession: 'While the early transit timings were as precise as instruments would allow, the measurements were dogged by the black drop effect.' The writer grants the astronomers' skill before naming the flaw.

Definition by dash and apposition: parallax angle, astronomical unit and the black drop effect are all defined inline. If a question uses a technical term, the definition sentence is almost always where the answer sits.

Author's attitude — warm and slightly amused: 'the extraordinary polymath', 'The person who deserves most sympathy', 'Ironically after travelling nearly 50,000 kilometres'. The writer enjoys the human story as much as the science.
