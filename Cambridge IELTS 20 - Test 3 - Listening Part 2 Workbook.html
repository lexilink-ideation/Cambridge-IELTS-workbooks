Listening Part 2

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 3 — Listening Part 2

Bidcaster Archaeology Dig · Multiple Choice + Map Labelling (Q11–20)

📋 Quick Review

A single speaker, Hayden, is a professional archaeologist welcoming visitors to a community excavation next to Bidcaster Castle. In the first half he tells the story of the dig — who started it, how a gold coin led to the discovery of a lost village, what has been found and what has not. In the second half he points at a site map and gives directions to four features. Questions 11–16 are 3-option multiple choice; questions 17–20 are map labelling with letters A–G.

Who is speaking? One speaker only: Hayden, a full-time archaeologist employed by the town council, giving an introductory talk to visitors on site. It is a monologue, so there is no second voice to correct or confirm anything — the corrections come from Hayden himself (“we thought… but it turned out to be…”).

The situation 情境: 考古学家 Hayden 在 Bidcaster 城堡旁边的社区考古工地上，向参观者介绍这个项目：它是怎么开始的（一枚金币）、三个夏天挖出了什么、还没挖到什么，最后对着一张工地地图指出四个地点的位置（Q17–20）。

Task type: Q11–16 multiple choice, A / B / C — choose the correct letter. Q17–20 map labelling, choose from A–G; there are seven letters but only four answers, so three letters are never used. Letters may not be used more than once.

💡 Listening strategy tip: This section is really two different tests in one. For Q11–16, underline the few words in the stem that fix the question focus (starting the project, still hoping to find, after this summer). Hayden mentions all three options almost every time — two of them as background, one as the fact you actually need. For Q17–20, do not try to hold the map in your head. Put your pen on the “you are here” marker at the bottom and physically trace the route as he speaks. The four features are given in the order 17, 18, 19, 20, but he uses three different kinds of description: a walking route, a compass direction, and a “biggest thing next to X” size comparison. 地图题一定要用笔跟着走，不要在脑子里记。
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • Q11 — three organisations in one sentence. The castle owners (A) lend their facilities, the town council (C) employs Hayden, but it was NHA, a charity that “asked me to join the project” and “sets up projects like this up and down the country” → B.
  • Q12 — two textbook methods are mentioned first. Hayden says coins are often found with metal detectors (C) or where rabbits have dug tunnels (B). Those are general facts. The word “Here” switches him to this case: a rainstorm washed the earth away → A.
  • Q13 — the grass is a decoy. Option B mentions structures “visible in the grass”, and the audio does say grassy area — but only as the place shown on the old maps and documents found “just by chance” in the library → A (a lucky discovery of old records).
  • Q14 — “still hoping to find” means NOT found yet. Broken pots (A) and bone tools (B) have already been found; jewellery is what they are “still waiting to uncover” → C.
  • Q15 — a negative sentence. “No other houses or huts so far” kills C; the “ancient palace” turned out to be a modern wall, which kills A. Only the “borders of an ancient field system” survives → B.
  • Q16 — the museum and the TV programme are both real, but neither is a plan. The TV documentary has already been shown, and the museum idea “we don’t know that for sure yet”. The confirmed plan is guided tours for school groups this autumn → C.

The verb is excavate, the place is a site, and the whole topic family will come back in Reading too.

excavatev.to dig carefully in the ground to find historical objects挖掘；发掘“One area we excavated in the first summer uncovered the site of a meeting hall.”The noun excavation area is the landmark used to locate the meeting hall in Q19.
volunteern.someone who works without being paid志愿者“Most of the people digging here are volunteers.”Signals “community project”. Contrast with Hayden, who is full-time and paid by the council — useful background for Q11.
charityn.an organisation that helps people or causes and does not exist to make money慈善机构“I was asked to join the project by NHA, a charity which sets up projects like this.”Answer to Q11 (B). Listen for the appositive comma: “NHA, a charity,…” — that comma is where the definition lives.
counciln.the elected group that runs a town or local area（地方）议会；市政府“I’m a full-time archaeologist for the town council.”Distractor for Q11: the council employs the speaker but did not start the project.
facilitiesn. (pl.)buildings, equipment or services provided for people to use设施“The owners let us use their facilities.”Another Q11 distractor. “Let us use” = permission, not responsibility.
metal detectorn.a machine that beeps when it finds metal under the ground金属探测器“Coins are often found by people using metal detectors.”Mentioned as a general method (option C in Q12), not as what happened here.
bury / buriedv.to put or cover something under the ground埋；被埋在地下“…to look for things buried in the ground.”Pronounced /ˈberi/ — it sounds like berry, not like bury looks.
uncoverv.to find something that was hidden or covered发现；揭开“We’re still waiting to uncover any such items.”Paraphrase family for Q14: uncover / find / discover / come across. “Still waiting to uncover” = have NOT found.
rainstormn.a period of very heavy rain暴雨“A walker found it on the ground after a rainstorm washed away some of the earth.”Answer to Q12 (A). On the paper it is “heavy rain”; in the audio it is “a rainstorm”. Same idea, different word.
remainsn. (pl.)the parts of something old that are still left遗迹；残骸“We found the remains of several buildings.”Always plural in this meaning. It appears in option A of Q15 (“the remains of a large palace”) as a distractor.
ruinedadj.badly damaged and no longer usable成为废墟的“500-year-old drawings of ruined buildings on the grassy area.”This phrase belongs to the old documents (Q13, option A), not to something anyone saw in the grass.
pottery / potsn.objects made of baked clay, such as bowls and jars陶器“We found… more broken pots than you can count.”Q14 option A says “everyday pottery” — already found, so it cannot be what they are hoping for.
broochn.a piece of jewellery pinned to clothing胸针“Normally you’d expect to find brooches and other jewellery.”Pronounced /brəʊtʃ/ — like “broach”. It is the concrete example that proves the answer to Q14 is jewellery.
jewelleryn.rings, necklaces and similar decorative objects首饰；珠宝“…brooches and other jewellery, but we’re still waiting to uncover any such items.”Answer to Q14 (C). Spelling: British jewellery / American jewelry — both accepted, but be consistent.
field systemn.the pattern of ancient farm fields still visible in the land古代田地系统“We can see the borders of an ancient field system.”Answer to Q15 (B). “Borders” in the audio = “outline” on the question paper.
hutn.a small, simply built house小屋；棚屋“No other houses or huts so far.”Q15 option C. The word IS said — inside a negative. Hearing the word is not the same as hearing the answer.
foundationsn. (pl.)the solid base built under a structure地基“This year we’ve identified the foundations of an ancient bridge.”Q17. The bridge itself is gone — only the base is left, which is why the label is on the riverbank.
divern.a person who swims underwater, often to search for something潜水员“A team of divers are in the river searching for lost objects.”Confirms that the Q17 answer is beside the river — a strong extra clue if you lose the route.
trackn.a narrow rough path小路；小径“You’ll see a smaller track leading off to the right.”Map-task vocabulary: path (main) vs track (smaller). The change of noun signals the turn.
bendv.to curve or turn (of a road or path)（道路）转弯“Keep going till the path bends to the left.”A bend is not a turning for you — the path curves, you keep walking. Candidates often turn left here by mistake.
rubbish pitn.a hole in the ground where waste was thrown垃圾坑“This is very near the castle walls in the northwest corner of the site.”Q18. Located by compass direction plus a landmark (the castle walls) — no walking route at all.
oystern.a shellfish eaten as food牡蛎；生蚝“We found oyster shells and fishbones.”Food waste is the evidence that dates the pit to the castle kitchen, not to the village.
post holen.a hole dug to hold an upright wooden post柱洞“It had two rows of post holes deep enough to support a large roof.”Explains why the meeting hall was important — big roof = big building = the largest structure (Q19).
structuren.a building or something that has been built建筑物；结构“It’s the largest structure in a central area of the site.”Q19 key clue. “Largest” + “central” + “next to the current excavation area” — three checks in one sentence.
information boardn.a sign that gives visitors facts about a place信息牌；指示牌“You turn right at the first information board you come to.”Q20 starting point. Map tasks nearly always use small printed icons like this as turning points.
guided tourn.a visit where someone shows you round and explains things导览；有讲解的参观“We’re putting on a series of guided tours for school groups this autumn.”Answer to Q16 (C). “Guided tours for school groups” = “organise school visits”.
documentaryn.a film or TV programme about real events纪录片“Maybe you saw the TV documentary about our project.”Q16 distractor B. The past tense “you saw” proves it has already happened, so it cannot be a plan.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening the audio never uses the exact words printed on the question paper. Recognising paraphrase is the whole skill. 雅思听力中，录音几乎不会重复题干原词——识别同义替换才是关键。
Paraphrase Example 1
Question wording“Who was responsible for starting the community project?”
What you hear“I’m a full-time archaeologist for the town council, but I was asked to join the project by NHA, a charity which sets up projects like this up and down the country.”
Key paraphraseresponsible for starting = sets up projects / asked me to join. “I work for the council” and “the owners let us use their facilities” are relationships, not origins.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question wording“How was the gold coin found?”
What you hear“Coins are often found by people using metal detectors… Or coins are uncovered when wild animals like rabbits have been digging tunnels. Here, a walker found it on the ground after a rainstorm washed away some of the earth and sand.”
Key paraphraseGeneral knowledge (“often”, “or”) → this case (“Here”). Rainstorm washed away earth = heavy rain removed soil.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question wording“What are the team still hoping to find?”
What you hear“We found the remains of several buildings and more broken pots than you can count. Normally you’d expect to find brooches and other jewellery, but we’re still waiting to uncover any such items.”
Key paraphrasestill hoping to find = still waiting to uncover = have NOT found. “We found” eliminates an option instead of choosing it.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question wording“What was found on the other side of the river? — the outline of fields”
What you hear“No other houses or huts so far, but we can see the borders of an ancient field system. At one point we found a long wall and thought it was an ancient palace, but it turned out to be a modern wall.”
Key paraphraseoutline = borders. Two options are destroyed by grammar: “no… huts” and “turned out to be modern”.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 5
Question wording“What do the team plan to do after work ends this summer?”
What you hear“This summer’s work will end soon… In the meantime, we’re putting on a series of guided tours for school groups this autumn. Oh, and maybe you saw the TV documentary… the objects are going to the town’s museum, but we don’t know that for sure yet.”
Key paraphraseorganise school visits = put on guided tours for school groups. Tense is the whole test: present continuous = plan; past = already done; “not for sure” = not a plan.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 6
Question wordingQ17 bridge foundations — which letter?
What you hear“Take the main path ahead of you. Go straight on and keep going till the path bends to the left. You’ll see a smaller track leading off to the right. Follow that to take you to the river where the divers are.”
Key paraphraseThe path bends left by itself — YOU do not turn. Your only turn is right, onto the smaller track. End point = the riverbank.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 7
Question wordingQ19 meeting hall and Q20 fish pond — which letters?
What you hearHall: “the largest structure in a central area of the site next to the current excavation area.” Pond: “turn right at the first information board… into the trees. Before you come out of the trees, you’ll see it on your right. If you reach the river, you’ve gone too far.”
Key paraphraseDescriptions (largest / central / next to) work like filters. “You’ve gone too far” is a negative clue that rules out any letter on the riverbank.

Review Quiz

Q1
C. He is a full-time archaeologist for the town council.
“I’m a full-time archaeologist for the town council, but I was asked to join the project by NHA.” The volunteers are the other diggers.
Q2
B. They are general ways coins are usually found, used here as distractors.
Note the generalising words: “Coins are often found… Or coins are uncovered…”. Only after “Here” does he describe this case.
Q3
A. Peter Swift, an amateur historian.
After the coin “hit the news”, Peter Swift contacted Hayden. The proof, however, came from the old maps and documents — that is what Q13 asks about.
Q4
B. They realised they were close to an important discovery.
‘Be on to something’ = have the first sign of a real discovery. It marks the end of the Q13 answer.
Q5
B. Making tools.
“The people who once lived here were skilled at making tools from animal bones.” Bones are therefore something already found — a Q14 distractor.
Q6
