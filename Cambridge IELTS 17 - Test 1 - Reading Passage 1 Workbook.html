📋 Passage Overview

A history of the London underground railway. As central London grew congested in the 1800s, solicitor Charles Pearson proposed an underground line to link stations and clear slums. The passage covers the funding struggle, the 'cut and cover' building method, the opening of the Metropolitan line in 1863, and later deep 'tube' lines. Question types: note completion (Q1–6) and TRUE / FALSE / NOT GIVEN (Q7–13).

💡 Reading tip: For 'ONE WORD ONLY' note completion, the answer is copied exactly from the passage — no changing the word form. For T/F/NG, FALSE needs the passage to contradict the statement; NOT GIVEN means the passage simply does not say.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

However, in 1846, a Royal Commission decided that the railways should not be allowed to enter the City, the capital's historic and business centre.
Main Clausea Royal Commission decided …
Subordinate / Relativethat the railways should not be allowed to enter the City (noun clause = what they decided); 'the capital's historic and business centre' = appositive renaming 'the City'
Grammar NoteReporting verb 'decided' + that-clause; a noun phrase in apposition after the comma.
Simple MeaningIn 1846 an official group ruled that trains could not come into the City centre.
中文1846年,皇家委员会决定不允许铁路进入伦敦金融城——首都历史与商业的中心。
His idea was to relocate the poor workers who lived in the inner-city slums to newly constructed suburbs, and to provide cheap rail travel for them to get to work.
Main ClauseHis idea was to relocate the poor workers … and to provide cheap rail travel …
Subordinate / Relativewho lived in the inner-city slums (relative clause defining the workers)
Grammar NoteTwo parallel infinitives ('to relocate … and to provide …') describe the single idea.
Simple MeaningHe wanted to move poor slum workers to new suburbs and give them cheap trains to work.
中文他的想法是把住在内城贫民窟的穷苦工人迁到新建的郊区,并为他们提供廉价的上班火车。
It was built just below street level using a technique known as 'cut and cover'.
Main ClauseIt was built just below street level
Subordinate / Relativeusing a technique known as 'cut and cover' (reduced clause = 'which used a technique that is known as…')
Grammar NotePassive main verb 'was built'; participle phrase 'using…' adds method.
Simple MeaningThey built it a little under the road using the 'cut and cover' method.
中文它采用一种叫做“明挖回填”的技术,建在略低于街面的位置。
Even as the Metropolitan began operation, the first extensions to the line were being authorised; these were built over the next five years.
Main Clausethe first extensions to the line were being authorised … these were built over the next five years
Subordinate / RelativeEven as the Metropolitan began operation (time clause)
Grammar NotePast continuous passive 'were being authorised'; semicolon joins two related independent clauses.
Simple MeaningJust as the line opened, extensions were already being approved and were built over five years.
中文就在大都会线开始运营时,首批延长线已获批准,并在此后五年内建成。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Problem → solution structure. The passage first states the problem (congestion, a ban on trains entering the City) and then Pearson's proposed solution (an underground railway). Watching this shape helps you place answers 1–5.

Cause and effect. Because a Royal Commission banned railways from the City, the overground stations formed a ring around it — an effect the writer states directly. Look for 'the result was…', 'this meant…'.

Chronology. Passage 1 moves in time order: 1846 ban → construction (three years, not 21 months) → 1863 opening → extensions over five years → 1880s deep tubes. Time markers keep the T/F/NG answers in order.
