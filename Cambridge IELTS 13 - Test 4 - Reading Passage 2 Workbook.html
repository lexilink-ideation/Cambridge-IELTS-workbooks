📋 Passage Overview

Saving the soil. Seven sections (A–G) on why more than a third of the world's soil is endangered. The passage explains what healthy soil does (food, antibiotics, carbon storage, flood prevention), why agriculture and synthetic fertilisers degrade it, Pius Floris's microbial cocktail, the UN Global Soil Map, and Pamela Chasek's 'zero net land degradation' goal.

💡 Reading tip: Q14–17 is a summary (ONE WORD ONLY), Q18–21 matches sentence beginnings to endings, and Q22–26 asks which section contains information. Note that some sections here run over two paragraphs — the letters mark sections, not paragraphs, so read to the next letter before deciding.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

A single gram of healthy soil might contain 100 million bacteria, as well as other microorganisms such as viruses and fungi, living amid decomposing plants and various minerals.
Main ClauseA single gram of healthy soil might contain 100 million bacteria
Subordinate / Relativeas well as other microorganisms such as viruses and fungi (addition) + living amid decomposing plants and various minerals (participle clause)
Grammar Noteas well as adds without changing the verb agreement. The final participle clause modifies the microorganisms, not the soil — and it contains the answer to Q14.
Simple MeaningOne gram of good soil holds a hundred million bacteria plus viruses and fungi, among rotting plants and minerals.
中文一克健康的土壤可能含有一亿个细菌，以及病毒和真菌等其他微生物，它们生活在正在分解的植物和各种矿物质之间。
Soil is also an ally against climate change: as microorganisms within soil digest dead animals and plants, they lock in their carbon content, holding three times the amount of carbon as does the entire atmosphere.
Main ClauseSoil is also an ally against climate change
Subordinate / Relativeas microorganisms… digest dead animals and plants (time/reason clause) + holding three times the amount of carbon… (participle) + as does the entire atmosphere (comparative with inversion)
Grammar NoteThe colon introduces the explanation. Note the inversion in as does the entire atmosphere — formal written English for 'as the entire atmosphere does'.
Simple MeaningSoil fights climate change: its microbes trap carbon, holding three times as much as the air.
中文土壤也是应对气候变化的盟友：当土壤中的微生物分解死去的动植物时，它们把其中的碳固定下来，其储碳量是整个大气层的三倍。
Humans tend not to return unused parts of harvested crops directly to the soil to enrich it, meaning that the soil gradually becomes less fertile.
Main ClauseHumans tend not to return unused parts of harvested crops directly to the soil
Subordinate / Relativeto enrich it (purpose infinitive) + meaning that the soil gradually becomes less fertile (result participle)
Grammar Notetend not to do — note the position of 'not'. The participle meaning that… is a compact way of saying 'and this means that…'. This sentence is the source of Q18.
Simple MeaningWe usually don't put leftover crop material back in the soil, so the soil slowly loses fertility.
中文人类通常不会把收获作物中未用的部分直接返回土壤以增加肥力，这意味着土壤会逐渐变得不那么肥沃。
More recently, we have found that indiscriminate use of fertilisers hurts the soil itself, turning it acidic and salty, and degrading the soil they are supposed to nourish.
Main Clausewe have found that indiscriminate use of fertilisers hurts the soil itself
Subordinate / Relativeturning it acidic and salty and degrading the soil they are supposed to nourish — two parallel result participles
Grammar NoteTwo coordinated participle clauses both describing the consequence. The relative in 'the soil (that) they are supposed to nourish' has its pronoun omitted.
Simple MeaningOverusing fertiliser makes soil acidic and salty, damaging the very soil it should feed.
中文最近我们发现，不加节制地使用化肥会伤害土壤本身，使其变得酸化和盐碱化，并使本应被滋养的土壤退化。
When they applied Floris's mix to the desert-like test plots, a good crop of plants emerged that were not just healthy at the surface, but had roots strong enough to pierce dirt as hard as rock.
Main Clausea good crop of plants emerged
Subordinate / RelativeWhen they applied Floris's mix… (time clause) + that were not just healthy at the surface, but had roots strong enough… (relative clause separated from its noun)
Grammar NoteThe relative clause is separated from 'plants' by the verb 'emerged' — a structure that regularly confuses readers. not just… but… links two properties.
Simple MeaningOn desert-like plots his mix produced healthy plants with roots that could break through rock-hard soil.
中文当他们把弗洛里斯的混合物施用于沙漠般的试验地块时，长出了一批优质植物：它们不仅表面健康，其根系还强壮到足以穿透坚硬如石的泥土。
Like the idea of carbon neutrality, it is an easily understood target that can help shape expectations and encourage action.
Main Clauseit is an easily understood target
Subordinate / RelativeLike the idea of carbon neutrality (comparison phrase) + that can help shape expectations and encourage action (relative clause)
Grammar NoteLike + noun phrase for comparison (not 'as'). This sentence answers Q21, since 'shape expectations and encourage action' maps onto making governments more aware.
Simple MeaningLike carbon neutrality, 'zero net land degradation' is simple enough to change what people expect and do.
中文与碳中和的理念一样，它是一个容易理解的目标，能够帮助塑造预期并促成行动。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Problem–consequence–solution (问题—后果—对策): The passage's overall shape. Sections A–B state the problem and why soil matters; C–D explain the causes (agriculture, then fertilisers); E–G offer partial solutions (Floris's cocktail, the Global Soil Map, Chasek's goal, protected zones). Each solution is introduced and then qualified — 'However, measures like this are not enough', 'But this is only a first step'.

Argument by accumulation (累积论证): Section B builds the case for soil's importance by stacking functions: grows food (95%) → source of nearly all antibiotics → possible weapon against resistant bacteria → stores three times the atmosphere's carbon → prevents floods (£233 million a year in UK damage). Notice how each function is bigger or less obvious than the last. Q14–16 simply pick three items out of this list.

Natural cycle versus human interruption (自然循环与人为中断): 'In the wild, when plants grow they remove nutrients from the soil, but then when the plants die and decay these nutrients are returned directly to the soil.' Then: 'Humans tend not to return unused parts of harvested crops…'. This contrast is the heart of Q18 and Q23 — the wild cycle is the explanation of how soil stayed healthy before farming.

Solution that becomes a problem (对策变成新问题): The Haber-Bosch process 'came as a solution' in the early 20th century — 'But over the past few decades, it has become clear this wasn't such a bright idea.' Chemical fertilisers release nitrous oxide, wash nitrogen into rivers, and turn soil acidic and salty. The ironic closing phrase — 'degrading the soil they are supposed to nourish' — is what Q19 tests.

Experimental evidence (实验证据): The Valladolid test follows the standard comparison structure: treated plots produce healthy plants with rock-piercing roots; 'The few plants that grew in the control plots, fed with traditional fertilisers, were small and weak.' Whenever a passage mentions a control group, expect a question about the contrast (here, Q20).

Communication as an obstacle (沟通障碍): Section G identifies a non-scientific problem: 'Most scientists don't speak language that policy-makers can understand, and vice versa.' Chasek's answer is a simple, memorable target modelled on carbon neutrality. The reasoning is that clarity itself causes action — which is why Q21's ending is about helping governments become more aware.
