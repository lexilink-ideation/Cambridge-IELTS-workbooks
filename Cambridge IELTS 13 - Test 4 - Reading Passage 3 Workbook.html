Cambridge IELTS 13 — Test 4 — Reading Passage 3

📋 Passage Overview

Book review: The Happiness Industry by William Davies. A sharply critical review of 'positive psychology'. The reviewer attacks its advocates as ignorant of philosophy, traces the idea back to Jeremy Bentham (and his many inventions), explains Bentham's two ways of measuring happiness, and follows the line through J B Watson's behaviourism to today's government 'behaviour change' units.

💡 Reading tip: This is an opinion piece, so Q35–40 are YES/NO/NOT GIVEN — you must separate the reviewer's view from Davies's, Layard's, Bentham's and Watson's. Underline the reviewer's own evaluative words: astonishingly crude, simple-minded, oblivious, illiterate, rich, lucid, arresting, always a threat.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Those who think in this way are oblivious to the vast philosophical literature in which the meaning and value of happiness have been explored and questioned, and write as if nothing of any importance had been thought on the subject until it came to their attention.
Main ClauseThose … are oblivious to the vast philosophical literature … and write as if nothing … had been thought
Subordinate / Relativewho think in this way (relative) + in which the meaning and value of happiness have been explored (relative with preposition) + as if nothing of any importance had been thought… (unreal comparison)
Grammar Noteas if + past perfect signals that the reviewer regards the belief as false. The formal in which replaces 'that… in'. This sentence answers Q27.
Simple MeaningThey ignore centuries of philosophy about happiness and write as if nobody had thought about it before them.
中文以这种方式思考的人，对探讨并质疑幸福之意义与价值的浩瀚哲学文献一无所知，并且写起文章来仿佛在此话题引起他们注意之前，从未有过任何重要的思考。
The Greek philosopher Aristotle may have identified happiness with self-realisation in the 4th century BC, and thinkers throughout the ages may have struggled to reconcile the pursuit of happiness with other human values, but for Bentham all this was mere metaphysics or fiction.
Main Clausebut for Bentham all this was mere metaphysics or fiction
Subordinate / Relativetwo parallel concessive clauses with may have + past participle, joined by and, before the contrastive main clause
Grammar Notemay have + past participle here is concessive ('it is true that they did'), not speculative. The structure is 'X may have…, and Y may have…, but…'.
Simple MeaningAristotle and later thinkers took happiness seriously and found it complicated; Bentham dismissed all that as fantasy.
中文希腊哲学家亚里士多德或许在公元前4世纪就把幸福等同于自我实现，历代思想家也或许一直努力调和对幸福的追求与其他人类价值，但对边沁而言，这一切不过是形而上学或虚构。
One of the virtues of this rich, lucid and arresting book is that it places the current cult of happiness in a well-defined historical framework.
Main ClauseOne of the virtues … is that it places the current cult of happiness in a well-defined historical framework
Subordinate / Relativethat it places… — a noun clause acting as the complement
Grammar NoteOne of the + plural noun + singular verb ('One of the virtues is'). A very common error point. Note the three-adjective praise, marking the reviewer's own opinion.
Simple MeaningA strength of Davies's excellent book is that it puts today's happiness craze in historical context.
中文这本内容丰富、条理清晰而引人入胜的书的优点之一，是它把当下的幸福崇拜置于一个界定清晰的历史框架之中。
Viewing happiness as a complex of pleasurable sensations, he suggested that it might be quantified by measuring the human pulse rate.
Main Clausehe suggested that it might be quantified by measuring the human pulse rate
Subordinate / RelativeViewing happiness as a complex of pleasurable sensations — a fronted participle clause giving the premise
Grammar NoteFronted participle expressing the reason or basis for the main clause. by + -ing gives the method. The subject of the participle must be 'he' — a rule students often break.
Simple MeaningBecause he saw happiness as physical pleasure, Bentham thought a pulse rate could measure it.
中文由于把幸福视为一系列愉悦感受的集合，他提出或许可以通过测量人的脉搏来对其加以量化。
When he became president of the American Psychological Association in 1915, he 'had never even studied a single human being': his research had been confined to experiments on white rats.
Main Clausehe 'had never even studied a single human being'
Subordinate / RelativeWhen he became president… in 1915 (time clause) + the colon introduces the explanation
Grammar NotePast perfect for what was already true at that past moment. The colon introduces evidence, and the quotation marks show the reviewer is citing Davies.
Simple MeaningBy 1915 Watson led American psychology, yet had only ever experimented on white rats.
中文当他在1915年成为美国心理学会主席时，他“甚至从未研究过任何一个人”：他的研究一直局限于对白鼠的实验。
But whatever its intellectual pedigree, the idea that governments should be responsible for promoting happiness is always a threat to human freedom.
Main Clausethe idea … is always a threat to human freedom
Subordinate / Relativewhatever its intellectual pedigree (concessive) + that governments should be responsible for promoting happiness (appositive noun clause)
Grammar Notewhatever + noun is a compact concession ('no matter what its history is'). The word always makes this the reviewer's strongest and most absolute claim — the source of Q40.
Simple MeaningHowever respectable its origins, making governments responsible for our happiness always endangers freedom.
中文但无论其思想渊源如何，认为政府应当负责促进幸福的观念，始终是对人类自由的威胁。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Attack on a position (对立场的批判): The review opens by quoting Layard at length and then demolishes him: 'It is an astonishingly crude and simple-minded way of thinking, and for that very reason increasingly popular.' Note the sting in 'for that very reason' — crudeness explains the popularity. Recognising where the quotation ends and the reviewer's judgement begins is the single most important reading skill for this passage.

Intellectual genealogy (思想谱系): The passage traces a line: Bentham (pleasure and absence of pain, measurable) → the money measure → Watson's behaviourism → modern management, advertising and the British 'Behaviour Insights Team'. Each link is a step from philosophy towards control. This chain is the reviewer's real argument, and it is what makes the final sentence about freedom land.

Contrast between thinkers (思想家的对比): Aristotle identified happiness with self-realisation; Bentham reduced it to pleasure and the absence of pain. The reviewer invokes Aristotle precisely to show that happiness might be more than pleasure — which is exactly what Q28 asks. Note that the reviewer never says Aristotle was right; he says Bentham dismissed too much.

Balanced evaluation of the book (对书的平衡评价): Although the review is hostile to positive psychology, it praises Davies: 'One of the virtues of this rich, lucid and arresting book…', 'We learn much that is interesting about how economic problems are being redefined and treated as psychological maladies.' Q35 tests this praise. There is also a small criticism — 'Surprisingly, Davies does not discuss the fact that Bentham meant his Panopticon… also as an instrument of control'.

Undermining by biographical detail (以传记细节削弱对方): The reviewer's most damaging move against Watson is a single fact: when he became president of the APA in 1915 he 'had never even studied a single human being'. The point is not that Watson was unqualified but that a model with 'no factual basis' now shapes government policy. Q37 tests exactly this — the answer is NO.

Author's attitude (作者态度): Consistently critical, sometimes sarcastic, and explicit at the end. The last paragraph makes two claims: (1) industrial societies appear to need ever-increasing happiness to motivate labour — hedged; (2) government responsibility for happiness is always a threat to freedom — absolute. Q39 rests on the first, Q40 on the second, and the difference in hedging is why one is YES and the other NO.
