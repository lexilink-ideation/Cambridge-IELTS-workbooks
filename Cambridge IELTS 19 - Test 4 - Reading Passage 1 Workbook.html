📋 Passage Overview

The impact of climate change on butterflies in Britain. Two thirds of British butterfly species have declined over 40 years. The passage explains how warming has pushed butterflies to 'advance their phenology' (become active earlier), why flexible multi-generation species benefit while single-generation specialists suffer, using examples such as the Small Blue and the High Brown Fritillary. Question types: True/False/Not Given and note completion.

💡 Reading tip: Group the butterflies as you read — 'flexible / multi-generation' (benefit) vs 'single-generation specialists' (at risk). Sorting the examples into these two groups makes the notes (Q7–13) quick to complete.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Only by arming themselves with an understanding of why butterfly numbers are down can conservationists hope to halt or reverse the decline.
Main ClauseConservationists can hope to halt or reverse the decline.
Subordinate / Relative'Only by arming themselves with an understanding of why butterfly numbers are down' (fronted 'only by' phrase).
Grammar NoteFronting 'Only by…' forces inversion: 'can conservationists hope'.
Simple MeaningOnly by understanding why numbers fall can conservationists hope to stop the decline.
中文只有通过弄清蝴蝶数量下降的原因，保护主义者才有望遏制或扭转这一下降趋势。
When an animal or plant starts to do something earlier in the year than it usually does, it is said to be 'advancing its phenology'.
Main ClauseIt is said to be 'advancing its phenology'.
Subordinate / Relative'When an animal or plant starts to do something earlier in the year than it usually does' (time clause + comparison).
Grammar Note'earlier… than it usually does' compares timing; passive 'is said to be'.
Simple MeaningDoing something earlier in the year than normal is called 'advancing its phenology'.
中文当动物或植物在一年中比平常更早开始做某事时，就说它在'提前其物候'。
Some species are able to go from caterpillar to butterfly twice or more per year, so that the individual butterflies you see flying in the spring are the grandchildren of the individuals seen a year previously.
Main ClauseSome species go from caterpillar to butterfly twice or more per year.
Subordinate / Relative'so that the individual butterflies… are the grandchildren…' (result clause) + 'you see flying in the spring' (reduced relative) + 'seen a year previously' (reduced relative).
Grammar Note'so that' introduces the result; two reduced relatives add detail.
Simple MeaningSome butterflies breed twice or more a year, so spring ones are 'grandchildren' of last year's.
中文有些物种一年能从毛毛虫变为蝴蝶两次或更多，因此你在春天看到的蝴蝶，是一年前那些个体的孙代。
This suggests that climate change, while undoubtedly not the sole cause, might have played a part in the downfall of this species.
Main ClauseThis suggests that climate change might have played a part in the downfall of this species.
Subordinate / Relative'while undoubtedly not the sole cause' (concession phrase inserted).
Grammar Note'while… not the sole cause' concedes that other causes exist.
Simple MeaningClimate change is not the only cause, but it probably helped this species decline.
中文这表明，气候变化虽然无疑并非唯一原因，但可能在该物种的衰落中起了作用。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & effect (knock-on): fewer butterflies → fewer caterpillars → less food for birds, bats and mammals. Trace the chain in paragraph 1.

Contrast (winners vs losers): flexible, multi-generation species benefit from earlier emergence; single-generation specialists (e.g. High Brown Fritillary) are harmed. This binary organises the whole passage.

Definition: 'phenology' = the timing of lifecycle events; 'advancing phenology' = doing them earlier. Understanding the term unlocks several questions.

Hope/qualification: 'All is not lost, however' — some single-generation species might add a second generation as the climate warms, potentially increasing.
