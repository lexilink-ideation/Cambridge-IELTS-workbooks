📋 Passage Overview

This passage, 'What's So Funny?', looks at the science of humour and laughter. Researchers such as Robert Provine argue that laughter is mainly social — we laugh far more with others than alone — and evolved before jokes as a form of communication. The passage discusses how the brain processes jokes (the right prefrontal cortex), how laughter is linked to bonding, and how it may have developed from the panting sounds of play in our ancestors.

💡 Reading strategy tip: Diagram-labelling (here, areas of the brain) requires you to scan for the label words in the text, not read in order. Use the word limit and copy the exact phrase the passage uses.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"Provine found that people are about 30 times more likely to laugh in a social setting than when they are alone."
Main ClauseProvine found that people are about 30 times more likely to laugh
Subordinate / Relativein a social setting than when they are alone (comparison 'more … than')
Grammar Note'X times more likely … than …' compares two situations; a 'that'-clause reports the finding.
Simple MeaningProvine found we laugh about 30 times more with others than by ourselves.
中文普罗文发现，人们在社交场合发笑的可能性大约是独处时的30倍。
"Laughter, it seems, is primarily a social signal, which evolved long before humour as we know it."
Main ClauseLaughter … is primarily a social signal
Subordinate / Relativewhich evolved long before humour as we know it (non-defining relative)
Grammar Note'it seems' is a comment insertion; the 'which' clause adds the evolutionary point.
Simple MeaningLaughter is mainly a way of signalling to others and came before jokes.
中文看来，笑主要是一种社交信号，它的出现远早于我们所理解的幽默。
"When we hear a joke, the right prefrontal cortex, an area linked to understanding, becomes active."
Main Clausethe right prefrontal cortex … becomes active
Subordinate / RelativeWhen we hear a joke (time clause); an area linked to understanding (appositive)
Grammar Note'When …' sets the condition; the appositive explains what the cortex does.
Simple MeaningHearing a joke switches on the right prefrontal cortex, the 'understanding' area.
中文当我们听到一个笑话时，与理解相关的右前额叶皮层会变得活跃。
"Because laughter is so contagious, a single person laughing can quickly set off a whole group."
Main Clausea single person laughing can quickly set off a whole group
Subordinate / RelativeBecause laughter is so contagious (cause clause)
Grammar Note'Because … so + adj' gives the reason; 'set off' = trigger.
Simple MeaningSince laughter spreads easily, one person laughing can make a whole group laugh.
中文由于笑极具感染力，一个人笑起来很快就能带动一整群人。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Evidence & Statistics — Provine's finding that we laugh ~30× more socially supports the claim that laughter is a social signal. Numbers back the main argument.

Cause & Effect — Laughter is contagious → one person → a whole group. Bonding is both cause and result.

Evolutionary reasoning — Laughter probably evolved from play/panting in our ancestors and before humour. Watch for 'evolved', 'ancestors', 'originated'.

Localisation in the brain — Understanding a joke activates the right prefrontal cortex; different regions do different jobs — key for diagram-labelling.
