📋 Quick Review

A student presents a Year 2 project on wildlife in city gardens. She explains how the group chose the topic (sparrow-hawks appearing in city gardens), how they established that gardens make up about a quarter of urban land, how they surveyed garden owners and made their own observations, and then reports on three species: frogs, hedgehogs and song thrushes. Question types: multiple choice (Q31–36) and table completion (Q37–40, ONE WORD ONLY).

💡 Listening tip: Q31–36 follows the classic research-report order — topic choice, background data, method 1, method 2, background reading, scope of findings. Use those six stages as anchors. In Q39 note the double negative: 'we had no difficulties with our efforts to count their numbers precisely' means it was easy to count them.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

Report language is formal and often uses double negatives. Practise unpacking it.
Q32 — Confirming the proportion
Option A
consulting some official documents
What you hear
'this was endorsed by looking at large-scale usage maps in the town land survey office - 24% to be precise'
Key paraphrase
'usage maps in the land survey office' = official documents; 'endorsed' = confirmed → A
Your turn
Q34 — Choice of gardens
Option B
which they considered to be representative
What you hear
'We deliberately chose smaller ones because they were by far the most typical in the city. The whole point was to look at the norm not the exception'
Key paraphrase
'the most typical' + 'the norm not the exception' = representative → B
Your turn
Q38 — Why hedgehogs do well
Table cell
safer from ______ when in cities
What you hear
'their predators are not finding it quite so attractive to leave their rural environment, so hedgehogs have a better survival rate in cities'
Key paraphrase
predators stay in the countryside → hedgehogs in cities are safer FROM predators
Your turn
Q39 — Counting hedgehogs
Table cell
easy to ______ them accurately
What you hear
'We had lots of sightings, so all in all we had no difficulties with our efforts to count their numbers precisely'
Key paraphrase
'no difficulties with… count… precisely' = easy to count them accurately → count
Your turn
