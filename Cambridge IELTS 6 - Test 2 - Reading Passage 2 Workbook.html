Cambridge IELTS 6 — Test 2 — Reading Passage 2

Ageing Well: Health Trends Among the Elderly · Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

This passage reports on a major American health study — the National Long-term Health Care Survey — which tracked more than 20,000 adults aged 65 and over for 14 years. The study's headline finding is that common age-related diseases such as arthritis, high blood pressure, dementia, and stroke are affecting a smaller share of the elderly population every year, and are appearing later in life than before.

The passage also explores why this is happening — pointing to advances in medicine, better childhood nutrition, more education, greater use of home medical aids, and increased physical activity. However, it notes that some illnesses (certain cancers, bronchitis) have worsened, likely due to pollution and smoking. It ends with a discussion of how independence, stress hormones, and social connection all affect elderly health.

💡 Reading strategy tip: This passage is data-rich — it contains many specific figures (14 years, 20,000 people, 80%, $200 billion, 50%). Underline numbers as you read. For the summary completion (Q14–22), scan the word bank first and think about part of speech — is a noun, adjective, or adverb needed? For sentence matching (Q23–26), find each stem in the passage and check all options before committing.

Key researchers mentioned:

• Kenneth Manton — demographer, Duke University (North Carolina); leads the survey analysis; links education to longevity.

• Carl Cotman — neuroscientist, UC Irvine; studies exercise and brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF).

• Teresa Seeman — social epidemiologist, USC Los Angeles; studies stress hormones, self-esteem, and loneliness in elderly people.

Exam questions at a glance:

• Q14–22: Summary completion — choose from a word bank (A–Q) to complete a paragraph about the study's findings.

• Q23–26: Sentence-ending matching — match four sentence beginnings to the correct ending from a list of eight (A–H).

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Each sentence below is taken from the passage. Study how each one is built — identify the main clause, any subordinate or relative clauses, and the overall meaning.

"The results of a 14-year study to be announced later this month reveal that the diseases associated with old age are afflicting fewer and fewer people and when they do strike, it is much later in life."
Main Clause The results … reveal that the diseases … are afflicting fewer and fewer people
Relative Clause "associated with old age" — modifies "the diseases"; tells us which diseases
Adverbial Clause "when they do strike, it is much later in life" — adds the timing condition
Grammar Note "fewer and fewer" = a gradual decrease (countable noun). "do strike" = emphatic auxiliary for contrast/emphasis
Simple Meaning A new study shows that age-related diseases are becoming less common, and even when they do occur, they happen at an older age than before.
中文 一项为期14年的研究结果表明，与老龄化相关的疾病正影响着越来越少的人，而且即便发病，也是在人生更晚的阶段。
"Although some of this can be attributed to a higher income, Manton believes it is mainly because educated people seek more medical attention."
Main Clause Manton believes it is mainly because educated people seek more medical attention
Concessive Clause "Although some of this can be attributed to a higher income" — admits a partial explanation before contrasting with the main point
Grammar Note "Although" introduces a concession — the writer acknowledges something before disagreeing with or qualifying it. "can be attributed to" = passive voice + modal
Simple Meaning While higher income plays a role, Manton thinks the main reason educated people live longer is that they visit doctors more often.
中文 尽管其中一部分原因可以归结于较高的收入，但曼顿认为，主要原因在于受过教育的人会更主动地寻求医疗帮助。
"He found that rats that exercise on a treadmill have raised levels of brain-derived neurotrophic factor coursing through their brains."
Main Clause He found that rats … have raised levels of BDNF coursing through their brains
Relative Clause "that exercise on a treadmill" — defines which rats (those that exercise)
Participle Phrase "coursing through their brains" — a present participle phrase describing BDNF in motion; "coursing" = flowing quickly
Simple Meaning Cotman discovered that rats that run on treadmills have more BDNF flowing through their brains.
中文 他发现，在跑步机上运动的老鼠，其大脑中流动的脑源性神经营养因子水平更高。
"In laboratory simulations of challenging activities such as driving, those who felt in control of their lives pumped out lower levels of stress hormones such as cortisol."
Main Clause those who felt in control of their lives pumped out lower levels of stress hormones
Prepositional Phrase "In laboratory simulations of challenging activities such as driving" — sets the context/setting
Relative Clause "who felt in control of their lives" — identifies the group of people
Grammar Note "such as" introduces examples. "pump out" = informal verb meaning to produce/release; note the vivid physical image
Simple Meaning In stress tests (like simulated driving), people who felt they were in control of their lives produced less cortisol.
中文 在驾驶等高难度活动的实验室模拟中，那些觉得自己掌控生活的人，分泌的压力激素（如皮质醇）水平更低。
"The research suggests that older people fare best when they feel independent but know they can get help when they need it."
Main Clause The research suggests that older people fare best
Adverbial Clause "when they feel independent but know they can get help when they need it" — a complex when-clause with an embedded when-clause inside it
Grammar Note "fare best" = do best / thrive most. "but" connects two conditions: independence AND access to support — both are needed
Simple Meaning Elderly people do best when they can live independently but also know that support is available if required.
中文 研究表明，老年人在感到独立自主、同时又知道需要时可以获得帮助的情况下，状态最佳。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Academic passages use specific logical patterns to organise information. Recognising these patterns will help you answer all question types faster and more accurately.

📌 Pattern 1 — Cause & Effect (因果关系)

Signal words because, due to, as a result, lead to, cause, result in, be linked to, attribute to

Example from the passage: "An increase in some cancers and bronchitis may reflect changing smoking habits and poorer air quality." → Cause: smoking + pollution → Effect: more cancer/bronchitis

Another example: "Chronically high levels of these hormones have been linked to heart disease." → Cause: chronic stress hormones → Effect: heart disease

Practice question: According to the passage, what causes some elderly people to have lower cortisol levels during challenging tasks?

Answer hint: Feeling in control of their lives.

📌 Pattern 2 — Contrast (对比/转折)

Signal words however, but, although, on the downside, while, whereas, despite, yet

Example from the passage: "Clearly, certain diseases are beating a retreat… But there may be other contributing factors." → The author qualifies the medical explanation with other possible causes.

Another example: "On the downside, the data also reveals failures in public health…" → Introduces a negative finding after a section of positive findings.

Exam tip: Contrast markers often signal that the NEXT sentence contradicts what you just read. Always check both sides when answering True/False/Not Given questions.

📌 Pattern 3 — Comparison (比较)

Signal words more than, less than, compared to, similarly, likewise, whereas

Example from the passage: "65-year-old women with fewer than eight years of schooling are expected, on average, to live to 82. Those who continued their education live an extra seven years." → The author compares two groups to show that education matters for longevity.

Practice question: How many more years do well-educated 65-year-old women live on average compared to those with less than 8 years of schooling?

Answer: Seven extra years (to age 89).

📌 Pattern 4 — Examples & Evidence (举例/证据)

Signal words for example, for instance, such as, including, like

Example from the passage: "For instance, the use of raised toilet seats has more than doubled since the start of the study, and the use of bath seats has grown by more than 50%." → These statistics are evidence for the claim that elderly people are using more home medical aids.

Exam tip: In sentence-matching or summary-completion questions, the answer often requires you to match an example in the question to the general claim in the passage, or vice versa.

📌 Pattern 5 — Problem / Solution (问题与解决方案)

Signal words the problem is, to solve, help prevent, one way to, may prevent

Example from the passage: Problem: brain function deteriorates with age. Proposed solution: exercise raises BDNF levels, which "may prevent the brains of active humans from deteriorating."

Another example: Problem: elderly people become dependent. Solution: home medical aids (raised toilet seats, bath seats) allow them to remain self-reliant.

📌 Pattern 6 — Author's Attitude & Hedging (作者态度与不确定语气)

Signal words may, might, could, suggest, appear, seem, it is believed, probably, likely

Examples from the passage:

• "…may reflect changing smoking habits" — the author is not 100% certain of the cause.

• "…may prove less of a financial burden than expected" — a prediction, not a fact.

• "…may help mental functioning" — possible benefit, not proven.

Exam tip: For True/False/Not Given questions, hedging words like "may" or "suggest" change the meaning. "Exercise may prevent brain decline" is NOT the same as "Exercise prevents brain decline" — the second would be an overstatement (→ False or NG).
