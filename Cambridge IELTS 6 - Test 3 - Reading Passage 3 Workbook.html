📋 Passage Overview

This science passage explains how scientists are searching for a "caloric-restriction (CR) mimetic" — a pill that could copy the anti-aging benefits of eating a very low-calorie diet, without requiring people to actually eat less. It reviews decades of animal research (rats and monkeys) showing that caloric restriction increases lifespan and reduces disease risk, then explains how one candidate drug, 2DG, works at the cellular level by interfering with glucose processing.

This is a dense academic science passage with technical vocabulary (glucose, ATP, free radicals), making it good practice for locating specific detail and following a cause-effect chain through a flow-chart completion task.

💡 Reading strategy tip: For the flow-chart (Q38–40), find the relevant paragraph first (the last one, "How a prototype caloric-restriction mimetic works"), then follow the described process step by step exactly as it appears in the text — the flow-chart mirrors the paragraph's structure precisely.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"But one intervention, consumption of a low-calorie yet nutritionally balanced diet, works incredibly well in a broad range of animals, increasing longevity and prolonging good health."
Main Clauseone intervention...works incredibly well in a broad range of animals
Subordinate Clause"increasing longevity and prolonging good health" (present participle clause, describing result)
Grammar NoteThe appositive phrase "consumption of a low-calorie...diet" renames "one intervention"; the -ing clause expresses simultaneous consequence
Simple MeaningEating a balanced, low-calorie diet is very effective at making many animal species live longer and healthier.
中文但有一种干预方法——即摄入低热量但营养均衡的饮食——在广泛的动物种类中效果显著，能延长寿命并延续健康。
"What is more, some of the treated animals survived longer than the oldest-living animals in the control group, which means that the maximum lifespan...not merely the normal lifespan, increased."
Main Clausesome of the treated animals survived longer than the oldest-living animals in the control group
Subordinate Clause"which means that the maximum lifespan...increased" (non-restrictive relative clause + noun clause)
Grammar Note"which" refers back to the entire preceding clause, not a single noun — a common academic device for summarising an implication
Simple MeaningSome treated animals outlived even the longest-living untreated animals, showing that caloric restriction raises the absolute maximum lifespan, not just the average.
中文此外，一些接受处理的动物比对照组中最长寿的动物活得还长，这意味着最长寿命（而不仅仅是平均寿命）也得到了延长。
"Various interventions, such as infection-fighting drugs, can increase a population's average survival time, but only approaches that slow the body's rate of aging will increase the maximum lifespan."
Main ClauseVarious interventions...can increase a population's average survival time, but only approaches...will increase the maximum lifespan
Subordinate Clause"that slow the body's rate of aging" (relative clause modifying "approaches")
Grammar Note"but" introduces a contrast between two similar-sounding but distinct outcomes (average survival vs. maximum lifespan) — a key logical distinction in the passage
Simple MeaningMany treatments can help more individuals survive longer on average, but only treatments that actually slow aging itself can extend the absolute upper limit of lifespan.
中文诸如抗感染药物等各种干预措施可以提高种群的平均存活时间，但只有能减缓身体衰老速度的方法才能延长最长寿命。
"It has proved toxic at some doses in animals and so cannot be used in humans. But it has demonstrated that chemicals can replicate the effects of caloric restriction; the trick is finding the right one."
Main ClauseIt has proved toxic...and so cannot be used in humans. But it has demonstrated that chemicals can replicate the effects of caloric restriction
Subordinate Clause"that chemicals can replicate the effects of caloric restriction" (noun clause, object of "demonstrated")
Grammar Note"so" signals result; the semicolon links a summary conclusion ("the trick is finding the right one") to the preceding claim
Simple Meaning2DG is too dangerous for humans, but it proves the concept works — scientists just need to find a safer chemical.
中文它在动物体内某些剂量下已被证明有毒，因此不能用于人类。但它证明了化学物质可以复制热量限制的效果——关键在于找到合适的那一种。
"Another hypothesis suggests that decreased processing of glucose could indicate to cells that food is scarce (even if it isn't) and induce them to shift into an anti-aging mode."
Main ClauseAnother hypothesis suggests [that clause]
Subordinate Clause"that decreased processing of glucose could indicate...and induce them to shift into an anti-aging mode" (noun clause, object of "suggests") with embedded concession "(even if it isn't)"
Grammar NoteParenthetical concession "(even if it isn't)" qualifies "scarce" — shows the signal can be a false alarm that still triggers a real cellular response
Simple MeaningA second theory: less glucose processing might trick cells into thinking food is short, even when it's not, causing them to switch into a protective, anti-aging state.
中文另一种假说认为，葡萄糖处理量的减少可能会向细胞发出食物匮乏的信号（即使实际并非如此），并促使细胞转入抗衰老模式。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect

Cause: caloric restriction reduces glucose processing and ATP synthesis. Effect: fewer free radicals are emitted, so cells suffer less damage (Theory 1) — or cells switch into a preservation-focused "anti-aging mode" (Theory 2).

Contrast

The passage contrasts "average survival time" (which many interventions can raise) with "maximum lifespan" (which only true anti-aging treatments can raise) — a distinction tested directly in Q32.

Comparison

Caloric-restricted monkeys are compared with control monkeys across several measures: body temperature, insulin levels, blood pressure, triglycerides, and blood glucose — all better in the restricted group.

Examples

The passage gives examples of species where caloric restriction has been studied: "yeast, fruit flies, worms, fish, spiders, mice, hamsters," and more recently rhesus and squirrel monkeys — building an evidence base from simple to complex organisms.

Definition

A "CR mimetic" is defined as a substance that "mimics the physiological effects of eating less without actually forcing people to eat less" — the central concept the whole passage explores.

Problem / Solution

Problem: humans are unlikely to sustain a 30% calorie reduction for years. Solution: find a pill (CR mimetic) that reproduces the same benefits chemically.

Chronology

Scientists recognised caloric restriction's value "more than 60 years ago" (in rats) → posed the CR-mimetic question "in the mid-1990s" → currently conducting long-term primate studies (nearly 15 years so far).

Author's Attitude

The tone is cautiously optimistic and objective — presenting real scientific uncertainty ("no compound...has been found yet") alongside genuine hope ("has fanned hope that CR mimetics can indeed be developed eventually").
