Practice Workbook — "Delivering the Goods"

📋 Passage Overview

This passage explains the revolution in international freight transport that has driven the huge growth in world trade. It argues that falling shipping costs — not just lower tariffs — are a major, often-overlooked reason trade has boomed.

The article traces how the type of goods traded has shifted from heavy raw materials to light manufactured goods and "weightless" digital products, and how technologies like containerisation and deregulation of trucking/rail have made moving cargo dramatically more efficient — though many countries still have far to go.

💡 Reading strategy tip: This passage has nine lettered paragraphs (A–I) and combines paragraph-matching with True/False/Not Given and a summary-completion task using a word bank. For T/F/NG, check carefully whether the passage confirms, contradicts, or simply never mentions the statement.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"Cheap labour may make Chinese clothing competitive in America, but if delays in shipment tie up working capital and cause winter coats to arrive in spring, trade may lose its advantages."
Main Clause 1Cheap labour may make Chinese clothing competitive in America
Conditional Clauseif delays in shipment tie up working capital and cause winter coats to arrive in spring
Grammar Note"but if..." introduces a conditional counter-argument to the main claim
Simple MeaningLow costs alone don't guarantee trade success — slow shipping can cancel out the advantage.
中文廉价劳动力也许能让中国服装在美国具有竞争力，但如果运输延误占用了流动资金，导致冬季外套春天才到货，贸易可能就失去了优势。
"Computer software can be 'exported' without ever loading it onto a ship, simply by transmitting it over telephone lines from one country to another."
Main ClauseComputer software can be "exported" without ever loading it onto a ship
Manner Clausesimply by transmitting it over telephone lines from one country to another
Grammar Note"by + gerund" expresses the method; scare quotes around 'exported' signal a non-literal use of the word
Simple MeaningDigital products cross borders instantly, without any physical shipping at all.
中文计算机软件无需装船就能被"出口"，只需通过电话线从一个国家传输到另一个国家。
"State ownership of railways and airlines, regulation of freight rates and toleration of anti-competitive practices, such as cargo-handling monopolies, all keep the cost of shipping unnecessarily high and deter international trade."
Subject (3-part list)State ownership... / regulation... / toleration of anti-competitive practices...
Main Verb Phraseall keep the cost of shipping unnecessarily high and deter international trade
Grammar NoteA long triple subject followed by a compound predicate ("keep... and deter...")
Simple MeaningGovernment control and unfair business practices make shipping needlessly expensive and reduce trade.
中文铁路和航空公司的国有制、运费管制以及对垄断等反竞争行为的容忍，都使运输成本居高不下，阻碍了国际贸易。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect: "The decline in trade barriers + falling shipping costs → explosion in international commerce." Falling freight costs are presented as an under-recognised cause of the trade boom.

Contrast: Paragraph B contrasts theory ("shipping costs do not matter") with reality ("the real world is full of frictions") — a classic academic-writing move of stating an assumption, then complicating it.

Comparison: Early-20th-century trade (heavy raw materials, high transport cost) is compared with today's trade (light manufactured/digital goods, low transport cost) to show how the composition of trade has shifted.

Problem → Solution: Problem — old cargo handling risked damage/theft and was slow. Solution — the container crane and standardised container sizes allowed fast, safe loading of "any box on any ship."

Chronology: The passage moves chronologically: turn of the 20th century → 1967 (all-container vessels) → mid-1970s (US deregulation begins) → 1985–1996 (productivity data) → today.
