📋 Passage Overview

This passage explains the Air Traffic Control (ATC) system in the United States. It describes how a 1956 collision over the Grand Canyon led to the FAA, how ATC developed from flags and beacons to radar and radio, and how US airspace is organised — controlled vs uncontrolled airspace, the VFR/IFR flight rules for different weather, and the lettered airspace classes (A–F) around airports and at altitude.

💡 Reading strategy tip: For Matching Headings, cover the paragraph and ask 'what is the ONE main point?' before looking at the list. For True/False/Not Given, treat a statement as Not Given unless you can point to the exact place that confirms or contradicts it — don't use outside knowledge.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"An accident that occurred in the skies over the Grand Canyon in 1956 resulted in the establishment of the Federal Aviation Administration."
Main ClauseAn accident … resulted in the establishment of the Federal Aviation Administration
Subordinate / Relativethat occurred in the skies over the Grand Canyon in 1956 (relative clause defining 'accident')
Grammar Note'that' introduces a defining relative clause; 'resulted in' = caused.
Simple MeaningA 1956 Grand Canyon crash caused the FAA to be created.
中文1956年发生在大峡谷上空的一起事故促成了美国联邦航空管理局的成立。
"It was only after the creation of the FAA that full-scale regulation of America's airspace took place."
Main Clausefull-scale regulation of America's airspace took place
Subordinate / RelativeIt was only after the creation of the FAA that … (cleft sentence for emphasis)
Grammar Note'It was only after … that …' emphasises the timing; nothing happened until the FAA.
Simple MeaningFull control of US airspace began only once the FAA existed.
中文只有在联邦航空管理局成立之后，美国空域才开始得到全面管理。
"In this way, the recreational pilot who simply wishes to go flying can stay in uncontrolled airspace, while the pilot who wants protection can enter the controlled airspace."
Main Clausethe recreational pilot … can stay in uncontrolled airspace … while the pilot … can enter the controlled airspace
Subordinate / Relativewho simply wishes to go flying / who wants protection (two relative clauses); while = contrast
Grammar NoteTwo 'who' clauses define the two pilots; 'while' contrasts their choices.
Simple MeaningCasual pilots can avoid the rules by staying in uncontrolled airspace; those wanting ATC help enter controlled airspace.
中文这样，只想随便飞行的休闲飞行员可以留在非管制空域，而想要保护的飞行员则可进入管制空域。
"The difference between Class E and A airspace is that in Class A, all operations are IFR, and pilots must be instrument-rated."
Main ClauseThe difference … is that … all operations are IFR, and pilots must be instrument-rated
Subordinate / Relativethat in Class A … (noun clause naming the difference)
Grammar Note'The difference … is that …' defines a contrast; 'must' = obligation.
Simple MeaningIn Class A airspace every flight must use instruments and the pilot must be qualified to do so.
中文E类与A类空域的区别在于：在A类空域，所有飞行都必须按仪表飞行规则进行，飞行员必须持有仪表等级资质。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect — The 1956 Grand Canyon accident resulted in the establishment of the FAA; the jet engine demanded rules to keep fast planes separated. Signal: resulted in, demanding, so, for.

Problem / Solution — Problem: crowded, fast-moving skies. Solution: nationwide controlled airspace, VFR/IFR rules, and lettered airspace classes.

Classification — Airspace is sorted by letter: F (uncontrolled) · E (below 5,490m) · A (above 5,490m) · D/C/B (small→major airports). Understanding the system answers Q19 & the T/F/NG items.

Contrast — VFR relies on visual cues; IFR relies on instruments. Class E allows both; Class A is IFR-only. Signal: however, whereas, the difference … is.
