Listening Part 4

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 4 — Listening Part 4

Birds of Prey near the Chembe Bird Sanctuary · Notes Completion (Q31–40)

📋 Quick Review

This is a university lecture about conflict between wildlife and people at the edges of protected areas. The speaker describes research carried out in Zambia, around the Chembe Bird Sanctuary, and explains why birds of prey (eagles, hawks and owls) matter to local small-scale farmers, why their numbers are falling, and what farmers do to keep their chickens safe. You complete a set of notes with ONE WORD ONLY for each of the ten gaps.

Who is speaking? One lecturer, speaking continuously in an academic style. There are no questions and no second voice, so you cannot rely on a change of speaker to tell you a new answer is coming — you have to follow the signposting language instead (For example… However… Another possibility would be…).

The situation 情境: 一位讲师介绍他在赞比亚 Chembe 鸟类保护区周边所做的研究。当地居民多为小农户，猛禽（鹰、隼、猫头鹰）为他们捕杀鼠类和蛇，还带来旅游收入；但猛禽数量正在下降——被车撞、被电线电死，也会被农民非法射杀或毒杀。最后讲师列举农民保护鸡群的几种办法及其效果。

Task type: Notes completion, Q31–40, with three sub-headings and bullet points. Instruction: ONE WORD ONLY — no numbers are needed anywhere in this set, and every answer is a single common noun or verb that you already know.

💡 Listening strategy tip: Part 4 notes have bold headings, and in this recording the three headings (importance → falling numbers → protecting chickens) are announced almost word for word by the lecturer: “these birds of prey provide important social and ecological benefits”, “their numbers are falling”, “so how else can farmers protect their chickens?”. Use those three sentences as checkpoints — if you hear one of them and you still have a blank above it, let that answer go and move on. The instruction is ONE WORD ONLY, so a two-word answer such as ‘heavy rain’ or ‘guard dog’ scores zero, even though it is what the audio says.
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • Q31 ‘rodents’ is already printed. The note reads ‘They destroy (31) and other rodents’, and the audio says ‘rodents such as rats’. The word you hear first (rodents) is the one you must NOT write — the answer is the example, rats.
  • Q32 must be plural. The audio says ‘a major danger to rural workers is snakes whose bite may be dangerous’. The noun bite is right next to the answer and is already on the question paper as ‘bitten by’. Write snakes, not ‘snake’ and not ‘bite’.
  • Q33: tourism, not tourists. The note says ‘encouraging (33) in the area’, and the sentence is about the economy and revenue — both of those words are distractors printed on the paper or spoken beside the answer. The gap needs the uncountable activity noun tourism.
  • Q34: the killer is the traffic, not the road. You hear ‘birds… on roads… are hit by fast-moving traffic’, and then ‘drivers in Zambia have to take special care at night’. Three tempting nouns; only traffic fits ‘killed by’.
  • Q35: one word only. The audio says ‘the heavy rain which can occur… in the months from December to April’. Writing ‘heavy rain’ is two words = 0 marks; writing ‘December’ matches the wrong idea. The answer is rain.
  • Q36 needs a base verb. The note reads ‘may illegally shoot them or (36) them’, so the gap must be a bare infinitive to match shoot. The audio says ‘or they may poison the birds’ — write poison, never ‘poisoning’ or ‘poisoned’.
  • Q38 is singular. ‘Most of the farmers had at least one dog’, and the note says ‘keeping a (38)’. The article a on the question paper is your instruction: write dog, not ‘dogs’.
  • Q39: the result, not the object. The audio describes children hitting pans with a metal spoon; ‘metal objects’ is already printed under the gap. What you write is the outcome of the hitting — the resulting noise.

ecosystemn.all the living things in an area and how they interact生态系统“can have negative effects on the ecosystem”Poisoning does not only kill the target bird — a good idea to reuse in Speaking Part 3 about the environment.

noisen.a loud or unpleasant sound噪音；响声“the resulting noise would succeed in driving away birds”Answer to Q39. You must write the result of hitting the pans, not the pans or the spoon.
combinationn.two or more things used together组合；结合“they were forced to use a combination”Answer to Q40. Spelling: com-bi-na-tion. Signalled by ‘rather than just using one method’.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening the audio never uses the exact words printed on the question paper. Recognising paraphrase is the whole skill. 雅思听力中，录音几乎不会重复题干原词——识别同义替换才是关键。
Paraphrase Example 1
Question wording“They destroy (31) and other rodents.”
What you hear“a lot of damage can be caused to farmers’ crops by rodents such as rats, which would consume the crops… if they weren’t hunted and killed by the birds.”
Key paraphrasedestroy = hunt and kill; ‘such as’ introduces the example you must write (rats), not the category (rodents)
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question wording“prevent farmers from being bitten by (32)”
What you hear“a major danger to rural workers is snakes whose bite may be dangerous or even fatal. And birds of prey have a major role in keeping their populations under control.”
Key paraphraseprevent farmers from being bitten = keep their populations under control; ‘rural workers’ = ‘farmers’
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question wording“They now support the economy by encouraging (33)”
What you hear“the sanctuary and its birds have also become increasingly important to the community in economic terms because at present, after a relatively slow start, tourism has become an important source of revenue.”
Key paraphrasesupport the economy = important in economic terms / a source of revenue → the activity itself is tourism
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question wording“killed by (34) when they are hunting or sleeping”
What you hear“Fatalities occur when birds alight on roads to catch and eat their prey and are hit by fast-moving traffic. Drivers… have to take special care at night as birds may regard the quieter roads as safe places to sleep.”
Key paraphrasehunting = catch and eat their prey; sleeping = regard the roads as safe places to sleep; the killer is the traffic
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 5
Question wording“electrocution… especially at times when there is a lot of (35)”
What you hear“Accidental deaths may also occur if these birds fly close to high-voltage power lines, as they may be electrocuted. This is a particular danger in the heavy rain which can occur in the region in the months from December to April.”
Key paraphrasea lot of ___ = heavy ___; the instruction ONE WORD ONLY forces you to drop ‘heavy’
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 6
Question wording“providing a (37) for chickens (expensive)”
What you hear“Another possibility would be to prevent the chickens from going outside at all and to keep them safe from predators inside a building, but this would cost far too much to be a practical solution.”
Key paraphrase(expensive) on the question paper = ‘would cost far too much to be a practical solution’
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 7
Question wording“A (40) of methods is usually most effective.”
What you hear“None of these methods was 100 per cent effective. So, as a result, the village people told us that rather than just using one method, they were forced to use a combination.”
Key paraphrasemost effective = the best of several imperfect options; ‘rather than just one’ → a combination
