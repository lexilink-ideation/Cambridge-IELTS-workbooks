📋 Passage Overview

Is the era of artificial speech translation upon us? The writer talks to experts (Waibel, Ochoa) about devices that translate speech between languages, the challenges of noise and etiquette, and whether such tools will replace shared languages. The conclusion argues software will add value to language skills but never replace them. Question types: multiple choice, sentence endings and Yes/No/Not Given.

💡 Reading tip: The writer's tone is balanced but ends by valuing human language skills. For YES/NO/NOT GIVEN, match the writer's opinion, especially in the final paragraphs, where the argument is clearest.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

A device may be able to recognise speech in a laboratory, or a meeting room, but will struggle to cope with the kind of background noise I can hear surrounding Professor Waibel as he speaks to me from Kyoto station.
Main ClauseA device may recognise speech in a lab, but will struggle to cope with background noise.
Subordinate / Relative'I can hear surrounding Professor Waibel' (reduced relative) + 'as he speaks to me from Kyoto station' (time clause).
Grammar Note'but' contrasts controlled conditions with real-world noise.
Simple MeaningTranslators work in quiet rooms but struggle with real background noise.
中文一台设备也许能在实验室或会议室里识别语音，但很难应对我在京都车站的怀贝尔教授周围听到的那种背景噪音。
It came to represent one of those devices that technology enthusiasts dream of long before they become practically realisable, like TVs flat enough to hang on walls.
Main ClauseIt came to represent one of those devices that technology enthusiasts dream of.
Subordinate / Relative'that technology enthusiasts dream of' (relative clause) + 'long before they become practically realisable' (time clause) + 'like TVs flat enough to hang on walls' (example).
Grammar Note'flat enough to hang' = adjective + enough + to-infinitive.
Simple MeaningThe Babel fish was a dreamed-of gadget, like once-impossible flat TVs.
中文它逐渐成为那类技术爱好者早在其真正可实现之前就梦寐以求的设备之一，就像薄到可以挂在墙上的电视机。
Etiquette-sensitive artificial translators could relieve people of the need to know these differing cultural norms.
Main ClauseEtiquette-sensitive translators could relieve people of the need to know these norms.
Subordinate / Relative'to know these differing cultural norms' (infinitive completing 'the need').
Grammar Note'relieve sb of sth' = free sb from sth.
Simple MeaningPolite translators could save people from learning different cultural rules.
中文具有礼仪意识的人工翻译器，或许能让人们不必去了解这些不同的文化规范。
Though the practical need for a common language will diminish, the social value of sharing one will persist.
Main ClauseThe social value of sharing a common language will persist.
Subordinate / Relative'Though the practical need for a common language will diminish' (concession clause).
Grammar Note'Though' concedes the practical decline before asserting the lasting social value.
Simple MeaningWe may need a shared language less, but its social value will remain.
中文尽管对通用语言的实际需求会减少，但共享一门语言的社会价值将会持续存在。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Claim vs reality: the title asks if the 'era' has arrived; the writer repeatedly notes it has not yet ('We haven't reached the future yet', 'a long way to go'). Track this tension.

Requirements list: a good system must be simultaneous, work offline, protect private data, cope with noise, and be socially aware. These map to several questions.

Cause & effect (adoption): earpieces will spread only if voice-controlled devices and artificial translation become common — one depends on the other (Q30).

Concession & conclusion: even with good software, 'it will never be as good as the real thing'; shared languages keep social value. The writer's final stance is clear for YES/NO judgements.
