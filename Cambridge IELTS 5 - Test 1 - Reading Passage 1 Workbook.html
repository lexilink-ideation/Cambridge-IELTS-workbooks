📋 Passage Overview

This passage, 'Johnson's Dictionary', describes how Samuel Johnson produced the first authoritative dictionary of English, published in 1755. It explains the state of English before Johnson, earlier dictionaries (Cawdray's 1604 word-list), the link between dictionaries and the rising middle class, and the huge scale of Johnson's work — 40,000 words, 114,000 quotations, six assistants (two of whom died), and its status as an unrivalled landmark for over a century.

💡 Reading strategy tip: For TRUE / FALSE / NOT GIVEN (Q8–13), test each statement against the text. FALSE means the passage says the opposite; NOT GIVEN means the passage is simply silent. Watch out for extra details the statement adds that the text never mentions.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"For the century before Johnson's Dictionary was published in 1755, there had been concern about the state of the English language."
Main Clausethere had been concern about the state of the English language
Subordinate / RelativeFor the century before Johnson's Dictionary was published in 1755 (fronted time phrase)
Grammar NotePast perfect ('had been') for a period leading up to a past point; the time phrase gives the century-long context.
Simple MeaningFor 100 years before 1755, people worried about the condition of English.
中文在约翰逊的词典于1755年出版之前的一个世纪里，人们一直担忧英语的状况。
"Beyond the practical need to make order out of chaos, the rise of dictionaries is associated with the rise of the English middle class."
Main Clausethe rise of dictionaries is associated with the rise of the English middle class
Subordinate / RelativeBeyond the practical need to make order out of chaos (prepositional phrase = 'in addition to')
Grammar Note'Beyond' here means 'apart from / in addition to'; 'is associated with' links two social trends.
Simple MeaningAs well as being useful, dictionaries grew because the middle class was growing.
中文除了整理混乱的实际需要之外，词典的兴起还与英国中产阶级的崛起有关。
"Working to a deadline, he had to draw on the best of all previous dictionaries, and to make his work one of heroic synthesis."
Main Clausehe had to draw on the best of all previous dictionaries … and to make his work one of heroic synthesis
Subordinate / RelativeWorking to a deadline (participle phrase = 'Because he was working to a deadline')
Grammar NoteThe -ing participle gives the reason; two parallel infinitives ('to draw on', 'to make') follow 'had'.
Simple MeaningBecause he had limited time, he used the best earlier dictionaries and combined them impressively.
中文由于要赶截止日期，他不得不借鉴以往所有最好的词典，并将自己的作品打造成一次了不起的综合。
"Unlike his predecessors, Johnson treated English very practically, as a living language, with many different shades of meaning."
Main ClauseJohnson treated English very practically, as a living language
Subordinate / RelativeUnlike his predecessors (contrast phrase); with many different shades of meaning (added detail)
Grammar Note'Unlike …' sets up a contrast with earlier writers; 'as a living language' shows how he saw English.
Simple MeaningDifferent from earlier writers, Johnson saw English as a real, changing language full of subtle meanings.
中文与前人不同，约翰逊非常务实地把英语当作一门有许多细微含义的活语言来对待。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect — Because there was no standard way of writing English, there was a demand for a dictionary to bring order. Signal words: led to, because, as a result.

Contrast — Earlier dictionaries listed only scholarly words, whereas Johnson treated English as a living language with everyday meanings. Signal: unlike, whereas, but.

Chronology — Cawdray (1604) → 17th-century dictionaries → Johnson's contract (1746/1764) → publication (1755) → 'unrivalled for a century'. Dates anchor detail questions.

Author's attitude — The writer clearly admires Johnson ('heroic synthesis', 'landmark'). Positive evaluative language signals a favourable view.
