Reading Passage 1

Cambridge IELTS 10 — Test 3 — Reading Passage 1: The Context, Meaning and Scope of Tourism

Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

A textbook-style survey of tourism in five paragraphs. It traces travel from prehistory to modern mass tourism, presents statistics showing tourism is the world's largest industry, then explains why its economic impact is so hard to measure — the industry is diverse and fragmented.

这是一篇教科书式的旅游业综述,五段 A–E。语言正式、数据密集,考点集中在段落主旨和数据是否被具体提及。

💡 Reading tip: Paragraph A is given as the example (viii), so you only need four headings for B–E. The two hardest are D and E: D is about the difficulty of measuring tourism's effects (v), while E is about its worldwide importance (vii). Both mention statistics, so decide by asking 'what is this paragraph for?'

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Travel has existed since the beginning of time, when primitive man set out, often traversing great distances in search of game, which provided the food and clothing necessary for his survival.
Main ClauseTravel has existed since the beginning of time
Subordinate / Relative'when primitive man set out' = relative clause of time; 'often traversing great distances in search of game' = participle clause; 'which provided the food and clothing necessary for his survival' = relative clause describing 'game'
Grammar NoteA chain of three subordinate structures, each hanging off the one before. Read left to right and let each clause attach to the nearest noun.
Simple MeaningPeople have travelled since the earliest times, when early humans went long distances to hunt animals for food and clothes.
中文旅行自古有之——早期人类为寻找猎物而出发,常常跋涉很远的距离,而猎物为其生存提供了食物和衣物。
The creation of the commercial airline industry following the Second World War and the subsequent development of the jet aircraft in the 1950s signalled the rapid growth and expansion of international travel.
Main ClauseThe creation … and the subsequent development … signalled the rapid growth and expansion of international travel
Subordinate / Relative'following the Second World War' and 'in the 1950s' = time phrases inside the compound subject
Grammar NoteA long compound subject ('The creation of X … and the subsequent development of Y') followed by a single verb. Students often mistake 'following the Second World War' for the main clause.
Simple MeaningCommercial airlines after WWII and jets in the 1950s made international travel grow fast.
中文二战后商业航空业的创建,以及随后20世纪50年代喷气式飞机的发展,标志着国际旅行的迅速增长与扩张。
Thus, tourism has a profound impact both on the world economy and, because of the educative effect of travel and the effects on employment, on society itself.
Main Clausetourism has a profound impact both on the world economy and … on society itself
Subordinate / Relative'because of the educative effect of travel and the effects on employment' = reason phrase inserted between 'and' and 'on society'
Grammar Note'Both … and …' is interrupted by a long reason phrase. Note that the reason relates ONLY to the social impact — this is exactly why Q7 ('promotes recreation') is NOT GIVEN.
Simple MeaningSo tourism strongly affects the world economy, and also society, because travel educates people and creates jobs.
中文因此,旅游业不仅对世界经济产生深远影响,而且由于旅行的教育作用和对就业的影响,也对社会本身产生深远影响。
However, the major problems of the travel and tourism industry that have hidden, or obscured, its economic impact are the diversity and fragmentation of the industry itself.
Main Clausethe major problems … are the diversity and fragmentation of the industry itself
Subordinate / Relative'that have hidden, or obscured, its economic impact' = defining relative clause describing 'problems'
Grammar NoteThe subject and complement are separated by an eight-word relative clause. Note 'problems … are the diversity and fragmentation' — TWO features, which makes Q8 TRUE.
Simple MeaningTourism's economic impact is hidden because the industry is so varied and so broken up.
中文然而,掩盖了旅游业经济影响的主要问题,正是该行业自身的多样性与分散性。
Since many of these businesses also serve local residents, the impact of spending by visitors can easily be overlooked or underestimated.
Main Clausethe impact of spending by visitors can easily be overlooked or underestimated
Subordinate / Relative'Since many of these businesses also serve local residents' = reason clause
Grammar Note'Since' here means 'because', not 'from a time'. Note that the passage says visitor spending is hard to separate from resident spending — it never compares their sizes, which is why Q9 is NOT GIVEN.
Simple MeaningBecause these businesses serve locals too, it is easy to miss or undercount what tourists spend.
中文由于这些企业中有许多也服务于当地居民,游客消费的影响很容易被忽视或低估。
However, because of problems of definition, which directly affect statistical measurement, it is not possible with any degree of certainty to provide precise, valid or reliable data about the extent of world-wide tourism participation or its economic impact.
Main Clauseit is not possible … to provide precise, valid or reliable data
Subordinate / Relative'because of problems of definition' = reason phrase; 'which directly affect statistical measurement' = non-defining relative clause; 'about the extent of world-wide tourism participation or its economic impact' = prepositional phrase
Grammar Note'It is not possible to…' with a long delayed infinitive. The triple adjective 'precise, valid or reliable' is emphatic. This sentence makes Q10 FALSE.
Simple MeaningBecause tourism is hard to define, no one can give accurate figures for how many people travel worldwide or what it is worth.
中文然而,由于定义上的问题直接影响到统计测量,人们无法有任何把握地提供关于全球旅游参与规模或其经济影响的精确、有效或可靠的数据。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

1. Chronology (时间顺序). Paragraph A is a historical sweep: prehistory (hunting) → trade, religion, war, migration → the Roman era (Pompeii, Herculaneum) → 'except during the Dark Ages' → recorded history. Paragraph B narrows to the modern era: the industrial revolution in England → after the Second World War → the 1950s jet aircraft → 1992 statistics. Date words are the fastest scanning targets in the whole passage.

2. Cause & effect (因果). The chain in paragraph B is explicit: the rise of the middle class + cheap transport → mass tourism; commercial airlines + jets → rapid growth of international travel; 'This growth led to the development of a major new industry'; 'In turn, international tourism became the concern of world governments since it provided employment and foreign exchange'. Note the two-step signal 'led to … in turn … since'.

3. Statistics as evidence (以数据为证). Paragraph C is almost entirely figures: $3.5 trillion gross output; over 12% of all consumer spending; 130 million jobs; almost 7% of all employees; over 6% of the world's GNP; $422 billion in capital investment. For TRUE/FALSE questions the danger is not the number itself but what it is a number of. Q6 fails because 6% refers to the world's GNP, not Australia's.

4. Problem statement (问题陈述). Paragraph D is a single sustained problem: the industry's diversity and fragmentation obscure its economic impact. Sub-problems follow in order — many businesses also serve locals; the concepts are 'amorphous'; no reliable information base exists. Then a classic turn: 'However, the nature of this very diversity makes travel and tourism ideal vehicles for economic development.' The problem becomes an advantage.

5. Contrast (对比). Paragraph E opens on a contrast that could be a topic sentence in any essay: 'Once the exclusive province of the wealthy, travel and tourism have become an institutionalised way of life for most of the population.' Then a second contrast closes the passage: enormous importance versus impossible measurement. Notice that the passage ends on the measurement problem — a strong clue to the writer's emphasis.

6. Hedging (模糊限定). The writer is careful: 'Historians suggest', 'was estimated to be', 'McIntosh and Goeldner (1990) suggest', 'Hawkins and Ritchie, quoting from data published by the American Express Company, suggest', 'it is not possible with any degree of certainty'. Every strong claim is attributed to someone. This is exactly why so many statements in this passage turn out to be NOT GIVEN — the passage is careful not to say more than it can prove.
