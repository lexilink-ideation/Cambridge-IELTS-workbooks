Cambridge IELTS 7 — Test 2 — Reading Passage 1

Practice Workbook — "Why Pagodas Don't Fall Down"

📋 Passage Overview

This passage explains a structural engineering mystery: why do Japan's tall wooden pagodas — some more than 1,000 years old — survive devastating earthquakes and typhoons that destroy modern buildings nearby? The writer explores how these pagodas differ from their Chinese ancestors, and describes the ingenious design features (loosely stacked floors, a free-hanging central pillar called the shinbashira, and extra-wide eaves) that let the buildings sway and absorb shock instead of breaking.

The passage combines historical background (how pagodas arrived from China), engineering explanation (the pendulum-like shinbashira), and a modern research angle (Professor Shuzo Ishida's laboratory experiments) — a common structure for Academic IELTS Reading Passage 1 texts.

💡 Reading strategy tip: This passage mixes historical facts, technical explanation, and comparison (China vs Japan). Before reading in detail, skim for these three layers — then when you reach True/False/Not Given and classification questions, you'll already know roughly where each type of information is located.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"In a land swept by typhoons and shaken by earthquakes, how have Japan's tallest and seemingly flimsiest old buildings — 500 or so wooden pagodas — remained standing for centuries?"
Main Clausehow have Japan's ... buildings remained standing for centuries? (a question)
Subordinate / Reduced Clause"swept by typhoons and shaken by earthquakes" — reduced relative clause modifying "a land" (= a land which is swept... and shaken...)
Grammar NotePast participle phrases act as reduced relative clauses; the dash-set phrase "500 or so wooden pagodas" is an appositive that renames "buildings".
Simple MeaningJapan has typhoons and earthquakes, so how have its old wooden pagodas survived for so long?
中文在一个饱受台风侵袭、地震摇撼的国度，日本最高、看似最不结实的古老建筑——大约500座木塔——是如何屹立数百年而不倒的？
"Yet in 826, with only pegs and wedges to keep his wooden structure upright, the master builder Kobodaishi had no hesitation in sending his majestic Toji pagoda soaring fifty-five metres into the sky — nearly half as high as the Kasumigaseki skyscraper built some eleven centuries later."
Main Clausethe master builder Kobodaishi had no hesitation in sending his majestic Toji pagoda soaring fifty-five metres into the sky
Subordinate / Reduced Clause"with only pegs and wedges to keep his wooden structure upright" (prepositional phrase of accompanying circumstance); "nearly half as high as..." (comparative phrase)
Grammar Note"with + noun + infinitive" shows limited means available; the dash introduces a comparison for emphasis.
Simple MeaningUsing only simple wooden pegs and wedges, the builder confidently built a 55-metre pagoda — almost half the height of a skyscraper built 1,100 years later.
中文然而在公元826年，仅凭木钉和木楔来维持木结构的直立，工匠空海毫不犹豫地将雄伟的东寺五重塔建到了55米高——几乎相当于1100多年后建成的霞关大厦高度的一半。
"The Chinese built their pagodas in brick or stone, with inner staircases, and used them in later centuries mainly as watchtowers."
Main ClauseThe Chinese built their pagodas in brick or stone... and used them... mainly as watchtowers
Subordinate / Reduced Clause"with inner staircases" — prepositional phrase describing an additional feature
Grammar NoteCompound predicate (built... and used...) joined by "and"; the "with" phrase is a descriptive modifier, not a full clause.
Simple MeaningChinese pagodas were made of brick/stone, had inner staircases, and were later used as watchtowers.
中文中国的宝塔多用砖石建造，内部设有楼梯，后来主要被用作瞭望塔。
"In fact, in some pagoda designs, it does not even rest on the ground, but is suspended from the top of the pagoda — hanging loosely down through the middle of the building."
Main Clauseit does not even rest on the ground, but is suspended from the top of the pagoda
Subordinate / Reduced Clause"hanging loosely down through the middle of the building" — present participle phrase adding descriptive detail (reduced relative clause)
Grammar Note"not... but..." parallel structure signals contrast between two possibilities.
Simple MeaningIn some pagodas, the central pillar (shinbashira) doesn't touch the ground — it hangs loosely from the top instead.
中文事实上，在某些宝塔设计中，中心柱甚至不落地，而是从塔顶悬挂下来——松散地垂挂在建筑物中央。
"What those early craftsmen had found by trial and error was that under pressure a pagoda's loose stack of floors could be made to slither to and fro independent of one another."
Main ClauseWhat those early craftsmen had found by trial and error was [that-clause]
Subordinate / Reduced Clause"that under pressure a pagoda's loose stack of floors could be made to slither to and fro independent of one another" — noun clause, subject complement
Grammar NoteThe "What..." clause functions as the sentence subject (nominal relative clause); the following "that" clause completes the meaning as a complement.
Simple MeaningThrough trial and error, ancient builders discovered that under pressure, the loosely stacked floors could slide independently back and forth.
中文那些早期工匠通过反复试验发现：在压力作用下，宝塔松散叠放的各层楼板可以彼此独立地来回滑动。
"With the eaves extending out on all sides like balancing poles, the building responds to even the most powerful jolt of an earthquake with a graceful swaying, never an abrupt shaking."
Main Clausethe building responds to even the most powerful jolt of an earthquake with a graceful swaying, never an abrupt shaking
Subordinate / Reduced Clause"With the eaves extending out on all sides like balancing poles" — absolute construction expressing reason/manner
Grammar Note"With + noun + participle" absolute construction; "never an abrupt shaking" is an elliptical parallel structure (= and never responds with an abrupt shaking).
Simple MeaningBecause the eaves stick out on every side like a tightrope walker's balance pole, the building sways gracefully in an earthquake instead of shaking suddenly.
中文由于屋檐像平衡杆一样向四面伸展，即使遇到最猛烈的地震冲击，建筑物也只是优雅地摇摆，而不会突然剧烈晃动。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect

Because Japanese pagodas taper (each floor is smaller than the one below) and are not connected between storeys, the loose floors can slide independently during an earthquake, which dissipates energy instead of letting it build up destructively.

Contrast

Chinese pagodas (brick/stone, inner staircases, used as watchtowers, porcelain tiles) are contrasted throughout with Japanese pagodas (wood, no staircase, art objects only, heavier earthenware tiles, wider eaves). Watch for signal words like "however", "yet", and "unlike" that mark this contrast.

Comparison

The Toji pagoda (55m, built 826) is compared to the Kasumigaseki skyscraper (36 storeys, built 1968) — "nearly half as high... built some eleven centuries later" — emphasising how advanced the ancient design was.

Examples

The passage supports its claims with concrete examples: the 1995 Hanshin earthquake (which spared the Toji pagoda), Shuzo Ishida's "shake-table" laboratory experiments, and the tightrope walker analogy for the eaves.

Definitions

Key term: shinbashira = the massive, trunk-like central pillar of a Japanese pagoda, which in some designs hangs from the top rather than resting on the ground, and acts like a stationary pendulum.

Chronology

6th century — pagoda concept arrives in Japan from China → 826 — Toji pagoda built by Kobodaishi → 1968 — Kasumigaseki skyscraper built (first with shock absorbers) → 1995 — Hanshin earthquake tests the Toji pagoda's resilience.

Author's Attitude

The writer's tone is admiring and impressed — phrases like "clearly, Japanese carpenters... knew a few tricks" and "anticipated concepts of modern structural engineering" show respect for the ingenuity of ancient, low-tech solutions compared to modern engineering.
