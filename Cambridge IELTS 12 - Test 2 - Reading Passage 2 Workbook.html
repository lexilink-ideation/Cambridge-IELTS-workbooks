📋 Passage Overview

'The Lost City' — how the American explorer Hiram Bingham came upon Machu Picchu in July 1911. The passage contrasts Bingham's dramatic 1948 book with his much flatter journal entries at the time, explains the false theory he defended for 40 years, and ends with the modern view that the site was a royal country estate. Question types: matching headings (Q14–20), TRUE / FALSE / NOT GIVEN (Q21–24) and sentence completion (Q25–26).

💡 Reading tip: The heading list contains two 'Bingham + noun' traps ('Bingham gains support' vs 'Bingham publishes his theory') and two description traps ('A dramatic description' vs 'Different accounts of the same journey'). Decide each paragraph's function — is it describing, comparing, or explaining? — before you look at the list.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

His goal was to locate the remains of a city called Vitcos, the last capital of the Inca civilisation.
Main ClauseHis goal was to locate the remains of a city called Vitcos
Subordinate / Relative'called Vitcos' = reduced relative clause (= 'which was called Vitcos'); 'the last capital of the Inca civilisation' = appositive renaming Vitcos.
Grammar NoteInfinitive complement after 'was' + two layers of post-modification.
Simple MeaningHe wanted to find the ruins of Vitcos, the Incas' final capital.
中文他的目标是找到一座名为维特科斯的城市遗迹——印加文明的最后一座都城。
When Bingham and his team set off down the Urubamba in late July, they had an advantage over travellers who had preceded them: a track had recently been blasted down the valley canyon to enable rubber to be brought up by mules from the jungle.
Main Clausethey had an advantage over travellers
Subordinate / Relative'When Bingham and his team set off down the Urubamba in late July' = time clause; 'who had preceded them' = relative clause; after the colon, an independent clause explains the advantage, ending with 'to enable rubber to be brought up' = infinitive of purpose.
Grammar NoteTime clause + main clause + colon of explanation; past perfect 'had preceded' / 'had been blasted' for earlier events.
Simple MeaningUnlike earlier travellers, Bingham's team could use a new track that had been blasted through the canyon so mules could carry rubber up from the jungle.
中文7月下旬,当宾厄姆一行沿乌鲁班巴河出发时,他们比前人多了一个优势:峡谷中新近炸开了一条小道,以便骡子把橡胶从丛林运上来。
Almost all previous travellers had left the river at Ollantaytambo and taken a high pass across the mountains to rejoin the river lower down, thereby cutting a substantial corner, but also therefore never passing through the area around Machu Picchu.
Main ClauseAlmost all previous travellers had left the river … and taken a high pass across the mountains
Subordinate / Relative'to rejoin the river lower down' = purpose; 'thereby cutting a substantial corner' and 'but also therefore never passing through the area around Machu Picchu' = two result participle phrases.
Grammar NotePast perfect for background + two '-ing' result clauses linked by 'but also therefore'. Note the double linker 'thereby' / 'therefore' marking consequence.
Simple MeaningEarlier travellers took a mountain shortcut, so they rejoined the river further down and never went near Machu Picchu.
中文几乎所有此前的旅行者都在奥扬泰坦博离开河道,翻越高山垭口后在下游重新回到河边,从而抄了一大段近路,但也因此从未经过马丘比丘一带。
In his book Lost City of the Incas, he relates that he made the ascent without having the least expectation that he would find anything at the top.
Main Clausehe relates …
Subordinate / Relative'that he made the ascent' = noun clause; 'without having the least expectation' = negative prepositional phrase; 'that he would find anything at the top' = a second noun clause defining 'expectation'.
Grammar NoteTwo 'that' clauses doing different jobs: the first reports, the second defines a noun. 'the least' intensifies the negative.
Simple MeaningIn his book he says he climbed the hill expecting nothing at all.
中文在《印加失落之城》一书中,他叙述说自己登上山顶时,丝毫没有期待会在上面发现什么。
We should remember, however, that Lost City of the Incas is a work of hindsight, not written until 1948, many years after his journey.
Main ClauseWe should remember … that Lost City of the Incas is a work of hindsight
Subordinate / Relative'not written until 1948' = reduced relative clause (= 'which was not written until 1948'); 'many years after his journey' = time phrase in apposition.
Grammar NoteModal 'should' + that-clause; a reduced passive relative clause adds the crucial date.
Simple MeaningRemember that the book was written in 1948, long after the trip, so it is shaped by later knowledge.
中文不过我们应当记住,《印加失落之城》是一部事后追述之作,直到1948年才写成,距他的旅程已过去多年。
There are no references to it by any of the Spanish chroniclers — and if they had known of its existence so close to Cusco they would certainly have come in search of gold.
Main ClauseThere are no references to it by any of the Spanish chroniclers
Subordinate / Relative'if they had known of its existence so close to Cusco' = third conditional if-clause; 'they would certainly have come in search of gold' = its result clause.
Grammar NoteThird conditional (had + past participle / would have + past participle) = an unreal past situation.
Simple MeaningNo Spanish chronicler mentions Machu Picchu; if they had known about it, they would have gone there looking for gold.
中文没有任何西班牙编年史家提到过它——如果他们知道在离库斯科这么近的地方有此城,必定会前来寻金。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Chronology as the backbone. A (1911 arrival and aim) → B (late July, the route) → C (24 July, the reluctant climb) → D (the vivid book description) → E (the 1948 book vs the 1911 journal) → F (the 1913 article and the false theory) → G (the modern explanation). Every heading answer follows from knowing where you are in this sequence.

Contrast between two accounts. Paragraph D gives the dramatic version; paragraph E immediately undercuts it ('We should remember, however…'). This is the single most important logical move in the passage — and it produces two different headings: 'A dramatic description' (D) and 'Different accounts of the same journey' (E).

Cause and effect in the route choice. A rubber track was blasted → Bingham could follow the river → he passed near Machu Picchu. Earlier travellers took a shortcut → they never saw it. This causal chain answers Q22 and Q25 together.

Motive and claim. Paragraph F explains why Bingham invented his 'big idea': 'it occurred to him that he could make a name for himself'. The writer then evaluates it: 'his vision… while a magnificent one, is inaccurate.' Concessive 'while' introduces the writer's judgement.

Question → accepted answer. Paragraph G opens with a puzzle ('why the site seems to have been abandoned') and closes with the currently accepted solution (a royal estate abandoned after Pachacuti's death). Recognising the question-then-answer shape tells you this paragraph is not about Bingham at all.
