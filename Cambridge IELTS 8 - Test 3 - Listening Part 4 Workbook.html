📋 Quick Review

A lecturer explains what can go wrong when businesses try to copy their own best practices. She notes that copying an existing process is common but often fails, discusses why relying on ‘experts’ is risky (their knowledge is incomplete), and describes two mistakes managers make when setting up a duplicate system, the causes of the resulting problems, and the solution. The tasks are multiple-choice plus notes completion (ONE WORD ONLY).

💡 Listening strategy tip: This is an academic lecture. Listen for the lecturer’s signposting of reasons, mistakes and solutions (‘one reason for failure…’, ‘two types of mistake…’, ‘what’s the solution?’). For ONE WORD ONLY notes, pick the single key noun; a two-word answer is marked wrong.

🔄 Paraphrase Training

In IELTS the question words are almost never the words you hear or read. Recognising paraphrase is the key skill. Study each example, then write your own sentence.
Paraphrase Example 1
Question / promptQ31: Compared with new processes, copying existing ones is …
What you hear / read"a less glamorous activity than pure innovation, but it actually happens more often."
Key paraphrase‘more frequent’ = happens more often → answer B (not more straightforward).
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question / promptQ32: Most research into repeating success has …
What you hear / read"A host of studies confirm this, covering a wide range of business settings."
Key paraphraseMany studies agree → ‘produced consistent findings’ (B); it’s not confined to the US.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question / promptQ34: An expert’s knowledge may be incomplete because …
What you hear / read"Some may be difficult to describe — learned on the job … but impossible to describe in a helpful way."
Key paraphrase‘some details are difficult for workers to explain’ → answer A.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question / promptQ35: A manager wrongly tries to create an ideal ___.
What you hear / read"trying to use the best parts of various different systems, in the hope of creating the perfect combination."
Key paraphraseONE WORD: combination.
