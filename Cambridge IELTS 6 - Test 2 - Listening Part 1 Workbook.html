Cambridge IELTS 6 — Test 2 — Listening Part 1

Children's Art & Craft Workshops · Synmouth Museum · Practice Workbook

📋 Quick Review

A woman phones Synmouth Museum to ask about its weekly children's art and craft workshops. A man (a museum staff member) answers and gives her practical details: the age rule for unaccompanied children, the venue address, how to open the security entrance, where to park, and how to make a booking. He then describes the next two Saturday workshops — their themes, what children should wear, and what materials to bring from home.

This is a classic Part 1 social/service call: two speakers, conversational English, answers are concrete facts that follow the order of the questions.

💡 Part 1 strategy: Read the notes and table before the recording starts. For each blank, predict the type of answer — a number, a name, a place, an object. Watch out for distractors: in Q3, two buttons are mentioned (green and red) — the answer is specific. In Q7–Q10, the table has four columns; know what type of information each column needs before you listen.

Unit at a glance

Task breakdown

Q1–5 (Note completion): A bullet-point fact sheet about the workshops. No more than two words and/or a number per blank. Information is given in order.

Q6–10 (Table completion): A table covering two upcoming Saturday workshops (16 Nov and 23 Nov). Columns: workshop title · special clothing · materials to bring. No more than two words per blank.

🔄 Paraphrase Training

IELTS Listening questions never use the exact same words as the audio. Each example below shows the question wording, the exact line from the script, and the key paraphrase to remember.
Q1 — Age Restriction
Question wording Adults must accompany children under ______
Exact script line
MAN: … we do ask that children below eight years of age are accompanied by an adult.
Key paraphrase accompany → accompanied by  |  under → below  |  Answer: 8
Your practice
Q2 — Address (Spelled Out)
Question wording Workshops held in: Winter House, ______ Street
Exact script line
MAN: Yes, it's Winter House. And that's in Tamer Street. WOMAN: Could you spell that please? MAN: Yes, T–A–M–E–R Street.
Key paraphrase held in → "it's in"  |  The name is spelled out letter-by-letter — write what you hear: T-A-M-E-R = Tamer
Your practice
Q3 — Security Entrance (Distractor!)
Question wording Security entrance: press the ______ to be let in
Exact script line
MAN: … there's a security entrance, so you need to press the green button for someone to let you in. Don't press the red button please, but don't worry, it's all clearly labelled.
Key paraphrase ⚠️ Distractor: "red button" is mentioned but is WRONG. Answer is "green button". Always listen for negation: "Don't press…"
Your practice
Q4 — Parking Location
Question wording Parking: leave car at the back of the ______
Exact script line
MAN: Your best bet is to leave your car at the back of the library – on a Saturday morning there are plenty of spaces there. It's right next door to the museum.
Key paraphrase "at the back of" (script) = "behind" (question)  |  "Your best bet is to" = informal way of saying "you should"  |  Answer: library
Your practice
Q5 — Booking Method
Question wording Book by calling the ______
Exact script line
MAN: Yes, and I must tell you, you really should book by calling the education department here. MAN: It's two hundred–seven–six–five. [= 200765]
Key paraphrase calling = phoning  |  Answer is two words: "education department" — don't write just "education"
Your practice
Q8 & Q10 — Materials (Indirect Requests)
Script (Q8)
MAN: And if possible, could you bring along bottle tops which the children might be able to use in the models, you know, as decoration?
Script (Q10)
MAN: … if you could search out some silver paper to bring along to use in the sessions … it looks like water, that'd be great.
Key paraphrase "could you bring along…?" = materials needed  |  "search out" = find and bring  |  The reason (it looks like water) confirms "silver paper" not just "paper"

Exercise 1 — Note Completion (Q1–5)

1.
8
Script: "children below eight years of age are accompanied by an adult." The answer is eight (not seven — a common mistake based on the example "all ages from five upwards are welcome").
2.
Tamer
Script: "T–A–M–E–R Street." The man spells it letter-by-letter because the woman asks. Write what you hear: T-A-M-E-R = Tamer.
3.
green button
Script: "press the green button … Don't press the red button." The red button is a distractor. Both "green button" and "green" are acceptable.
4.
library
Script: "leave your car at the back of the library." "At the back of" = "behind" in the question. The library is right next to the museum.
5.
education department
Script: "book by calling the education department here." Two-word answer — writing only "education" is incomplete. The direct number given is 200765.
