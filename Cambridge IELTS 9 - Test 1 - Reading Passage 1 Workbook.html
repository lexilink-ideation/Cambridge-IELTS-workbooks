📋 Passage Overview

William Henry Perkin — the man who invented synthetic dyes. This biography traces how a teenage London chemistry student, while trying to make artificial quinine for malaria, accidentally produced a deep purple substance instead. He recognised its commercial value, patented it, left college and built a dye factory. His colour mauve became a fashion craze, and synthetic dyes later became essential to medical research. Question types: True / False / Not Given (Q1–7) and short-answer questions (Q8–13).

💡 Reading tip: Biography passages follow chronology, so the answers appear in the same order as the questions. For TRUE/FALSE/NOT GIVEN, be strict: FALSE means the passage says the opposite; NOT GIVEN means the passage simply never says. Five of the seven statements here contain a detail the writer never mentions or reverses — read the exact wording, not the general topic.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

But it was a chance stumbling upon a run-down, yet functional, laboratory in his late grandfather's home that solidified the young man's enthusiasm for chemistry.
Main ClauseIt was … that solidified the young man's enthusiasm for chemistry. (cleft sentence)
Subordinate / Relative'a chance stumbling upon a run-down, yet functional, laboratory in his late grandfather's home' = the emphasised noun phrase.
Grammar NoteAn it-cleft ('It was X that Y') puts the spotlight on X. 'run-down, yet functional' is a contrastive pair of adjectives; 'late' here means deceased.
Simple MeaningFinding an old but usable lab in his dead grandfather's house is what made him love chemistry.
中文但真正让这位年轻人对化学的热情坚定下来的，是他偶然在已故祖父家中发现的一间破旧却仍能使用的实验室。
Thus, when Hofmann made some passing comments about the desirability of a synthetic substitute for quinine, it was unsurprising that his star pupil was moved to take up the challenge.
Main ClauseIt was unsurprising that his star pupil was moved to take up the challenge.
Subordinate / Relative'when Hofmann made some passing comments…' (time/cause clause) + 'that his star pupil was moved…' (that-clause as real subject).
Grammar Note'it' is a dummy subject; the real subject is the that-clause. 'passing comments' = remarks made casually, in passing.
Simple MeaningBecause Hofmann casually said a man-made quinine substitute would be useful, his best student naturally decided to try.
中文因此，当霍夫曼随口提到人工合成奎宁替代品颇为可取时，他最得意的学生受此激励而接下挑战，也就不足为奇了。
And, proving the truth of the famous scientist Louis Pasteur's words 'chance favours only the prepared mind', Perkin saw the potential of his unexpected find.
Main ClausePerkin saw the potential of his unexpected find.
Subordinate / Relative'proving the truth of… Louis Pasteur's words' = a participial phrase describing the whole main action.
Grammar NoteA present participle ('proving') attaches a comment to the main clause. Note: Pasteur is quoted, not an influence on Perkin — this is the trap in Q7.
Simple MeaningPerkin realised how useful his accidental discovery could be — which shows Pasteur was right that luck helps prepared minds.
中文珀金看到了这一意外发现的潜力，正印证了著名科学家巴斯德那句话——「机遇只偏爱有准备的头脑」。
Indeed, the purple colour extracted from a snail was once so costly that in society at the time only the rich could afford it.
Main ClauseThe purple colour was once so costly.
Subordinate / Relative'extracted from a snail' (reduced relative = which was extracted) + 'that… only the rich could afford it' (result clause).
Grammar Noteso + adj + that + result. The reduced relative removes 'which was'.
Simple MeaningPurple dye from snails was so expensive that only rich people could buy it.
中文事实上，从蜗牛中提取的紫色曾经如此昂贵，以致当时社会上只有富人才买得起。
He asked advice of Scottish dye works owner Robert Pullar, who assured him that manufacturing the dye would be well worth it if the colour remained fast and the cost was relatively low.
Main ClauseHe asked advice of Robert Pullar.
Subordinate / Relative'who assured him that…' (non-defining relative) containing 'if the colour remained fast and the cost was relatively low' (conditional).
Grammar NoteA relative clause carries a that-clause, which in turn carries an if-clause — three levels of embedding.
Simple MeaningHe asked Robert Pullar, a Scottish dye factory owner, who told him the dye would be worth making if it didn't fade and wasn't costly.
中文他向苏格兰染厂主罗伯特·普勒请教，后者向他保证：只要颜色不褪且成本相对低廉，生产这种染料就很值得。
Utilising the cheap and plentiful coal tar that was an almost unlimited byproduct of London's gas street lighting, the dye works began producing the world's first synthetically dyed material in 1857.
Main ClauseThe dye works began producing the world's first synthetically dyed material in 1857.
Subordinate / Relative'Utilising the cheap and plentiful coal tar…' (participial phrase) + 'that was an almost unlimited byproduct…' (defining relative).
Grammar NoteA fronted participial phrase explains the means. 'Utilising' = 'By using'.
Simple MeaningBy using cheap coal tar left over from London's gas street lights, the factory made the first synthetically dyed cloth in 1857.
中文利用伦敦煤气路灯所产生的近乎无限的廉价副产品——煤焦油，这家染厂于 1857 年生产出了世界上第一批合成染色的织物。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & effect chain: demand for quinine outstripped supply → Hofmann remarked a synthetic substitute was desirable → Perkin took up the challenge → he failed but produced a purple sludge → he saw its commercial potential. Following this chain lets you predict where each answer sits.

Contrast (natural vs synthetic): 'Historically, textile dyes were made from such natural sources as plants and animal excretions… It was against this backdrop that Perkin's discovery was made.' The whole paragraph exists to make the synthetic dye look revolutionary.

Concession: 'Despite his best efforts, however, he did not end up with quinine. Instead, he produced a mysterious dark sludge.' 'Despite / however / instead' mark a failure that becomes a success.

Exemplification: 'Some of these, such as the glandular mucus of snails, were difficult to obtain…' and 'For instance, they were used to stain previously invisible microbes.' Examples usually sit right after the general claim they illustrate.

Author's attitude: the writer is admiring — 'perhaps the most fascinating of all Perkin's reactions', 'what would have been particularly pleasing to Perkin'. Attitude words like these tell you the passage will not criticise him, which helps you reject negative options.

Chronology markers: 1838 (born) → 1853 (Royal College, aged 15) → 1856 (holiday experiment) → 1857 (first dyed material) → 1859, 1863, late 1860s (later dyes) → today. Dates are the fastest way to locate an answer in a biography.
