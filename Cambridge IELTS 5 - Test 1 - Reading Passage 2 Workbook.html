📋 Passage Overview

This passage, 'Nature or Nurture?', describes Stanley Milgram's famous Yale obedience experiment. Volunteer 'teachers' were told to give increasing electric shocks to a 'pupil' (really an actor) for wrong answers. Although observers predicted almost no one would go to the top voltage, over 60% obeyed to the maximum. The writer weighs two explanations — a built-in genetic aggression instinct versus the power of the social/institutional setting — which is the core question of sociobiology.

💡 Reading strategy tip: When a passage weighs two competing explanations, track which paragraph belongs to which side. Questions often ask you to attribute an idea to the correct theory (genetic vs social), so underline signal words like alternative, in contrast, one explanation.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"Milgram told each volunteer that the experiment was in the noble cause of education, and was designed to test whether punishing pupils would have a positive effect on learning."
Main ClauseMilgram told each volunteer that the experiment was in the noble cause of education
Subordinate / Relativeand was designed to test whether punishing pupils would have a positive effect on learning (2nd 'that'-clause)
Grammar NoteReported speech with two parallel 'that'-clauses; 'whether' introduces an embedded question.
Simple MeaningMilgram said the study was about improving education by seeing if punishment helps pupils learn.
中文米尔格拉姆告诉每位志愿者，这项实验是为了教育这一崇高目的，用来测试惩罚学生是否对学习有正面作用。
"The supposed ‘pupil’ was in reality an actor hired by Milgram to simulate receiving the shocks."
Main ClauseThe supposed 'pupil' was in reality an actor
Subordinate / Relativehired by Milgram to simulate receiving the shocks (reduced relative + purpose infinitive)
Grammar Note'hired …' = 'who was hired'; 'to simulate' shows purpose.
Simple MeaningThe 'pupil' was really an actor paid to pretend he was being shocked.
中文所谓的“学生”实际上是米尔格拉姆雇来假装受到电击的演员。
"How can we possibly account for this vast discrepancy between what calm, rational people predict and what pressured but cooperative ‘teachers’ actually do?"
Main ClauseHow can we possibly account for this vast discrepancy
Subordinate / Relativebetween what … people predict and what … 'teachers' actually do (two nominal 'what'-clauses)
Grammar NoteA rhetorical question; 'between X and Y' compares prediction with reality.
Simple MeaningWhy is there such a big gap between what people think they'd do and what they really do?
中文我们究竟如何解释冷静理性的人所预测的与承受压力却顺从的“老师”所实际做的之间的巨大差异？
"An action such as shocking a victim, which in isolation appears evil, acquires a completely different meaning when placed in this setting."
Main ClauseAn action such as shocking a victim … acquires a completely different meaning
Subordinate / Relativewhich in isolation appears evil (non-defining relative); when placed in this setting (time/condition phrase)
Grammar NoteNon-defining 'which' adds comment; 'when placed' = 'when it is placed'.
Simple MeaningShocking someone seems evil on its own, but feels different inside a trusted scientific study.
中文像电击受害者这样的行为，单独看似乎是邪恶的，但放在这种情境中就获得了完全不同的意义。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Prediction vs Reality — Experts predicted only ~1 in 1,000 would give 450 volts; in fact over 60% did. This discrepancy is the puzzle the passage explores.

Two competing explanations — (1) Nature: an inherited aggression instinct. (2) Nurture: the trusted institutional setting. Signal: An alternative to this notion is…

Cause & Effect — The lab's legitimacy → subjects trust it → an evil act acquires a different meaning → they obey. Follow the chain of reasoning.

Author's stance — The writer stays balanced, presenting both sides and calling the choice 'the problem of modern sociobiology' rather than favouring one.
