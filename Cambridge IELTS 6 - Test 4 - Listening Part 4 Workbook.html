Cambridge IELTS 6 — Test 4 — Listening Part 4

Asiatic Lions and the Gir Sanctuary · Practice Workbook

📋 Quick Review

A lecturer describes the Asiatic lion, a sub-species that split away from the African lion almost 100,000 years ago. The talk covers its history across Europe, the Middle East and India, its physical differences from African lions, and the threats now facing the small surviving population at the Gir Sanctuary in India.

This is a typical Part 4 academic monologue: one long, structured talk with many facts, numbers and dates delivered in a fixed order, usually with no repetition.

💡 Part 4 strategy: Before the recording starts, read through the note headings and predict what type of word is missing (a number, a date, an adjective, a place name). Because the talk moves quickly and doesn't repeat, keep your pen moving to the next gap the instant you catch an answer — don't linger.

🔄 Paraphrase Training

Academic lectures often restate facts and dates using different wording than the question notes. Study each contrast, then practise.
Paraphrase 1 — Origin of the Sub-Species
Question wordingAsiatic lions developed as a separate sub-species: ______ years ago
Audio meaningThe lecturer says these lions branched off from their African cousins almost 100,000 years back, evolving their own distinct features over time.
Key paraphrase"branched off" = split off / developed as a separate sub-species
Your practice
Paraphrase 2 — Physical Differences
Question wordingHow do Asiatic lions differ physically from African lions?
Audio meaningThe lecturer explains that they tend to be a bit smaller, the mane is noticeably shorter, and most have a long fold of skin along their belly that you don't often see on African lions.
Key paraphrase"a long fold of skin along their belly" = a fold of skin on the underside
Your practice
Paraphrase 3 — The Limited Gene Pool
Question wordingWhy do the Gir lions have a limited gene pool?
Audio meaningThe lecturer explains that practically every lion alive today is descended from a small group that a wealthy prince took under his wing and protected.
Key paraphrase"took under his wing" = took a special interest in / protected
Your practice
Paraphrase 4 — When Lions Attack Humans
Question wordingLions sometimes attack humans, especially during ______
Audio meaningThe lecturer notes that attacks tend to happen more often when water is scarce, during dry spells.
Key paraphrase"when water is scarce, during dry spells" = during times of drought
