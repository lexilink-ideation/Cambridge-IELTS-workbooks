📋 Quick Review

This passage asks whether animals dream. It explains REM and non-REM sleep in humans, then reviews evidence from rats, pigeons, whales and dolphins, and octopuses (Heidi and Costello). It ends by noting how hard it is to know what an animal’s inner dream experience is really like.

💡 Reading strategy tip: For table/summary completion, find the animal in the passage first, then read around it for the exact word. For True/False/Not Given, watch for strong words like "never" or "now possible" — the passage often says the opposite or says nothing.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"During this stage our eyes move, even though our eyelids are closed."
Main Clauseour eyes move
Subordinate / Relative Clauseeven though our eyelids are closed (concession)
Grammar Note"even though" = although. It concedes a surprising contrast (eyes move but eyelids shut).
Simple MeaningIn REM sleep the eyes move around although the eyelids stay shut.
中文在这个阶段，即使眼睑闭着，我们的眼睛也会转动。
"They found the patterns were very similar and concluded that the sleeping rats were dreaming about going through the maze."
Main ClauseThey found the patterns were very similar and concluded that …
Subordinate / Relative Clausethat the sleeping rats were dreaming about going through the maze (noun clause)
Grammar NoteTwo reporting verbs ("found", "concluded") each take a that-clause (the second "that" is written).
Simple MeaningThe brain patterns matched, so researchers decided the rats were dreaming about the maze.
中文他们发现脑波非常相似，于是得出结论：睡着的老鼠正梦见自己穿过迷宫。
"These include whales and dolphins, which do not shut down their entire brain when they sleep, but only half of it, keeping the rest awake."
Main ClauseThese include whales and dolphins
Subordinate / Relative Clausewhich do not shut down their entire brain … but only half of it (relative clause) + "keeping the rest awake" (participle)
Grammar Note"which" adds extra information. "not … but …" contrasts whole vs. half. "keeping …" is a participle of result.
Simple MeaningWhales and dolphins only put half their brain to sleep and keep the other half awake.
中文其中包括鲸鱼和海豚，它们睡觉时不会让整个大脑休眠，而只休眠一半，另一半保持清醒。
"We are quick to interpret the twitching limbs and quiet barks of sleeping dogs, but the truth is that we don’t know if there is an internal experience of chasing rabbits that comes along with that."
Main ClauseWe are quick to interpret the twitching limbs and quiet barks of sleeping dogs
Subordinate / Relative Clausebut … we don’t know if there is an internal experience … that comes along with that
Grammar Note"but" contrasts our quick assumption with the honest doubt. "if" introduces an indirect question; "that comes along with" is a relative clause.
Simple MeaningWe rush to explain a sleeping dog’s movements, but we really can’t tell whether it actually feels it is chasing rabbits.
中文我们很快就去解读睡狗抽动的四肢和轻声吠叫，但事实是，我们并不知道是否真有"追兔子"的内在体验伴随其中。
"And if this is the case, it is possible that better understanding of these purposes might shed light on the true purpose of our own dreams."
Main Clauseit is possible that … might shed light on the true purpose of our own dreams
Subordinate / Relative Clauseif this is the case (conditional)
Grammar Note"if this is the case" = if that is true. "might shed light on" = might help explain (tentative modal).
Simple MeaningIf that is true, understanding why animals dream could help explain why we dream too.
中文如果情况如此，那么更好地理解这些目的，或许能揭示我们自己做梦的真正目的。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Follow how the writer builds and limits the argument about animal dreams.

Contrast

The writer repeatedly contrasts REM vs non-REM sleep, and mammals vs non-mammals. Signal words: in contrast, but, however, that said. Whales/dolphins are a key contrast case (no REM sleep).

Cause & Effect

Because REM sleep leaves animals more vulnerable to temperature, whales and dolphins avoid it. Because dreams replay events, they help integrate memories. Trace each because → so.

Evidence & Example

Each claim is supported by a study or example: rats in a maze, pigeons’ visual brain regions, the octopuses Heidi and Costello. Learn to separate the claim from the example.

Hedging / Uncertainty

The writer is careful: "may also dream", "unlikely to be universal", "we don’t know", "it is possible". These hedges are essential for choosing NOT GIVEN over TRUE/FALSE.
