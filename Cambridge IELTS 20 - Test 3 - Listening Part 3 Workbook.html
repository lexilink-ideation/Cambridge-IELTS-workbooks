Listening Part 3

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 3 — Listening Part 3

Theatre Programmes Project · Multiple Choice + Matching (Q21–30)

📋 Quick Review

Two students, Maya and Finn, are comparing notes on a joint project about theatre programmes (called playbills in the USA). They discuss how programmes are produced today, what happened in early British theatre, how eighteenth- and nineteenth-century programmes differed, and what changed during and after World War II. Finally they look at pictures of four specific programmes for their slides. Questions 21–26 are 3-option multiple choice; questions 27–30 are matching (four shows, six possible comments A–F).

Who is speaking? Two university students — Maya and Finn — planning a joint presentation. They agree far more often than they disagree, so the exam tests whether you notice who introduces each idea and whether the partner confirms it (“That’s right”) or softens it.

The situation 情境: Maya 和 Finn 在讨论他们关于「剧院节目单 theatre programmes（美国叫 playbills）」的小组作业：现在的节目单由公司制作并靠广告赚钱；早期英国剧院的节目单相当于一份「合同」；18 世纪和 19 世纪的节目单各有优点；二战使英国节目单缩成一张对折的纸。最后他们挑选四份节目单的图片放进 PPT（Q27–30）。

Task type: Q21–26 multiple choice, A / B / C. Q27–30 matching: four shows, six comments A–F — choose FOUR, so two letters are never used and no letter is repeated.

💡 Listening strategy tip: Part 3 multiple choice is really a test of who thinks what and how strongly. Mark the stem verbs before you listen: was pleased, was surprised, feels… should include, both think, doesn’t fully understand. Then listen for the matching attitude signal in the audio — “I’m glad”, “I hadn’t realised”, “we should definitely include”, “that’s right”, “what I don’t really get is…”. For the matching task (Q27–30), read all six options first and reduce each to one keyword: controversial / a country / audiences / a project / insights / artwork. The four shows come in the order printed, and two options are never used. 先把六个选项各压缩成一个关键词，再听。
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • Q21 — all three options are touched in three consecutive clauses. He struggled to find useful websites (kills C), the topic is one of the module convenor’s research areas (kills A), and “no one else on the course is doing the same as us” is what he is “glad” about → B.
  • Q22 — the mistaken belief is the OPPOSITE of what is said. “Contrary to what many people think, theatres don’t hire people to do the programmes.” So the wrong belief is that theatres pay companies → A. Advertising is mentioned, but never as “more than previously”.
  • Q23 — “surprised” is signalled by “I hadn’t realised”. The famous cast and the huge crowds are shared background. The new information is that naming an actor created an agreement with the audience that could be broken → C (a kind of contract).
  • Q24 — touring and outdoor streets are both in the audio. The company “arrived in a town” (touring) and paraded “around the streets” (outdoors) — but the point Maya wants explained is why they had to advertise themselves: most people were illiterate → A (promoted their own plays).
  • Q25 — “both think” needs two voices. Colour and variety of designs belong to the nineteenth century. Maya praises eighteenth-century programmes because “they told the theatre-goers so many things” and Finn adds writer, plot and history → C (more informative).
  • Q27–30 — two comments are never used. Nothing in the discussion is called controversial (A) and nothing is said to have attracted audiences (C). If you are tempted by them, you have matched a word rather than a meaning.

playbilln.the American word for a theatre programme（美）演出节目单“I didn’t know theatre programmes are called playbills in the USA.”UK programme = US playbill / program. The word returns in Q26, where the USA is contrasted with the UK.

hirev.to pay someone to do a job for you雇用“Theatres don’t hire people to do the programmes.”Paraphrase of ‘pay companies to produce them’ in option A of Q22.
rightsn. (pl.)legal permission to publish or use something版权；出版权“Companies buy the rights to publish programmes on the theatre’s behalf.”Money flows the other way from what most people assume — that reversal is the whole answer.
on behalf ofphr.instead of, or representing, someone else代表；代替“…to publish programmes on the theatre’s behalf.”Very common in Listening Part 3 and in work contexts. Note the fixed preposition on.
advertising spacen.room in a publication that businesses pay to use广告版面“…then make their money selling advertising space within the programme booklet.”Option C in Q22 twists this into ‘far more adverts than previously’ — a comparison that is never made.
castn.all the actors in a play or film演员阵容“It said that the cast was always very important.”Comes back in Q28: the articles reveal ‘the thoughts and feelings of the cast’.
draw a crowdv. phr.to attract a large number of people吸引大批观众“Big names would draw huge crowds.”Shared background, not the answer to Q23. Careful — it looks like option C in the matching task (‘effective at attracting audiences’), which is never used.
accuse (sb of sth)v.to say that someone has done something wrong指责；控告“People accused the theatre of breaking their agreement with the audience.”Fixed pattern: accuse someone of + -ing.
agreementn.an arrangement that both sides promise to keep协议；约定“…breaking their agreement with the audience.”Answer to Q23 (C). agreement = a kind of contract. The audio never uses the word ‘contract’.
refundn.money given back to a customer退款“They would demand refunds, and if they didn’t get them, there were riots.”Stress falls on the first syllable for the noun (RE-fund), the second for the verb (re-FUND).
riotn.violent public disorder by a crowd骚乱；暴动“If they didn’t get them, there were riots.”The extreme reaction is what proves the programme functioned as a binding promise (Q23).
illiterateadj.unable to read or write不识字的“Lots of ordinary people at that time were illiterate.”The Q24 cause. If people cannot read, a printed programme cannot advertise — so actors advertised in person.
of limited valuephr.not very useful作用有限“Theatre programmes were of limited value in advertising plays.”A formal way of saying ‘not much use’. Notice it is a criticism, not a compliment.
paradev.to walk in a group through the streets so people notice you游行；招摇过市“They’d parade around the streets in their costumes, beating drums.”This is the act of self-promotion that makes Q24 answer A, even though the streets are outdoors (option B).
upcomingadj.happening soon即将到来的“…announcing their upcoming performances.”Synonym family: forthcoming / future / coming. ‘Announcing upcoming performances’ = promoting their own plays.
resemblev.to look like or be similar to类似于；像“Wasn’t it in the 19th century that programmes started to resemble programmes today?”Also the key verb in matching option F (‘It resembles an artwork’) for Q27.
superioradj.better than something else更好的；更优越的“I think 18th-century programmes were superior.”Q25 signal. A comparative judgement word — the reason for it comes immediately after ‘because’.
plotn.the story of a play, novel or film剧情；情节“And about the writer, the plot, and sometimes the history of the play.”Finn’s list is what makes the answer ‘both think’ — he is adding to Maya’s point about information.
undergov.to experience a change or process经历（变化）“The dramatic change they underwent during World War II.”Irregular: undergo / underwent / undergone. Formal academic verb — useful in Writing too.
impose restrictions onv. phr.to officially limit the use of something对……施加限制“When the government imposed restrictions on the use of paper.”Sets up the contrast in Q26: restrictions in the UK only, not in the USA.
shortagen.a situation where there is not enough of something短缺“I know there were paper shortages after the war, but only for 5 or 10 years.”The numbers are the puzzle: shortages lasted 5–10 years, but nothing changed for over 25 — hence Q26.
decorativeadj.attractive to look at; used as decoration装饰性的；好看的“The programme looks very decorative. Good enough to put in a frame on the wall.”Q27 (F). decorative + beautiful images + frame on the wall = resembles an artwork.
insightn.a clear or deep understanding of something洞见；深入的了解“…so we can learn how the production was created and the thoughts and feelings of the cast.”Q28 (E). The word ‘insight’ is never said — you must build it from ‘learn how…’ + ‘thoughts and feelings’.
survivingadj.still existing after a long time尚存的；留存下来的“It’s said to be the earliest surviving document printed on Australia’s first printing press.”Q29 (B). ‘Earliest surviving’ + a named country = historically significant for a country.
digitisedadj.converted into computer files数字化的“It comes from the British Library’s digitised collection of programmes.”Q30 (D). ‘Started a few years ago’ = recent; a library digitisation programme = a project.
comprisev.to consist of; to be made up of包含；由……构成“It already comprises over 200,000 programmes.”Formal synonym of ‘contain’ / ‘include’. Never say ‘is comprised of’ in careful writing.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening the audio never uses the exact words printed on the question paper. Recognising paraphrase is the whole skill. 雅思听力中，录音几乎不会重复题干原词——识别同义替换才是关键。
Paraphrase Example 1
Question wording“Finn was pleased to discover that their topic…”
What you hear“Even though I struggled to find many useful websites, I’m glad we picked this subject. No one else on the course is doing the same as us. Although it is one of the research areas of the module convenor.”
Key paraphrasepleased = I’m glad. The two “concession” words — Even though and Although — wrap the two wrong options around the right one.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question wording“A mistaken belief about theatre programmes is that…”
What you hear“Contrary to what many people think, theatres don’t hire people to do the programmes. In fact, companies buy the rights to publish programmes on the theatre’s behalf.”
Key paraphrasea mistaken belief = contrary to what many people think. Then INVERT: the false idea is that theatres DO hire (= pay) companies.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question wording“Programmes were seen as a kind of contract”
What you hear“I hadn’t realised that if the programme named a famous actor, that’s who the public expected to perform. And if that didn’t happen, people accused the theatre of breaking their agreement with the audience.”
Key paraphrasecontract = agreement that can be broken. The word ‘contract’ is never spoken — you build it from expected + breaking their agreement + refunds.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question wording“Why companies of actors promoted their own plays”
What you hear“Lots of ordinary people at that time were illiterate, so theatre programmes were of limited value in advertising plays. When a company of actors arrived in a town, they’d parade around the streets in their costumes, beating drums and announcing their upcoming performances.”
Key paraphrasepromote = advertise / announce upcoming performances. ‘Arrived in a town’ (touring) and ‘the streets’ (outdoors) are true details but not the point being explained.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 5
Question wording“Compared to nineteenth-century programmes, eighteenth-century ones were more informative”
What you hearMaya: “Unlike programmes from the 18th century, they always used colour and there was a greater variety of designs. But personally I think 18th-century programmes were superior because they told the theatre-goers so many things, including about the actors.” Finn: “And about the writer, the plot, and sometimes the history of the play.”
Key paraphrasemore informative = told them so many things. ‘Unlike’ assigns colour and design variety to the NINETEENTH century, so A and B describe the wrong century.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 6
Question wording“Maya doesn’t fully understand why…”
What you hear“What I don’t really get is that after the war, they didn’t go back to being more than one sheet or change in any way for over 25 years. I know there were paper shortages after the war, but only for 5 or 10 years.”
Key paraphrasedoesn’t fully understand = what I don’t really get. The clause after ‘I know’ is the part she DOES understand — never the answer.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 7
Question wordingQ27–Q30 — four shows, four comments
What you hearRuy Blas: “very decorative… good enough to put in a frame on the wall” → F. Man of La Mancha: “not because of the pictures, but because it contains articles… how the production was created” → E. Jane Shore: “the earliest surviving document printed on Australia’s first printing press” → B. The Sailors’ Festival: “the British Library’s digitised collection… started a few years ago” → D.
Key paraphraseCompress each option to one keyword: artwork / insights / a country / a recent project. Note “not because of the pictures” — the speaker actively blocks option F for the second show.

Review Quiz

Q1
B. a playbill
“I didn’t know theatre programmes are called playbills in the USA.” The word matters again in Q26, where the UK and USA are contrasted.
Q2
C. He struggled to find many useful websites.
“Even though I struggled to find many useful websites…” — that is the exact opposite of ‘not difficult to research’.
Q3
B. They sell advertising space in the booklet.
They buy the rights to publish, then “make their money selling advertising space within the programme booklet”. Option A is the mistaken belief in Q22.
Q4
B. A named star did not appear and refunds were refused.
“They would demand refunds, and if they didn’t get them, there were riots.” This is the evidence that the programme worked like a contract (Q23).
Q5
C. New information — often the answer to a ‘was surprised’ question.
Attitude verbs in the stem (surprised, pleased) have matching signals in the audio. Train yourself to hear them.
Q6
B. Many ordinary people could not read.
“Lots of ordinary people at that time were illiterate. So theatre programmes were of limited value in advertising plays.” This is the cause behind Q24.
