Motivating Employees under Adverse Conditions · Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

This is a business/management passage about how managers can keep employees motivated when an organisation is shrinking or in decline. It presents "Six Key Points" — from matching people to jobs, through goal-setting, rewards, and fairness in reward distribution — and ends with a study comparing what clerical and production workers each value most.

Unlike Passage 1, this text uses explicit headings ("Key Point One" to "Key Point Six"), making it a strong example for the "matching headings" question type, which tests whether you understand each paragraph's main idea rather than just its keywords.

💡 Reading strategy tip: For matching-headings questions (Q14–18), read the FIRST sentence of each Key Point carefully — it usually states the main idea directly. Beware of headings that use similar-sounding words to a paragraph's content but don't match its actual argument.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"When an organisation is shrinking, the best and most mobile workers are prone to leave voluntarily... those with the highest skills and experience."
Main Clausethe best and most mobile workers are prone to leave voluntarily
Subordinate Clause"When an organisation is shrinking" (time/condition clause)
Grammar Note"prone to" + infinitive expresses tendency; the sentence sets up a cause (shrinking) and its likely effect (skilled staff leaving)
Simple MeaningIn a declining company, the most skilled and employable staff are the ones most likely to quit by choice.
中文当一个组织正在萎缩时，最优秀、流动性最强的员工往往会主动离职——他们正是技能和经验最丰富的人。
"If the job to be filled is a managerial post in a large bureaucratic organisation, a candidate who has a high need for power and a low need for affiliation should be selected."
Main Clausea candidate...should be selected
Subordinate Clause"If the job to be filled is a managerial post" (condition clause)  +  "who has a high need for power and a low need for affiliation" (relative clause modifying "candidate")
Grammar NotePassive voice ("should be selected") emphasises the process, not the selector; relative clause adds two specific personality criteria
Simple MeaningFor a management role in a big, formal company, choose someone who wants power more than social closeness.
中文如果要填补的是大型官僚组织中的管理职位，应该选择权力需求高、归属需求低的候选人。
"If participation and the culture are incongruous, employees are likely to perceive the participation process as manipulative and be negatively affected by it."
Main Clauseemployees are likely to perceive the participation process as manipulative and be negatively affected by it
Subordinate Clause"If participation and the culture are incongruous" (condition clause)
Grammar NoteTwo parallel predicates ("perceive...as" and "be...affected") share the same subject — compact academic sentence structure
Simple MeaningIf asking employees to participate doesn't fit the company culture, they may feel it's a trick rather than genuine involvement.
中文如果参与方式与企业文化不协调，员工可能会认为这种参与过程带有操纵性，并因此产生负面情绪。
"Since employees have different needs, what acts as a reinforcement for one may not for another."
Main Clausewhat acts as a reinforcement for one may not for another
Subordinate Clause"Since employees have different needs" (reason clause)  +  "what acts as a reinforcement for one" (nominal relative clause as subject)
Grammar NoteEllipsis: "may not [act as a reinforcement] for another" — the repeated verb phrase is omitted for economy, a common academic writing device
Simple MeaningBecause people want different things, a reward that motivates one employee may not motivate another.
中文由于员工的需求各不相同，对一个人有强化作用的东西，对另一个人未必有同样效果。
"Such findings suggest that one person's equity is another's inequity, so an ideal should probably weigh different inputs and outcomes according to employee group."
Main ClauseSuch findings suggest [that clause]; ...an ideal should probably weigh different inputs and outcomes
Subordinate Clause"that one person's equity is another's inequity" (noun clause, object of "suggest")  +  "so...according to employee group" (result clause)
Grammar Note"so" introduces a logical consequence drawn from the preceding finding — typical of academic argument structure (finding → implication)
Simple MeaningWhat feels fair to one group may feel unfair to another, so an ideal reward system should value things differently for different groups.
中文这些发现表明，一个人的公平可能是另一个人的不公平，因此理想的做法应该根据员工群体的不同来权衡投入与产出。
"Regardless of whether goals are achievable or well within management's perceptions of the employee's ability, if employees see them as unachievable they will reduce their effort."
Main Clausethey will reduce their effort
Subordinate Clause"Regardless of whether goals are achievable..." (concession clause)  +  "if employees see them as unachievable" (condition clause)
Grammar Note"Regardless of whether" introduces a concession — the objective truth doesn't matter; only employee perception drives the outcome
Simple MeaningEven if a goal is genuinely achievable, employees will try less hard if THEY believe it's impossible.
中文无论目标客观上是否可以实现，如果员工认为目标无法实现，他们就会减少努力。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect

Cause: an organisation shrinks and pay increases become rare or pay cuts occur. Effect: the most skilled, mobile employees leave voluntarily, while less mobile employees remain — worsening the company's talent pool.

Contrast

Key Point One contrasts high achievers (who need independence and challenge) with employees who prefer stability — not everyone is motivated by high independence, variety, and responsibility.

Comparison

The final study compares clerical workers (who rank "quality of work" and "job knowledge" highly) against production workers (who rank "intelligence" and "personal involvement" highly, and rate "advancement" much higher than clerical workers do).

Examples

Key Point Five gives concrete examples of increasing reward visibility: "openly communicating everyone's remuneration," "publicising performance bonuses," and "allocating annual salary increases in a lump sum."

Definition

"Equity" (Key Point Six) is defined through comparison: employees perceive fairness when their rewards/outcomes are proportional to their inputs (effort, skill, experience) relative to others.

Problem / Solution

Problem: employees who see goals as unachievable reduce effort. Solution: managers must ensure employees feel capable of reaching goals and view the appraisal process as valid (Key Point Three).

Chronology

Unlike Passage 1, this text is not chronological — it's organised thematically by six sequential "Key Points," each building logically on employee motivation theory.

Author's Attitude

The writer takes a neutral, analytical, academic stance — presenting management theory and evidence (e.g. the clerical/production worker study) without strong personal opinion, typical of a business-studies text.
