Listening Part 4

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 1 — Listening Part 4

Reclaiming Urban Rivers · Notes Completion (Q31–40)

📋 Quick Review

This is an academic lecture with one speaker and no interruptions. The talk explains how city rivers were once used for transport, fishing and pleasure, how industry and growing populations destroyed them, and how cities such as London, Los Angeles and Paris are now bringing their rivers back to life. It ends with a proposal to move passengers and, above all, parcel deliveries off the roads and back onto the water. You complete a set of notes with ONE WORD ONLY for each answer.

Who is speaking? A single lecturer giving a talk on urban planning and rivers. There is no second voice, so the only signposts you get are discourse markers: For example…, In addition…, But in recent years…, Of course…

The situation 情境: 一场关于「城市河流复兴」的讲座。结构完全按照题目上的三个小标题走：① 历史背景（河流原本用于交通、捕鱼、休闲；工业与人口增长带来污水和工厂污染，1957年泰晤士河被宣布「生物学意义上死亡」）；② 近期改善（海豹与鲸鱼回归、仓库改成餐厅与公寓、洛杉矶的河滨公园与艺术展示、巴黎夏季人造沙滩）；③ 交通设想（渡轮载客、货运驳船＋电动自行车、未来用无人机送货）。

Task type: Notes completion with bullet points under three headings. Instruction: ONE WORD ONLY — not ‘one word and/or a number’, so no figures are needed and no two-word answer is accepted.

💡 Listening strategy tip: The notes have three printed headings — Historical background, Recent improvements, Transport possibilities — and the lecture follows them in that exact order. Use the headings as checkpoints: if you hear Paris and you are still looking for Q33, you have missed one, so jump forward immediately. Then use the bullet layout: each bullet is one idea, and the answer is almost always a concrete noun (a building, an animal, a vehicle, a place). Because the instruction is ONE WORD ONLY, an answer like ‘cargo bikes’ or ‘electric bikes’ scores zero — write bikes. Check the grammar of the printed line too: ‘(37) are created’ demands a plural.
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • Q31 — sewage vs factories. The bullet directly above already says ‘sewage from houses’, so ‘sewage’ cannot be the answer. The gap needs the source of the other pollution: factories built beside the river. ‘Waste materials’ is what they discharge, not what stands on the bank.
  • Q32 — word class shift. The speaker says ‘the Thames was dead in biological terms’; the notes print ‘declared biologically ___’. The adverb has moved, so you write the adjective dead, not ‘biological’.
  • Q33 — seals is already printed. The line says ‘Seals and even a (33)…’, so writing ‘seals’ scores zero. ‘A’ also tells you the answer is a singular countable noun: whale. The fact that the rescue failed is irrelevant.
  • Q37 — the plural is forced by the verb. ‘(37) are created’ needs beaches, not ‘beach’. Deck chairs, palm trees and drinks are all mentioned in the same sentence as decoration.
  • Q38 — a lot of numbers around the answer. ‘2 billion’ is already printed; you need the vehicle. Istanbul, San Francisco and New York are examples, not answers. Write the singular ferry after ‘travel by’.
  • Q39–40 — ONE WORD ONLY. The audio says ‘cargo bikes, electric ones’ and ‘carried out by drone’. Since ‘electric’ is printed, write bikes; and write drone even though the idea sounds plural.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening the audio never uses the exact words printed on the question paper. Recognising paraphrase is the whole skill. 雅思听力中，录音几乎不会重复题干原词——识别同义替换才是关键。
Paraphrase Example 1
Question wording“Pollution from (31) on the river bank”
What you hear“In addition, other types of pollution increased as factories were built beside the river and discharged their waste materials into the water.”
Key paraphrasebuilt beside the river = on the river bank. ‘Other types of pollution’ tells you this is NOT the sewage already printed above.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question wording“Declared biologically (32)”
What you hear“Scientists at London’s Natural History Museum declared that the Thames was dead in biological terms, as the water was too filthy to support any kind of life.”
Key paraphrasein biological terms → biologically; the gap takes the adjective dead
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question wording“Seals and even a (33) have been seen”
What you hear“You can see seals swimming in the water. And recently, people had to try to rescue a whale which had got lost and swum up the river from the sea by mistake.”
Key paraphraseThe notes report what has been SEEN; the audio tells a rescue story. The rescue failed, but the whale was still there.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question wording“Warehouses are converted to restaurants and (34)”
What you hear“Warehouses that were once used for storing goods are now being converted into expensive restaurants and also into apartments with river views.”
Key paraphraseThe paper compresses ‘converted into… and also into…’ into ‘restaurants and ___’. Listen past the first item.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 5
Question wording“Display (36) projects”
What you hear“It’s been proposed that facilities could be provided for displaying projects related to various kinds of art that have been produced by local people.”
Key paraphraseThe audio wraps the answer inside a long noun phrase; the notes strip it to two words. Hunt for the head noun.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 6
Question wording“(37) are created on the sides of the river every summer”
What you hear“In Paris during the summer months of July and August… the banks are transformed into beaches where people can relax in deck chairs.”
Key paraphrasethe banks = the sides of the river; transformed into = are created. ‘Are’ forces the plural beaches.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 7
Question wording“electric (39) or, in future, by (40)”
What you hear“The parcels could be taken the last few kilometres to their final destination on cargo bikes, electric ones… and in future, the final stage could even be carried out by drone, although at present this isn’t allowed.”
Key paraphraseONE WORD ONLY: bikes (electric is printed) and drone (singular after ‘by’)
Your practice

Review Quiz

Q1
B. As the quickest way to cross the city, and for fishing and pleasure trips.
Three uses match the printed note ‘transport, fishing and recreation’. ‘Boat trips just for pleasure’ = recreation.
Q2
C. The number of inhabitants used to be very small.
‘Rivers had always been used for this purpose, but when the number of inhabitants was so small, that wasn’t such a problem.’ The change came when cities passed a million people.
Q3
B. It is already printed in the bullet point above the gap.
Always read the lines around the gap. Information already given cannot be the answer — the gap needs the other source of pollution: factories.
Q4
A. That the Thames was dead in biological terms.
The water was ‘too filthy to support any kind of life’. On the paper this becomes ‘declared biologically dead’ (Q32).
Q5
B. The rescue failed, but the cause was disorientation, not pollution.
‘Unfortunately, they didn’t succeed, but the problem was disorientation rather than the quality of the water.’ The answer to Q33 is still whale.
Q6
B. They are extremely expensive because river views are in great demand.
‘Astronomical’ = extremely high. Note that the answer to Q34 is the plain word apartments, not the descriptive language around it.
Q7
B. Facilities for sports and a natural environment for relaxing.
The architect wants to ‘revitalise the banks… and make a park there’ (Q35). The art displays (Q36) are a separate, additional proposal.
Q8
A. Traffic is banned and the banks become beaches.
Deck chairs, potted palm trees and sunbathing are decoration around the one-word answer beaches (Q37), which must be plural because the notes say ‘are created’.
Q9
B. It is not fast — but neither is a car stuck in traffic.
‘Admittedly’ introduces a concession. Numbers are actually ‘set to rise further’, and Istanbul, San Francisco and New York are just examples.
