📋 Passage Overview

What is exploration? A reflective essay in which the writer asks what real exploration means today. He weighs the views of several famous explorers — Peter Fleming, Ran Fiennes, Chris Bonington, Robin Hanbury-Tenison and Wilfred Thesiger — on whether exploration is about self-discovery, usefulness to others, or reaching untouched places, before giving his own view. Question types include multiple choice (Q27–32), matching explorers to statements (Q33–37) and summary completion (Q38–40).

💡 Reading tip: In 'match the person' tasks, the names are scattered through the passage and each may be used more than once. Underline each explorer's name and read only the sentences around it. Watch for opinion words ('argues', 'believes', 'in his view').

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

For Peter Fleming, who travelled through remote parts of Brazil and Asia, the means of transport mattered as much as the destination itself.
Main Clausethe means of transport mattered as much as the destination itself
Subordinate / Relative'who travelled through remote parts of Brazil and Asia' = non-defining relative clause adding information about Fleming
Grammar NoteA subject ('For Peter Fleming') + a non-defining relative clause + 'as much as' comparison.
Simple MeaningPeter Fleming, who explored remote Brazil and Asia, thought how you travel is as important as where you go.
中文对于曾游历巴西和亚洲偏远地区的彼得·弗莱明来说,交通方式与目的地本身同样重要。
Wilfred Thesiger, by contrast, insisted that exploration should serve the people among whom one travels, rather than simply satisfy the traveller's curiosity.
Main ClauseWilfred Thesiger … insisted that exploration should serve the people …
Subordinate / Relative'by contrast' = discourse marker; 'that exploration should serve the people among whom one travels' = noun clause; 'rather than simply satisfy…' = contrast
Grammar NoteReporting verb 'insisted' + that-clause; 'rather than' contrasts two purposes.
Simple MeaningThesiger, on the other hand, said exploration should help the local people, not just satisfy the explorer.
中文相比之下,威尔弗雷德·塞西格坚持认为,探索应当造福所到之处的人们,而不只是满足旅行者的好奇心。
Although some argue that self-discovery lies at the heart of every journey, the writer suggests that this inward focus is not essential to exploration.
Main Clausethe writer suggests that this inward focus is not essential to exploration
Subordinate / Relative'Although some argue that self-discovery lies at the heart of every journey' = concession clause containing its own that-clause
Grammar NoteConcession ('Although…') + main clause with a reporting verb and that-clause.
Simple MeaningSome say every journey is really about self-discovery, but the writer thinks that isn't essential to exploration.
中文尽管有人认为自我发现是每次旅程的核心,作者却认为这种向内的关注对探索来说并非必需。
In his own view, there is little need for further exploration of the Earth's land surface, except to answer specific scientific questions such as how buffalo feed.
Main Clausethere is little need for further exploration of the Earth's land surface
Subordinate / Relative'In his own view' = viewpoint phrase; 'except to answer specific scientific questions such as how buffalo feed' = exception with an embedded question ('how buffalo feed')
Grammar Note'there is little need' + an 'except to…' exception containing an indirect question.
Simple MeaningHe thinks we hardly need to explore more of Earth's land, except to answer precise science questions like how buffalo eat.
中文在他看来,除了回答诸如水牛如何进食这类具体的科学问题外,已几乎不需要再对地球陆地表面进行探索。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Compare and contrast. The essay lines up several explorers with different definitions of exploration. Markers like 'by contrast', 'similarly', 'in his own view' tell you whose opinion is being given — essential for the matching task (Q33–37).

Opinion vs fact. Passage 3 is largely about views and definitions, so the multiple-choice questions test attitude. Read the sentences around each name for verbs of opinion ('argues', 'insisted', 'believes').

Concession then position. The writer often states others' views ('Although some argue…') before giving his own ('the writer suggests…'). The summary (Q38–40) captures his personal position, so read the final paragraphs carefully.
