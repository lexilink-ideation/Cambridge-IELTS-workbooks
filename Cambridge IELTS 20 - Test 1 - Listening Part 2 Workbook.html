Listening Part 2

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 1 — Listening Part 2

Edelman Pottery Talk · Multiple Choice + Multiple Answer (Q11–20)

📋 Quick Review

This is a monologue: a professional potter called Heather McCallum welcomes a group of visitors to Edelman Pottery and talks to them before they go into the workshop. In the first half she explains why pottery matters to archaeologists, why people take her classes and what visitors should wear; in the second half she walks them round the workshop and describes the three things you need — a kiln, a potter’s wheel and tools. There are six single-answer multiple-choice questions and two “choose TWO letters” questions.

Who is speaking? One speaker only: Heather McCallum, a professional potter at Edelman Pottery. She is giving a short introductory talk to visitors who are about to make a pot for the first time.

The situation 情境: 陶艺师 Heather 在参观者进入工作室前做介绍。前半部分讲陶器为什么能保存下来、考古学家能从中看出什么、学员来上课的动机、以及她自己最喜欢陶艺的哪一点；后半部分带大家看工作室里的三样东西：窑（kiln）、拉坯机（potter’s wheel）和工具（tools）。

Task type: Q11–16 = multiple choice, choose A, B or C (one answer each). Q17–18 and Q19–20 = choose TWO letters, A–E. In the two-letter questions the order does not matter, and you get one mark per correct letter.

💡 Listening strategy tip: For 3-option multiple choice, underline the difference between A, B and C before you listen — here the three options are usually all mentioned, but only one answers the actual question. Q14 is the clearest example: calming, messy and physical benefits are all in the recording, but the question asks what Heather values most, so you must wait for the signal ‘what I love most is…’. For the two multiple-answer questions (17–18 and 19–20) do the opposite: instead of hunting for the right letters, cross out the ones she contradicts. Heather often says the opposite of an option (‘odd names you may find hard to remember’ kills ‘memorable names’; ‘I wouldn’t recommend spending money on them’ kills ‘worth buying’).
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • Q11 — “stands the test of time” is an idiom, not the answer. The answer comes from the next sentence: baskets and pictures don’t survive in the earth the way pots do.
  • Q12 — two different things are read from a pot. The clay and the tool scratches tell you where a pot came from; the impressions tell you what it was used for. The question asks about USE, so ‘the clay it was made with’ is a trap.
  • Q14 — all three options appear. ‘Messy’ is why it is fun for children; ‘good exercise for hands and wrists’ is a benefit for the elderly. Only the concentration is what she loves most.
  • Q16 — aprons vs clothes vs jewellery. All three are said, but only jewellery is something she reminds them of (‘as we said in our email’). Aprons are provided, and she only hopes their clothes are old.
  • Q17–18 — “since their invention” is not a date. She never says when kilns were invented, and she says a kiln is hard to keep in a house rather than explaining where to put one.
  • Q19–20 — negative wording. ‘Odd names that you may find hard to remember’ is the OPPOSITE of ‘memorable names’, and ‘I wouldn’t recommend spending money on them’ is the OPPOSITE of ‘worth buying’.

artefactn.an object made by a person, especially an old one studied by historians人工制品；文物“The shape of an artefact may have been lost.”British spelling artefact, American artifact. Both accepted, but be consistent.

impressionn.a mark left on a soft surface when something is pressed onto it压痕；印记“Archaeologists can tell whether the pots were for storage or cooking by examining the impressions on the clay.”Key to Q12. ‘Impressions’ + ‘scratches’ = ‘the marks that are on it’. Note the other meaning of impression (= an opinion) is NOT used here.
scratchn.a thin mark cut into a surface by something sharp划痕“The scratches from tools… can reveal where the pots came from.”Part of the same paraphrase family as impressions → marks (Q12).
revealv.to show or make known something that was hidden揭示；显示“The clay itself can reveal where the pots came from.”Listening synonym of show / tell you / indicate. Often introduces a fact you must catch.
ancestorn.a member of your family who lived a long time ago祖先“Like our ancestors, they hope that something they create will also last longer than they do.”The sentence right before the Q13 answer — it tells you the topic has moved to students’ motives.
outlivev.to live or last longer than someone or something else比……活得久；比……长久“Something they create will also last longer than they do.”Key to Q13. The question paper prints outlive them; the audio says last longer than they do and after their death.

stressn.worry and pressure caused by a difficult situation压力“That focus takes you away from the stresses of everyday life.”Escaping stress = a calming effect (Q14 option A). This is the paraphrase you must make.
arthritisn.a painful illness that makes the joints stiff and swollen关节炎“It’s also good exercise for hands and wrists and helps with arthritis.”This is a real physical benefit — but it applies to elderly people, so it is the Q14 distractor (option C), not the answer.
messyadj.dirty and untidy脏乱的；弄得一团糟的“It’s a fun activity for children because it’s so messy.”Q14 distractor (option B): messy is why it is fun for children.
apronn.a piece of clothing worn over the front to keep your clothes clean围裙“We’ll provide aprons later.”Q16 distractor (option A): the pottery provides them, so visitors are not asked to put them on.
jewelleryn.rings, necklaces, watches and similar decorative objects首饰；珠宝“Please remove any watches, necklaces, etc.”Key to Q16. The audio lists examples (watches, necklaces); the question paper uses the category word jewellery. UK spelling has double L.
kilnn.a very hot oven used for baking clay so that it becomes hard窑；陶窑“A kiln, which is basically a very hot oven where you fire the pottery.”Pronounced /kIln/ — the N is often almost silent, which is why it sounds like ‘kil’. Subject of Q17–18.
firev.to bake clay objects in a kiln to make them hard烧制（陶器）“A very hot oven where you fire the pottery.”A technical verb: nothing to do with dismissing someone or with flames here.
permanentlyadv.for ever; in a way that will not change永久地“This allows anything you’ve made to set permanently in shape.”Part of the kiln’s FUNCTION (Q17–18 answer A): remove water → set permanently.
domesticadj.used in the home; not industrial家用的“Domestic ovens don’t really get hot enough.”Supports Q17–18 answer E: what people use instead of a kiln.
crackv.to break so that a line appears on the surface开裂“Eventually the clay will crack and fall apart.”Collocation: crack and fall apart. A warning, not a safety procedure.
toxicadj.poisonous; harmful to health有毒的“Kilns release toxic compounds into the air.”She says you need to know about safety procedures — but she never explains them, so ‘ways of keeping them safe’ is NOT an answer.
revolutionizev.to change something completely and for the better彻底改变；使发生革命性变化“Its invention revolutionised the pottery industry.”Said about the potter’s wheel, not the kiln — do not carry it over to Q17–18.
sculptv.to shape a material such as clay or stone into a figure雕塑；塑形“Your hands can make all kinds of shapes and curves without relying on a sculpting tool.”Noun forms: sculpture (the object), sculptor (the person).
handlev.to touch, hold and control something with your hands处理；操作“There are some basic tools that you will need to handle the clay on the wheel.”Key to Q19–20 (C). ‘You will need’ = they are essential.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening the audio never uses the exact words printed on the question paper. Recognising paraphrase is the whole skill. 雅思听力中，录音几乎不会重复题干原词——识别同义替换才是关键。
Paraphrase Example 1
Question wording“Pottery lasts longer in the ground than other art forms.” (Q11 A)
What you hear“We know this because it stands the test of time. Things like baskets and pictures don’t survive in the earth in the same way that pots do.”
Key paraphrasesurvive in the earth = last in the ground; the comparison is made by saying other things do NOT do it
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question wording“Archaeologists identify the use of pottery from the marks that are on it.” (Q12 B)
What you hear“Archaeologists can tell whether the pots were for, say, storage or cooking by examining the impressions on the clay.”
Key paraphraseimpressions / scratches = marks; ‘whether pots were for storage or cooking’ = their USE
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question wording“They want to make something that will outlive them.” (Q13 C)
What you hear“They hope that something they create will also last longer than they do… might say something about humanity many years after their death.”
Key paraphraseoutlive somebody = last longer than they do = still exist after their death
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question wording“What she values most is its calming effect.” (Q14 A)
What you hear“As far as I’m concerned, what I love most is the concentration you need… That focus takes you away from the stresses of everyday life.”
Key paraphraseconcentration / focus + escaping stress = a calming effect. ‘What I love most’ answers ‘value MOST’.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 5
Question wording“Most visitors have never made a pot before.” (Q15 B)
What you hear“Like nearly everyone who comes here, I’m sure this is the first time you will have tried the art. So we’ll keep things simple today.”
Key paraphrasenearly everyone who comes here = most visitors; the first time you have tried it = never done it before
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 6
Question wording“They should take off their jewellery.” (Q16 C)
What you hear“As we said in our email, please remove any watches, necklaces, etc. and put them somewhere safe… We’ll provide aprons later.”
Key paraphrasewatches + necklaces = the category word jewellery; ‘as we said in our email’ = a reminder
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 7
Question wording“What some people use instead of a kiln.” (Q17–18 E)
What you hear“You may think, ‘Can’t I use my oven?’ Well, that’s possible, but domestic ovens don’t get hot enough… Some people fire pottery in a fire pit outside.”
Key paraphraseA rhetorical question plus ‘some people…’ = alternatives to the real thing

11
A
‘Things like baskets and pictures don’t survive in the earth in the same way that pots do.’ = it lasts longer in the ground. B and C are never claimed.
12
B
‘Archaeologists can tell whether the pots were for storage or cooking by examining the impressions on the clay.’ Impressions/scratches = marks. The clay reveals where pots came from (not use), and the shape ‘may have been lost’.
13
C
‘They hope that something they create will last longer than they do… many years after their death.’ = outlive them.
14
A
‘What I love most is the concentration… that focus takes you away from the stresses of everyday life.’ = a calming effect. Messiness is for children; exercise for hands and wrists is for elderly people.
15
B
‘Like nearly everyone who comes here, I’m sure this is the first time you will have tried the art.’
16
C
‘As we said in our email, please remove any watches, necklaces, etc.’ Watches and necklaces = jewellery, and ‘as we said in our email’ = a reminder. Aprons are provided; she only hopes their clothes are old.
17 & 18
A, E (in either order)
A (function): ‘a kiln removes the water from clay at temperatures of around 1,000°C. This allows anything you’ve made to set permanently in shape.’ E (alternatives): ‘Can’t I use my oven?… Some people fire pottery in a fire pit outside.’ She gives no invention date (B), does not explain safety methods (C), and says a kiln is hard to keep at home rather than where to put one (D).
19 & 20
C, E (in either order)
C (essential): ‘there are some basic tools that you will need to handle the clay on the wheel.’ E (provided): ‘We can provide these.’ A is wrong (the names, not the tools, are hard); B is contradicted by ‘I wouldn’t recommend spending money on them yet’; D is the opposite of ‘odd names… hard to remember’.

Review Quiz

Q1
B. Because pots survive in the earth when other objects do not.
‘It stands the test of time. Things like baskets and pictures don’t survive in the earth in the same way that pots do.’ That is Q11 answer A.
Q2
C. What the pot was used for.
‘Whether the pots were for, say, storage or cooking’ = the USE. The clay and the tool scratches tell you where they came from — that is the Q12 trap.
Q3
A. It rules out option C, the basic shape of it.
If the shape is often lost, the shape cannot be the reliable evidence the question asks about. Listen for options being eliminated, not only confirmed.
Q4
C. say something about humanity after they die.
‘Their work… might say something about humanity many years after their death.’ This is Q13 answer C: something that will outlive them.
Q5
B. It is good exercise for hands and wrists.
‘If you’re elderly, it’s also good exercise for hands and wrists and helps with arthritis.’ This is why ‘physical benefits’ is wrong for Q14 — it is not what SHE values most.
Q6
B. She says messiness makes pottery fun for children, not that she values it most.
Classic 3-option MCQ design: all three options are said, but the question adds a limit (‘value MOST’, and about HER). Match the whole question, not the keyword.
Q7
C. It is their first attempt at pottery.
‘Like nearly everyone who comes here, I’m sure this is the first time you will have tried the art.’ = Q15 answer B on the paper.
Q8
B. The pottery will provide them later.
‘We’ll provide aprons later.’ So option A of Q16 (‘put on their aprons’) is a distractor, even though the word is clearly heard.

Function: it removes water from clay at about 1,000°C so objects set permanently. Alternatives: a domestic oven or a fire pit outside. She says kilns have changed little “since their invention” but gives no date, and she says a kiln is hard to keep in a house rather than where to put one.
Q10
B. Because she tells you safety procedures exist but does not explain ways of keeping them safe.
The task word is explain. Naming a topic in one clause (‘you also need to know about safety procedures’) is not explaining it.
Q11

Direct opposite of Q19–20 option D. Also note ‘rather than go through them all now, I’ll just name a few as we go along’.
Q12
B. She says ‘we can provide these, and I wouldn’t recommend spending money on them yet’.
The same sentence gives you one correct letter (E: available for participants) and eliminates another (B). In multiple-answer questions, one sentence often does both jobs.
