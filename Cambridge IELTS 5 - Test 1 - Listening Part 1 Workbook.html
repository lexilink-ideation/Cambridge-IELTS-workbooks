📋 Quick Review

A customer phones the Dreamtime Travel Agency to ask about the "Whale Watch Experience" — a two-day coastal tour. The receptionist, Tracy, explains the transport, schedule, accommodation, what is included, and the booking process, then takes the caller's details.

This is a classic IELTS Listening Part 1 telephone enquiry: one speaker asks questions about a product or service, and the other gives factual details — dates, prices, percentages, and a reference code.

💡 Listening strategy tip: Part 1 answers usually appear in the same order as the questions on the page. Before you listen, underline the keywords in each gap (e.g. "transport," "cost," "deposit") — this tells you exactly what kind of word or number to listen for.

🔄 Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening, the audio never uses the exact words in the question. Recognising paraphrases is the key skill. Study each example below, then try your own.
Paraphrase Example 1 — Type of Transport
Question wordingType of transport used
Audio meaning"We use a minibus rather than a coach — it keeps things more personal for our customers."
Key paraphrase"rather than a coach" signals the answer is minibus, NOT coach
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2 — What's Included Free
Question wordingActivities included free of charge
Audio meaning"The guided bushwalk doesn't cost anything extra, and neither does using the table tennis tables — but the fishing trip is an additional twelve dollars."
Key paraphrase"doesn't cost anything extra" = included free; "additional" = extra cost
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3 — Current Price
Question wordingCost of the tour
Audio meaning"It's normally around three hundred dollars, but right now we've actually dropped it to two hundred and eighty."
Key paraphrase"dropped it to" = reduced the price to; always take the current, not the usual, price
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4 — Booking Deadline
Question wordingLatest date to make a booking
Audio meaning"You do need to get your booking in at least a fortnight before you travel."
Key paraphrase"a fortnight" = 14 days / two weeks
Your practice
