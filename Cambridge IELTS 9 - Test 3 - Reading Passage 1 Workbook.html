📋 Passage Overview

Attitudes to language. The passage explains why language arguments become heated, then contrasts two opposed schools: prescriptivism (one variety is inherently better and should be imposed) and descriptivism (the grammarian's job is to record usage, not judge it). It traces 18th-century grammar writing, quotes Joseph Priestley as an early descriptivist, and notes that today both sides caricature each other. Question types: Yes/No/Not Given (Q1–8), summary completion from a word list (Q9–12) and purpose multiple choice (Q13).

💡 Reading tip: This is a two-camp passage, so build a two-column table as you read: prescriptivists = rules, correct/incorrect, impose a standard versus descriptivists = describe, record diversity, custom of speaking. Q9–12 then fills itself in. Note Q7 carefully: descriptivism is dated to 'the second half of the 18th century', so the claim that it appeared only after that century is NO.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Arguments can start as easily over minor points of usage as over major policies of linguistic education.
Main ClauseArguments can start as easily over minor points of usage as over major policies.
Subordinate / Relative'as easily… as…' = comparative structure of equality.
Grammar Note'as + adv + as' says two very different things produce the same result — the small and the large matter equally.
Simple MeaningPeople argue just as fiercely about tiny usage details as about big education policies.
中文人们对细微用法问题的争论，可能与对重大语言教育政策的争论同样容易爆发。
No part of society or social behaviour is exempt: linguistic factors influence how we judge personality, intelligence, social status, educational standards, job aptitude, and many other areas of identity and social survival.
Main ClauseLinguistic factors influence how we judge personality, intelligence, social status…
Subordinate / Relative'No part of society or social behaviour is exempt' = an independent claim before the colon; the colon introduces the evidence list.
Grammar NoteA colon links a general statement to its supporting detail. This sentence supplies Q3's answer directly.
Simple MeaningLanguage affects everything — how we judge someone's character, intelligence, class, education and job suitability.
中文社会或社会行为的任何部分都不能幸免：语言因素影响着我们对人格、智力、社会地位、教育水平、工作能力以及身份与社会生存等诸多方面的判断。
In its most general sense, prescriptivism is the view that one variety of language has an inherently higher value than others, and that this ought to be imposed on the whole of the speech community.
Main ClausePrescriptivism is the view that…
Subordinate / RelativeTwo coordinated that-clauses: 'that one variety… has a higher value' and 'that this ought to be imposed…'.
Grammar NoteA definition sentence with two parallel that-clauses — the belief plus the action it demands. Q9 rests on this.
Simple MeaningPrescriptivism believes one form of a language is naturally better and that everyone should be made to use it.
中文从最广泛的意义上说，规定主义认为某一语言变体本身就比其他变体更有价值，并且应当把它强加于整个语言社群。
The authoritarian nature of the approach is best characterised by its reliance on 'rules' of grammar.
Main ClauseThe authoritarian nature of the approach is best characterised by its reliance on 'rules' of grammar.
Subordinate / RelativeNo subordinate clause — a compact passive statement.
Grammar NotePassive 'is characterised by' presents the writer's analysis as neutral description. The quotation marks around 'rules' signal mild scepticism. Q10's answer is here.
Simple MeaningWhat makes this approach authoritarian is that it depends on grammatical 'rules'.
中文这种做法的专断性质，最能体现在它对语法「规则」的依赖上。
This approach is summarised in the statement that it is the task of the grammarian to describe, not prescribe - to record the facts of linguistic diversity, and not to attempt the impossible tasks of evaluating language variation or halting language change.
Main ClauseThis approach is summarised in the statement that…
Subordinate / Relative'that it is the task of the grammarian to describe, not prescribe' (that-clause) + the dash introduces a restating apposition.
Grammar NoteThe dash expands 'describe, not prescribe' into a fuller contrast. 'the impossible tasks of' is the writer reporting the descriptivist view — key to Q6.
Simple MeaningDescriptivists say a grammarian should describe language, not lay down rules — record variety, not judge it or try to stop change.
中文这一取向可概括为：语法学家的任务是描写而非规定——记录语言多样性的事实，而不是去尝试评价语言变异或阻止语言变化这些不可能完成的任务。
In the second half of the 18th century, we already find advocates of this view, such as Joseph Priestley, whose Rudiments of English Grammar (1761) insists that 'the custom of speaking is the original and only just standard of any language'.
Main ClauseWe already find advocates of this view.
Subordinate / Relative'such as Joseph Priestley' (exemplification) + 'whose Rudiments of English Grammar insists that…' (relative clause with 'whose') + the quoted that-clause.
Grammar Note'already' is doing heavy work: it tells you descriptivism existed within the 18th century, which makes Q7 NO.
Simple MeaningDescriptivist ideas already existed in the later 1700s — Joseph Priestley argued that how people actually speak is the only real standard.
中文早在 18 世纪下半叶，我们就已能找到这一观点的支持者，例如约瑟夫·普利斯特里，他在《英语语法要义》（1761）中坚持认为「说话的习惯是任何语言唯一正当的原始标准」。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Problem → two responses → present-day conflict: the passage opens with why language debate is heated, presents prescriptivism, then descriptivism as its opposite, and closes with how each side now caricatures the other. Q13 (purpose) follows directly from this shape: the writer presents a historical account of differing views.

Definition: 'In its most general sense, prescriptivism is the view that…' — a textbook definition sentence. Whenever a passage formally defines a term, expect at least one question anchored there (here, Q9).

Enumeration: 'The aims of these early grammarians were threefold: (a) they wanted to codify… (b) they wanted a means of settling disputes… (c) they wanted to point out common errors…' Numbered aims are easy to locate and easy to test.

Contrast pairs: prescribed vs proscribed; describe vs prescribe; right vs wrong; radical liberalism vs elitist conservatism. The whole passage is built on binary oppositions — track them and the summary answers become obvious.

Concession: 'These attitudes are still with us… Nevertheless, there is an alternative point of view.' 'Nevertheless' marks the pivot from camp one to camp two — the structural hinge of the passage.

Cause & effect: language is public behaviour (cause) → usages are noticed and criticised (effect) → it is easy to hurt and be hurt (effect). Q1 sits at the top of this chain.

Author's attitude — neutral historian, mildly critical of extremes: the writer uses scare quotes around 'rules', 'correctly' and 'improve', and calls both modern camps guilty of 'painting unreal pictures'. He takes no side, which is why Q13's answer is B and not A or D.
