📋 Passage Overview

Having a laugh. The passage reports psychological research on humour and laughter: how laughter develops in babies and exists in some animals, how 'laugh tracks' were invented, how listeners worldwide can tell friends from strangers by their laughter, how laughter signals social status, and how humour can actually make people persist longer at boring tasks. Question types: multiple choice (Q27–31), summary with a word list (Q32–36) and YES/NO/NOT GIVEN (Q37–40).

💡 Reading tip: For YES/NO/NOT GIVEN, you are testing the writer's claims. NO needs a clear contradiction; NOT GIVEN means the claim is simply not made. For the word-list summary, decide the part of speech first, then choose from the box.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Humans start developing a sense of humour as early as six weeks old, when babies begin to laugh and smile in response to stimuli.
Main ClauseHumans start developing a sense of humour as early as six weeks old
Subordinate / Relative'when babies begin to laugh and smile in response to stimuli' = time/relative clause modifying 'six weeks old'
Grammar NoteMain clause + a 'when' clause adding the circumstance.
Simple MeaningPeople begin to develop humour by about six weeks, when babies laugh and smile at things around them.
中文人类早在六周大时就开始发展幽默感,那时婴儿会对刺激作出发笑和微笑的反应。
These were intended to help people at home feel like they were in a social situation, such as a crowded theatre.
Main ClauseThese were intended to help people at home feel like they were in a social situation
Subordinate / Relative'such as a crowded theatre' = example phrase; 'like they were in a social situation' = manner clause after 'feel'
Grammar NotePassive main verb 'were intended' + infinitive 'to help … feel' + example phrase.
Simple MeaningLaugh tracks were meant to make home viewers feel as if they were in a crowd, like a busy theatre.
中文这些(笑声轨)旨在让家中的观众感觉自己身处一种社交场合,比如拥挤的剧院。
A team led by Christopher Oveis found that high-status individuals had different laughs from low-status individuals, and that strangers' judgements of an individual's social status were influenced by the quality of their laughter.
Main ClauseA team … found that … and that …
Subordinate / Relative'led by Christopher Oveis' = reduced relative clause; two parallel 'that' noun clauses report the two findings
Grammar NoteReporting verb 'found' + two coordinated that-clauses ('that … and that …').
Simple MeaningOveis's team found that high- and low-status people laugh differently, and that strangers judge status from how someone laughs.
中文由克里斯托弗·奥维斯带领的团队发现,高地位者与低地位者的笑声不同,而且陌生人对一个人社会地位的判断会受到其笑声特质的影响。
Although humour has been found to help relieve stress and facilitate social relationships, the traditional view of task performance implies that individuals should avoid things that may distract them from task goals.
Main Clausethe traditional view of task performance implies that individuals should avoid things …
Subordinate / Relative'Although humour has been found to help relieve stress and facilitate social relationships' = concession clause; 'that may distract them from task goals' = relative clause defining 'things'
Grammar NoteConcession ('Although…') + main clause with a reporting verb and a relative clause.
Simple MeaningEven though humour reduces stress and helps relationships, the old view says people should avoid distractions from their goals.
中文尽管幽默被发现有助于缓解压力、促进社会关系,但关于任务表现的传统观点却认为,人们应避免那些可能使其偏离任务目标的事物。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Study-by-study structure. Passage 3 moves through separate studies (laugh tracks, Santa Cruz, San Diego, Australian National University). Each MCQ targets one study — locate the right one before choosing.

Emphasis words. The first paragraph 'emphasises' laughter's value to research. MCQ 27 tests exactly what is stressed, so weigh 'provide … rich resources for studying human psychology' against the distractors.

Claim testing (YES/NO/NG). The final questions test the writer's position on humour and performance. Read the concluding paragraph carefully: humour is called 'not only enjoyable but … energising'.
