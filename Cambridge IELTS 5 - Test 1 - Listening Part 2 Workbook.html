📋 Quick Review

A single presenter on the radio consumer programme "Buyer Beware" reviews three budget-price baby cots for new parents: the Baby Safe, the Choice Cots, and the Mother's Choice. For each product the presenter describes good points, problems, and a final verdict.

This is a typical IELTS Listening Part 2 monologue — one speaker giving structured information, usually about products, places, or services, often using a "good points / bad points" pattern.

💡 Listening strategy tip: In Part 2 monologues, listen for signpost language such as "on the plus side," "however," "the main problem," and "overall, we'd rate this..." These phrases tell you exactly when the speaker is switching from good points to bad points to the final verdict.

🔄 Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening, the audio never uses the exact words in the question. Recognising paraphrases is the key skill. Study each example below, then try your own.
Paraphrase Example 1 — Ease of Assembly
Question wordingGood point: easy to assemble
Audio meaning"On the plus side, the Choice Cots were very simple to put together."
Key paraphrase"simple to put together" = easy to assemble
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2 — Safety Concern
Question wordingA safety hazard
Audio meaning"The space between the bars was too wide — our testers felt a baby's head could genuinely get trapped there."
Key paraphrase"too wide... could get trapped" = a safety hazard
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3 — Overall Rating
Question wordingOverall rating: excellent value
Audio meaning"Taking everything into account, we'd call this cot a best buy."
Key paraphrase"best buy" = top recommendation / excellent value
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4 — Minor Fault
Question wordingA minor problem
Audio meaning"The pictures were simply stuck on and could come off — though the maker has promised to stop doing this."
Key paraphrase"come off" = detach; described as minor because the maker will fix it
