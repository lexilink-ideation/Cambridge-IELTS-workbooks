📋 Passage Overview

This passage, 'The Birth of Scientific English', traces how English became the dominant language of science. It explains that early scientists (like Newton) often wrote in Latin, the international scholarly language, but that a shift towards writing in national languages — and eventually English — grew with trade, wider readerships and national pride. It discusses why Latin was used, why the change happened, and how scientific writing developed its own precise style.

💡 Reading strategy tip: History-of-ideas passages ask about reasons and changes over time. Note WHY Latin was used and WHY it was abandoned — questions often contrast the two periods.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"For centuries, Latin served as the international language of scholarship, so that a scientist in one country could be read by scholars in another."
Main ClauseLatin served as the international language of scholarship
Subordinate / Relativeso that a scientist in one country could be read by scholars in another (result clause)
Grammar Note'so that' shows purpose/result; the passive 'could be read' keeps focus on the scientist.
Simple MeaningScientists used Latin so scholars everywhere could read their work.
中文几个世纪以来，拉丁语一直是国际学术语言，因此一个国家的科学家能被另一个国家的学者阅读。
"A second reason for writing in Latin may, paradoxically, have been to restrict the readership to those who were properly educated."
Main ClauseA second reason … may … have been to restrict the readership
Subordinate / Relativeto those who were properly educated (relative clause defining the readership); paradoxically (comment adverb)
Grammar Note'may have been' = past speculation; 'paradoxically' flags a surprising point.
Simple MeaningLatin may also have been used, oddly, to keep readers limited to the well-educated.
中文用拉丁语写作的第二个原因，或许矛盾地，是为了把读者限制在受过良好教育的人当中。
"As trade expanded and more people learned to read, there was growing pressure to write in the vernacular."
Main Clausethere was growing pressure to write in the vernacular
Subordinate / RelativeAs trade expanded and more people learned to read (double 'as' clause = as/while/because)
Grammar Note'As' introduces two simultaneous causes; 'to write' is the infinitive after 'pressure'.
Simple MeaningAs trade grew and literacy spread, people increasingly wanted writing in everyday language.
中文随着贸易扩大、更多人识字，用本地语言写作的压力越来越大。
"Scientific English gradually developed conventions that made reports clearer and reduced the ambiguity of everyday language."
Main ClauseScientific English gradually developed conventions
Subordinate / Relativethat made reports clearer and reduced the ambiguity of everyday language (relative clause)
Grammar Note'that' introduces a relative clause with two parallel verbs ('made', 'reduced').
Simple MeaningOver time, scientific English created rules that made reports clear and less ambiguous.
中文科学英语逐渐形成了一些惯例，使报告更清晰，并减少了日常语言的歧义。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect (why Latin) — Latin was international + prestigious + limited to the educated → scientists chose it. Note each reason separately.

Change over time — Trade + literacy + national pride → shift from Latin to the vernacular and then English. Contrast the 'before' and 'after'.

Purpose / Result — Writing in Latin 'so that' scholars abroad could read it — but also to restrict readership. Two opposite purposes exist together.

Development of style — Scientific English created conventions to be precise and reduce ambiguity. Feature → benefit.
