📋 Passage Overview

'Bring back the big cats' — John Vesty argues for rewilding Britain, beginning with archaeological evidence that the lynx survived far later than was thought. He explains why large predators create biodiversity, criticises the 'preservation-jar' model of conservation, and points to Europe, where the lynx population has tripled since 1970. Question types: multiple choice (Q14–18), summary completion with a word list (Q19–22) and YES / NO / NOT GIVEN (Q23–26).

💡 Reading tip: This is a persuasive article, so the writer's opinion is everywhere. For YES / NO / NOT GIVEN, distinguish what he claims from what he merely reports. He reports the European population tripled; he never says conservationists were surprised — hence NOT GIVEN.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Until this discovery, the lynx was presumed to have died out in Britain at least 6,000 years ago, before the inhabitants of these islands took up farming.
Main Clausethe lynx was presumed to have died out in Britain at least 6,000 years ago
Subordinate / Relative'Until this discovery' = time phrase; 'before the inhabitants of these islands took up farming' = time clause.
Grammar NotePassive reporting 'was presumed' + perfect infinitive 'to have died out' (the dying happened before the presuming).
Simple MeaningBefore this find, people believed the lynx had disappeared from Britain over 6,000 years ago, before farming began.
中文在这一发现之前,人们一直推测猞猁至少在6000年前就已在英国灭绝——那时这些岛上的居民还没有开始务农。
Were it not for the animal's backside having worn away with time, we could have been certain, as the lynx's stubby tail is unmistakable.
Main Clausewe could have been certain
Subordinate / Relative'Were it not for the animal's backside having worn away with time' = inverted conditional (= 'If it were not for…'); 'as the lynx's stubby tail is unmistakable' = reason clause.
Grammar NoteFormal inversion replaces 'if'. 'having worn away' = a perfect gerund after a possessive.
Simple MeaningIf the carving's rear had not worn away, we would know for sure, because a lynx's short tail is unmistakable.
中文若不是这只动物的后半身随时间磨损殆尽,我们本可以确定无疑,因为猞猁那截短尾是绝不会认错的。
One of the most striking findings of modern ecology is that ecosystems without large predators behave in completely different ways from those that retain them.
Main ClauseOne of the most striking findings of modern ecology is …
Subordinate / Relative'that ecosystems without large predators behave in completely different ways from those that retain them' = noun clause; 'that retain them' = relative clause modifying 'those' (= ecosystems).
Grammar NoteSuperlative subject + that-clause; 'those' avoids repeating 'ecosystems'.
Simple MeaningModern ecology has found that ecosystems without big predators work completely differently from ones that still have them.
中文现代生态学最引人注目的发现之一是:没有大型捕食者的生态系统,其运作方式与保留了大型捕食者的生态系统完全不同。
It has tried to preserve the living world as if it were a jar of pickles, letting nothing in and nothing out, keeping nature in a state of arrested development.
Main ClauseIt has tried to preserve the living world
Subordinate / Relative'as if it were a jar of pickles' = unreal comparison clause; 'letting nothing in and nothing out' and 'keeping nature in a state of arrested development' = two result participle clauses.
Grammar Note'as if + were' = an unreal comparison (subjunctive). Two parallel '-ing' clauses expand the metaphor.
Simple MeaningBritish conservation has treated nature like a sealed pickle jar, allowing no change and stopping natural development.
中文它试图像保存一罐腌菜那样保存这个生命世界:不让任何东西进出,使自然处于停滞发展的状态。
The lynx requires deep cover, and as such presents little risk to sheep and other livestock, which are supposed, as a condition of farm subsidies, to be kept out of the woods.
Main ClauseThe lynx requires deep cover, and … presents little risk to sheep and other livestock
Subordinate / Relative'as such' = for that reason; 'which are supposed… to be kept out of the woods' = non-defining relative clause; 'as a condition of farm subsidies' = inserted condition phrase.
Grammar Note'as such' links cause to consequence; the inserted phrase splits 'are supposed' from 'to be kept'.
Simple MeaningLynx need dense forest, so they threaten sheep very little — and farm subsidies already require livestock to stay out of woodland.
中文猞猁需要茂密的隐蔽处,因此对绵羊和其他牲畜几乎不构成威胁——按照农业补贴的条件,这些牲畜本来就应被隔离在林地之外。
As with wolves, bears, beavers, boar, bison, moose and many other species, the lynx has been able to spread as farming has left the hills and people discover that it is more lucrative to protect charismatic wildlife than to hunt it.
Main Clausethe lynx has been able to spread
Subordinate / Relative'As with wolves, bears… and many other species' = comparison phrase; 'as farming has left the hills and people discover…' = reason clause with two parts; 'that it is more lucrative to protect charismatic wildlife than to hunt it' = noun clause.
Grammar NoteTwo different uses of 'as' in one sentence — first 'like', then 'because'. Read carefully.
Simple MeaningLike wolves and bears, the lynx has spread because farming has retreated from the hills and people find protecting wildlife pays better than hunting it.
中文与狼、熊、河狸、野猪、野牛、驼鹿等许多物种一样,随着农业退出山地,并且人们发现保护有魅力的野生动物比猎杀它们更有利可图,猞猁得以扩散。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Evidence → symbol → movement. The article opens with archaeology (a bone, a stone cross) to establish that the lynx survived later than believed, then turns the animal into 'the totemic animal of a movement': rewilding. Concrete evidence first, abstract cause second — a standard persuasive shape.

Definition then justification. 'Rewilding means the mass restoration of damaged ecosystems' is followed immediately by 'Above all, it means bringing back missing species', then by the ecological reason: predators create niches for hundreds of species. The word 'Above all' tells you which part of the definition matters most.

Criticism by metaphor. British conservation is attacked as a pickle jar — 'letting nothing in and nothing out'. The writer's charge is not that it failed, nor that it is changing, but that its whole approach is wrong. That distinction decides Q16 (C, not A or B).

Counter-intuitive benefit. Protecting large sea areas from fishing would 'greatly boost catches in the surrounding seas'. The industry's own behaviour 'could not be more damaging to its own interests'. Whenever an argument says a restriction will help the restricted party, expect a question — here Q17.

Britain versus Europe. The last two paragraphs contrast a Europe where 'large-scale rewilding is happening almost everywhere' with Britain, where 'attitudes are just beginning to change'. That contrast makes Q23 NO — Britain would be far from the first — and grounds the hopeful ending.
