Cambridge IELTS 10 — Test 4 — Reading Passage 2: Second Nature

Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

Psychologists used to believe personality was fixed in childhood. Positive psychologists now argue that admired qualities — optimism, passion, joy, courage — are largely habitual behaviours that can be learned. The passage illustrates each quality with a real person: Peterson, Fajgenbaum, Segerstrom, Streeter, Silvia, Kashdan, Zappaterra, Pedeleose and Pury.

这是一篇心理学科普,八个小节 A–H,每节配一个真实人物的故事。人名配对题和小节配对题是核心考点。

💡 Reading tip: This passage has THREE question types that all depend on the same skill — knowing who said what and where. Before answering anything, spend 90 seconds writing each name in the margin next to its section. Note that section G contains two stories (Zappaterra and Kashdan) and section H contains two people (Pedeleose and Pury).

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Psychologists have long held that a person's character cannot undergo a transformation in any meaningful way and that the key traits of personality are determined at a very young age.
Main ClausePsychologists have long held that … and that …
Subordinate / Relativetwo parallel that-clauses: 'that a person's character cannot undergo a transformation in any meaningful way' and 'that the key traits of personality are determined at a very young age'
Grammar Note'Hold that' = 'believe that' (formal). Two coordinated noun clauses share one reporting verb. The whole sentence is the source of Q14 and Q15.
Simple MeaningPsychologists used to believe character cannot really change and that personality is fixed very early.
中文心理学家长期以来认为,一个人的性格无法发生任何有意义的转变,而且人格的关键特质在很小的年龄就已确定。
What they're discovering is that many of these qualities amount to habitual behaviour that determines the way we respond to the world.
Main ClauseWhat they're discovering is that many of these qualities amount to habitual behaviour
Subordinate / Relative'What they're discovering' = nominal relative clause acting as subject; 'that determines the way we respond to the world' = relative clause describing 'habitual behaviour'
Grammar NoteA 'what'-clause as subject is a common academic emphasis device (= 'Their discovery is that…').
Simple MeaningThey are finding that these qualities are really habits that shape how we react to the world.
中文他们正在发现的是:这些品质中有许多其实等同于习惯性行为,而正是这种行为决定了我们回应世界的方式。
However, developing qualities requires mastering a range of skills which are diverse and sometimes surprising.
Main Clausedeveloping qualities requires mastering a range of skills
Subordinate / Relative'which are diverse and sometimes surprising' = non-defining relative clause describing 'skills'
Grammar NoteGerund as subject ('developing qualities') and gerund as object ('mastering'). This sentence supplies Q17 ('a wide variety of different skills').
Simple MeaningBut to build these qualities you must learn many different, sometimes unexpected, skills.
中文然而,培养这些品质需要掌握一系列多样、有时甚至出人意料的技能。
Inherently introverted, he realised early on that as an academic, his reticence would prove disastrous in the lecture hall.
Main Clausehe realised early on that … his reticence would prove disastrous in the lecture hall
Subordinate / Relative'Inherently introverted' = reduced adjective clause (= 'Because he was inherently introverted'); 'as an academic' = role phrase
Grammar NoteAn adjective phrase in front position expresses reason. 'Prove' here means 'turn out to be'. This sentence is the evidence for Q22.
Simple MeaningBeing naturally shy, he saw early on that as a lecturer his quietness would be a disaster.
中文他天性内向,很早就意识到:作为一名学者,他的沉默寡言在讲堂上将会是灾难性的。
She recommends you train yourself to pay attention to good fortune by writing down three positive things that come about each day.
Main ClauseShe recommends (that) you train yourself to pay attention to good fortune
Subordinate / Relative'by writing down three positive things' = phrase of means; 'that come about each day' = relative clause
Grammar Note'Recommend (that) + subject + bare verb' — the 'that' and the subjunctive are common in academic English. Directly answers Q20.
Simple MeaningShe advises writing down three good things each day so you learn to notice good luck.
中文她建议你训练自己去留意好运——每天写下三件发生的积极的事。
According to Cynthia Pury, a psychologist at Clemson University, Pedeleose's story proves the point that courage is not motivated by fearlessness, but by moral obligation.
Main ClausePedeleose's story proves the point
Subordinate / Relative'According to Cynthia Pury, a psychologist at Clemson University' = attribution with apposition; 'that courage is not motivated by fearlessness, but by moral obligation' = appositive noun clause explaining 'the point'
Grammar Note'Not X but Y' is a strong contrastive structure. 'The point that…' is an appositive clause. This is the source of Q21.
Simple MeaningPury says the story shows courage comes from a sense of duty, not from having no fear.
中文克莱姆森大学心理学家辛西娅·普里认为,佩德利奥斯的经历证明了这一点:勇气并非源于无所畏惧,而是源于道德义务。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

1. Old view versus new view (旧观点 vs 新观点). The passage opens by stating the traditional position ('Psychologists have long held that…') and immediately overturning it ('However, researchers have begun looking more closely at ways we can change'). This 'however' is the hinge of the whole text, and the summary in Q14–18 is simply a compressed version of the first two paragraphs.

2. Claim + illustration (观点 + 例证). Every quality is introduced abstractly and then given a human face: extroversion → Peterson; optimism under hardship → Fajgenbaum; optimistic behaviour → Segerstrom's three-good-things exercise; passion → Tanya Streeter; the discipline of passion → Silvia and Kashdan; joy → Zappaterra; courage → Pedeleose and Pury. When a question describes a situation, look for the story; when it states a principle, look for the quotation.

3. Counter-intuitive claims (反直觉的观点). Three claims deliberately surprise the reader: (a) 'to bring more joy and passion into your life, you must be open to experiencing negative emotions'; (b) passions are rewarding because 'they require discipline, hard work and ability'; (c) courage comes not from fearlessness but from moral obligation. IELTS loves these, because a careless reader will assume the opposite.

4. Cause & effect (因果). Several small chains run through the text: reticence → disastrous lecturing → deliberate practice → spontaneous extroversion; accident → no support services → founding a support group; unhappy research → eight-month break → switching labs; bullying manager → careful documentation → the manager leaves. Each chain occupies one section, which is why Q23–26 (section matching) is answerable from the chain alone.

5. Contrast (对比). The most tested contrast is between avoiding failure and seeking success: 'are you hoping to get through a business lunch without embarrassing yourself, or are you thinking about how fascinating the conversation might be?' Note also the physical/ordinary contrast that opens section H: 'Usually, we think of courage in physical terms but ordinary life demands something else.'

6. Structure of the conclusion (结论结构). The final short paragraph offers two timescales: 'Over the long term, picking up a new character trait may help you move toward being the person you want to be. And in the short term, the effort itself could be surprisingly rewarding.' Whenever a passage ends with a long-term/short-term pair, expect a question about the writer's overall message.
