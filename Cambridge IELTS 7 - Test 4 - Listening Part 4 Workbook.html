Monosodium Glutamate (MSG) Lecture · Practice Workbook

📋 Quick Review

A university lecturer gives a talk about Monosodium Glutamate (MSG), a flavour enhancer widely used in Japanese and Chinese cooking. The lecture covers its discovery history (Kikunae Ikeda, 1908), how it became mass-produced from 1956 onward, its chemical composition, and the idea of a "fifth taste" alongside sweet, sour, bitter and salty — including researcher John Prescott's theory of why each taste exists.

This is a typical Part 4 academic lecture: one speaker, an uninterrupted monologue, and answers that appear in order as the talk progresses. The task mixes multiple choice with note completion, so you must switch smoothly between question types without losing your place.

💡 Part 4 strategy: Because there is no second chance to hear a repeated question, keep your eyes moving down the page in sync with the talk. For note completion, predict the word type (noun, percentage, adjective) before you listen — this lecture uses several precise numbers and percentages.

🔄 Paraphrase Training

IELTS Listening questions never use the exact same words as the audio. Study each contrast below, note the key paraphrase, then practise.
Paraphrase 1 — Main Topic of the Lecture
Question wordingThe speaker says the main topic of the lecture is ______
Audio meaningThe lecturer says she will explore why MSG is popular and, more importantly, how it enhances the flavour of food — i.e. the mechanism behind it.
Key paraphrase"how does it enhance the flavour of food?" = the way monosodium glutamate works
Your practice
Paraphrase 2 — The 1908 Discovery
Question wordingIn 1908, scientists in Japan ______
Audio meaningThe ingredient in kombu seaweed responsible for improved flavour was found, that year, to actually be glutamate — a scientific identification, not an invention.
Key paraphrase"actually discovered to be glutamate" = identified glutamate
Your practice
Paraphrase 3 — The 1956 Change
Question wordingWhat change occurred in the manufacture of glutamate in 1956?
Audio meaningBefore 1956, production was slow and expensive. That year, the process sped up and factories began making far more MSG, a trend that has continued ever since.
Key paraphrase"industrial production increased dramatically" = it began to be manufactured on a large scale
Your practice
Paraphrase 4 — Why We Can Detect Glutamate
Question wordingOur ability to detect glutamate makes sense because it is so ______ naturally.
Audio meaningThe lecturer explains this ability is evolutionarily logical because glutamate is the amino acid found most frequently, out of all amino acids, in naturally occurring foods.
Key paraphrase"the amino acid which is most common in natural foods" = it is so common naturally
