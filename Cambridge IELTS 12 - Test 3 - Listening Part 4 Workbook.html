📋 Quick Review

A lecture on the effects of environmental change on birds, focusing on mercury (Hg). It explains where mercury comes from, how it enters the food chain, the research of Claire Varian-Ramos on bird behaviour and bird song, the advantages of lab-based studies, and finally the implications for humans. Note completion, ONE WORD ONLY.

💡 Listening tip: Science lectures signal new answers with 'what wasn't known until recently…', 'she's particularly focusing on…', 'what you may not know is…'. Each of those phrases in this talk sits immediately before a gap. Train your ear to treat them as alarms.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

Science notes strip out the speaker's hedging. Practise compressing a full explanation into one noun.
Q31 — the newly affected birds
Question note
It has also recently been found to affect birds which feed on ___
What you hear
'what wasn't known until quite recently is that those that eat insects can also be affected'
Key paraphrase
recently been found = wasn't known until quite recently; feed on = eat → insects
Your turn
Q33 — where song comes from
Question note
the effects on bird song (usually learned from a bird's ___)
What you hear
'a young bird seems to acquire this skill by listening to the songs produced by its father, rather than by any other bird'
Key paraphrase
learned from = acquires by listening to; 'rather than by any other bird' excludes 'mother' → father
Your turn
Q35 — the consequence
Question note
this may have a negative effect on birds' ___
What you hear
'they're less attractive to female birds, and so it can affect their chances of reproduction'
Key paraphrase
negative effect on ___ = affect their chances of ___ → reproduction (or breeding)
Your turn
Q40 — the human cost
Question note
New regulations for mercury emissions will affect everyone's energy ___
What you hear
'these will need billions of dollars to implement, and increase costs for everyone'
Key paraphrase
affect everyone's energy ___ = increase ___ for everyone → costs (prices / bills)
Your turn

🧠 Mini Dictation

Environmental-science chunks. Note the passive verbs: released, deposited, ingested, excreted.
1.mercury's one of the hundred and twenty or so elements that make up all matter
2.when coal is burned it releases mercury into the atmosphere
3.if it's ingested by a fish it's not excreted, it stays in the fish's body
4.she's looking for more subtle sub-effects
5.a young bird acquires this skill by listening to the songs produced by its father
6.the songs they produce aren't as complex as those produced by other birds
7.the experimenter has a much higher level of control
8.an unborn child can be affected if the food eaten by its mother contains high levels of mercury
