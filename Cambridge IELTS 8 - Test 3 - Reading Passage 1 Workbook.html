📋 Passage Overview

This passage, ‘Striking Back at Lightning with Lasers’, describes efforts in the US and Japan to control lightning. It explains the dangers and costs of lightning, an older method using rockets trailing wires, and a newer, safer laser technique led by Jean-Claude Diels: a laser strips electrons from atoms to create a conducting path that guides lightning to Earth. It also covers the main obstacle — the laser’s size — and the funding needed for a commercial system.

💡 Reading strategy tip: For the summary with a word list, first predict the kind of word each gap needs, then match the mechanism step by step. For Yes/No/Not Given about the writer’s views, watch hedging words (‘promising’, ‘hoping’) — and choose Not Given when the writer’s opinion simply is not stated.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"As the clouds roll in, a leisurely round of golf can become a terrifying dice with death — out in the open, a lone golfer may be a lightning bolt’s most inviting target."
Main Clausea leisurely round of golf can become a terrifying dice with death
Subordinate / RelativeAs the clouds roll in (adverbial time clause) / out in the open … (added explanation)
Grammar Note‘As …’ sets the time; the dash adds a vivid example; ‘dice with death’ = dangerous gamble.
Simple MeaningWhen storms arrive, playing golf becomes very dangerous — an exposed golfer is an easy target.
中文当乌云滚滚而来时，悠闲的一轮高尔夫可能变成与死神的可怕赌博——在空旷处，独自的高尔夫球手可能是闪电最诱人的目标。
"If a laser could generate a line of ionisation in the air all the way up to a storm cloud, this conducting path could be used to guide lightning to Earth."
Main Clausethis conducting path could be used to guide lightning to Earth
Subordinate / RelativeIf a laser could generate a line of ionisation … (conditional clause)
Grammar NoteSecond conditional ‘If … could …, … could …’ describes a hypothetical mechanism.
Simple MeaningIf a laser made an ionised line up to the cloud, that path could steer the lightning safely down.
中文如果激光能在空气中一直到风暴云生成一条电离通道，这条导电通路就可用来把闪电引向地面。
"To stop the laser itself being struck, it would not be pointed straight at the clouds; instead it would be directed at a mirror, and from there into the sky."
Main Clauseit would not be pointed straight at the clouds; instead it would be directed at a mirror
Subordinate / RelativeTo stop the laser itself being struck (infinitive of purpose)
Grammar Note‘To stop …’ gives the purpose; the semicolon and ‘instead’ contrast the two set-ups.
Simple MeaningSo the laser is not hit, it fires at a mirror rather than straight at the clouds.
中文为避免激光本身被击中，它不会直接对准云层，而是先射向一面镜子，再从那里射入天空。
"But they have not yet come up with the $5 million that EPRI says will be needed to develop a commercial system, by making the lasers yet smaller and cheaper."
Main Clausethey have not yet come up with the $5 million
Subordinate / Relativethat EPRI says will be needed to develop a commercial system (relative clause)
Grammar NotePresent perfect ‘have not yet come up with’ = still missing; ‘that … will be needed’ defines the money.
Simple MeaningThe companies still have not provided the $5m EPRI says is needed to make smaller, cheaper lasers.
中文但他们尚未拿出EPRI所说的、开发商用系统（把激光做得更小更便宜）所需的500万美元。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Problem / Solution — Problem: lightning kills people and damages power supplies. Solution being tested: use lasers to make clouds discharge safely on command.

Cause & Effect — A laser extracts electrons from atoms → creates a line of ionisation → a conducting path → guides lightning to Earth before an uncontrollable surge.

Contrast — Rockets can trigger lightning but are costly, unreliable (40% failure) and dangerous in populated areas; lasers aim to be safe. Signal: but, however, and anyway.

Cautious claims — The writer reports hopes, not certainties: ‘a promising system’, ‘he’s hoping for good news’, ‘if all goes well’. Notice hedging for Yes/No/Not Given.
