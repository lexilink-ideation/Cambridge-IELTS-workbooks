📋 Passage Overview

Often titled How much higher? How much faster?, this passage asks whether there are limits to human sporting performance. It reviews the factors behind improving records — genetics, scientific training (plyometrics), nutrition, biomechanics — and notes that revolutionary ideas (like the Fosbury flop) can still come from athletes themselves.

💡 Reading tip: True / False / Not Given tests the information in the text. 'False' needs a direct contradiction; 'Not Given' means the idea simply is not there. Don't use outside knowledge about sport.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

For the so-called power events — that require a relatively brief, explosive release of energy, like the 100-metre sprint and the long jump — times and distances have improved ten to twenty per cent.
Main Clausetimes and distances have improved ten to twenty per cent
Subordinate / Relativethat require a relatively brief, explosive release of energy (relative clause defining 'power events')
Grammar Notethe dashes hold a defining relative clause and an example; the main clause resumes after them.
Simple MeaningIn power events like the sprint and long jump, results have improved by 10–20%.
中文在所谓的力量项目（需要短暂而爆发性地释放能量，如100米短跑和跳远）中，成绩和距离提高了百分之十到二十。
Over the past century, the composition of the human gene pool has not changed appreciably, but with increasing global participation in athletics it is more likely that individuals possessing the unique complement of genes for athletic performance can be identified early.
Main Clauseit is more likely that individuals … can be identified early
Subordinate / Relativebut with increasing global participation… (contrast); possessing the unique complement of genes (participle phrase)
Grammar Note'but with + noun' shows a changing condition; 'possessing…' modifies 'individuals'.
Simple MeaningGenes haven't changed much, but as more people worldwide take part, talented athletes are spotted earlier.
中文过去一个世纪，人类基因库的构成没有明显变化，但随着全球参与体育运动的人增多，拥有独特运动基因的人更有可能被及早发现。
Biomechanics specialists put their minds to comprehending something that was too complex and unorthodox ever to have been invented through their own mathematical simulations.
Main ClauseBiomechanics specialists put their minds to comprehending something
Subordinate / Relativethat was too complex and unorthodox ever to have been invented through their own mathematical simulations (relative clause)
Grammar Note'too … to …' shows impossibility; 'put one's mind to' = concentrate on.
Simple MeaningThe specialists worked to understand a move too complex and unusual for their own models ever to have created.
中文生物力学专家们努力去理解一种东西——它太复杂、太不合常规，以至于绝不可能由他们自己的数学模拟发明出来。
So many variables enter into the equation, and our understanding in many cases is fundamental.
Main ClauseSo many variables enter into the equation, and our understanding … is fundamental
Subordinate / Relative(compound sentence; 'in many cases' is an adverbial)
Grammar Note'enter into the equation' = are involved; here 'fundamental' means basic/incomplete.
Simple MeaningSo many factors are involved, and in many cases our knowledge is still very basic.
中文如此多的变量参与其中，而我们在许多情况下的理解仍然只是最基础的。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Comparison: power events (10–20% improvement) vs endurance events (more dramatic, e.g. the marathon ~30% faster).

Cause ranking: genetics is 'the most important factor', but is 'only the first step'; training, nutrition and biomechanics add more.

Example → analysis: the Fosbury flop was invented by an athlete first, then explained later by biomechanics.

Author's caution: Raglin stresses performance is 'vexingly complex' and our understanding is 'fundamental' — records will still be broken.
