📋 Quick Review

Katie asks Joe for advice on her film studies presentation about film adaptations of Shakespeare's plays. They work through the stages of the talk and what still needs preparing (Q21–24, table), then discuss six specific films — Ran, Much Ado About Nothing, Romeo & Juliet, Hamlet, Prospero's Books and Looking for Richard (Q25–30, matching comments).

💡 Listening tip: In the matching task the comment is often expressed by the speaker as a story detail, not as a judgement. 'Moving it to 16th century Japan instead of 16th century Britain' means F (a different country), while 'moves the action into the present day' means E (a different period). Read all seven options carefully before you start.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

Matching-comment tasks demand you convert a narrative detail into an abstract label.
Q25 — Ran
Option wording
sets the original in a different country
What you hear
'using the same situation and story, but moving it to 16th century Japan instead of 16th century Britain'
Key paraphrase
Same century, different country → F, not E. Watch this contrast carefully
Your turn
Q27 — Romeo & Juliet
Option wording
presents the play in a different period from the original
What you hear
'the 1996 film, which moves the action into the present day… even though they're speaking in the English of the original play'
Key paraphrase
different period = moves into the present day → E
Your turn
Q28 — Hamlet
Option wording
is too similar to another kind of film
What you hear
'it's more like a typical action hero movie — there are loads of special effects, but no unifying interpretation' / 'All show and no substance'
Key paraphrase
too similar to another kind of film = more like an action hero movie → C
Your turn
Q23 — what to prepare
Question note
Prepare some ___
What you hear
'Will you have enough time?… OK, well, I'd better do some slides instead'
Key paraphrase
The first idea (film clips) is abandoned; 'instead' signals the real answer → slides
Your turn
