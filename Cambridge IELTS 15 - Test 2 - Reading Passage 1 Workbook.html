📋 Passage Overview

Could urban engineers learn from dance? The writer argues that transport engineering relies on models that value smooth traffic flow but ignore how people actually experience a city. The example of pedestrian guard rails shows how a 'logical' design can divide communities and push people into dangerous crossings. The writer suggests engineers could learn from choreography — 'thinking with the body'. Question types: paragraph matching (Q1–6) and summary completion (Q7–13).

💡 Reading tip: For 'which paragraph contains…', hunt for the specific detail named in the question, not the general topic. For summary completion, the summary follows the order of the passage — track it paragraph by paragraph.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

The guard rails that will be familiar to anyone who has attempted to cross a British road were an engineering solution to pedestrian safety based on models that prioritise the smooth flow of traffic.
Main ClauseThe guard rails … were an engineering solution to pedestrian safety
Subordinate / Relative'that will be familiar to anyone who has attempted to cross a British road' = relative clause defining 'guard rails'; 'based on models that prioritise the smooth flow of traffic' = reduced clause + a further relative clause
Grammar NoteSubject + two stacked relative clauses; a participle phrase ('based on…') adds the source.
Simple MeaningThose familiar road barriers were designed to keep pedestrians safe using models that put smooth traffic first.
中文任何试图横穿英国马路的人都熟悉的护栏,是一种以行人安全为目的的工程方案,而这方案建立在优先考虑交通顺畅的模型之上。
In doing so they make crossings feel longer, introducing psychological barriers greatly impacting those that are the least mobile, and encouraging others to make dangerous crossings to get around the guard rails.
Main Clausethey make crossings feel longer
Subordinate / Relative'introducing psychological barriers…' and 'encouraging others to make dangerous crossings…' = two participle phrases describing the results
Grammar NoteMain clause + two parallel '-ing' participle phrases showing consequences.
Simple MeaningAs a result, crossings feel longer, which especially affects the least mobile people and pushes others into dangerous crossings around the rails.
中文这样一来,他们使过马路显得更漫长,给行动最不便的人带来心理障碍,并促使其他人绕过护栏进行危险的横穿。
If their designers had had the tools to think with their bodies — like dancers — and imagine how these barriers would feel, there might have been a better solution.
Main Clausethere might have been a better solution
Subordinate / Relative'If their designers had had the tools to think with their bodies … and imagine how these barriers would feel' = third-conditional if-clause; '— like dancers —' = inserted comparison
Grammar NoteThird conditional (past unreal): 'If … had had …, there might have been …'.
Simple MeaningIf the designers could have 'felt' the barriers like dancers do, they might have found a better answer.
中文如果设计者当初拥有像舞者那样用身体思考、去想象这些障碍感受如何的工具,或许就会有更好的解决方案。
Kirsh argues that by using the body to simulate outcomes, McGregor is able to imagine solutions that would not be possible using purely abstract thought.
Main ClauseKirsh argues that … McGregor is able to imagine solutions
Subordinate / Relative'that … McGregor is able to imagine solutions' = noun clause; 'by using the body to simulate outcomes' = means phrase; 'that would not be possible using purely abstract thought' = relative clause defining 'solutions'
Grammar NoteReporting verb 'argues' + that-clause containing a 'by + -ing' means phrase and a relative clause.
Simple MeaningKirsh says that, by trying things out with his body, McGregor imagines solutions that pure abstract thinking could not reach.
中文基尔希认为,通过用身体来模拟结果,麦格雷戈能够想到纯粹抽象思维无法得出的解决方案。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Contrast: model vs experience. The passage repeatedly opposes what looks 'logical' in an engineering model with how it 'feels' in real life. Watch for 'but', 'yet', 'appear counter-intuitive' — these mark the writer's key argument.

Cause and effect chain. Guard rails → longer crossings → psychological barriers → dangerous crossings → divided communities → rails now being removed. Following this chain answers the summary (Q7–13).

Proposal / analogy. The second half draws an analogy with choreography ('thinking with the body'). Q1–6 test where specific ideas appear, so read for signposts like 'a suggested method', 'above all'.
