📋 Passage Overview

'Music and the emotions' — neuroscientist Jonah Lehrer on why music moves us. A Montreal study using fMRI and PET found that music releases dopamine, and that dopamine neurons in the caudate peak about 15 seconds before a listener's favourite moment. The writer then uses Leonard Meyer's 1956 analysis of Beethoven to argue that emotion in music comes from delayed, unfulfilled expectation. Question types: summary completion (Q27–31), multiple choice (Q32–36) and sentence endings (Q37–40).

💡 Reading tip: Multiple choice with four options rewards the reader who identifies the writer's emphasis, not just a matching keyword. Ask of each paragraph: what is the writer most impressed by here? In paragraph 2 it is the precision of the data; in paragraph 3 it is the timing of the neural response.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

On the one hand, music is a purely abstract art form, devoid of language or explicit ideas. And yet, even though music says little, it still manages to touch us deeply.
Main Clausemusic is a purely abstract art form / it still manages to touch us deeply
Subordinate / Relative'devoid of language or explicit ideas' = reduced relative clause; 'even though music says little' = concession clause; 'On the one hand… And yet…' = a contrast frame.
Grammar NoteDouble contrast: 'On the one hand… And yet…' plus 'even though'. Stacking two contrast markers signals a paradox, which is the paragraph's whole point.
Simple MeaningMusic has no words or clear ideas, yet it still moves us profoundly.
中文一方面,音乐是一种纯粹抽象的艺术形式,没有语言,也没有明确的观念。然而,尽管音乐几乎什么都没说,它依然能深深打动我们。
Because the scientists were combining methodologies (PET and fMRI), they were able to obtain an impressively exact and detailed portrait of music in the brain.
Main Clausethey were able to obtain an impressively exact and detailed portrait of music in the brain
Subordinate / Relative'Because the scientists were combining methodologies' = reason clause; the bracket names the two methods.
Grammar NoteReason clause first, result second. 'impressively' is the writer's evaluation — this single adverb answers Q33.
Simple MeaningBy using PET and fMRI together, the scientists got a remarkably precise picture of music in the brain.
中文由于科学家们把两种方法(PET与fMRI)结合使用,他们得以获得一幅精确得令人印象深刻的、关于音乐在大脑中作用的详细图景。
What is rather more significant is the finding that the dopamine neurons in the caudate — a region of the brain involved in learning stimulus-response associations, and in anticipating food and other 'reward' stimuli — were at their most active around 15 seconds before the participants' favourite moments in the music.
Main ClauseWhat is rather more significant is the finding
Subordinate / Relative'that the dopamine neurons… were at their most active around 15 seconds before…' = noun clause defining 'the finding'; the dashes hold an appositive explaining 'the caudate'.
Grammar NoteA 'What is…' cleft sentence used for emphasis; a long appositive between dashes that you can skip on first reading.
Simple MeaningMore importantly, dopamine neurons in the caudate peaked about 15 seconds before the listener's favourite moment.
中文更为重要的发现是:尾状核——大脑中参与学习刺激-反应联系、并预期食物及其他“奖赏”刺激的区域——中的多巴胺神经元,在参与者最喜爱的音乐时刻之前约15秒时最为活跃。
While music can often seem (at least to the outsider) like a labyrinth of intricate patterns, it turns out that the most important part of every song or symphony is when the patterns break down, when the sound becomes unpredictable.
Main Clauseit turns out that the most important part of every song or symphony is when the patterns break down
Subordinate / Relative'While music can often seem… like a labyrinth of intricate patterns' = concession clause; two parallel 'when' clauses act as the complement of 'is'.
Grammar Note'While' = although; 'it turns out that…' introduces a surprising conclusion; the bracket is an aside.
Simple MeaningMusic may look like a maze of patterns, but the key moment is when the pattern breaks and the sound becomes unpredictable.
中文虽然音乐(至少在外行看来)常常像一座错综复杂的图案迷宫,但事实证明,每首歌曲或交响乐最重要的部分,恰恰是图案崩解、声音变得不可预测的时刻。
This is why composers often introduce a key note in the beginning of a song, spend most of the rest of the piece in the studious avoidance of the pattern, and then finally repeat it only at the end.
Main Clausecomposers often introduce a key note … spend most of the rest of the piece … and then finally repeat it
Subordinate / Relative'This is why' = a result frame pointing back to the previous sentence; three parallel verbs share one subject.
Grammar NoteThree-part parallel verb structure ('introduce… spend… repeat'), sequenced by 'in the beginning', 'most of the rest', 'only at the end'.
Simple MeaningThat is why composers state a key note early, avoid the pattern for most of the piece, and only repeat it at the very end.
中文这就是为什么作曲家常常在乐曲开头引入一个主音,在其余大部分篇幅中刻意回避这一模式,直到最后才终于将其重现。
According to Meyer, it is the suspenseful tension of music, arising out of our unfulfilled expectations, that is the source of the music's feeling.
Main Clauseit is the suspenseful tension of music … that is the source of the music's feeling
Subordinate / Relative'arising out of our unfulfilled expectations' = participle clause modifying 'tension'.
Grammar NoteAn 'it is X that…' cleft sentence, used to place all the emphasis on 'the suspenseful tension of music'.
Simple MeaningMeyer says the emotional power of music comes from the tension created when our expectations are not met.
中文迈耶认为,正是音乐中那种由我们未被满足的期待所引发的悬念式张力,才是音乐情感的来源。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Paradox as an opening. Paragraph 1 sets a puzzle — music has no words, yet it moves us physically — and lists five bodily reactions (pupils, pulse, blood pressure, skin conductance, cerebellum). The writer's emphasis is on how intense those physical responses are, which is exactly what Q32 asks.

Expected finding vs surprising finding. Paragraph 2 ends with dopamine — 'this finding isn't particularly surprising'. Paragraph 3 opens with 'What is rather more significant…' — the timing. The whole passage turns on that contrast, and it decides Q34.

Question → hypothesis → evidence. 'The question, of course, is what all these dopamine neurons are up to. Why are they so active in the period preceding the acoustic climax?' The following two paragraphs answer it: because dopamine neurons adapt to predictable rewards, so unpredictability keeps them firing.

Musicological support. Meyer's Beethoven analysis is introduced 'To demonstrate this psychological principle' — that is, as support for the Montreal findings, not as an opposing view. Q35 tests whether you noticed that framing verb.

Old theory vs new theory. The final paragraph contrasts 'connotative' meaning (music refers to the outside world) with 'embodied' meaning (emotion arises from the music's own unfolding patterns). Whenever a passage names two competing theories, expect a question on each — here Q36 and Q40.
