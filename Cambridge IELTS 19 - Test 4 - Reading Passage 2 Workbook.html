📋 Passage Overview

Deep-sea mining. Bacteria from the ocean floor could yield new medicines, but the same seabed is rich in metals that mining companies want. The passage sets scientists urging caution (Upton, Copley, Tunnicliffe and legal researchers) against companies promoting mining (Johnston), and weighs the environmental risks. Question types: paragraph matching, matching features (people) and summary completion.

💡 Reading tip: This passage is a debate. As you read, tag each named person as 'pro-caution' or 'pro-mining' and note their key point — this makes the 'match the person' task (Q18–23) much faster.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

But Upton, and other scientists who view the deep ocean as a prospecting ground for new medicines, fear such potential will be lost in the rush to exploit the deep sea's equally rich metal and mineral resources.
Main ClauseUpton and other scientists fear such potential will be lost.
Subordinate / Relative'who view the deep ocean as a prospecting ground for new medicines' (relative clause) + 'in the rush to exploit… resources' (phrase).
Grammar NoteThe relative clause describes the scientists; 'in the rush to…' gives the reason.
Simple MeaningScientists fear medical potential will be lost as mining for metals speeds ahead.
中文但厄普顿以及其他把深海视为新药勘探地的科学家担心，在争相开发深海同样丰富的金属和矿产资源的过程中，这种潜力会永远丧失。
Mining corporations argue that deep-sea exploration could help diversify the supply of metals and point to the fact that demand for resources such as copper and cobalt is soaring.
Main ClauseMining corporations argue X and point to the fact that demand is soaring.
Subordinate / Relative'that deep-sea exploration could help diversify the supply' (noun clause) + 'that demand… is soaring' (noun clause).
Grammar NoteTwo 'that'-clauses report the corporations' claims.
Simple MeaningCompanies say seabed mining would widen metal supply as demand rises fast.
中文采矿公司辩称，深海开采有助于使金属供应多样化，并指出对铜、钴等资源的需求正在飙升。
Despite arising in the last half century, the 'new global gold rush' of deep-sea mining shares many features with past resource scrambles.
Main ClauseThe 'new global gold rush' shares many features with past resource scrambles.
Subordinate / Relative'Despite arising in the last half century' (concession phrase).
Grammar Note'Despite + -ing' concedes that it is recent yet still resembles the past.
Simple MeaningThough new, deep-sea mining repeats the patterns of earlier resource rushes.
中文尽管出现于近半个世纪，深海采矿这场'全球新淘金热'却与过去的资源争夺有许多共同之处。
She argues that active vents must be off-limits for mining to protect the new knowledge and biotechnology spin-offs they can deliver.
Main ClauseShe argues that active vents must be off-limits for mining.
Subordinate / Relative'to protect the new knowledge and biotechnology spin-offs' (purpose infinitive) + 'they can deliver' (reduced relative).
Grammar Note'to protect…' states the purpose; 'they can deliver' = 'that they can deliver'.
Simple MeaningShe says active vents must be banned from mining to protect their scientific value.
中文她主张活跃的热液喷口必须禁止采矿，以保护它们所能带来的新知识和生物技术衍生成果。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Two-sided debate: medical/ecological value (Upton, Copley, Tunnicliffe, legal researchers) vs commercial value (Johnston, mining corporations). The passage weighs both sides.

Cause & effect (mining impact): extraction → sediment plumes + waste discharge → disturbed seafloor and threatened biodiversity. Trace the environmental chain in paragraphs E–F.

Comparison for emphasis: the Moon, Mars and Venus 'have all been mapped… in much greater detail' than the deep sea — used to stress how little we know (Q22).

Concession & caution: companies claim low impact, 'But critics say' ecosystems could be destroyed — the writer balances claims against warnings.
