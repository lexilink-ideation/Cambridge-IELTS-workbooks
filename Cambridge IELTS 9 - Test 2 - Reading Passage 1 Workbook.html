📋 Passage Overview

Classroom noise and auditory function deficit. This New Zealand research article argues that noisy classrooms seriously harm children who have difficulty processing speech — those with hearing impairment, autistic spectrum disorders (ASD) and attention deficit disorders (ADD/ADHD). It reviews the causes of classroom noise, describes each disorder, and calls for international standards. Question types: matching information to sections (Q1–6), short answer (Q7–10), choose TWO letters (Q11–12) and purpose multiple choice (Q13).

💡 Reading tip: Q1–6 ask which lettered section contains a type of information — these are NOT in passage order, so do them last, after you know what each section does. Label each section with a 3-word summary first. For Q13 (the writer's overall purpose), look at the opening and closing paragraphs, not the middle detail.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Hearing impairment or other auditory function deficit in young children can have a major impact on their development of speech and communication, resulting in a detrimental effect on their ability to learn at school.
Main ClauseHearing impairment or other auditory function deficit can have a major impact on their development of speech and communication.
Subordinate / Relative'resulting in a detrimental effect on their ability to learn at school' = participial phrase showing consequence.
Grammar Note'resulting in' chains a second effect onto the first — a two-step cause-and-effect in one sentence.
Simple MeaningHearing problems in young children badly affect their speech, which then harms their learning at school.
中文幼儿的听力损伤或其他听觉功能缺陷会严重影响其言语与交流能力的发展，进而对其在校学习能力产生不利影响。
Modern teaching practices, the organisation of desks in the classroom, poor classroom acoustics, and mechanical means of ventilation such as air-conditioning units all contribute to the number of children unable to comprehend the teacher's voice.
Main Clause[Four listed factors] all contribute to the number of children unable to comprehend the teacher's voice.
Subordinate / Relative'unable to comprehend the teacher's voice' = reduced relative clause (= who are unable to…).
Grammar NoteA four-item list forms one long subject; 'all' resumes it before the verb. This sentence supplies Q11/12.
Simple MeaningTeaching methods, desk layout, bad room acoustics and air conditioning all increase the number of children who cannot hear the teacher.
中文现代教学方式、教室课桌的摆放、糟糕的教室声学条件，以及空调等机械通风设备，都会增加听不清教师讲话的学生人数。
Education researchers Nelson and Soli have also suggested that recent trends in learning often involve collaborative interaction of multiple minds and tools as much as individual possession of information.
Main ClauseNelson and Soli have suggested that…
Subordinate / Relative'that recent trends… involve collaborative interaction… as much as individual possession of information' = reported that-clause.
Grammar Note'as much as' balances two things being compared. 'have suggested' = a hypothesis, not proven fact — which is exactly what Q3 asks for.
Simple MeaningTwo researchers think modern learning involves group work as much as individual knowledge.
中文教育研究者纳尔逊与索利还提出，近期的学习趋势往往既涉及个体对信息的掌握，也同样涉及多人与多种工具之间的协作互动。
While the detrimental effects of noise in classroom situations are not limited to children experiencing disability, those with a disability that affects their processing of speech and verbal communication could be extremely vulnerable.
Main ClauseThose with a disability… could be extremely vulnerable.
Subordinate / Relative'While the detrimental effects… are not limited to children experiencing disability' (concession) + 'that affects their processing of speech' (defining relative).
Grammar Note'While' here means 'although', not 'during' — a very common IELTS trap.
Simple MeaningNoise harms all children, but those whose disability affects speech processing are especially at risk.
中文虽然教室噪音的不利影响并不仅限于有残疾的儿童，但那些残疾影响其言语与口头交流处理能力的儿童可能格外脆弱。
Children experiencing these disorders find it difficult to screen out unimportant information, and focus on everything in the environment rather than attending to a single activity.
Main ClauseChildren find it difficult to screen out unimportant information, and focus on everything…
Subordinate / Relative'rather than attending to a single activity' = contrastive -ing phrase.
Grammar Note'find it difficult to + verb' uses a dummy 'it'. 'rather than + -ing' contrasts two behaviours.
Simple MeaningChildren with ADD cannot filter out unimportant things, so they notice everything instead of concentrating on one task.
中文患有这些障碍的儿童难以过滤掉不重要的信息，他们会关注环境中的一切，而不是专注于单一活动。
It is probable that many undiagnosed children exist in the education system with 'invisible' disabilities, whose needs are less likely to be met than those of children with known disabilities.
Main ClauseIt is probable that many undiagnosed children exist in the education system.
Subordinate / Relative'with invisible disabilities' (prepositional phrase) + 'whose needs are less likely to be met than those of…' (relative clause with comparison).
Grammar Note'those of' avoids repeating 'the needs of'. 'It is probable that' hedges the claim.
Simple MeaningMany children probably have undiagnosed, hidden disabilities, and their needs get less attention than those of diagnosed children.
中文教育体系中很可能存在许多未被确诊、带有「隐形」障碍的儿童，他们的需求比已确诊儿童的需求更不容易得到满足。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Problem → evidence → solution: the passage opens with the problem (hearing deficits harm learning), builds evidence section by section (noise causes, then each disorder), and closes with the solution (international standards). Knowing this shape tells you Q4 must be near the end and Q6 near the start.

Cause & effect chain: modern teaching + desk layout + poor acoustics + air conditioning → more noise → children cannot comprehend the teacher → they fail to learn to their potential. Q11/12 sit at the first link of this chain.

Classification: 'The auditory function deficits in question include hearing impairment, autistic spectrum disorders (ASD) and attention deficit disorders (ADD/ADHD).' One sentence lists three categories, and the next three sections expand them in order — that is Q5's answer.

Concession: 'While the detrimental effects of noise… are not limited to children experiencing disability, those with a disability… could be extremely vulnerable.' The writer concedes a general truth to sharpen a specific claim.

Hedging (attributed hypothesis): 'Nelson and Soli have also suggested that…' — the writer marks this as someone's proposal, not established fact. Q3 asks for exactly 'a hypothesis', so hedged language is the signal.

Definition: 'Autism is considered a neurological and genetic life-long disorder that causes discrepancies in the way information is processed.' Formal definitions usually open a section and tell you what that section covers.

Author's attitude — concerned and advocating: 'increasing concern', 'extremely vulnerable', 'It is imperative that…'. The writer is not comparing methods or offering fixes; he is raising awareness — which decides Q13.
