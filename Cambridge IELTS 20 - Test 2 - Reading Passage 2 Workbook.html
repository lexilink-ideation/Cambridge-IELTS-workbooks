Reading Passage 2

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 2 — Reading Passage 2

Procrastination · Matching Information + Summary Completion + Multiple Choice (Q14–26)

📋 Passage Overview

A psychologist explains why we put off important tasks and how to stop. The central claim is counter-intuitive: procrastination is not laziness or bad time management but poor mood management — we avoid tasks that threaten our self-worth, feel briefly better, then feel guilty, which makes us procrastinate again. The writer then sets out the costs (worse grades, academic misconduct, lower income and job stability, poorer physical and mental health) and finishes with two practical routes out: controlling distractions and reframing the task, and forgiving ourselves instead of being harshly self-critical.

What the passage argues: That procrastination is an emotion-regulation problem, not a time-management or character problem; that avoiding a task gives real but temporary mood repair which then rebounds as guilt; and that because the cause is emotional, the cure must be emotional too — self-compassion and reframing, not harsher self-discipline.

段落结构 Structure: A：定义与常见场景，指出自责是最糟糕的应对方式。B：破除误解——不是懒惰或不会管理时间，而是情绪管理不良；脑成像显示慢性拖延者在“威胁识别”与“情绪调节”脑区存在差异。C：哪些人、哪些任务最容易拖延（低自尊者、完美主义者、备考等高情绪负担任务），以及“逃避—短暂舒缓—内疚—再拖延”的循环。D：代价之一，效率与诚信——德国3,000名学生研究、美国22,000名员工调查。E：代价之二，身心健康——抑郁焦虑、头痛感冒、睡眠差、心脏健康风险高63%。F：两条出路——管理环境与重新看待任务；原谅自己、不过度自责。

Task type: Q14–16 Matching information to paragraphs A–F (NB: a letter may be used more than once — and here B is used twice). Q17–22 Summary completion, ONE WORD ONLY from the passage. Q23–24 and Q25–26: choose TWO letters from A–E — both marks are lost if you choose three.

💡 Reading strategy tip: Three task types on six lettered paragraphs, and the order is different for each. Matching information (Q14–16) does not follow the passage order, so do it last, after the summary has already forced you to read paragraphs B and C closely. Summary completion (Q17–22) does follow the order and covers only paragraphs B and C, so it is the fastest six marks in the paper — do it first, and remember the limit is ONE WORD ONLY from the passage, so ‘poor time management’ or ‘a sense of guilt’ would both score zero. For the two choose-TWO-letters questions, the five options are deliberately all plausible advice or plausible findings: the only test is whether the passage actually says it. Tick each option against a specific sentence, and reject anything you cannot point to.
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • Q14 and Q16 are both paragraph B — the instruction says ‘you may use any letter more than once’ for a reason. Candidates who assume one letter per question get at least one of these wrong.
  • Q15 is in paragraph F, not paragraph A — paragraph A says ‘We all do it from time to time’, which feels like the answer. But Q15 asks about the realisation, i.e. actively reminding yourself: ‘We should remind ourselves that we’re not the first person to procrastinate, nor the last’ (F).
  • Q17 laziness, not ‘poor time management’ — the passage gives both misconceptions in one phrase, but the summary already uses the second one in its next sentence (‘an inability to organise time efficiently’). Cross off what the summary has already said.
  • Q18 anxious, not ‘anxiety’ — the gap follows ‘cause us to feel’, so it needs the adjective the passage uses: ‘makes us anxious’. Paragraph E contains the noun anxiety, in a different context — the wrong form and the wrong paragraph.
  • Q19 threats must be plural — ‘areas of the brain linked to detection of threats’. Copy the plural exactly as printed.
  • Q23–24 rejects the tempting options — B (poorer quality work), D (less enjoyment) and E (worse relationships) all sound reasonable, but the US survey reports only two things: less annual income (A) and less employment stability (C).
  • Q25–26 rejects familiar self-help advice — B (manageable aims), C (rewarding yourself) and D (prioritising) are standard productivity tips that this passage never gives. It offers only distraction control (E) and self-forgiveness (A). Note that the ‘reward’ in paragraph C is a description of the problem, not a recommendation.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Academic Reading sentences are long because they stack clauses, not because the words are hard. Find the main verb first, then peel off the extra layers. 先找主句谓语动词，再逐层剥离修饰成分。
“Contrary to popular belief, procrastination is not due to laziness or poor time management.”
Main ClauseProcrastination is not due to laziness or poor time management.
Subordinate / Relative‘Contrary to popular belief’ = a fronted prepositional phrase signalling that a common assumption is about to be corrected.
Grammar NoteThe pattern Contrary to X, Y always sets up a correction: X is what people think, Y is what is true. Note the negative scope — not due to A or B denies both causes, not just the first.
Simple MeaningMost people think procrastination means you are lazy or bad at managing time. That is wrong.
中文与人们普遍的看法相反，拖延并非源于懒惰或时间管理不善。
“If just thinking about the task threatens our sense of self-worth or makes us anxious, we will be more likely to put it off.”
Main ClauseWe will be more likely to put it off.
Subordinate / Relative‘If just thinking about the task threatens… or makes…’ = a conditional clause with two verbs sharing one subject.
Grammar NoteA first conditional (if + present, will + verb). The subject of the if-clause is a gerund phrase, just thinking about the task — the thinking, not the task itself, is what does the damage. That is the whole argument of paragraph B in one word.
Simple MeaningIf merely thinking about a job makes us feel worthless or worried, we are more likely to delay it.
中文如果仅仅是想到这项任务就会威胁到我们的自我价值感或让我们感到焦虑，我们就更可能把它拖下去。
“Research involving brain imaging has found that areas of the brain linked to detection of threats and emotion regulation are actually different in people who chronically procrastinate compared to those who don’t procrastinate frequently.”
Main ClauseResearch has found that areas of the brain are actually different.
Subordinate / Relative‘involving brain imaging’ and ‘linked to detection of threats and emotion regulation’ = two reduced relative clauses; ‘compared to those who…’ = a comparison phrase.
Grammar NoteTwo reduced relatives (which involves… / which are linked to…) stack modifiers before the verb, which is why the main verb are arrives so late. Find areas… are different first, then add the layers back.
Simple MeaningBrain scans show that the parts of the brain handling danger and feelings look different in habitual procrastinators.
中文涉及脑成像的研究发现，与不常拖延的人相比，长期拖延者大脑中与威胁识别和情绪调节相关的区域确实存在差异。
“Afterwards, people tend to be left with a sense of guilt that not only increases their negative mood, but also reinforces their tendency to procrastinate.”
Main ClausePeople tend to be left with a sense of guilt.
Subordinate / Relative‘that not only increases… but also reinforces…’ = a defining relative clause containing a correlative pair.
Grammar Notenot only… but also… joins two verbs sharing the subject that (= guilt). This is the sentence that closes the vicious cycle, and the noun guilt is the answer to Q22 — one word, without ‘a sense of’.
Simple MeaningAfterwards people feel guilty, which makes their mood worse and makes them delay things even more.
中文事后，人们往往会产生内疚感，这种内疚不仅加重了负面情绪，还强化了他们拖延的倾向。
“In one study of over 700 people, I found people prone to procrastination had a 63% greater risk of poor heart health after accounting for other personality traits and demographics.”
Main ClauseI found people prone to procrastination had a 63% greater risk of poor heart health.
Subordinate / Relative‘In one study of over 700 people’ = the setting; ‘after accounting for other personality traits and demographics’ = a qualifying phrase.
Grammar NoteThe final phrase is what makes this science rather than opinion: after accounting for means other explanations were statistically removed first. Academic writers add these qualifiers precisely so that a Yes/No/Not Given item can test whether you noticed them.
Simple MeaningIn a study of more than 700 people, habitual procrastinators were 63% more likely to have heart problems, even allowing for other differences between them.
中文在一项涉及700多人的研究中，我发现在排除其他人格特质与人口统计因素后，易拖延者出现心脏健康问题的风险高出63%。
“We should admit that we feel bad, but not be overly critical of ourselves.”
Main ClauseWe should admit that we feel bad. / We should not be overly critical of ourselves.
Subordinate / Relative‘that we feel bad’ = a noun clause acting as the object of admit.
Grammar NoteTwo imperatives share the modal should, joined by but: accept the feeling, reject the self-attack. This single sentence is the source of option A in Q25–26 (‘not judging ourselves harshly’) and the direct answer to the ‘berating ourselves’ of paragraph A.
Simple MeaningAdmit you feel bad about it, but do not attack yourself for it.
中文我们应当承认自己感觉糟糕，但不要过分苛责自己。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

IELTS Reading tests whether you follow the relationship between ideas: cause, contrast, example, definition, sequence and the writer’s own attitude. 抓住句子之间的逻辑关系，比逐词翻译更重要。

Cause & effect — the cycle the whole passage is built on

Task threatens self-worth → we avoid it → we escape the negative emotion → “This is rewarding, and it conditions us to use procrastination to repair our mood” → the relief is temporary → guilt arrives → guilt “reinforces their tendency to procrastinate” → back to the start. Every question in this passage sits somewhere on that loop. If you can draw the loop from memory, Q17–22 take four minutes.

Contrast — ‘Contrary to popular belief’ is the hinge of the argument

Paragraph B opens by naming two false causes (laziness, poor time management) and replacing them with a third (poor mood management). This single move gives you Q14 (‘false assumptions’) and Q17 (laziness). Watch for the other reversals too: “In the long run, however, procrastination isn’t an effective way of managing emotions” — the word however turns the short-term benefit into a long-term cost.

Comparison — who is being compared with whom

Three comparisons carry marks. (1) Chronic procrastinators vs people who don’t procrastinate frequently → brain differences (Q16). (2) Employees who procrastinate vs those who do not → less income, less employment stability (Q23–24). (3) People prone to procrastination vs others → 63% greater heart risk. Before you answer any comparison question, name both sides out loud — the wrong options usually compare the wrong pair.

Examples — ‘such as’ and ‘for example’ point straight at answers

“Tasks that are emotionally loaded or difficult, such as preparing for exams” gives you Q20; “study-related misconduct, such as cheating and plagiarism” and “health issues, such as headaches, flu and colds” illustrate the general claims before them. In summary completion the gap is very often the example, because the general statement is already paraphrased in the summary.

Definition — the passage defines its own key terms

“Procrastination is the habit of delaying a necessary task, usually by focusing on less urgent, more enjoyable, and easier activities instead” (paragraph A) and “The ‘mood repair’ we experience is temporary” (paragraph C, in quotation marks). When a writer defines a term in the first line, that line is almost always the topic sentence — and matching-information questions about ‘a definition of…’ point there.

Problem → solution — only paragraph F contains recommendations

Paragraphs A–E describe the problem and its costs; paragraph F is the only place the writer tells you what to do: manage your environment, fend off distractions, remind yourself why the task matters, forgive yourself, do not be overly critical, remember you are not the only one. That is why both answers to Q25–26 come from F, and why plausible tips like ‘set manageable aims’ or ‘reward yourself’ must be rejected — they are simply not there.

Evidence and hedging — how the writer signals how sure she is

Compare “Scientific studies suggest”, “This makes sense if we consider” and “procrastination also correlates with” with the flat statements “Research… has found” and “I found”. Correlates with is not causes. Any answer option claiming that procrastination causes heart disease would go beyond what this passage says.

The writer’s attitude — sympathetic, first-person, anti-self-blame

Notice the pronouns: “We all do it from time to time”, “my research”, “I found”. The writer includes herself among procrastinators and explicitly rejects self-punishment: berating ourselves is “one of the worst things we can do”. The passage therefore cannot support any option recommending stricter self-discipline — a useful filter for Q25–26.

🎯 Question-Type Skill Practice

Skill 1 — Matching information to paragraphs (Q14–16)

These questions do NOT follow the order of the passage, and one letter is used twice. Do them last, and match the function of the information, not just its topic.

  • 14. mention of false assumptions about why people procrastinate → B — ‘Contrary to popular belief, procrastination is not due to laziness or poor time management.’ ‘Popular belief’ that is then denied = a false assumption.
  • 15. reference to the realisation that others also procrastinate → F — ‘We should remind ourselves that we’re not the first person to procrastinate, nor the last.’ Paragraph A’s ‘We all do it from time to time’ is a statement of fact, not a realisation we are told to have.
  • 16. neurological evidence of a link between procrastination and emotion → B — ‘Research involving brain imaging has found that areas of the brain linked to detection of threats and emotion regulation are actually different…’ Brain imaging = neurological evidence.

Method: read the three statements, underline the noun that names the type of information (assumptions / realisation / evidence), then skim each paragraph asking ‘is there a sentence doing that job here?’ Do not hunt for topic words — every paragraph is about procrastination.

Skill 2 — Summary Completion (Q17–22): ONE WORD ONLY

The summary paraphrases paragraphs B and C in order. Predict the word class for each gap before you go back to the text.

  • Q17 laziness (NOUN) — ‘the result of ___’. The passage names two false causes; the summary’s next sentence already uses the other one (‘an inability to organise time efficiently’ = poor time management).
  • Q18 anxious (ADJECTIVE) — ‘cause us to feel ___’. Passage: ‘or makes us anxious’. The noun anxiety (paragraph E) would be the wrong form and the wrong place.
  • Q19 threats (PLURAL NOUN) — ‘identifying ___’ = ‘detection of threats’.
  • Q20 exams (PLURAL NOUN) — ‘Getting ready to take ___’ = ‘preparing for exams’. ‘Take exams’ requires the plural.
  • Q21 perfectionists (PLURAL NOUN — people) — ‘either ___ or those with low self-esteem’. Self-esteem is already used, so the other group is the answer.
  • Q22 guilt (NOUN) — ‘a feeling of ___’ = ‘a sense of guilt’. Write the noun alone.

Check: read the finished summary straight through. If a sentence reads oddly, you have almost certainly taken the right idea in the wrong grammatical form.

Skill 3 — Choose TWO letters (Q23–24 and Q25–26)

Five options, two marks. Treat each option as a separate True / Not Given decision and demand a sentence for it.

Q23–24: comparisons between employees who procrastinate and those who do not → A and C

  • A. Their salaries are lower. → YES: ‘participants who said they regularly procrastinated had less annual income’, reinforced by ‘annual income decreased by US$15,000’.
  • B. The quality of their work is inferior. → NO: the passage mentions a toll on productivity and on student performance, but never the quality of employees’ work.
  • C. They don’t keep their jobs for as long. → YES: ‘less employment stability’.
  • D. They don’t enjoy their working lives as much. → NO: job satisfaction is never mentioned.
  • E. They have poorer relationships with colleagues. → NO: colleagues are never mentioned.

Q25–26: recommendations for getting out of the cycle → A and E

  • A. not judging ourselves harshly. → YES: ‘We should admit that we feel bad, but not be overly critical of ourselves’, plus ‘Forgiving ourselves and feeling compassion’.
  • B. setting ourselves manageable aims. → NO: never mentioned.
  • C. rewarding ourselves for tasks achieved. → NO — and note the trap: ‘rewarding’ appears in paragraph C, but there it describes the problem (avoidance feels rewarding), not a recommendation.
  • D. prioritising tasks according to their importance. → NO: the passage says to remind ourselves why a task is important, which is reframing, not ranking a to-do list.
  • E. avoiding things that stop us concentrating. → YES: ‘strategies that can help us fend off distractions’.

Rule: in choose-TWO questions the wrong options are usually true of the world but absent from the passage. Never reward an option for sounding sensible.

Skill 5 — Matching headings practice (extra)

Match each lettered paragraph to the heading that fits it best. A heading must cover the whole paragraph, not one striking statistic.

  • Paragraph A → A familiar habit, and the wrong response to it — not ‘How to write a difficult message’, which is only an example.
  • Paragraph B → Correcting a common misunderstanding.
  • Paragraph C → Who procrastinates, and why relief does not last.
  • Paragraph D → The price paid at college and at work — not ‘Cheating among German students’, a single study inside it.
  • Paragraph E → Effects on the body and the mind.
  • Paragraph F → Two practical ways to break the cycle.

Watch out: D and E both list negative consequences. D is about achievement and money; E is about health. Headings are separated by the domain, not by the fact that both are bad news.

Skill 6 — Yes / No / Not Given thinking (extra)

These statements are about the writer’s views rather than plain facts. Decide before you read the reason.

  • Criticising yourself is an effective way to stop procrastinating. → NO — ‘berating ourselves won’t stop us procrastinating again. In fact, it’s one of the worst things we can do.’
  • Procrastination has been proved to cause heart disease. → NO — the writer says procrastination ‘correlates with’ health problems and reports a ‘greater risk’, which is not a claim of cause.
  • Procrastinators are aware that they should be getting on with the task. → YES — ‘deep down we know that we should just be getting on with it’.
  • Perfectionists procrastinate more than people with low self-esteem. → NOT GIVEN — both groups are named, but they are never ranked against each other.
  • Avoiding a difficult task makes people feel better immediately. → YES — ‘we also avoid the negative emotions associated with them. This is rewarding…’ but the relief is ‘temporary’.
