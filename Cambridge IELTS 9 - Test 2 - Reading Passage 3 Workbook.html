📋 Passage Overview

A neuroscientist reveals how to think differently. The passage explains neuroeconomics and the idea of the iconoclast — someone who does what others say cannot be done. Iconoclasts' brains differ in three ways: perception, fear response and social intelligence. The brain takes perceptual shortcuts to save energy; novelty breaks those shortcuts but triggers fear; and selling ideas requires social skill. Question types: multiple choice (Q27–31), Yes/No/Not Given (Q32–37) and sentence endings (Q38–40).

💡 Reading tip: The three-way structure (perception → fear → social intelligence) is announced in paragraph 2 and then followed exactly. Use it as a map. For Q32–37 (Yes/No/Not Given) note that the writer is careful: he says fear inhibits and iconoclasts do not let it inhibit them — he never says fear is easy to overcome. That distinction decides Q35.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

These discoveries have led to the field known as neuroeconomics, which studies the brain's secrets to success in an economic environment that demands innovation and being able to do things differently from competitors.
Main ClauseThese discoveries have led to the field known as neuroeconomics.
Subordinate / Relative'known as neuroeconomics' (reduced relative) + 'which studies the brain's secrets to success' (non-defining relative) + 'that demands innovation…' (defining relative on 'environment').
Grammar NoteThree stacked relative structures. Q27 is answered by the middle one: neuroeconomics studies success in a competitive environment.
Simple MeaningThese findings created neuroeconomics, which studies how the brain succeeds in a competitive world that requires new ideas.
中文这些发现催生了被称为神经经济学的领域，它研究大脑在要求创新、要求做事有别于竞争对手的经济环境中取得成功的秘密。
This definition implies that iconoclasts are different from other people, but more precisely, it is their brains that are different in three distinct ways: perception, fear response, and social intelligence.
Main ClauseIt is their brains that are different in three distinct ways. (it-cleft)
Subordinate / Relative'This definition implies that iconoclasts are different from other people' (first clause) + the colon introduces a three-item list.
Grammar NoteThe it-cleft corrects and sharpens the first clause — not the people, the brains. This is exactly what Q28 tests.
Simple MeaningIconoclasts differ from others, but really it is their brains that differ, in three ways: perception, fear response and social intelligence.
中文这一定义意味着反传统者与他人不同，但更确切地说，不同的是他们的大脑——体现在三个不同方面：感知、恐惧反应和社交智能。
Thus it will draw on both past experience and any other source of information, such as what other people say, to make sense of what it is seeing.
Main ClauseIt will draw on both past experience and any other source of information.
Subordinate / Relative'such as what other people say' (exemplification) + 'to make sense of what it is seeing' (infinitive of purpose containing a nominal clause).
Grammar Note'both A and B' coordinates two sources. Q29 depends on the phrase 'past experience'.
Simple MeaningThe brain uses past experience and other information, like other people's opinions, to understand what it sees.
中文因此，大脑会同时借助过去的经验以及其他任何信息来源（例如他人的说法），来理解自己所看到的东西。
We think our perceptions of the world are real, but they are only biological and electrical rumblings.
Main ClauseWe think our perceptions are real, but they are only biological and electrical rumblings.
Subordinate / RelativeTwo independent clauses joined by 'but'.
Grammar NoteA short, punchy contrast sentence placed between long ones — the writer uses it to land the key claim behind Q30.
Simple MeaningWe believe what we perceive is real, but it is only electrical and biological activity.
中文我们以为自己对世界的感知是真实的，但它们不过是生物与电信号的躁动而已。
Iconoclasts, either because they were born that way or through learning, have found ways to work around the perceptual shortcuts that plague most people.
Main ClauseIconoclasts have found ways to work around the perceptual shortcuts.
Subordinate / Relative'either because they were born that way or through learning' (parenthetical reason) + 'that plague most people' (defining relative).
Grammar Note'either… or…' offers two possible causes without choosing between them — careful hedging.
Simple MeaningWhether by birth or by learning, iconoclasts have found ways round the mental shortcuts that trouble everyone else.
中文反传统者——无论是天生如此还是通过后天学习——都找到了绕开困扰大多数人的感知捷径的方法。
But fear of public speaking, which everyone must do from time to time, afflicts one-third of the population. This makes it too common to be considered a mental disorder.
Main ClauseFear of public speaking afflicts one-third of the population. / This makes it too common to be considered a mental disorder.
Subordinate / Relative'which everyone must do from time to time' = non-defining relative clause.
Grammar Note'too + adj + to be considered' expresses a disqualifying degree. Q37 asks whether it is an illness — the passage says explicitly it is NOT.
Simple MeaningA third of people fear public speaking, which is so common that it is not classed as a mental illness.
中文但对公开演讲的恐惧——每个人都难免要面对——困扰着三分之一的人口。这使得它过于普遍，以致不被视为一种精神疾病。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Definition → elaboration: the passage defines 'iconoclast' in one sentence, then devotes the rest to the three brain functions named in paragraph 2. Question order (27–40) follows that structure exactly.

Cause & effect: limited energy budget (cause) → the brain takes perceptual shortcuts (effect) → we mistake perception for reality (effect) → most people cannot think originally (effect). Q29 and Q30 both sit on this chain.

Contrast: 'iconoclasts embrace novelty while most people avoid things that are different.' A single 'while' sentence supplies the answer to Q33 and rules out the wording of Q34.

Concession and anticipation of objections: 'Naysayers might suggest that the brain is irrelevant, that thinking in an original way is more a matter of personality than brain function. But the field of neuroeconomics was born out of the realization that…' The writer raises the counter-argument in order to defeat it — this is why option C in Q28 (personality) is a trap.

Problem within a solution: novelty is the cure for perceptual shortcuts, but 'the problem with novelty, however, is that it tends to trigger the brain's fear system'. Each solution creates the next problem, which is how the passage moves forward.

Qualification / hedging: 'either because they were born that way or through learning', 'These may seem like trivial phobias'. The writer constantly qualifies, which is why several statements are NOT GIVEN rather than YES or NO.

Author's attitude — enthusiastic but balanced: iconoclasts 'create new opportunities', yet also 'face alienation and failure'. The final paragraph gives both sides, which is exactly what Q38 and Q40 test.
