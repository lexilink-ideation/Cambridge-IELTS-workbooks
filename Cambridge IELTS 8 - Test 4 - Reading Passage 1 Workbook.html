📋 Passage Overview

This passage, ‘Land of the Rising Sun’, examines why Japanese pupils do better in maths than pupils in England and Wales. It describes lower-secondary schools, the role of the central authority Monbusho (textbooks and national curriculum), the typical lesson pattern (including pupils marking their own homework), how schools support slower learners (individual help and ‘Juku’ tuition), and the attitudes — hard work and accuracy — behind the success.

💡 Reading strategy tip: For section-heading matching, read the first and last sentences of each section to catch its main idea. For Yes/No/Not Given, base each answer on the writer’s claims — choose Not Given when the text simply does not say (e.g. comparing private and state schools).

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"Not only did Japanese pupils at age 13 have better scores of average attainment, but there was also a larger proportion of ‘low’ attainers in England."
Main ClauseJapanese pupils … have better scores … but there was also a larger proportion of ‘low’ attainers in England
Subordinate / RelativeNot only did … but … also … (paired correlative with inversion)
Grammar Note‘Not only did X …, but … also …’ links two findings; note the inversion after ‘Not only’.
Simple MeaningJapanese 13-year-olds scored higher, and England also had more low-scoring pupils.
中文不仅日本13岁学生的平均成绩更高，而且英格兰的“低分”学生比例也更大。
"Pupils mark their own homework: this is an important principle in Japanese schooling as it enables pupils to see where and why they made a mistake."
Main ClausePupils mark their own homework … it enables pupils to see where and why they made a mistake
Subordinate / Relativeas it enables pupils to see … (adverbial clause of reason)
Grammar NoteThe colon introduces an explanation; ‘as’ = because; ‘enables … to’ = makes it possible.
Simple MeaningStudents mark their own work so they can understand their mistakes.
中文学生自己批改作业：这是日本教育的一个重要原则，因为它能让学生看到自己在哪里、为什么犯了错误。
"The impression is that the logical nature of the textbooks, combined with the relative homogeneity of the class, renders work sheets unnecessary."
Main ClauseThe impression is that … renders work sheets unnecessary
Subordinate / Relativethat the logical nature of the textbooks, combined with the relative homogeneity of the class (noun clause with inserted phrase)
Grammar Note‘renders X unnecessary’ = makes X not needed; ‘combined with’ adds a second cause.
Simple MeaningBecause the textbooks are logical and the class is similar in level, worksheets are not needed.
中文人们的印象是：教科书逻辑严密，加上班级水平相对一致，使得练习纸变得没有必要。
"The view of repetitively boring lessons and learning the facts by heart, which is sometimes quoted in relation to Japanese classes, may be unfair and unjustified."
Main ClauseThe view of repetitively boring lessons and learning the facts by heart … may be unfair and unjustified
Subordinate / Relativewhich is sometimes quoted in relation to Japanese classes (non-defining relative clause)
Grammar NoteThe ‘which’ clause adds information between commas; ‘may be’ hedges the claim.
Simple MeaningThe idea that Japanese lessons are boring rote-learning is possibly unfair and wrong.
中文有时人们把日本课堂描述为枯燥重复、死记硬背，这种看法可能并不公平、也没有根据。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Comparison / Contrast — Japan vs England & Wales: similar spending on education, but Japan has higher, more consistent maths attainment and fewer low attainers. Signal: better … than, larger proportion, whereas.

Cause & Effect — Self-marked homework lets pupils learn from mistakes; strong class identity makes pupils help each other; valued education leads to hard work and accuracy.

Process / Sequence — A maths lesson follows a fixed pattern: homework on the board → teacher comments → topic explained slowly → class questions → individual work → teacher circulates.

Attitude — The Japanese view — ‘if you work hard enough you can do almost anything’ — plus supportive classes and no in-class competition, explains the success (Section F).
