📋 Quick Review

A presentation on the history of coffee and its economic and social importance. The speaker traces coffee from wild bushes in Ethiopia, through the Arab world and the coffee houses of Constantinople, into seventeenth-century Europe, then examines colonial production systems, the rise of tea in Britain, and the price collapse of the nineteenth century.

💡 Listening tip: ONE WORD ONLY, and the notes are organised by chronology (1522 → 1623 → 17th century → colonisation → 1776 → 19th century). Keep your eye on the dates in the question paper — they tell you exactly where you are in the talk.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

History lectures hide answers in long subordinate clauses. Practise finding the head noun.
Q31 — the 1623 order
Note wording
the ruler ordered the ___ of every coffee house
What you hear
'the ruler of Constantinople demanded the destruction of all the coffee houses in the city'
Key paraphrase
ordered = demanded; all = every → the noun is destruction
Your turn
Q34 — the naming of types
Note wording
Types of coffee were often named according to the ___
What you hear
'the names… like Mocha or Java coffee, were often taken from the port they were shipped to Europe from'
Key paraphrase
taken from = named according to → port
Your turn
Q37 — the comparison
Note wording
Coffee became nearly as important as ___
What you hear
'it became nearly as important as sugar production, which was grown under very similar conditions'
Key paraphrase
word-for-word, but ONE WORD ONLY → sugar
Your turn
Q40 — the industrial reason
Note wording
work might continue through the ___
What you hear
'their work didn't stop when it got dark: they might have to continue throughout the night'
Key paraphrase
when it got dark = at night
Your turn
