📋 Passage Overview

The Problem of Scarce Resources examines how health-care resources should be shared out. From the 1950s people realised resources are finite ('limits to growth'); at the same time health-care came to be seen as a basic right that the state must help provide. Rising costs and an ageing population then created a 'doomsday' clash between demand and resources.

💡 Reading tip: For the classification task (1945–50 / 1950–80 / after 1980), find the date signals in the text ('in the 1950s and 1960s', 'by the late 1970s', 'during the 1980s') and match each idea to its period.

Subordinate / Relativeso that they are distributed in both the most just and most efficient way (purpose/result clause)
Grammar Note'so that' shows the goal; 'both … and …' links two aims.
Simple MeaningDeciding how to share health-care resources fairly and efficiently is not a new problem.

Looking back, it now seems quite incredible that in the national health systems that emerged after the war, it was assumed without question that all the basic health needs of any community could be satisfied.
Main Clauseit now seems quite incredible that … it was assumed … that all the basic health needs … could be satisfied
Subordinate / Relativein the national health systems that emerged after the war (relative clause); two 'that' noun clauses nested
Grammar Note'it was assumed that…' is an impersonal passive; note the nested 'that' clauses.
Simple MeaningLooking back, it seems unbelievable that post-war health systems simply assumed every basic health need could be met.
中文回想起来，如今看来相当难以置信：在战后出现的国家医疗体系中，人们曾毫无疑问地假定，任何社群的所有基本健康需求都能得到满足。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Two opposite trends at once: resources seen as finite, while health-care is seen as a basic right — the passage's central tension.

Analogy: 'limits to growth' in energy/environment is used to explain limits in health-care resources.

Right → obligation: a right to health-care 'generates an obligation' for the state to ensure provision.

Cause of the crisis: rising costs (3.8%→7% of GDP) plus an ageing population produce the '1980s doomsday scenario'.
