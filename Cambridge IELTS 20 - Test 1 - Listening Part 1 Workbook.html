Listening Part 1

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 1 — Listening Part 1

Restaurant Recommendations · Notes Completion (Q1–10)

📋 Quick Review

In this Part 1 conversation two friends are chatting informally. One woman needs to book a restaurant for her sister’s 30th birthday, and her friend recommends three places: The Junction, Paloma and The Audley. As they talk about each one, they mention the location, why it is worth going to, and one or two drawbacks such as price, a deposit or a small choice of dishes. You must complete a three-column note table with ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER for each answer.

Who is speaking? Two friends (both female), talking casually. There is no “official” speaker, so the language is relaxed and full of everyday expressions.

The situation 情境: 一位女士要为姐姐的30岁生日订餐厅，朋友向她推荐了三家：The Junction、Paloma 和 The Audley。三家餐厅依次出现，答案也按这个顺序出现。

Task type: Notes completion in a table — Name of restaurant / Location / Reason for recommendation / Other comments. Instruction: ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER. Writing two words = 0 marks.

💡 Listening strategy tip: The notes are organised as a table with three restaurants in rows. The speakers discuss the restaurants one after the other, so the answers come in order — but inside each restaurant the order of the columns is not guaranteed. Before the audio starts, read the column headings (Location / Reason for recommendation / Other comments) and decide what type of word each gap needs: a food type, a place, an adjective or a number. “Reason for recommendation” gaps almost always follow a positive word (amazing, best, excellent, famous); “Other comments” gaps often follow a negative or limiting word (only, limited, not cheap, you need to…).
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • 50 vs 30 — £50 appears twice (Paloma’s deposit, evening price at The Audley) but the answer for Q9 is 30.
  • 30 appears twice — the sister’s 30th birthday and the £30 set lunch.
  • tiny vs average — the distractor is inside the question, the answer is in the reply.
  • reviews must be plural; Spanish needs a capital letter.

📚 Vocabulary Review

Word / PhraseTypeSimple Meaning中文Example SentenceIELTS Listening Note
recommendv.to say that something is good and worth trying推荐“I’d definitely recommend that place for a special occasion.”The whole table is built on ‘Reason for recommendation’ — listen for why, not just what.
occasionn.a particular event, especially an important or special one场合；特殊的日子“For a special occasion, I think the price is fine.”‘Special occasion’ = birthday, wedding, anniversary. Sets the topic of the whole conversation.
keen onphr.very interested in something; enjoying it a lot热衷于；很喜欢“It’s good for people who are especially keen on fish.”Q1 paraphrase: the question says ‘keen on’, the audio says ‘If you like fish…’.
freshadj.recently caught, picked or made; not old新鲜的“The fish is always really fresh.”Very common food adjective. Do not confuse with ‘flesh’ when you hear it quickly.
dishn.one prepared item of food on a menu一道菜“There are lots of interesting dishes to choose from.”In restaurant topics ‘dish’ means the food, not the plate.
atmospheren.the general feeling or mood of a place氛围；气氛“It’s got a great atmosphere.”A praise word, but rarely the answer — it usually sits next to the real answer as a distractor.
roofn.the top outside surface of a building屋顶；天台“You can go up on the roof and have a drink.”Answer to Q2. Write roof — the audio does not say ‘rooftop’ or ‘terrace’.
spectacularadj.extremely impressive to look at壮观的；令人惊叹的“It’s very popular because the views are spectacular.”Synonym family: incredible / amazing / stunning. Praise words flag a ‘reason’ answer nearby.
formaladj.following strict social rules; not relaxed正式的“If you want somewhere a bit less formal, try Paloma.”‘Less formal’ = more relaxed / more casual. Comparatives often signal a change of topic.
reputationn.the opinion that people generally have about something名声；口碑“It’s only been open a few months but it’s got a great reputation.”Frequently paraphrased as ‘reviews’, ‘what people say’ or ‘coverage in the press’.
depositn.money paid in advance to hold a booking押金；订金“You need to pay a £50 deposit to book a table.”Money + booking = classic Part 1 detail. Note it belongs to ‘Other comments’, not to the reason.
vegetarianadj./n.not containing (or not eating) meat or fish素食的；素食者“There is a limited selection of vegetarian food on the menu.”Answer to Q4. Spelling trap: v-e-g-e-t-a-r-i-a-n.
limitedadj.small in amount, number or choice有限的；很少的“The selection of those would be quite limited.”A negative signal word — it points to a drawback in the ‘Other comments’ column.
selectionn.a range of things available to choose from选择范围；种类“A limited selection of dishes.”Paraphrase family: choice / range / variety / options.
pressn.newspapers and journalists as a group媒体；新闻界“There’s been a lot about it in the press.”Connects directly to the answer ‘reviews’ in Q7 — the press writes the reviews.
reviewn.a written opinion about a restaurant, film or book评论；评价“It’s had excellent reviews from all the newspapers.”Answer to Q7. The note says ‘All the ___ are’, so you need the PLURAL reviews.
sourcev.to obtain something from a particular place or supplier采购；获取（原料）“Everything has to be sourced within a short distance.”The audio uses this verb instead of the word ‘local’ — you must make the jump yourself (Q8).
localadj.from the nearby area, not from far away本地的；当地的“She only likes cooking with local products.”Answer to Q8. Clues: ‘within a short distance’ + ‘nothing flown in from abroad’.
ingredientn.one of the foods used to make a dish食材；原料“The restaurant only uses local ingredients.”Spelling: in-gre-di-ent. Here it is printed on the question paper, so you only add the adjective.
portionn.the amount of food served to one person份量；一份“It’s not the kind of place you leave hungry, with tiny portions.”Q10 asks about portion size, so the gap needs an ADJECTIVE, not a number.
averageadj.normal or usual; neither large nor small一般的；中等的“I imagine the portions would be average.”Answer to Q10. It is the opposite of the distractor ‘tiny’ used in the question before it.
reasonableadj.fair and not too expensive合理的；价格公道的“That’s quite reasonable for a top-class restaurant.”Price adjectives usually sit right next to the number you need to write down.
set lunchn.a fixed-price lunch menu with limited choices午市套餐“The set lunch costs £30 per person.”‘£30 a head’ in the audio = ‘£30 per person’ on the question paper (Q9).

✏️ Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening the audio never uses the exact words printed on the question paper. Recognising paraphrase is the whole skill. 雅思听力中，录音几乎不会重复题干原词——识别同义替换才是关键。
Paraphrase Example 1
Question wording“Good for people who are especially keen on (1)”
What you hear“The food’s amazing. If you like fish, it’s probably the best restaurant in town for that.”
Key paraphrasekeen on = like / be very interested in / be a fan of
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question wording“The (2) is a good place for a drink”
What you hear“Before dinner, you can go up on the roof and have a drink. It’s really nice up there.”
Key paraphrasea good place for a drink = it’s really nice up there + you can have a drink
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question wording“A limited selection of (4) food on the menu”
What you hear“I’d say the selection of those would be quite limited” — replying to a question about dishes for friends who have stopped eating meat.
Key paraphrasepeople who have stopped eating meat = vegetarians → vegetarian food
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question wording“All the (7) are very good”
What you hear“There’s been a lot about it in the press… it’s had excellent reviews from all the newspapers.”
Key paraphrasevery good = excellent; newspapers/press write → reviews (plural, because of ‘are’)
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 5
Question wording“Only uses (8) ingredients”
What you hear“She only likes cooking with local products… everything has to be sourced within a short distance and absolutely nothing flown in from abroad.”
Key paraphrasesourced within a short distance / nothing from abroad = local
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 6
Question wording“Set lunch costs (9) per person”
What you hear“You could go for the set lunch… £30 a head. In the evening, I think it would be more like £50.”
Key paraphraseper person = a head / each. Two figures are given — match the one to lunch.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 7
Question wording“Portions probably of (10) size”
What you hearWoman: “It’s not the kind of place you leave feeling hungry, though, is it? With tiny portions?” Friend: “No… I imagine they’d be average.”
Key paraphraseThe question contains the distractor (tiny); the ANSWER is in the reply (average).

Review Quiz

Q1
B. She is celebrating her sister’s 30th birthday.
The friend went to The Junction for her own mother’s 50th — that is the distractor. The woman is booking for her sister’s 30th.
Q2
C. like
‘If you like fish…’ = ‘people who are especially keen on fish’.
Q3
A. The views from it are spectacular.
‘It’s very popular as the views are spectacular.’ Note the opposite of D: you actually do need to book.
Q4
C. It is in an old building next to the cinema.
‘It’s in a really beautiful old building on Bow Street’ — ‘right beside the cinema’. A describes The Junction; B describes The Audley.
Q5
B. A couple of her friends have stopped eating meat.
‘Stopped eating meat’ = vegetarian. The friend then says the selection of those would be quite limited.
Q6
B. A-U-D-L-E-Y
The speaker spells it out because the woman has not heard of it: A-U-D-L-E-Y → Audley. Always write the letters as you hear them.
Q7
A. She does not follow news and press coverage.
The friend had just said there had been ‘a lot about it in the press’. This line sets up the later answer reviews.
Q8
C. They are local.
‘Within a short distance’ + ‘nothing flown in from abroad’ = local (Q8). D is the exact opposite.
Q9
A. The price is fair, not too high.
‘Reasonable’ describes price. It signals that the number you need (£30) is about to be said.
Q10
C. £30, because the note asks about the set lunch.
Match the number to its label. £50 is the evening estimate (and also Paloma’s deposit). The note specifically says Set lunch, so £30 is correct.
Q11
B. ‘The reviews I’ve read didn’t mention that. I imagine they’d be average.’
A contains the distractor tiny, inside a question. In IELTS Listening the answer is almost always in the reply, not the question.
Q12
B. Predict the word type for each gap and follow the restaurants in order.
Answers follow the order of the three restaurants. Predicting NOUN / ADJECTIVE / NUMBER for each gap stops you writing a grammatically impossible word.
