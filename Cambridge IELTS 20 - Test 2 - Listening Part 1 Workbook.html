Listening Part 1

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 2 — Listening Part 1

Support for Carers · Table / Notes Completion (Q1–10)

📋 Quick Review

A woman has given up her job to look after her elderly mother at home, and she meets a former colleague for a chat. The friend explains what practical support the local council can offer a carer: first an assessment of the mother’s needs, then various kinds of financial and practical help. You complete a set of notes with ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER for each answer.

Who is speaking? Two friends — a woman who has left work to become a full-time carer for her mother, and a former colleague from her office who happens to know how the council system works. Very informal, lots of everyday spoken English.

The situation 情境: 一位女士为了照顾年迈的母亲辞去了工作，感觉与朋友同事渐渐疏远。老同事告诉她，当地议会（local council）可以为照护者提供实际帮助：先做一次"母亲需求评估"（assessment of needs），再根据评估结果提供交通费、家务帮助等支持。笔记按"好处→评估内容→可获得的支持"三段推进，答案顺序与录音一致。

Task type: Notes completion (bulleted notes under three headings). Instruction: ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER. Every answer here is in fact a single word — writing two words scores zero.

💡 Listening strategy tip: The notes are laid out as a set of bullet points under three headings — what support gives the carer, the assessment, and the types of support offered. The headings move forward in the same order as the conversation, so once you have found Q1 you simply follow the bullets down the page. Notice that most gaps sit after a short signpost: ‘how much…’, ‘loss of…’, ‘preventing a…’, ‘help to reduce…’. Read those signposts first and decide the word type: ‘how much’ wants an uncountable noun, ‘a’ wants a singular countable noun, ‘(6) her’ wants an -ing form because it is the subject of a phrase. Predicting the grammar stops you writing a word that cannot possibly fit.
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • time (Q2) vs a break (Q1) — both are about time. Q1 is what the carer gains (‘they sometimes need a break’); Q2 is what the assessor asks about (‘the amount of time you spend’).
  • dressing is printed on the paper — ‘help with getting dressed’ is the example already given, so do NOT write dressing for Q3. Keep listening: the answer is the woman’s own addition, shower.
  • money (Q4) is hidden inside ‘whether she can cope using money’ — the note says ‘dealing with’, which is the paraphrase of ‘cope with’.
  • lifting (Q6) must be the -ing form. The audio says ‘Lifting her, for example’, but many candidates write the bare verb lift and lose the mark.
  • petrol vs insurance (Q9) — the note already prints ‘fuel’, which is petrol. The second, extra item is insurance. Two claimable items are mentioned; only one is missing.
  • stress (Q10) — the audio says ‘minimize it’, using a pronoun. You must look back one sentence to find what it refers to.

eligibleadj.allowed to have or do something because you meet the conditions有资格的；符合条件的“Once they’ve done this assessment, if I’m eligible, what happens next?”This question moves the recording into the third section (Q8–Q10). A useful signal word.
taskn.a piece of work that has to be done任务；活儿“They’ll probably ask you what sorts of tasks you do for your mother.”Printed on the paper as ‘what types of tasks are involved’; the audio says ‘what sorts of tasks’ — types = sorts.
showern.a wash under a spray of water; the equipment for this淋浴“I help her get into the shower in the morning.”Answer to Q3. The note says ‘helping her have a ___’ = ‘help her get into the…’.
copev.to deal successfully with a difficult situation应付；处理“Whether she can cope using money.”Paraphrase family: cope with = deal with = manage. This is the bridge to the answer money (Q4).
memoryn.the ability to remember things记忆力“She’s started to have quite bad problems with her memory.”Answer to Q5. The paper says ‘loss of ___’; the audio says ‘problems with…’. Uncountable here — do not write ‘memories’.
liftv.to raise something or someone up抬起；举起“Lifting her, for example.”Answer to Q6 is the -ing form: lifting. The note reads ‘(6) her’, so a gerund is the only grammatical fit.
falln.an accident in which a person drops to the ground摔倒；跌倒“How to avoid the possibility of your mum having a fall.”Answer to Q7. ‘Avoid the possibility of’ = ‘preventing’. Singular, because the note prints ‘a ___’.
financiallyadv.in a way that relates to money在经济上；财务上“They might support you financially.”This adverb opens the last note section: everything after it is about money.
transportn.the system or cost of moving people from place to place交通；运输“They might help you with transport costs.”Printed on the paper, so use it as your landmark for Q8.
taxin.a car with a driver that you pay to take you somewhere出租车“If you have to get a taxi to take your mother for an appointment.”Answer to Q8. Very easy word, very easy to miss — it is said once, quickly, inside a long example.
appointmentn.an arranged time to see a doctor or other professional预约；（就诊）预约“To take your mother for an appointment.”Health-topic vocabulary. Here it is only context around the answer taxi.
claimv.to ask officially for money you have a right to申领；索赔“Could I claim for the petrol?”‘Claim for X’ signals a money item. Both petrol and insurance are claimed — only one is the gap.
petroln.the liquid fuel used in cars (US: gasoline)汽油“Could I claim for the petrol?”The note already prints fuel, which means petrol. Writing ‘petrol’ for Q9 is therefore wrong.
insurancen.money paid regularly so a company will pay if there is an accident保险“And you can claim for the insurance, too.”Answer to Q9. Spelling: in-s-u-r-a-n-c-e. The word ‘too’ tells you it is the extra, second item.
houseworkn.the cleaning and tidying done in a home家务“If you need help with the housework, they can arrange for someone to come.”One word, no space. Printed on the paper here, but a common gap word in other tests.
stressn.worry and tension caused by a difficult situation压力；精神紧张“It seems to me you’re under quite a bit of stress.”Answer to Q10. The note says ‘help to reduce ___’; the audio says ‘advice on how to minimize it’. Follow the pronoun back.
minimizev.to make something as small as possible使减到最少；尽量减少“They may be able to give you some advice on how to minimize it.”Direct paraphrase of ‘reduce’ on the question paper — the trigger for Q10.
lose touch withphr.to stop having contact with someone失去联系“I’m losing touch with my friends and colleagues.”The opening line, which sets up why the carer needs ‘a break’ (Q1).

✏️ Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening the audio never uses the exact words printed on the question paper. Recognising paraphrase is the whole skill. 雅思听力中，录音几乎不会重复题干原词——识别同义替换才是关键。
Paraphrase Example 1
Question wording“This can give the carer… a (1)”
What you hear“Carers sometimes need time for all the other responsibilities they have… and also that they sometimes need a break.”
Key paraphraseThe first bullet (‘time for other responsibilities’) is printed on the paper. The word ‘also’ tells you the second bullet is coming.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question wording“how much (2) the caring involves”
What you hear“They’d want to know the amount of time you spend looking after your mother every day.”
Key paraphrasehow much X = the amount of X. ‘How much’ also proves the noun is uncountable.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question wording“helping her have a (3)”
What you hearFriend: “if she needs help with getting dressed, for example” — Woman: “And also, I help her get into the shower in the morning.”
Key paraphraseThe example in the audio (dressing) is already printed on the paper; the ANSWER is the extra item the other speaker adds.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question wording“dealing with (4)”
What you hear“They’ll probably ask you… whether she can cope using money.” — “That’s becoming a bit of a problem.”
Key paraphrasedeal with = cope with = manage. The confirmation (‘that’s becoming a problem’) tells you this really is a difficulty.

Paraphrase Example 5
Question wording“(6) her” and “preventing a (7)”
What you hearFriend: “are there any physical difficulties…? Lifting her, for example.”… “advice about… how to avoid the possibility of your mum having a fall.”
Key paraphrasepreventing = avoiding the possibility of. And the note layout ‘(6) her’ forces the -ing form lifting.

Key paraphrasefuel = petrol, so petrol is already on the paper. ‘too’ introduces the missing second item.

Review Quiz

Q1
B. She needed more time to care for her mother.
The colleague says ‘you must be relieved to have more time to look after your mother’. The word ‘break’ in option C is a distractor from Q1.
Q2
B. a period of rest
‘They sometimes need a break’ = a rest from caring. That is the answer to Q1, and it must be singular because the note prints ‘a ___’.
Q3
B. the amount of time you spend looking after your mother
‘How much’ = ‘the amount of’, and it also proves the noun must be uncountable, so time fits.
Q4
B. It is already printed on the question paper as an example.
The paper lists ‘help with dressing’ above the gap. Words already printed can never be the answer — keep listening for the speaker’s extra example, shower.
Q5
B. The mother finds handling money difficult now.
The woman replies ‘that’s becoming a bit of a problem. She used to be very good at it, but not any more.’ This confirms money for Q4.
Q6
C. the -ing form: lifting
The phrase is the subject of a bullet point, so a gerund is needed. The audio also says ‘Lifting her, for example’ — copy exactly what you hear.
Q7
C. how to avoid the possibility of your mum having a fall
‘Avoid the possibility of’ is the paraphrase of ‘preventing’. Options A and B are about the carer’s back, which belongs to Q6.
Q8
B. It signals the move from the assessment to the types of support.
This is a section-change signal: everything after it belongs to Q8–Q10 on the second page of the notes.
Q9
C. ‘Fuel’ is already printed on the paper and means the same thing.
The note reads ‘fuel and (9)’. Fuel = petrol, so the missing item is the other one the friend adds: insurance.
Q10
B. stress
One sentence earlier: ‘you’re under quite a bit of stress’. Reduce = minimize, so Q10 is stress. Pronouns often hide the link to the answer.
Q11
B. once or twice a week
‘They can arrange for someone to come along once or twice a week.’ A frequency detail like this is not tested here, but the same phrasing is often a gap in other tests.
Q12
B. Read the three headings first and predict the word type for each bullet.
There is no second listening in IELTS. The headings (support / assessment / types of support) tell you where the recording is, and the small words (a, how much, loss of) tell you the grammar.
