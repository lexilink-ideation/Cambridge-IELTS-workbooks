Cambridge IELTS 10 — Test 4 — Reading Passage 1: The Megafires of California

Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

Wildfires in the western United States — especially Southern California — are getting bigger, hotter and more erratic. The passage gives five causes: drought, a century of fire suppression that let underbrush build up, climate change, longer fire seasons, and building homes in wooded areas. The second half reports that California's preparedness has improved a great deal.

这是一篇新闻报道式说明文。前半段讲原因(填空题的来源),后半段讲应对与改进(判断题的来源),分界非常清楚。

💡 Reading tip: Notes-completion (Q1–6) always follows the passage in order, and here every answer is in the first four paragraphs. The T/F/NG items (Q7–13) all come from the second half. Split the passage in two before you start and you will never search in the wrong place.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

There's a reason fire squads battling more frequent blazes in Southern California are having such difficulty containing the flames, despite better preparedness than ever and decades of experience fighting fires fanned by the 'Santa Ana Winds'.
Main ClauseThere's a reason (that) fire squads … are having such difficulty containing the flames
Subordinate / Relative'battling more frequent blazes in Southern California' = reduced relative describing the squads; 'despite better preparedness than ever and decades of experience' = concession phrase; 'fighting fires fanned by the 'Santa Ana Winds'' = two more reduced clauses stacked
Grammar NoteFour reduced structures in one sentence. Notice the contact relative after 'reason' (the word 'that' is omitted) — very common in journalism.
Simple MeaningFirefighters in Southern California struggle to control fires even though they are better prepared and more experienced than ever. There is a reason for this.
中文尽管准备之充分前所未有,又有数十年扑救“圣安娜风”助燃火灾的经验,南加州的消防队在应对日益频繁的火灾时仍如此难以控制火势——这是有原因的。
Megafires, also called 'siege fires', are the increasingly frequent blazes that burn 500,000 acres or more — 10 times the size of the average forest fire of 20 years ago.
Main ClauseMegafires are the increasingly frequent blazes
Subordinate / Relative'also called 'siege fires'' = reduced relative in apposition; 'that burn 500,000 acres or more' = defining relative clause; '— 10 times the size of the average forest fire of 20 years ago' = appositive comparison after a dash
Grammar NoteDefinition sentence: term + alternative name + defining clause + comparison. This single sentence supplies Q2.
Simple MeaningMegafires burn at least 500,000 acres — ten times the average forest fire of twenty years ago.
中文“巨型火灾”(也称“围困型火灾”)是指日益频繁、燃烧50万英亩以上的大火——相当于二十年前平均森林火灾规模的十倍。
Another reason, experts say, is related to the century-long policy of the US Forest Service to stop wildfires as quickly as possible. The unintentional consequence has been to halt the natural eradication of underbrush, now the primary fuel for megafires.
Main ClauseAnother reason is related to the century-long policy … / The unintentional consequence has been to halt the natural eradication of underbrush
Subordinate / Relative'experts say' = interrupting reporting clause; 'to stop wildfires as quickly as possible' = infinitive defining the policy; 'now the primary fuel for megafires' = appositive describing underbrush
Grammar NoteTwo sentences forming one causal unit: policy → unintended consequence. The final appositive is the source of Q4.
Simple MeaningFor a century the Forest Service put out fires fast; the accidental result is that brush was never cleared, and it is now the main fuel.
中文专家说,另一个原因与美国林务局长达一个世纪的“尽快扑灭野火”政策有关。其非本意的后果是,阻止了林下灌木的自然清除,而这些灌木如今成了巨型火灾的主要燃料。
'We are increasingly building our homes in fire-prone ecosystems,' says Dominik Kulakowski … 'Doing that in many of the forests of the western US is like building homes on the side of an active volcano.'
Main ClauseWe are increasingly building our homes in fire-prone ecosystems / Doing that … is like building homes on the side of an active volcano
Subordinate / Relative'Doing that in many of the forests of the western US' = gerund phrase as subject; 'like building homes on the side of an active volcano' = simile
Grammar NoteA gerund phrase as the subject of the second sentence, followed by a simile with 'is like + -ing'.
Simple MeaningWe keep building houses in places that burn — which is like building on an active volcano.
中文“我们正越来越多地把住宅建在易燃的生态系统之中,”多米尼克·库拉科夫斯基说……“在美国西部的许多森林里这样做,就好比在活火山的山坡上盖房子。”
Stung in the past by criticism of bungling that allowed fires to spread when they might have been contained, personnel are meeting the peculiar challenges of neighborhood- and canyon-hopping fires better than previously, observers say.
Main Clausepersonnel are meeting the peculiar challenges … better than previously
Subordinate / Relative'Stung in the past by criticism of bungling' = past participle clause of reason; 'that allowed fires to spread' = relative clause; 'when they might have been contained' = time clause with past modal; 'observers say' = reporting tag
Grammar NoteThe sentence opens with a past participle clause (= 'Because they have been stung…'). 'Might have been contained' = a past possibility that did not happen. This sentence proves Q9 TRUE.
Simple MeaningAfter being criticised for handling fires badly, staff now deal much better with fires that jump between neighbourhoods and canyons.
中文过去曾因处置失当、致使本可控制的火势蔓延而受到批评的刺痛,如今消防人员在应对在社区与峡谷之间跳跃的火灾这一特殊挑战上,比以往做得更好——观察人士如是说。
'Notwithstanding all the damage that will continue to be caused by wildfires, we will no longer suffer the loss of life endured in the past because of the fire prevention and firefighting measures that have been put in place,' he says.
Main Clausewe will no longer suffer the loss of life
Subordinate / Relative'Notwithstanding all the damage that will continue to be caused by wildfires' = concession phrase with a relative clause; 'endured in the past' = reduced relative describing 'loss of life'; 'because of the fire prevention and firefighting measures that have been put in place' = reason phrase with a relative clause
Grammar NoteConcession + main clause + reason, with three embedded relative clauses. 'Notwithstanding' = 'despite'. This sentence makes Q13 FALSE.
Simple MeaningEven though wildfires will keep causing damage, deaths will no longer be as high, thanks to the new prevention and firefighting measures.
中文“尽管野火仍将继续造成种种损失,但由于已经落实的防火与灭火措施,我们将不再遭受过去那样的生命损失,”他说。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

1. Problem & explanation (问题与解释). The first half asks a single question: why are Southern California's fires now so hard to fight? Five answers follow, and the passage numbers three of them for you: 'One explanation…', 'Another reason…', then 'Three other factors contribute to the trend… First is climate change… Second is fire seasons… Third is increased construction of homes.' Those ordinal markers are gifts — they map directly onto the note-completion gaps.

2. Unintended consequence (非本意的后果). The most interesting piece of logic in the passage is the reversal: a policy designed to reduce fire damage has increased it. Stopping every small fire prevented 'the natural eradication of underbrush', which is 'now the primary fuel for megafires'. IELTS often builds TRUE/FALSE items around this kind of paradox, so mark it when you see it.

3. Comparison with the past (与过去比较). Almost every fact is comparative: fires are 'hotter, faster, and spread more erratically than in the past'; megafires are '10 times the size of the average forest fire of 20 years ago'; fire seasons are '78 days longer than they were 20 years ago'; temperatures are up 1°F. The whole note-completion task is headed 'compared to the past' — so every answer must be a comparative fact.

4. The turn (转折). 'That said, many experts give California high marks for making progress on preparedness in recent years.' This two-word signal ('That said') divides the passage. Everything before it is bad news; everything after it is good news. Q8 to Q13 all live on the good-news side, which is why four of them are TRUE or FALSE rather than NOT GIVEN.

5. Then and now (今昔对照). The second half is a list of past complaints answered by present improvements: dilapidated equipment → 'more up-to-date engines, planes, and helicopters … have been fulfilled'; poor communications with other jurisdictions → 'a commission examined and revamped communications procedures'; criticism of bungling → 'personnel are meeting the peculiar challenges … better than previously'. Each pair is one question.

6. Voices and attribution (引语与出处). Four named speakers carry the argument: Dominik Kulakowski (biologist — the volcano simile), Terry McHale (firefighters' union — open space and state support), Kim Zagaris (Office of Emergency Services — communications), Randy Jacobs (lawyer and evacuee — overall improvement). When a question names a person, go straight to the quotation marks; when it does not, the claim is usually the journalist's own summary.
