Listening Part 3

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 1 — Listening Part 3

Research into Loneliness · Multiple Answer + Multiple Choice (Q21–30)

📋 Quick Review

Two students, Tamara and Dev, discuss what they have found in their research into loneliness and plan the presentation they are going to give. They talk about why loneliness has been rising for twenty years, which health risks are supported by solid evidence, what they think of the evolutionary theory of loneliness, and how loneliness relates to depression and to solitude. Six of the ten questions ask for TWO letters, and almost all of them require the students to agree — so one speaker’s opinion is never enough.

Who is speaking? Two university students preparing a presentation: Tamara (female) and Dev (male). They take turns raising a topic, and the other one either backs it up or pushes back.

The situation 情境: 两名学生 Tamara 和 Dev 讨论他们关于「孤独感 loneliness」的研究，并准备做展示。内容依次是：孤独感上升的原因、哪些健康风险有可靠证据、进化论解释、孤独与抑郁症的关系，以及独处（solitude）与孤独（loneliness）的区别。注意题目问的是「两人都认为 both / agree」，所以必须听两个人的态度。

Task type: Q21–22, Q23–24, Q25–26 = choose TWO letters, A–E (order does not matter; one mark per correct letter). Q27–30 = multiple choice, A, B or C, under the heading ‘Loneliness and mental health’.

💡 Listening strategy tip: Every “choose TWO” question here contains the words both or agree. That single word changes the whole task: you are not listening for a topic being mentioned, you are listening for the second speaker’s reaction. Build a quick five-line grid before the audio (A–E down the side, Tamara and Dev across the top) and put a tick, a cross or a question mark in each cell. Agreement signals: I think you’re right / Yeah, it’s true / True / unquestionably / Good point / Me neither. Disagreement or doubt signals: But… / I’m not sure / I don’t know about that / I suppose so / not exactly reliable. A topic that gets a “but” is almost always a wrong letter, however clearly it was mentioned.
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • Q21–22 — every option is mentioned. Social media, longer lifespans and smaller families are all raised and then rejected. Only urban design (‘I think you’re right’) and the mobile workforce (‘Yeah, it’s true’) get agreement.
  • Q21–22 — “I suppose so” is not agreement. Tamara raises smaller families, Dev objects that this changed decades ago, and she gives in. A reluctant ‘I suppose so’ followed by ‘a more plausible explanation’ means the first idea has been dropped.
  • Q23–24 — obesity and smoking are comparisons, not risks. Loneliness is compared to smoking 15 cigarettes a day and to obesity. Dev then says he doubts the evidence for those claims.
  • Q23–24 — strong evidence words vs weak evidence words. Cancer: ‘supported by other studies’; immune system: ‘no doubt’, ‘unquestionably… sound’. Dementia: ‘not exactly reliable’; heart disease: ‘hard to reach a judgement’, ‘too subjective’.
  • Q29 — the answer is the idea Tamara adds, not the one Dev quotes. ‘More optimistic and relaxed’ sounds exactly like option C, but Tamara replies ‘I don’t know about that’. The idea they both accept is feeling ‘connected with their community’.
  • Q30 — “which I appreciate” blocks option B. Dev accepts that some solitude is good for well-being; what neither of them understands is why anyone would enjoy being alone.

Word / PhraseTypeSimple Meaning中文Example SentenceIELTS Listening Note
lonelinessn.the unhappy feeling of being without company孤独感“Loneliness has been increasing steadily for the last 20 years.”The topic word. Keep it separate from solitude, which is being alone by choice (Q30).
solituden.the state of being alone, often by choice and enjoyed独处“A certain amount of solitude is beneficial for well-being.”Key to Q30. ‘Being alone and being lonely aren’t the same thing.’
eye-openern.something that teaches you a surprising new fact让人大开眼界的事“It’s been a real eye-opener.”Opening line of the discussion — a signal that surprising research findings are coming.
steadilyadv.gradually and continuously, without stopping稳步地；持续地“Loneliness has been increasing steadily for the last 20 years.”Trend language. Also useful in Writing Task 1.
lifespann.the length of time a person lives寿命“So nothing really to do with longer lifespans.”Q21–22 distractor (D): it is raised only to be dismissed — loneliness affects young people just as much.
isolatedadj.alone and separated from other people孤立的；与外界隔绝的“Far from making me feel isolated, it actually does the opposite.”Note ‘far from + -ing’ = the exact opposite is true. Kills option A (social media).
high-riseadj.describing a very tall building with many floors高层的“People living in high-rise flats with not much opportunity to speak to their neighbours.”Evidence for Q21–22 (C). High-rise flats = a feature of urban design.
extended familyn.a family group including grandparents, aunts, cousins and so on大家庭；扩展家庭“People… don’t live in large extended family groups.”The opposite of a nuclear family (option B) — the option Dev rejects.
plausibleadj.reasonable and likely to be true看起来合理的；可信的“A more plausible explanation is that people are having to move around for work.”A comparative signal: ‘a MORE plausible explanation’ means the previous idea has just been downgraded. Points straight to answer E.
claimn./v.a statement that something is true, without proof yet说法；断言“There are claims that loneliness has as much impact as smoking 15 cigarettes a day.”In academic Listening, ‘claims’ usually introduces something that will be questioned.
obesityn.the condition of being extremely overweight肥胖症“Or similar to the risks caused by obesity.”Q23–24 distractor (D): obesity is what loneliness is compared to, not a risk caused by loneliness.
immune systemn.the body’s natural defence against illness免疫系统“There’s no doubt that loneliness contributes to a weakened immune system.”Answer to Q23–24 (A). Confirmed by ‘unquestionably the data on that is sound’.
dementian.an illness that damages memory and thinking, usually in old age痴呆症“Some found no association between loneliness and dementia and others found the opposite.”Q23–24 distractor (B): contradictory findings = ‘not exactly reliable’.
cardiovascularadj.relating to the heart and blood vessels心血管的“A lot of research on cardiovascular disease and whether loneliness contributes to that.”Q23–24 distractor (E): plenty of research, but ‘hard to reach a judgement’.
associationn.a connection or link between two things关联；联系“Some found no association between loneliness and dementia.”Paraphrase family: association / link / connection / relationship / correlation.
subjectiveadj.based on personal feelings rather than facts主观的“The responses from participants were too subjective.”A criticism of research quality — a clear signal that the evidence is NOT solid.
soundadj.reliable and based on good reasoning可靠的；站得住脚的“Unquestionably the data on that is sound.”Adjective use, not the noun ‘a sound’. ‘Sound data’ = solid evidence (Q23–24).
evolutionaryadj.relating to how living things developed over a very long time进化的“What did you think about the evolutionary theory of loneliness?”Signals the start of the Q25–26 section. Noun: evolution; verb: evolve.
convincingadj.making you believe that something is true有说服力的“I thought the idea… quite convincing.”Careful: Tamara finds part of the theory convincing. That is NOT one of the shared opinions in Q25–26.
neuronn.a nerve cell in the brain神经元“A group of neurons in our brains which influence social behaviour.”Part of the claim Tamara says there is not enough evidence for → supports answer B.
conclusivelyadv.in a way that proves something completely决定性地；确凿地“A few studies support the theory, but not conclusively enough.”Key to Q25–26 (B). Followed directly by ‘more evidence is needed’.
clinicaladj.relating to the medical treatment of patients临床的“Unlike depression, loneliness has no recognised clinical form.”Sets up Q27: no clinical form → no diagnosis → no treatment.
diagnosisn.the act of identifying an illness诊断“There’s no available diagnosis or effective treatments.”Key to Q27. Add ‘that’s not likely to change’ and you get ‘doubt there will ever be a cure’.
relate tophr. v.to understand and identify with someone’s experience产生共鸣；感同身受“Everyone will be able to relate to that.”Key to Q28. ‘Everyone can relate to it’ = a situation most students will recognise.
embarrassedadj.feeling ashamed or uncomfortable about something尴尬的；难为情的“A lot of students were probably too embarrassed to admit to it.”An extra comment, not the reason for choosing the example — do not let it pull you to Q28 A.
optimisticadj.expecting good things to happen乐观的“Interactions with shop assistants… make people feel more optimistic.”Q29 distractor (C = ‘feel more positive’). Tamara answers ‘I don’t know about that’, so it is not agreed.
connectedadj.feeling that you are part of a group有联系的；有归属感的“It must make people feel more connected with their community.”Key to Q29 (A). connected with your community = a sense of belonging.
strike up a conversationphr.to start talking to someone you do not know主动攀谈“You need to be a certain kind of person to be able to just strike up a conversation.”A qualification, not a disagreement — Dev has already said ‘True’.
beneficialadj.having a good effect有益的“A certain amount of solitude is beneficial for well-being.”Dev accepts this (‘which I appreciate’), which is why option B is wrong in Q30.
can’t standphr.to hate; to be unable to tolerate受不了；无法忍受“Some people can’t stand being by themselves while others love it.”The contrast ‘can’t stand… while others love it’ is what Tamara calls ‘strange’ — the route to Q30 C.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening the audio never uses the exact words printed on the question paper. Recognising paraphrase is the whole skill. 雅思听力中，录音几乎不会重复题干原词——识别同义替换才是关键。
Paraphrase Example 1
Question wording“Responsible for the increase: urban design” (Q21–22 C)
What you hearTamara: “loneliness has a lot to do with the way cities are designed. People living in high-rise flats with not much opportunity to speak to their neighbours.” Dev: “I think you’re right.”
Key paraphrasethe way cities are designed = urban design; ‘I think you’re right’ is the agreement you need for ‘BOTH’
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question wording“Responsible for the increase: a mobile workforce” (Q21–22 E)
What you hearTamara: “A more plausible explanation is that people are having to move around for work and often end up living miles away from their family.” Dev: “Yeah, it’s true.”
Key paraphrasemove around for work = a mobile workforce. ‘A MORE plausible explanation’ also tells you the previous option (smaller families) has been dropped.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question wording“Based on solid evidence: a weakened immune system” (Q23–24 A)
What you hearDev: “there’s no doubt that loneliness contributes to a weakened immune system.” Tamara: “Unquestionably the data on that is sound.”
Key paraphraseno doubt + unquestionably + sound data = solid evidence, and both speakers say it
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question wording“It needs further investigation” (Q25–26 B)
What you hearTamara: “I don’t think there’s enough evidence to claim…” Dev: “a few studies support the theory, but not conclusively enough. More evidence is needed.”
Key paraphrasenot enough evidence / more evidence is needed = needs further investigation
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 5
Question wording“There will never be a medical cure for loneliness” (Q27 A)
What you hearDev: “unlike depression, loneliness has no recognised clinical form.” Tamara: “There’s no available diagnosis or effective treatments, and that’s not likely to change.”
Key paraphraseno treatment + not likely to change = they doubt a cure will EVER exist
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 6
Question wording“A situation most students will recognise” (Q28 B)
What you hearTamara: “how lonely I was when I started university, being away from home for the first time.” Dev: “Good idea. Everyone will be able to relate to that.”
Key paraphraseeveryone can relate to it = most students will recognise it. The later comment about being ‘embarrassed’ is not the reason.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 7
Question wording“It creates a sense of belonging” (Q29 A)
What you hearDev: “interactions with shop assistants… make people feel more optimistic and relaxed.” Tamara: “I don’t know about that, but it must make people feel more connected with their community.” Dev: “True.”
Key paraphraseconnected with their community = a sense of belonging. ‘I don’t know about that’ cancels the ‘more positive’ option.

Official Test Answers Questions 21–30

21 & 22
C, E (in either order)
C (urban design): ‘loneliness has a lot to do with the way cities are designed… high-rise flats’ → Dev: ‘I think you’re right.’ E (a mobile workforce): ‘people are having to move around for work’ → Dev: ‘Yeah, it’s true.’ A is rejected (‘more good than harm’), D is rejected (‘nothing really to do with longer lifespans’), B is rejected by Dev (‘that all changed decades ago’).
23 & 24
A, C (in either order)
A (weakened immune system): ‘there’s no doubt’ + ‘unquestionably the data on that is sound’. C (cancer): the Finnish study (+10 per cent) and ‘those findings have been supported by other studies’, confirmed by ‘you’re right about that one’. B is ‘not exactly reliable’, E is ‘hard to reach a judgement’, D is only a comparison Dev doubts.
25 & 26
A, B (in either order)
A (little practical relevance): ‘this theory is not really useful when it comes to solving the problem of loneliness today’ → Dev: ‘true’. B (needs further investigation): Tamara ‘there’s not enough evidence’ + Dev ‘not conclusively enough. More evidence is needed.’ Neither calls it misleading or hard to understand, and neither wants it more widely accepted.
27
A
Dev: ‘unlike depression, loneliness has no recognised clinical form.’ Tamara: ‘There’s no available diagnosis or effective treatments, and that’s not likely to change’ — Dev agrees. That is doubt about any future medical cure. They never say the mental-health link is exaggerated (B) or express frustration (C).
28
B
Tamara suggests describing how lonely she was starting university; Dev: ‘Good idea. Everyone will be able to relate to that.’ ‘Relate to’ = recognise. The remark about students being ‘too embarrassed to admit to it’ is an aside, not the reason.
29
A
Dev quotes studies saying strangers make people ‘more optimistic and relaxed’, but Tamara answers ‘I don’t know about that, but it must make people feel more connected with their community’ and Dev says ‘True.’ Connected with your community = a sense of belonging. C is the point they do NOT share.
30
C
Tamara: ‘It’s strange the way some people can’t stand being by themselves while others love it.’ Dev accepts that some solitude is beneficial (‘which I appreciate’ — so B is understood, not puzzling) but ‘being alone isn’t something I actually like… I’d never choose to go on holiday alone’; Tamara: ‘Me neither.’ They also state clearly that solitude and loneliness are NOT the same, which rules out A.
