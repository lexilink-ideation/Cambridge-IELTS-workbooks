Cambridge IELTS 7 — Test 3 — Reading Passage 2

"Population Movements and Genetics" — Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

This passage explores how genetic research has reshaped our understanding of when and how the first humans reached the Americas. It compares older evidence (archaeology, fossils) with newer genetic techniques — blood-protein variants (Gm allotypes) and mitochondrial DNA — that support a "three-wave" theory of migration across the Bering Strait: ancestors of Central/South American Indians, then the Na-Dene (Navajo/Apache), then the Inuit and Aleut.

The passage also brings in non-genetic evidence — dental and linguistic research — which broadly supports the same three-wave pattern, though the text ends by urging caution about exact migration dates.

💡 Reading strategy tip: This is a seven-section passage (A–G) that opens with a "Matching Headings" task. Read each paragraph's first and last sentence first to grasp its main idea before matching a heading — headings test overall paragraph purpose, not small details.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"The best information on early population movements is now being obtained from the 'archaeology of the living body', the clues to be found in genetic material."
Main Clause"The best information... is now being obtained from..."
Subordinate/Appositive"the clues to be found in genetic material" (appositive phrase explaining the metaphor)
Grammar NotePresent continuous passive ("is... being obtained") shows an ongoing process; the infinitive "to be found" functions almost like "which are found."
Simple MeaningScientists now get the best clues about ancient migration by studying genes, not just bones/tools.
中文关于早期人口迁移的最佳信息，如今正来自"活体考古学"——即存在于遗传物质中的线索。
"Thus, by comparing the Gm allotypes of two different populations, one can establish their genetic 'distance', which itself can be calibrated to give an indication of the length of time since these populations last interbred."
Main Clause"one can establish their genetic 'distance'"
Subordinate/Relative Clause"by comparing..." (method clause); "which itself can be calibrated..." (relative clause on "distance"); "since these populations last interbred" (time clause)
Grammar NoteChain of clauses builds a cause→method→result relationship, typical of academic explanation sentences.
Simple MeaningComparing blood-protein types between two groups lets scientists estimate how long ago they last mixed genetically.
中文因此，通过比较两个不同人群的Gm血型变异体，可以确定它们的遗传"距离"，而这一距离又可用来推算这些人群最后一次通婚的时间。
"According to Turner, this ties in with the idea of a single Paleo-Indian migration out of North Asia, which he sets at before 14,000 years ago by calibrating rates of dental micro-evolution."
Main Clause"this ties in with the idea of a single Paleo-Indian migration"
Subordinate/Relative Clause"which he sets at before 14,000 years ago" (relative clause modifying "migration")
Grammar Note"by + -ing" (calibrating) shows the method used to reach the date estimate.
Simple MeaningTurner's dental evidence supports the idea of one early migration wave, which he dates to more than 14,000 years ago.
中文特纳认为，这与"古印第安人一次性从北亚迁入"的观点相符，他通过校准牙齿微观演化速率，将该迁移时间定在一万四千年前。
"Greenberg is in a minority among fellow linguists, most of whom favour the notion of a great many waves of migration to account for the more than 1,000 languages spoken at one time by American Indians."
Main Clause"Greenberg is in a minority among fellow linguists"
Subordinate/Relative Clause"most of whom favour..." (non-defining relative clause); "to account for..." (infinitive of purpose); "spoken at one time by..." (reduced passive relative clause)
Grammar Note"most of whom" is a formal relative structure (quantifier + relative pronoun) common in academic writing.
Simple MeaningMost linguists disagree with Greenberg — they believe there were many migration waves to explain over 1,000 Native American languages.
中文格林伯格在语言学家中属于少数派，大多数同行更倾向于"多次迁移"的说法，以解释美洲印第安人曾使用的一千多种语言。
"Dates given for the migrations should nevertheless be treated with caution, except where supported by hard archaeological evidence."
Main Clause"Dates... should... be treated with caution"
Subordinate Clause"except where supported by hard archaeological evidence" (elliptical conditional/exception clause)
Grammar Note"except where" introduces an exception; the full form would be "except in cases where [dates] are supported by..."
Simple MeaningWe shouldn't fully trust migration dates unless solid archaeological proof backs them up.
中文然而，关于迁移的具体年代仍应谨慎对待，除非有确凿的考古证据支持。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect

Because proteins naturally "drift" and produce variants across generations, populations that have not interbred for a long time develop measurably different Gm allotypes — allowing scientists to calculate genetic "distance" between groups.

Contrast

Greenberg's single "Amerind" language family theory is contrasted with the majority linguistic view of many separate migration waves — the passage presents both sides before noting that genetic/dental evidence now favours Greenberg.

Comparison

Three independent types of evidence — blood-protein genetics (Williams), mitochondrial DNA (Wallace), and teeth (Turner) — are compared, and all three converge on a similar three-wave migration pattern, strengthening the theory.

Examples

Specific named studies are used as supporting examples: Williams's 5,000-person blood sample, Wallace's three Native American groups (Pima-Papago, Maya, Ticuna), and Turner's "thousands of specimens" of teeth.

Definitions

Technical terms are defined in footnotes: "New World," "modern Native American," "Inuit and Aleut," "DNA," "crown/root," "incisor/premolar/molar" — always check these for question clues.

Chronology

The three waves are dated in sequence: Wave 1 (Paleo-Indian) more than 15,000 years ago; Wave 2 (Na-Dene) 14,000–12,000 years ago, with a later move to the US around 600–700 years ago; Wave 3 (Eskimo-Aleut ancestors) about 10,000–9,000 years ago.

Author's Attitude

The tone is cautious and evidence-based — the author presents converging evidence supportively but ends with an explicit warning that "dates... should nevertheless be treated with caution," showing balanced scientific scepticism rather than overclaiming certainty.
