Bullying · Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

This passage reports on the Sheffield Anti-Bullying Intervention Project, led by Professor Peter Smith, which studied bullying in British schools and ways to reduce it. It explains how common bullying is, the harm it causes to both victims and bullies later in life, and how schools have shifted from denying the problem to actively preventing it. The passage outlines the practical steps — from clear policies to playground supervision — that have proven effective at reducing bullying.

💡 Reading strategy tip: This passage moves from problem (how common bullying is, its effects) to explanation (why attitudes changed) to solution (practical steps schools can take). Identifying this three-part structure helps you predict where specific answers are likely to be located.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"Whereas schools once claimed, 'There is no bullying at this school,' most now admit, 'There is not much bullying here, but when it occurs we have a clear policy for dealing with it.'"
Main Clausemost now admit, "There is not much bullying here..."
Subordinate Clause"Whereas schools once claimed, 'There is no bullying at this school'" (contrast clause); the quoted statements function as the direct objects of "claimed" and "admit"
Grammar Note"Whereas" introduces a contrast between a past denial and a present acknowledgement; direct quotations are nested inside the subordinate and main clauses
Simple MeaningSchools used to deny bullying existed, but now openly admit it happens and explain how they handle it.
中文过去学校常说“我们学校没有欺凌现象”，而现在大多数学校承认：“这里欺凌不多，但一旦发生，我们有明确的处理政策。”
"Children who are bullied persistently are more likely, as adults, to have difficulty forming close relationships with other people."
Main ClauseChildren...are more likely...to have difficulty forming close relationships with other people
Subordinate Clause"who are bullied persistently" (relative clause modifying "Children"); "as adults" (parenthetical adverbial); "forming close relationships with other people" (gerund phrase, object of "difficulty")
Grammar NoteThe relative clause defines which children are being discussed; "more likely to + infinitive" expresses probability
Simple MeaningVictims of ongoing bullying often struggle with relationships later in life.
中文长期遭受欺凌的孩子成年后更有可能在与他人建立亲密关系方面遇到困难。
"Three factors, taken together, explain why schools have become better at tackling bullying: greater awareness of the problem, more resources for teachers, and clear evidence that anti-bullying programmes actually work."
Main ClauseThree factors...explain why schools have become better at tackling bullying
Subordinate Clause"taken together" (reduced clause, = "when taken together"); "why schools have become better at tackling bullying" (embedded question, object of "explain")
Grammar NoteThe reduced clause omits the subject and auxiliary for conciseness; the colon introduces a list in apposition to "three factors"
Simple MeaningAwareness, resources, and proof of success together explain why schools now handle bullying more effectively.
中文三个因素共同解释了学校为何在应对欺凌方面做得更好：对该问题认识的提高、教师可获得的资源增多，以及有明确证据表明反欺凌项目确实有效。
"After Norway launched a national campaign against bullying, an evaluation of 42 schools found that bullying had been halved within two years."
Main Clausean evaluation of 42 schools found that bullying had been halved within two years
Subordinate Clause"After Norway launched a national campaign against bullying" (time clause); "that bullying had been halved within two years" (noun clause, object of "found")
Grammar NotePast perfect passive "had been halved" shows an action completed before the evaluation's reporting point; "After" establishes the sequence of events
Simple MeaningNorway's anti-bullying campaign cut bullying by half over two years, according to a follow-up study.
中文挪威发起全国反欺凌运动后，一项针对42所学校的评估发现，两年内欺凌现象减少了一半。
"For an anti-bullying policy to be effective, it should be built gradually, through consultation with pupils, parents and staff, rather than simply imposed by the head teacher."
Main Clauseit should be built gradually, through consultation with pupils, parents and staff
Subordinate Clause"For an anti-bullying policy to be effective" (for-to infinitive clause of condition); "rather than simply imposed by the head teacher" (reduced contrastive clause, = "rather than being simply imposed...")
Grammar Note"For X to be effective" is a for-to infinitive construction expressing condition; "rather than" contrasts two methods, with "being" omitted from the second verb
Simple MeaningA good anti-bullying policy needs input from the whole school community, not just top-down orders from the principal.
中文一项有效的反欺凌政策应当通过与学生、家长和教职员工的协商逐步建立，而不是仅仅由校长强行推行。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect

Cause: persistent bullying in childhood. → Effect: victims are more likely to have interpersonal relationship difficulties as adults; children who bully persistently are more likely to be convicted of antisocial offences later in life.

Contrast

Primary schools show more overall bullying (up to a quarter of pupils reporting some experience) but secondary schools, though showing less bullying overall, produce cases that are particularly "recalcitrant" or hard to resolve.

Comparison

Norway's national evaluation (bullying halved across 42 schools in two years) is comparable to the Sheffield project's own findings (16 primary + 7 secondary schools, most reducing bullying) — both show monitored interventions work.

Examples

The passage gives examples of bullying types: verbal (taunting, hurtful nicknames), physical (kicking, shoving), and indirect (social exclusion) — useful for matching-feature style questions.

Definition

A clear anti-bullying "policy" is defined as one that states explicitly what counts as bullying, what will be done if it occurs, what records are kept, and who is informed.

Problem / Solution

Problem: schools historically denied bullying existed ("There is no bullying at this school"). Solution: developing an explicit, consultative anti-bullying policy backed by curriculum work, individual/group support, and playground supervision.

Chronology

Awareness of bullying's severity grows → resource packs are published (Action Against Bullying, 1992; Supporting Schools Against Bullying, 1993; Irish Guidelines, 1993) → evaluation studies (Norway, Sheffield) confirm effectiveness → schools adopt formal policies.

Author's Attitude

The writer is optimistic and constructive, concluding that with these developments schools "can expect to largely prevent the most serious bullying" — a confidence grounded in evaluated evidence rather than mere hope.
