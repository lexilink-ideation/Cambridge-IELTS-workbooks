📋 Quick Review

Steven Godfrey phones a letting agency because he and three friends want to rent a house in town. The agent records his requirements (bedrooms, location, rent, length of let), then describes several available houses with their rooms, rent and a problem with each. Finally they discuss the facilities being built in the popular Devon Close area. It is a typical Part 1 enquiry with a form and a table to complete.

💡 Listening strategy tip: Answers come in order and follow the form and table. Listen for the ‘but’ that signals the real answer (‘around £500 … but we could go up to £600’), and match each house to its problem (no garage, big garden, noisy, etc.). Word limit here: ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER.

🔄 Paraphrase Training

In IELTS the question words are almost never the words you hear or read. Recognising paraphrase is the key skill. Study each example, then write your own sentence.
Paraphrase Example 1
Question / promptQ1: preferred location …
What you hear / read"It’d be nice to be central." — "most houses of that size are in the suburbs."
Key paraphraseThe customer’s wish (central) is the answer, not the agent’s warning (suburbs).
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question / promptQ2: maximum monthly rent …
What you hear / read"We’d like something around £500 … but we could go up to £600 if we have to."
Key paraphraseThe ‘but … up to £600’ gives the maximum; ignore the first figure (£500).

Paraphrase Example 3
Question / promptQ5: problem with the Mead Street house …
What you hear / read"there’s a big garden." — "We’ll be too busy to look after it."
Key paraphraseThe drawback is the big garden they can’t maintain.

Paraphrase Example 4
Question / promptQ9–10: facilities open to the public NOW …
What you hear / read"the swimming pool’s already opened…" (sports centre still ‘under construction’).
Key paraphrase‘at the moment’ rules out the unfinished sports centre → concert hall (B) + swimming pool (E).
