Listening Part 2

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 2 — Listening Part 2

Town Volunteer Scheme · Matching + Multiple Choice (Q11–20)

📋 Quick Review

Steve, who organises the volunteer scheme for Elmley Town Council, is welcoming a group of new volunteers. In the first half he describes six activities volunteers carry out — street patrols, concerts, community groups, the town magazine, lunches for retired people and the website — and you must match each one to the volunteer’s role from a box of nine options. In the second half he turns to practical matters: the three town festivals, the qualities volunteers need, the September start dates and the annual thank-you event, tested by four multiple-choice questions.

Who is speaking? One speaker only — Steve, the organiser of the volunteer scheme at Elmley Town Council, giving an induction talk to a group of people who have just signed up. Semi-formal monologue.

The situation 情境: 英国小镇 Elmley 的镇议会（Town Council）有一个志愿者计划。负责人 Steve 在新志愿者见面会上介绍：前半段讲六项志愿活动，要求把每项活动与志愿者的"具体职责"配对（九选六，多出三个干扰项）；后半段讲三个节庆活动、志愿者需要具备的素质、九月的培训与上岗时间，以及每年答谢志愿者的活动，共四道单选题。

Task type: Q11–16 matching — choose SIX answers from a box of NINE (A–I), each option used once. Q17–20 multiple choice with three options (A, B or C). No spelling risk at all here — you only write letters, so the whole battle is comprehension.

💡 Listening strategy tip: For matching from a box (Q11–16) there are nine options but only six answers, so three options are pure distractors and every one of them will be mentioned somewhere in the recording. Use the 45 seconds of reading time to group the options by idea: A/G are both about performing at an event, F/I are both about tickets and seats, B/C are both about businesses/publicity. Then, while you listen, follow the six activities (which come strictly in order) rather than hunting for the options. Ask one question each time: what does the volunteer actually DO here? For the MCQs (Q17–20) all three options are always mentioned; listen for the comparison or the correction that separates them.
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • F (selling tickets) vs I (helping people find their seats) at the concerts — the audio does mention tickets (‘people buy tickets then don’t check them’), but the volunteers’ job is to get the audience ‘to the right place’, so the answer is I.
  • C (contacting local businesses) is said clearly during the magazine section — but the talk states that advertising is ‘the responsibility of the council’s advertising department’, NOT the volunteers. The volunteers collect feedback (E).
  • G (introducing guest speakers) vs A (providing entertainment) at the retired people’s lunches — there are no guest speakers; the volunteers themselves ‘put on a show… songs and short plays’, so A.
  • Festival sizes (Q17) — the biggest clue is length, and it points the WRONG way. The science festival is only two days but needs the most volunteers because it uses many venues.
  • Q18: three qualities are all mentioned — personal interest, flexibility AND getting on with people. Only the last is marked with ‘What’s essential though’.
  • Three September dates (Q19) — the 2nd is training, the 9th is when duties begin, the 23rd is a review chat. The question asks when they start working.
  • Q20: party and barbecue are both real — but they happened in previous years. ‘Forthcoming’ signals the NEXT one: a canal trip.

updatev.to add the newest information to something更新“It’s updated every day with information about future activities.”Describes the website; the volunteers do not update it, they publicise it (B).
publicityn.the activity of making the public aware of something宣传；公开推广“Volunteers are asked to help by making sure residents know about it.”Q16 answer (B). ‘Making sure residents know about it’ IS publicity.

essentialadj.completely necessary必不可少的；至关重要的“What’s essential though is being able to get on well with other people.”Q18 answer signal. In MCQs, superlative-strength words (essential, most important, above all) mark the answer.
at short noticephr.with very little warning before something happens临时通知；仓促之间“So there can be changes at short notice.”Explains why flexibility is mentioned — useful context, but not the answer to Q18.
timetablev.to arrange for something to happen at a particular time排班；安排时间表“We’ll be timetabling you for duties the following week from the 9th.”Q19 answer signal. ‘Timetabled for duties’ = starting work.
dutyn.a task that is part of your job or role职责；值班任务“Timetabling you for duties the following week.”Q19 asks when volunteers ‘start working’ — that is duties, not training.
annualadj.happening once every year一年一度的“As a thank you to the volunteers we arrange an annual event.”Printed in Q20. Do not confuse with ‘in recent years’, which introduces the past events.
forthcomingadj.happening or coming soon即将到来的“Our forthcoming event is a trip along the canal.”Q20 answer signal. Forthcoming = next. The party and barbecue are past events.
sign upphr. v.to put your name on a list to join something报名；登记参加“If you’d like to attend, you can sign up once you start work.”A very common phrasal verb in Part 2 talks about clubs, courses and events.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening the audio never uses the exact words printed on the question paper. Recognising paraphrase is the whole skill. 雅思听力中，录音几乎不会重复题干原词——识别同义替换才是关键。
Paraphrase Example 1
Question wordingOption D — giving advice to visitors (Q11)
What you hear“Tourists often ask how to get to a particular shop and they might also be grateful for recommendations about what to visit.”
Key paraphrasevisitors = tourists; advice = directions + recommendations. Nobody says the word ‘advice’.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question wordingOption I — helping people find their seats (Q12)
What you hear“Part of the volunteers’ role is to get everyone in the audience to the right place… people buy tickets then don’t check them and head for the wrong section of the hall.”
Key paraphrasethe right place in a hall = their seats. Tickets are mentioned only to explain the problem — volunteers do not sell them (F).
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question wordingOption H — encouraging cooperation between local organisations (Q13)
What you hear“The volunteers talk about how groups can help each other. For instance, a writing group might want to travel… but may not know that another club has a coach they could travel in.”
Key paraphrasegroups helping each other = cooperation between local organisations. The coach example is the proof.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question wordingOption E — collecting feedback on events (Q14)
What you hear“That’s the responsibility of the council’s advertising department. We depend on volunteers though to find out what people think of events they’ve attended and any suggestions they have.”
Key paraphraseThe word though is the switch: everything before it is somebody else’s job. What people think + suggestions = feedback.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 5
Question wordingOption A — providing entertainment (Q15)
What you hear“The volunteers welcome the guests and… some volunteers put on a show, usually around half an hour of songs and short plays.”
Key paraphrasesongs + short plays = entertainment. ‘Welcome the guests’ is a real duty but it is not one of the nine options.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 6
Question wordingOption B — providing publicity about a council service (Q16)
What you hear“The town council has a website… and volunteers are asked to help by making sure residents know about it… we want as many people as possible to use it.”
Key paraphrasemaking sure people know about something = publicity; the council website = a council service.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 7
Question wordingQ18 — the most important requirement
What you hear“It’s a good idea to help at the festival you’re most interested in… We try to use volunteers who are flexible though… What’s essential though is being able to get on well with other people.”
Key paraphraseget on well with other people = interpersonal skills. Rank the language: ‘a good idea’ < ‘we try to’ < ‘what’s essential’.
Your practice

Review Quiz

Q1
B. to create a sense of community and make people feel welcome
Steve states this in his opening: volunteers ‘help to create a sense of community… and make residents and visitors feel welcome at local events’.
Q2
B. Tickets are mentioned only to explain why people go to the wrong section.
The audience ‘buy tickets then don’t check them’ — that is the problem the volunteers solve by guiding people to the right place (I).
Q3
B. that one local group can help another
A writing group wants to travel; another club already owns a coach. Volunteers make the connection — option H, cooperation between local organisations.
Q4
C. the council’s advertising department
Said explicitly. This single sentence is what removes option C (contacting local businesses) from the volunteers’ role in Q14.
Q5
B. They send a summary to the editors.
‘The volunteers then send a summary to the editors.’ This confirms their role is collecting feedback (E), not writing content.
Q6
C. The volunteers put on half an hour of songs and short plays.
Songs and short plays = entertainment. A and B are true but are not among the nine options, and no guest speakers appear, so G is impossible.
Q7
B. It uses quite a lot of venues although it is only two days long.
The comparison ladder is book (3 days) → more for music (a week) → ‘even more’ for science. Length is the distractor; venues are the reason.
Q8
C. What’s essential though is…
In MCQ tasks, strength of language decides. ‘Essential’ outranks ‘a good idea’ (personal interest) and ‘we try to’ (flexibility).
Q9
B. Volunteers have a week of training.
‘A week’s training starting on the 2nd… we’ll be timetabling you for duties the following week from the 9th.’ Q19 asks about starting work = the 9th.
Q10
C. to find out how volunteers feel and what support they need
Steve says they ‘have a chat with each of you to find out how you feel about being a volunteer and what extra support you need’. This third date is a classic Q19 distractor.
Q11
B. It happened last year.
‘In recent years we’ve had a party… and last year a barbecue.’ The forthcoming event is the canal trip — a boat trip (A).
Q12
B. Group similar options in advance, then follow the six activities in order.
Word matching (C) is exactly how the distractors catch you — tickets, businesses and speakers are all audible. The activities, unlike the options, come in a fixed order.
