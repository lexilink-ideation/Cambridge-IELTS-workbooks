Listening Part 3

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 2 — Listening Part 3

Choosing a Human Geography Topic · Matching Opinions + Multiple Choice (Q21–30)

📋 Quick Review

Two university students, Rosie and Colin, are trying to narrow down a topic for their human geography assignment. First they run through five aspects of the subject — population, health, economies, culture and poverty — and you must match each one to the opinion the two of them share. They then settle on urban issues and discuss problems in their own cities, new developments on the outskirts, disused industrial sites, Masdar City in Abu Dhabi and an English eco town, tested by five multiple-choice questions.

Who is speaking? Two undergraduates, Rosie and Colin, planning an assignment together. Neither is an expert; they think aloud, disagree politely and correct each other, which is exactly what creates the distractors.

The situation 情境: 两名大学生 Rosie 和 Colin 要为"人文地理"（human geography）作业选题。他们先逐一评价五个分支领域——人口、健康、经济、文化、贫困，每个领域配一句共同看法（七选五，顺序不按题号）；随后把范围缩小到城市问题，讨论各自城市的主要问题、城郊新建设施、废弃工业用地再开发，以及阿布扎比的 Masdar City 和英国的 Greenhill Abbots 生态城，最后五题为单选。

Task type: Q21–25 matching — choose FIVE opinions from a box of SEVEN (A–G), each used once. Q26–30 multiple choice, three options each. You write only letters, so accuracy of understanding is everything.

💡 Listening strategy tip: This is the hardest matching format in the test: opinions, not facts. Seven options, five answers, and the answers do not follow the order printed in the box — they follow the order the students happen to raise the topics. So keep your eyes on the five aspects (population, health, economies, culture, poverty) and let the options come to you. Before you listen, translate each option into one short idea: A = too vague, B = not on our course, C = few statistics, D = easy to research, E = unreliable data, F = no research exists, G = interesting. Then listen for the students’ reaction word: ‘lots of facts’, ‘too general’, ‘taken off the syllabus’, ‘deliberately manipulated’. For the MCQs, remember that in a two-speaker discussion the answer is usually the point both students agree on, not the first idea either of them offers.
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • The five aspects are NOT discussed in question order. The box lists Population, Health, Economies, Culture, Poverty, but the students jump around. Tick each aspect off as it is named rather than waiting for the next number.
  • D (easy to find facts) vs C (only a small number of statistics) — population brings ‘lots of facts and statistics’, which means D. C is the opposite and is never true of any topic here.
  • “Maybe a bit boring” is not an option. Candidates hear a negative comment and reach for A or F. The examinable idea is the availability of facts, not the students’ enthusiasm.
  • A (too vague) belongs to culture — the lecture was ‘too general’ with ‘no useful examples’. It is easy to attach this to health, which they actually found interesting (G).
  • Q26: crime is stated first (theft and robbery) and homelessness is stated last, but Rosie calls unemployment ‘a more serious issue’ that is ‘getting worse and worse’, and says homelessness is ‘not quite so bad as it was’.
  • Q27: shopping centres are the loudest detail — but they are followed by a complaint (city centre shops closing). Only the conference centres get a positive response from both students.
  • Q29: three green features of Masdar City — recycling, pedestrianisation and renewable energy. The deciding line is ‘let’s stick to talking about power sources’.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening the audio never uses the exact words printed on the question paper. Recognising paraphrase is the whole skill. 雅思听力中，录音几乎不会重复题干原词——识别同义替换才是关键。
Paraphrase Example 1
Question wordingOption D — It will be easy to find facts about this (Q21, Population)
What you hear“That would be all about population density and migration and so on. Lots of facts and statistics. Maybe a bit boring.”
Key paraphraselots of facts and statistics = easy to find facts. ‘A bit boring’ is a feeling, not one of the seven options — ignore it.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question wordingOption B — This may not be relevant to their course (Q23, Economies)
What you hear“I thought that had been taken off the syllabus for this year… I’m not sure, but it might be best to avoid it.”
Key paraphraseoff the syllabus = not part of the course = may not be relevant. Note the hedging (‘I thought’, ‘I’m not sure’) — that is why the option says may not.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question wordingOption A — The information given about this was too vague (Q24, Culture)
What you hear“We had that lecture about culture and geography last week. I didn’t get much out of that. It was all too general and the lecturer didn’t give any useful examples.”
Key paraphrasetoo general + no examples = too vague. Colin confirms: ‘I hardly took any notes.’
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question wordingOption E — The facts about this may not be reliable (Q25, Poverty)
What you hear“Dr Lee was saying that you have to be careful with some of the figures relating to poverty. They’re sometimes deliberately manipulated.”
Key paraphrasefigures = facts/data; deliberately manipulated = not reliable. Nobody says ‘no research has been done’, so F is wrong.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 5
Question wordingQ26 — the main problem in Rosie’s city
What you hearColin: “Things like theft and robbery.” Rosie: “Yes, but where I come from, that's linked to another more serious issue, which is that a lot of people don't have jobs. That’s getting worse and worse… people who are homeless, though that’s not quite so bad as it was.”
Key paraphraseunemployment is never named: ‘don’t have jobs’ is the paraphrase. ‘More serious’ ranks it above crime; ‘not so bad as it was’ downgrades housing.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 6
Question wordingQ28 — the drawback of developing disused industrial sites
What you hearRosie: “aren’t the buildings architecturally significant?” Colin: “Not really. And… they often used quite dangerous materials, chemicals… the whole site has to be made safe.” Rosie: “That can’t be cheap. And I bet it’s often not budgeted for.” Colin: “You’re right.”
Key paraphrasenot budgeted for = unexpected. The two other options are explicitly rejected: ‘not really’ (historic buildings) and ‘not doing any harm to the natural environment’.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 7
Question wordingQ29 — what Masdar City will illustrate
What you hear“a city which set out to depend entirely on renewable energy… totally pedestrianised too… big plans for recycling… But let’s stick to talking about power sources.”
Key paraphraserenewable energy = sustainable energy use; ‘power sources’ confirms which of the three green features they will actually write about.

Q1
B. choose and narrow down a topic for an assignment
‘Let’s think about the different aspects of human geography and see if we can narrow the topic down a bit to help us decide.’
Q2
B. Rosie says population involves lots of facts and statistics.
C is the exact opposite of what she says. ‘Lots of facts and statistics’ gives you D, easy to find facts.
Q3
B. The topic is no longer part of the course.
This is the paraphrase behind option B, ‘may not be relevant to their course’. The hedged follow-up, ‘it might be best to avoid it’, confirms it.
Q4
A. It was too general / no useful examples were given.
Vague = general + unsupported by examples. Both students react the same way, which is why it can be a shared opinion.
Q5
B. They are sometimes deliberately manipulated.
Dr Lee’s warning is the source of option E. Note that C would be option F, which is never said about any topic.
Q6
B. Lack of jobs is the most serious and is getting worse; homelessness has improved.
She calls it ‘another more serious issue’ and says it is ‘getting worse and worse’, while homelessness is ‘not quite so bad as it was’. Hence C, unemployment.
Q7
C. Colin points out they have caused city-centre shops to close down.
The question asks what the students are happy about. The shopping centres come with a complaint attached, so they fail the attitude test.
Q8
