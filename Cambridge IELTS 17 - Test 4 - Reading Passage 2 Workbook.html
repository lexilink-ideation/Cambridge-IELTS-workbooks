📋 Passage Overview

Does education fuel economic growth? Professor Sheilagh Ogilvie has built a huge database from the belongings and records of southwest German villagers (1600–1900) to test a puzzle: history shows little evidence that literacy causes growth. Powerful guilds may have blocked people from using their skills. Question types: section matching (Q14–18), summary completion (Q19–22) and two 'choose TWO' sets (Q23–26).

💡 Reading tip: Summary completion says 'ONE WORD from the passage' — the word must fit the grammar of the gap. For 'choose TWO' about a named section, only options supported in that section count.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Ogilvie, who discovered the inventories in the archives of two German communities 30 years ago, believes they may hold the answer to a conundrum that has long puzzled economists.
Main ClauseOgilvie … believes they may hold the answer to a conundrum
Subordinate / Relativewho discovered the inventories… 30 years ago (non-defining relative clause); that has long puzzled economists (defining relative clause describing 'conundrum')
Grammar NoteA non-defining relative clause interrupts the subject; a second relative clause defines the object.
Simple MeaningOgilvie, who found the inventories 30 years ago, thinks they may solve a long-standing puzzle.
中文奥格尔维在30年前于两个德国社区的档案中发现了这些清单,她认为它们或许能解答一个长期困扰经济学家的难题。
Between 1600 and 1900, England had only mediocre literacy rates by European standards, yet its economy grew fast and it was the first country to industrialise.
Main ClauseEngland had only mediocre literacy rates … yet its economy grew fast and it was the first country to industrialise
Subordinate / Relative(no subordinate clause — 'yet' joins contrasting independent clauses)
Grammar Note'yet' signals contrast between average literacy and fast growth.
Simple MeaningEngland's literacy was only average, but its economy still grew fast and industrialised first.
中文1600年至1900年间,按欧洲标准英格兰的识字率仅属中等,但其经济增长迅速,并率先实现工业化。
When Juliana continued taking jobs reserved for male guild members, she was summoned before the guild court and told to pay a fine equivalent to one third of a servant's annual wage.
Main Clauseshe was summoned before the guild court and told to pay a fine
Subordinate / RelativeWhen Juliana continued taking jobs reserved for male guild members (time clause); reserved for male guild members (reduced relative clause); equivalent to one third of a servant's annual wage (phrase describing the fine)
Grammar NoteTime clause + passive main verbs; 'reserved for' = 'which were reserved for'.
Simple MeaningBecause she kept doing men-only guild jobs, she was called to court and fined heavily.
中文当朱莉安娜继续从事只限男性行会成员的工作时,她被传唤到行会法庭,并被要求缴纳相当于仆人年薪三分之一的罚款。
Huge amounts are spent improving education in developing countries, but this spending can fail to deliver economic growth if restrictions block people from using their education in economically productive ways.
Main ClauseHuge amounts are spent improving education … but this spending can fail to deliver economic growth
Subordinate / Relativeif restrictions block people from using their education in economically productive ways (conditional clause)
Grammar NoteContrast with 'but'; conditional 'if…' gives the circumstance under which spending fails.
Simple MeaningBig spending on education may not raise growth if rules stop people using what they learned.
中文发展中国家在改善教育上投入巨大,但如果各种限制阻止人们以经济生产性的方式运用所学,这些投入可能无法带来经济增长。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Problem framing. Section B states the puzzle with counter-examples: low-literacy England grew fast; high-literacy Germany/Scandinavia grew slowly. These counter-examples answer the literacy questions (Q23–24).

Evidence from cases. Individual stories (Ana Regina and Magdalena; Juliana Schweickherdt) illustrate how guild rules blocked skilled work — the source of the summary (Q19–22) and Q16.

Method and implication. 'Holding wealth constant' lets the team isolate education's effect; the wider point is that institutions (like guilds) can stop education producing growth — relevant to Q14 and today.
