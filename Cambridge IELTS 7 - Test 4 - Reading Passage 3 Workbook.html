Effects of Noise · Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

This academic passage reviews psychological research on how noise affects human performance and behaviour. It explains that people can adapt to loud noise fairly quickly, but that predictability and control matter more than volume — unpredictable noise causes more errors and fatigue, even if it isn't louder than predictable noise.

The passage finishes by looking at long-term, chronic noise exposure, citing a study comparing schoolchildren near a busy airport with children in quiet neighbourhoods, showing that noise effects can last for years, not just minutes.

💡 Reading strategy tip: This passage cites several named researchers and years (Glass and Singer 1972, Broadbent 1957, Finkelman and Glass 1970, Cohen et al. 1980/1981). Build a quick mental list of "who found what" — Matching Features questions in this passage depend entirely on keeping these studies separate.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"Most of us have had the experience of having to adjust to sleeping in the mountains or the countryside because it was initially 'too quiet', an experience that suggests that humans are capable of adapting to a wide range of noise levels."
Main ClauseMost of us have had the experience of having to adjust...
Subordinate Clause"because it was initially 'too quiet'" (reason clause); "an experience that suggests that..." (appositive + relative clause + noun clause)
Grammar NoteThe appositive phrase "an experience that suggests..." renames the whole preceding idea and adds a relative clause containing a further noun clause — a layered structure typical of academic writing
Simple MeaningMany people find total silence hard to sleep in at first, which shows humans can adapt to many different noise levels.
中文我们大多数人都有过这样的经历：在山区或乡村睡觉时一开始很不适应，因为那里"太安静了"，这一经历表明人类能够适应各种不同的噪音水平。
"But there are limits to adaptation and loud noise becomes more troublesome if the person is required to concentrate on more than one task."
Main Clausethere are limits to adaptation and loud noise becomes more troublesome
Subordinate Clause"if the person is required to concentrate on more than one task" (conditional clause)
Grammar Note"if" introduces a condition that specifies exactly when adaptation fails — this qualifies and limits the previous paragraph's claim
Simple MeaningAdapting to noise has its limits — it becomes harder when someone must focus on multiple tasks at once.
中文但适应能力是有限的，如果一个人需要同时专注于一项以上的任务，噪音就会变得更加令人困扰。
"We are much more able to 'tune out' chronic background noise, even if it is quite loud, than to work under circumstances with unexpected intrusions of noise."
Main ClauseWe are much more able to "tune out" chronic background noise...than to work under circumstances...
Subordinate Clause"even if it is quite loud" (concessive conditional clause, inserted mid-sentence); "with unexpected intrusions of noise" (prepositional phrase modifying "circumstances")
Grammar Note"more able to X than to Y" is a comparative structure with parallel infinitive phrases; the concessive clause is embedded between the two halves of the comparison
Simple MeaningPeople can more easily ignore constant loud background noise than cope with noise that appears suddenly and unexpectedly.
中文我们更能够"屏蔽掉"长期存在的背景噪音，即使它相当大声，而相比之下，在有意外噪音突然出现的环境下工作则要困难得多。
"If the individual knows that he or she can control the noise, this seems to eliminate both its negative effects at the time and its after-effects."
Main Clausethis seems to eliminate both its negative effects at the time and its after-effects
Subordinate Clause"If the individual knows that..." (conditional clause); "(that) he or she can control the noise" (noun clause, object of "knows")
Grammar Note"both X and Y" creates a parallel structure listing two types of effects that are eliminated together, emphasising the completeness of control's benefit
Simple MeaningSimply believing you can turn off the noise removes both its immediate and its later negative impact.
中文如果个人知道自己可以控制噪音，这似乎既能消除噪音当时带来的负面影响，也能消除其后续影响。
"It should be noted that the two groups of children had been carefully matched by the investigators so that they were comparable in age, ethnicity, race, and social class."
Main ClauseIt should be noted that the two groups of children had been carefully matched by the investigators
Subordinate Clause"that the two groups...had been matched" (noun clause, subject of "it should be noted"); "so that they were comparable in..." (purpose clause)
Grammar Note"It should be noted that" is an impersonal passive structure common in academic writing to add an important caveat without naming who is noting it
Simple MeaningResearchers deliberately made sure the two child groups were similar in background, so the noise comparison would be fair.
中文需要指出的是，研究人员对这两组儿童进行了精心匹配，使他们在年龄、种族、族裔和社会阶层方面具有可比性。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect

Cause: subjects were exposed to unpredictable bursts of noise while completing a task. → Effect: even though performance during the noise stayed similar across groups, unpredictable noise led to significantly more errors on a later proofreading task.

Contrast

The passage contrasts short-term noise bursts (which people adapt to within minutes) with chronic, day-after-day noise exposure (which shows "no evidence of adaptability" in the Cohen et al. school study).

Comparison

Table 1 compares proofreading errors across four conditions: loud/unpredictable (40.1 errors), loud/predictable (31.8), soft/unpredictable (36.7), soft/predictable (27.4) — showing predictability matters more than volume.

Examples

The passage gives concrete task examples to illustrate multi-tasking under noise: monitoring three dials at once (like a pilot), and repeating numbers while tracking a moving line with a steering wheel.

Definition

"Control," as a variable reducing noise's negative effects, is explained precisely: even if a person never actually turns the noise off, simply knowing they *could* is enough to eliminate both immediate and after-effects.

Problem / Solution

Problem: chronic noise near a busy airport was linked to higher blood pressure and greater distractibility in schoolchildren, with no adaptation over time. Implication: unlike brief noise bursts, environments with constant chronic noise pose a genuine long-term concern requiring attention (e.g. moving classrooms), though even that only partly reduced the effect a year later.

Chronology

The passage builds an argument in stages: general adaptability claim → limits of adaptability (multi-tasking) → predictability as the key variable → control as a second variable → chronic, long-term exposure as the final and most serious concern.

Author's Attitude

The writer's tone is cautious and evidence-driven, carefully citing named studies (Glass and Singer, Broadbent, Finkelman and Glass, Cohen et al.) rather than making sweeping claims, and explicitly flagging the more serious, lasting risks of chronic noise exposure.
