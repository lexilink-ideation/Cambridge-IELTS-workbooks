📋 Passage Overview

This passage describes Alaska's commercial salmon fisheries and how they have been managed sustainably for decades. It explains Alaska's geography, the cultural and ecological importance of salmon, and the "In-Season Abundance-Based Management" system that lets biologists halt fishing at any moment to protect fish populations.

The passage then tells the story of a 1999–2000 certification review by the Marine Stewardship Council (MSC), which nearly failed when salmon runs unexpectedly collapsed due to climate factors — yet Alaska's fisheries were ultimately certified as environmentally responsible in September 2000.

💡 Reading strategy tip: This passage is packed with specific numbers, dates and statistics (catch weights, years, percentages). For True/False/Not Given questions, check figures very precisely — a small change in a number (e.g. "over 90%" vs "90% of all Pacific salmon") can flip an answer from TRUE to FALSE.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"The 'predictable abundance of salmon allowed some native cultures to flourish,' and 'dying spawners feed bears, eagles, other animals, and ultimately the soil itself.'"
Main Clause(quoted claim 1) allowed some native cultures to flourish / (quoted claim 2) dying spawners feed bears, eagles...
Subordinate ClauseNo subordinate clause — this is two coordinated quoted statements joined by "and," each a simple main clause
Grammar NoteDirect quotations from a cited author (Susan Ewing) are woven into the sentence — a technique for supporting a claim with an authoritative voice
Simple MeaningA reliable, predictable salmon supply helped native communities thrive, and salmon that die after spawning provide food for wildlife and even nourish the soil.
中文"可预测的丰富鲑鱼资源让一些原住民文化得以繁荣"，而"死去的产卵鱼为熊、鹰、其他动物乃至土壤本身提供了养分。"
"With the onset of statehood, however, the State of Alaska took over management of its own fisheries, guided by a state constitution which mandates that Alaska's natural resources be managed on a sustainable basis."
Main Clausethe State of Alaska took over management of its own fisheries
Subordinate Clause"guided by a state constitution" (reduced participle clause); "which mandates that..." (relative clause); "that Alaska's natural resources be managed..." (noun clause, subjunctive mood after "mandates")
Grammar Note"mandates that + base verb (be managed)" uses the subjunctive form common after verbs of command/requirement in formal English
Simple MeaningOnce Alaska became a state, it began managing its own fisheries under a constitutional rule requiring sustainable resource use.
中文然而，随着阿拉斯加成为一个州，该州开始接管自己渔业的管理，并依据州宪法的规定，要求其自然资源必须以可持续的方式进行管理。
"It is this management mechanism that has allowed Alaska salmon stocks — and, accordingly, Alaska salmon fisheries — to prosper, even as salmon populations in the rest of the United States are increasingly considered threatened or even endangered."
Main ClauseIt is this management mechanism that has allowed Alaska salmon stocks...to prosper
Subordinate Clause"that has allowed...to prosper" (relative clause in a cleft sentence); "even as salmon populations...are increasingly considered..." (concessive time clause)
Grammar Note"It is X that..." is a cleft sentence emphasising the management mechanism as the specific cause; "even as" introduces a simultaneous contrast
Simple MeaningThis particular management system is the reason Alaska's salmon are thriving, while salmon elsewhere in the US are increasingly at risk.
中文正是这种管理机制使阿拉斯加的鲑鱼资源乃至其渔业得以兴旺，而与此同时，美国其他地区的鲑鱼种群却日益被认为面临威胁甚至濒危。
"The crisis was completely unexpected, but researchers believe it had nothing to do with impacts of fisheries."
Main ClauseThe crisis was completely unexpected / researchers believe (that) it had nothing to do with impacts of fisheries
Subordinate Clause"(that) it had nothing to do with impacts of fisheries" (noun clause, object of "believe")
Grammar Note"but" joins two independent clauses to show contrast; the second clause reports researchers' belief using an implied "that"
Simple MeaningNo one predicted the crisis, and scientists don't think fishing itself caused it.
中文这场危机完全出乎意料，但研究人员认为，它与渔业活动的影响毫无关系。
"Certification is for an initial period of five years, with an annual review to ensure that the fishery is continuing to meet the required standards."
Main ClauseCertification is for an initial period of five years
Subordinate Clause"with an annual review to ensure that..." (reduced adverbial phrase); "that the fishery is continuing to meet..." (noun clause, object of "ensure")
Grammar Note"with + noun phrase" adds accompanying circumstance information without forming a full clause — a compact way to add detail
Simple MeaningThe certification lasts five years at first, and is checked every year to confirm standards are still being met.
中文认证的初始有效期为五年，并每年进行一次审查，以确保该渔业持续符合所要求的标准。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect

Cause: overfishing between 1940 and 1959. → Effect: salmon populations crashed so severely that Alaska was declared a federal disaster area in 1953, prompting a complete change in fisheries management after statehood.

Contrast

The passage contrasts Alaska's thriving, well-managed salmon fisheries with salmon populations in the rest of the United States, which are "increasingly considered threatened or even endangered."

Comparison

The passage compares statewide harvests across decades: around 25 million salmon at the start of statehood management, rising to well over 100 million (and sometimes over 200 million) annually during the 1990s.

Examples

Specific examples of "In-Season Abundance-Based Management" in action are given: biologists sitting in streamside counting towers, using sonar, watching from aeroplanes, and talking to fishermen to track adult fish.

Definition

The Marine Stewardship Council (MSC) is defined via a footnote as a joint venture between WWF and Unilever, and functionally described as a body that certifies fisheries meeting high environmental standards.

Problem / Solution

Problem: salmon runs in western Alaska collapsed unexpectedly right before the MSC's certification decision, threatening the outcome. Solution: the state reacted quickly by closing all fisheries, including subsistence fishing, to protect the resource — and certification was still granted months later.

Chronology

The passage moves through Alaska's fishery history in order: 1940s–1959 overfishing crisis → 1959 statehood and constitutional mandate → 1990s record harvests → 1999 MSC review commissioned → 1999–2000 unexpected collapse → September 2000 certification granted.

Author's Attitude

The tone is largely admiring and factual, presenting Alaska's fishery management as a genuine success story, while still acknowledging the uncertainty and risk involved in the certification process during the 1999–2000 crisis.
