Cambridge IELTS 10 — Test 2 — Reading Passage 3: Museums of Fine Art and their Public

Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

Why do people travel to see the original Mona Lisa when reproductions are everywhere? The writer argues that museums deliberately promote the special status of the original, and that this creates a 'treasure house' atmosphere which intimidates visitors, discourages personal response and hands criticism over to the art historian.

这是一篇观点性很强的艺术评论。作者的立场非常明确:博物馆的做法限制了普通观众的体验。做YES/NO/NOT GIVEN时,一定要抓住这条主线。

💡 Reading tip: The summary in Q27–31 has twelve options for five gaps, so use grammar to eliminate: 'has depended on ___ for so long' needs an uncountable noun or plural; 'the ___ are the most important thing' needs a plural; 'instruct ___ to produce copies' needs people. Half the options can be crossed out before you read.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

This might be explained by the fact that the novel has evolved precisely because of technological developments that made it possible to print out huge numbers of texts, whereas oil paintings have always been produced as unique objects.
Main ClauseThis might be explained by the fact
Subordinate / Relative'that the novel has evolved precisely because of technological developments' = appositive noun clause; 'that made it possible to print out huge numbers of texts' = relative clause; 'whereas oil paintings have always been produced as unique objects' = contrast clause
Grammar NoteThree clauses stacked after 'the fact'. 'Whereas' signals a direct contrast — this is where Q27 (mass production) comes from.
Simple MeaningNovels developed because printing allowed huge numbers of copies; oil paintings have always been one-offs.
中文这或许可以这样解释:小说之所以得以发展,正是因为技术进步使大量印刷文本成为可能,而油画则一向是作为独一无二的作品被创作出来的。
With novels, the reader attends mainly to the meaning of words rather than the way they are printed on the page, whereas the 'reader' of a painting must attend just as closely to the material form of marks and shapes in the picture as to any ideas they may signify.
Main Clausethe reader attends mainly to the meaning of words … whereas the 'reader' of a painting must attend just as closely to the material form … as to any ideas
Subordinate / Relative'rather than the way they are printed on the page' = contrastive phrase; 'as … as' = comparison of equality; 'they may signify' = relative clause
Grammar NoteDouble contrast: 'rather than' inside one half, 'whereas' between the halves, plus an 'as … as' equality. Q28's answer ('underlying ideas') comes from 'the meaning of words'.
Simple MeaningNovel readers focus on what the words mean, not how they look; painting viewers must watch the physical marks as carefully as the ideas.
中文读小说时,读者主要关注词语的含义,而非它们在页面上的印刷方式;而“阅读”一幅画的人,必须像关注画作可能表达的观念那样,同样仔细地关注画面中痕迹与形状的物质形态。
In a society that judges the personal status of the individual so much by their material worth, it is therefore difficult not to be impressed by one's own relative 'worthlessness' in such an environment.
Main Clauseit is difficult not to be impressed by one's own relative 'worthlessness'
Subordinate / Relative'In a society that judges the personal status of the individual so much by their material worth' = prepositional phrase containing a relative clause; 'in such an environment' = adverbial
Grammar Note'It is difficult not to' = a double negative meaning 'you cannot help'. Note the ironic quotation marks around 'worthlessness'. This sentence is the whole of Q32.
Simple MeaningIn a society that measures people by money, you cannot help feeling worthless surrounded by priceless art.
中文在一个如此以物质财富来衡量个人地位的社会里,身处这样的环境中,人很难不为自己相对的“无价值感”所触动。
Evidently, nothing the viewer thinks about the work is going to alter that value, and so today's viewer is deterred from trying to extend that spontaneous, immediate, self-reliant kind of reading which would originally have met the work.
Main Clausenothing … is going to alter that value, and so today's viewer is deterred from trying to extend that … kind of reading
Subordinate / Relative'the viewer thinks about the work' = contact relative clause (no 'that'); 'which would originally have met the work' = relative clause with a past modal
Grammar NoteNote the omitted relative pronoun after 'nothing'. 'And so' introduces the consequence — this is the exact evidence for Q33 (their personal reaction is of no significance).
Simple MeaningSince nothing a viewer thinks can change the price, viewers are put off responding personally, as people once did.
中文显然,观者对作品的任何想法都不会改变其价值,因此今天的观者被劝退,不再尝试展开那种自发的、直接的、自主的解读方式——而作品最初本应以这种方式被迎接。
A fundamental difference between paintings and other art forms is that there is no prescribed time over which a painting is viewed.
Main ClauseA fundamental difference … is that there is no prescribed time
Subordinate / Relative'over which a painting is viewed' = relative clause with preposition + which
Grammar Note'Preposition + which' is formal written English ('over which' = 'that a painting is viewed over'). Directly answers Q35.
Simple MeaningUnlike other art forms, a painting has no set length of viewing time.
中文绘画与其他艺术形式的一个根本区别在于:观看一幅画并没有规定的时长。
If appropriate works of fine art could be rendered permanently accessible to the public by means of high-fidelity reproductions, as literature and music already are, the public may feel somewhat less in awe of them.
Main Clausethe public may feel somewhat less in awe of them
Subordinate / Relative'If appropriate works of fine art could be rendered permanently accessible … by means of high-fidelity reproductions' = conditional clause; 'as literature and music already are' = comparison clause with ellipsis
Grammar NoteSecond conditional (could → may). 'As literature and music already are' = 'as literature and music are already rendered accessible' — the verb is left out.
Simple MeaningIf good reproductions made fine art permanently available, as books and recordings do, people would be less intimidated by it.
中文如果能够借助高保真复制品,使合适的美术作品像文学和音乐那样永久地向公众开放,公众对它们的敬畏感或许会有所减弱。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

1. Problem & solution (问题与方案). The whole passage is one long problem statement with a solution in the final paragraph. Problem: museums promote originals, which intimidates visitors and kills personal response. Solution: make fine art 'permanently accessible to the public by means of high-fidelity reproductions, as literature and music already are'. Obstacle: 'those who seek to maintain and control the art establishment' — which is exactly why Q40 is NO.

2. Comparison (比较). The argument runs on a single sustained comparison: painting vs novel (unique object vs mass production; material form vs meaning; no prescribed time vs prescribed temporal sequence) and art criticism vs literary criticism ('spontaneous, participatory criticism … can be found in abundance in criticism of classic works of literature, but is absent from most art history'). Every time the writer mentions novels, music or theatre, it is to expose something about painting.

3. Cause & effect (因果链). Follow it in order: museums present works as treasure → guards, ropes, display cases and architecture reinforce this → visitors feel 'worthless' → visitors are deterred from personal response → the displacement effect plus sheer volume produces superficial viewing → the art historian's specialised approach becomes dominant → spontaneous criticism is suppressed. Each link is a separate question in the paper.

4. Concession (让步). The writer is fair before attacking: 'they accept that fine art is more rewardingly viewed in its original form'; 'despite an implicit recognition that the spread of good reproductions can be culturally valuable'; 'This is in perfect harmony with the museum's function'. Notice how each concession is followed by a but or an Unfortunately.

5. Author's attitude markers (态度标记). Read these words and the writer's position is obvious: Unfortunately (twice), severe limitations, particularly distressing, put paid to, suppressed, too much to ask. The scare quotes around 'treasure houses', 'worthlessness', 'authentic' and 'original' show scepticism about those labels. For YES/NO/NOT GIVEN, any statement that praises the current museum model will be NO.

6. What is NOT discussed (无信息). Two of the final five questions are NOT GIVEN. Q36 adds 'using a range of media' — the passage discusses the art historian's approach but never says which media it should use. Q39 adds a condition about selling reproductions only if they are high quality — the passage advocates high-fidelity reproductions but says nothing about sales conditions. Train yourself to spot the extra qualifier that the passage never mentions.
