📋 Passage Overview

Driverless cars. The passage examines the rise of automated vehicles: the different levels of automation (from driver assistance to full self-driving), the claimed benefits — fewer accidents (since most are caused by human error), less congestion, and new car-sharing models that could cut private ownership — and the uncertainty over how total mileage might change. Question types include paragraph matching (Q14–18), summary completion (Q19–22) and multiple choice — choose TWO (Q23–26).

💡 Reading tip: For 'which paragraph contains…' matching, scan for the specific detail, not the topic — the same theme can appear in several paragraphs. For summary completion, predict the part of speech before you search.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Although fully driverless cars are not yet common on public roads, many vehicles already include features, such as automatic braking, that take over part of the driving task.
Main Clausemany vehicles already include features … that take over part of the driving task
Subordinate / Relative'Although fully driverless cars are not yet common' = concession clause; 'such as automatic braking' = example; 'that take over…' = relative clause defining 'features'
Grammar NoteConcession ('Although…') + main clause containing an example phrase and a relative clause.
Simple MeaningEven though full self-driving cars are rare, many cars already do part of the driving, like braking by themselves.
中文虽然全自动驾驶汽车在公路上还不常见,但许多车辆已经具备诸如自动刹车之类、能接管部分驾驶任务的功能。
Because the great majority of road accidents are caused by human error, supporters argue that removing the driver could dramatically improve safety.
Main Clausesupporters argue that removing the driver could dramatically improve safety
Subordinate / Relative'Because the great majority of road accidents are caused by human error' = reason clause; 'that removing the driver could…' = noun clause (what supporters argue)
Grammar NoteFront reason clause + reporting verb 'argue' + that-clause with a gerund subject ('removing').
Simple MeaningSince people cause most crashes, supporters say taking the driver away could make roads much safer.
中文由于绝大多数交通事故都是人为失误造成的,支持者认为去掉司机可以大大提高安全性。
If people are able to summon a shared vehicle whenever they need one, private ownership may decline, but it is unclear whether the total distance driven will rise or fall.
Main Clauseprivate ownership may decline, but it is unclear whether …
Subordinate / Relative'If people are able to summon a shared vehicle…' = conditional clause; 'whether the total distance driven will rise or fall' = noun clause after 'unclear'
Grammar NoteConditional 'if' clause + two independent clauses joined by 'but', the second holding a whether-clause.
Simple MeaningIf people can call a shared car anytime, fewer may own cars — but nobody knows if total driving will go up or down.
中文如果人们随时都能召唤一辆共享车辆,私家车拥有量可能下降,但行驶总里程是升还是降尚不清楚。
Engineers caution that widespread automation will demand not only reliable technology but also new rules and, in some cases, changes to the roads themselves.
Main ClauseEngineers caution that widespread automation will demand …
Subordinate / Relative'that widespread automation will demand not only … but also …' = noun clause with a 'not only … but also' structure
Grammar NoteReporting verb 'caution' + that-clause using correlative conjunctions 'not only … but also'.
Simple MeaningEngineers warn that mass automation needs reliable tech, new rules, and sometimes changes to the roads.
中文工程师警告说,大规模自动化不仅需要可靠的技术,还需要新的规则,有时甚至需要改造道路本身。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Claim and counter-claim. The passage balances benefits (safety, less congestion) against uncertainties (mileage, cost, rules). For 'choose TWO' questions, look for the points the writer clearly endorses, not every point mentioned.

Cause and effect. Human error → accidents → automation could cut them; shared vehicles → less ownership → uncertain effect on mileage. Tracking these chains answers the summary (Q19–22).

Contrast signposts. Words like 'although', 'but', 'however' and 'it remains to be seen' mark where the writer qualifies a claim — exactly where NOT GIVEN-style traps and 'choose TWO' distractors live.
