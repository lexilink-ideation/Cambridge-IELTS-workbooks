📋 Passage Overview

Environmental practices of big businesses. The writer argues that companies may maximise short-term profit by harming the environment, and that big businesses can in turn pressure their suppliers. Using the example of BSE and a fast-food company, he claims the public ultimately holds the power — by rewarding good behaviour and punishing bad — so changes in public attitudes are essential for change. Question types: summary with a word list (Q27–31), multiple choice (Q32–34) and YES/NO/NOT GIVEN (Q35–40).

💡 Reading tip: This is an argument, so YES/NO/NOT GIVEN tests the writer's opinion. NO = the writer would disagree; NOT GIVEN = he never expresses a view. For the word-list summary, match both meaning and grammar.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Depending on the circumstances, a business may maximize the amount of money it makes, at least in the short term, by damaging the environment and hurting people.
Main Clausea business may maximize the amount of money it makes
Subordinate / Relative'Depending on the circumstances' = condition phrase; 'it makes' = contact relative clause; 'by damaging the environment and hurting people' = means phrase
Grammar NoteFront condition phrase + main clause + a 'by + -ing' means phrase.
Simple MeaningIn some situations a business can make the most money, at least for a while, by harming the environment and people.
中文视情况而定,企业至少在短期内可能通过破坏环境、损害他人来使其利润最大化。
But for five years the meat packers refused to follow these, claiming that they would be too expensive to obey.
Main Clausethe meat packers refused to follow these
Subordinate / Relative'for five years' = time phrase; 'claiming that they would be too expensive to obey' = participle phrase with an embedded that-clause
Grammar NoteMain clause + an '-ing' participle phrase reporting their reason.
Simple MeaningFor five years the meat packers wouldn't obey the new rules, saying they were too costly.
中文但在五年间,肉类加工商拒绝执行这些规定,声称遵守它们代价太高。
However, when a major fast-food company then made the same demands after customer purchases of its hamburgers plummeted, the meat industry complied within weeks.
Main Clausethe meat industry complied within weeks
Subordinate / Relative'when a major fast-food company then made the same demands' = time clause; 'after customer purchases of its hamburgers plummeted' = time clause inside it
Grammar NoteTwo nested time clauses ('when… after…') + main clause.
Simple MeaningBut when a big fast-food chain demanded the same after its burger sales crashed, the meat industry obeyed within weeks.
中文然而,当一家大型快餐公司在其汉堡销量暴跌后提出同样要求时,肉类行业在几周内就照办了。
I predict that in the future, just as in the past, changes in public attitudes will be essential for changes in businesses' environmental practices.
Main ClauseI predict that … changes in public attitudes will be essential for changes in businesses' environmental practices
Subordinate / Relative'that … will be essential …' = noun clause; 'just as in the past' = comparison phrase
Grammar NoteReporting verb 'predict' + that-clause + an inserted 'just as…' comparison.
Simple MeaningI predict that, as before, shifts in public attitudes will be essential to change how businesses treat the environment.
中文我预测,未来正如过去一样,公众态度的转变将是企业环境行为改变的关键。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Claim → example → conclusion. The writer states a claim (businesses may profit by harming), gives the BSE/fast-food example, and draws a conclusion (the public is ultimately responsible). Tracking this shape answers the summary and MCQs.

Cause and effect. Public concern → government rules → industry refuses → fast-food chain demands (after sales fall) → industry complies. Q32–34 test who has power and why.

Writer's stance (YES/NO/NG). The final paragraphs make the writer's opinion explicit ('empowering and hopeful', 'the public must accept higher prices'). Match statements to what he actually claims.
