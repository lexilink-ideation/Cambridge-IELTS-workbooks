📋 Passage Overview

This passage, 'Flawed Beauty: the problem with toughened glass', explains why toughened (tempered) glass, although strong, sometimes shatters without warning. Tiny nickel-sulphide particles trapped inside the glass can slowly expand, acting 'like a coiled spring', until the whole toughened pane suddenly breaks. The passage describes how toughened glass is made, why the flaw occurs, and what engineers do to reduce the risk.

💡 Reading strategy tip: Technical passages reward careful reading of process and cause. For matching and completion, keep the mechanism straight: how the glass is made, and why it later fails.

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Process — heat glass → cool surface fast → surface in compression, core in tension → stronger glass. Keep the making steps in order.

Cause of failure — trapped nickel-sulphide particle → slow expansion → 'coiled spring' → sudden shatter. The flaw is internal, not external.

Contrast (strength vs flaw) — Toughened glass is very strong, yet can fail 'without warning'. The title 'Flawed Beauty' captures this paradox.

Solution — Engineers heat-soak or test the glass to trigger faulty panes early, reducing later spontaneous breakage.
