📋 Passage Overview

'UK companies need more effective boards of directors' — after the 2008 financial crisis, the writer argues that the classic model of part-time non-executive directors no longer works. Seven sections cover the spread of blame, the pressure of scrutiny, the committee problem, the 'professional board' proposal, short-termism, executive pay battles, and corporate ethics. Question types: matching headings (Q27–33), YES / NO / NOT GIVEN (Q34–37) and sentence completion (Q38–40).

💡 Reading tip: Corporate-governance vocabulary is dense but repetitive. Learn six words — board, director, non-executive, governance, scrutiny, remuneration — and most of the passage unlocks. For matching headings, notice that each section states its topic in the first or second sentence.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Following the 2008 financial meltdown, which resulted in a deeper and more prolonged period of economic downturn than anyone expected, the search for explanations in the many post-mortems of the crisis has meant blame has been spread far and wide.
Main Clausethe search for explanations … has meant blame has been spread far and wide
Subordinate / Relative'Following the 2008 financial meltdown' = time phrase; 'which resulted in a deeper and more prolonged period of economic downturn than anyone expected' = non-defining relative clause containing a comparison; 'blame has been spread far and wide' = noun clause after 'has meant'.
Grammar NoteA 21-word insertion separates the subject from its verb. Find 'the search… has meant' first, then read the rest.
Simple MeaningAfter the 2008 crash, which caused a worse and longer recession than expected, investigations into its causes have blamed many different parties.
中文2008年金融崩溃引发了比任何人预料都更深、更漫长的经济下行;此后,人们在众多危机剖析中寻找解释,结果是责任被广泛地分摊到各方身上。
At the simplest and most practical level, the time involved in fulfilling the demands of a board directorship has increased significantly, calling into question the effectiveness of the classic model of corporate governance by part-time, independent non-executive directors.
Main Clausethe time involved in fulfilling the demands of a board directorship has increased significantly
Subordinate / Relative'involved in fulfilling the demands of a board directorship' = reduced relative clause modifying 'the time'; 'calling into question the effectiveness of the classic model…' = result participle clause.
Grammar NoteReduced relative + '-ing' result clause. The '-ing' clause carries the writer's argument, so never skip it.
Simple MeaningBeing a director now takes much more time, which makes the old part-time non-executive model look ineffective.
中文从最简单实际的层面看,履行董事会成员职责所需的时间已大幅增加,这使人质疑由兼职独立非执行董事进行公司治理这一经典模式的有效性。
Often, board business is devolved to committees in order to cope with the workload, which may be more efficient but can mean that the board as a whole is less involved in fully addressing some of the most important issues.
Main Clauseboard business is devolved to committees
Subordinate / Relative'in order to cope with the workload' = purpose phrase; 'which may be more efficient but can mean…' = sentential relative clause referring to the whole idea; 'that the board as a whole is less involved…' = noun clause.
Grammar NoteSentential 'which' + a 'may… but can…' concession pair. The 'but' half is the criticism, and the source of Q37.
Simple MeaningWork is often handed to committees, which is efficient but means the full board engages less with the biggest issues.
中文为了应付工作量,董事会事务常被下放给各委员会;这或许更有效率,但也可能意味着董事会整体较少参与充分处理某些最重要的议题。
However, more professional and better-informed boards would have been particularly appropriate for banks where the executives had access to information that part-time non-executive directors lacked, leaving the latter unable to comprehend or anticipate the 2008 crash.
Main Clausemore professional and better-informed boards would have been particularly appropriate for banks
Subordinate / Relative'where the executives had access to information' = relative clause of place; 'that part-time non-executive directors lacked' = relative clause modifying 'information'; 'leaving the latter unable to comprehend or anticipate the 2008 crash' = result participle clause.
Grammar NoteThird-conditional style 'would have been' + a chain of relative clauses. 'the latter' refers back to the non-executive directors — this is the sentence that answers Q38.
Simple MeaningProfessional boards would have suited banks especially, where executives had information that part-time directors did not, leaving those directors unable to foresee the 2008 crash.
中文然而,更专业、信息更充分的董事会本会特别适合银行:在那里,高管掌握着兼职非执行董事所缺乏的信息,以致后者无法理解或预见2008年的崩溃。
The tyranny of quarterly reporting can distort board decision-making, as directors have to 'make the numbers' every four months to meet the insatiable appetite of the market for more data.
Main ClauseThe tyranny of quarterly reporting can distort board decision-making
Subordinate / Relative'as directors have to "make the numbers" every four months' = reason clause; 'to meet the insatiable appetite of the market for more data' = purpose phrase.
Grammar Note'as' = because. The metaphor 'tyranny' carries the writer's disapproval — evaluative nouns are opinion markers.
Simple MeaningReporting every quarter warps board decisions, because directors must hit targets every four months to satisfy the market's endless demand for data.
中文季度报告的暴政会扭曲董事会的决策,因为董事们必须每四个月“做出数字”,以满足市场对更多数据的永不满足的胃口。
The chair of the remuneration committee can be an exposed and lonely role, as Alison Carnwath, chair of Barclays Bank's remuneration committee, found when she had to resign, having been roundly criticised for trying to defend the enormous bonus to be paid to the chief executive; the irony being that she was widely understood to have spoken out against it in the privacy of the committee.
Main ClauseThe chair of the remuneration committee can be an exposed and lonely role
Subordinate / Relative'as Alison Carnwath… found' = comparison clause; 'chair of Barclays Bank's remuneration committee' = appositive; 'when she had to resign' = time clause; 'having been roundly criticised…' = perfect participle clause; 'the irony being that…' = absolute construction.
Grammar NoteAn unusually long sentence built from one main clause plus five subordinate elements. Read it in chunks, following the semicolon as a natural break.
Simple MeaningChairing a pay committee is exposed and lonely: Alison Carnwath resigned after being criticised for defending a huge bonus — ironically, she was thought to have opposed it privately.
中文薪酬委员会主席可能是一个暴露而孤独的角色。巴克莱银行薪酬委员会主席艾莉森·卡恩沃思对此深有体会:她因试图为支付给首席执行官的巨额奖金辩护而备受抨击,最终不得不辞职;讽刺的是,人们普遍认为她曾在委员会内部对此表示反对。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause → consequence chain across sections. Crisis (A) → public scrutiny (B) → heavier workload → devolution to committees (C) → a radical proposal (D). Each section is the consequence of the one before it, which is why the headings must be assigned in sequence rather than individually.

Blame distribution. Section A names five external parties — governments, regulators, central banks, auditors, and bank directors — all 'in the frame'. A list of institutions being blamed is the signature of heading iv.

Efficiency versus engagement. Section C sets up a trade-off: committees are 'more efficient' but the full board becomes 'less involved'. The writer resolves it against efficiency: 'boxes are ticked rather than issues tackled'. Recognising which side the writer takes answers Q37.

Proposal with caveats. Section D offers the professional board, then immediately lists risks — stepping on management's toes, recruitment, remuneration, independence — before conceding it 'would not be appropriate for all companies'. A proposal presented with its own objections is still a proposal (heading viii).

Short-termism as a system, not a fault. Section E blames structures rather than people: 'Regulatory requirements and the structure of the market encourage this behaviour', quarterly reporting, automated trading, incentive schemes. Q39 comes straight from 'short-term financial metrics'.
