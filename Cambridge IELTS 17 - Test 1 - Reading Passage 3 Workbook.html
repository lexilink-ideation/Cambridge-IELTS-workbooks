📋 Passage Overview

A book review: Anna Keay reviews Charles Spencer's To Catch a King, the story of the six-week hunt for King Charles II after his 1651 defeat at the Battle of Worcester. The review praises Spencer's readable, even-handed telling and reflects on why Charles treasured the story. Question types: summary with a list of phrases (Q27–31), YES / NO / NOT GIVEN (Q32–35) and multiple choice (Q36–40).

💡 Reading tip: YES / NO / NOT GIVEN tests the writer's opinion, not facts. NO means the writer would disagree; NOT GIVEN means the writer never states a view. Watch opinion signals: 'clearly', 'succeeds in', 'even-handed'.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

After his father was executed by the Parliamentarians in 1649, the young Charles II sacrificed one of the very principles his father had died for and did a deal with the Scots.
Main Clausethe young Charles II sacrificed one of the very principles … and did a deal with the Scots
Subordinate / RelativeAfter his father was executed… in 1649 (time clause); (that) his father had died for (contact relative clause defining 'principles')
Grammar NoteOpening time clause; a relative clause with the relative pronoun omitted ('principles [that] his father had died for').
Simple MeaningAfter his father was killed in 1649, Charles gave up a belief his father had died for and made a deal with the Scots.
中文1649年父亲被议会派处决后,年轻的查理二世放弃了父亲为之牺牲的一条原则,转而与苏格兰人达成协议。
His intention when asking Pepys to commit his story to paper was to ensure that this most extraordinary episode was never forgotten.
Main ClauseHis intention … was to ensure …
Subordinate / Relativewhen asking Pepys to commit his story to paper (time/adverbial phrase); that this most extraordinary episode was never forgotten (noun clause = what he wanted to ensure)
Grammar NoteSubject 'his intention' + linking verb 'was' + infinitive; embedded that-clause.
Simple MeaningBy asking Pepys to write it down, Charles wanted to make sure this amazing story was never forgotten.
中文查理请佩皮斯把这段经历记录下来,目的是确保这段极不寻常的故事永不被遗忘。
This makes it all the more interesting that Charles II himself loved the story so much ever after.
Main ClauseThis makes it all the more interesting
Subordinate / Relativethat Charles II himself loved the story so much ever after (extraposed subject clause introduced by 'it')
Grammar Note'it' is a placeholder subject; the real subject is the that-clause at the end.
Simple MeaningThat makes it even more interesting that Charles loved telling the story for the rest of his life.
中文这更让人觉得有趣——查理二世本人此后一直如此钟爱这个故事。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Narrative + evaluation. The review mixes the story (what happened to Charles) with the reviewer's evaluation of the book. Separate the two: Q27–31 test the story; Q32–40 test the reviewer's claims and purpose.

Author's attitude. Signal phrases like 'succeeds in', 'even-handed', 'doesn't quite hit the mark' tell you the reviewer's stance — essential for the YES/NO/NG questions and the final MC.

Cause and effect / irony. The passage notes an irony: Charles celebrated what was essentially a defeat. Recognising this contrast answers Q38.
