📋 Passage Overview

Tidal power. Undersea turbines driven by tidal currents could become a major renewable energy source for Britain. Six paragraphs (A–F) cover how they work and why tides beat wind, the sites already identified, the Southampton research and the first test station at Lynmouth, the design and environmental advantages, the Alderney project, and the technical problem of cavitation. Question types: matching information to paragraphs (Q14–17), choose FIVE letters (Q18–22) and diagram labelling (Q23–26).

💡 Reading tip: Q18–22 asks which FIVE claims the writer actually makes — there are ten options, so five are traps. Do not accept a claim just because it sounds plausible for tidal power; find the sentence. For example, option B ('would replace all other forms of energy') overstates 'could close gas, coal and nuclear plants', and option G (resistance from fuel industries) is never mentioned at all.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Operating on the same principle as wind turbines, the power in sea turbines comes from tidal currents which turn blades similar to ships' propellers, but, unlike wind, the tides are predictable and the power input is constant.
Main ClauseThe power in sea turbines comes from tidal currents. / The tides are predictable and the power input is constant.
Subordinate / Relative'Operating on the same principle as wind turbines' (fronted participial phrase) + 'which turn blades similar to ships' propellers' (defining relative) + 'unlike wind' (comparison insert).
Grammar NoteA participial opener sets up a comparison, then 'but, unlike wind' reverses it. This one sentence supplies Q18 option A.
Simple MeaningSea turbines work like wind turbines, but tides — unlike wind — are predictable and give constant power.
中文海洋涡轮机的工作原理与风力涡轮机相同，其动力来自转动叶片（类似船用螺旋桨）的潮流；但与风不同的是，潮汐是可预测的，输入功率也是恒定的。
Unlike wind power, which Britain originally developed and then abandoned for 20 years allowing the Dutch to make it a major industry, undersea turbines could become a big export earner to island nations such as Japan and New Zealand.
Main ClauseUndersea turbines could become a big export earner.
Subordinate / Relative'which Britain originally developed and then abandoned for 20 years' (non-defining relative) + 'allowing the Dutch to make it a major industry' (participial result).
Grammar NoteA long relative clause is embedded inside the 'Unlike…' phrase before the main clause even begins. Q16 asks for 'a previous attempt by Britain to find an alternative source of energy' — it is here.
Simple MeaningBritain invented wind power then gave it up for 20 years, letting the Dutch dominate; undersea turbines could instead earn Britain a lot from exports.
中文与英国最初开发、随后又放弃达二十年、从而让荷兰将其发展为主要产业的风能不同，海底涡轮机有可能成为面向日本、新西兰等岛国的重要出口创汇产业。
Tidal sites have already been identified that will produce one sixth or more of the UK's power - and at prices competitive with modern gas turbines and undercutting those of the already ailing nuclear industry.
Main ClauseTidal sites have already been identified.
Subordinate / Relative'that will produce one sixth or more of the UK's power' (relative clause separated from its noun) + the dash adds the price comparison.
Grammar NoteThe relative clause is extraposed — moved away from 'sites' to the end for emphasis. 'those' = the prices of the nuclear industry.
Simple MeaningSites have been found that could supply over a sixth of UK electricity, at prices matching gas and beating nuclear.
中文目前已确认的潮汐站址可产生英国六分之一甚至更多的电力，其价格可与现代燃气轮机竞争，并低于已陷入困境的核工业。
A marine turbine blade needs to be only one third of the size of a wind generator to produce three times as much power.
Main ClauseA marine turbine blade needs to be only one third of the size of a wind generator.
Subordinate / Relative'to produce three times as much power' = infinitive of result/purpose.
Grammar NoteTwo contrasting fractions/multiples in one short sentence: one third the size yet three times the power. Watch these numbers closely.
Simple MeaningA sea turbine blade only needs to be a third as big as a wind one to make three times the power.
中文海洋涡轮机叶片只需达到风力发电机的三分之一大小，就能产生三倍的电力。
The towers will stick out of the water and be lit, to warn shipping, and also be designed to be lifted out of the water for maintenance and to clean seaweed from the blades.
Main ClauseThe towers will stick out of the water and be lit… and also be designed to be lifted out of the water.
Subordinate / Relative'to warn shipping' + 'for maintenance and to clean seaweed from the blades' = infinitive/prepositional purposes.
Grammar NoteThree purposes hang off one subject. Q23 sits in the last of them.
Simple MeaningThe towers will rise above the water with lights to warn ships, and can be lifted out for repairs and to clean the blades.
中文塔架将露出水面并配有灯光以警示航船，同时设计成可吊出水面进行维护并清除叶片上的海藻。
One technical difficulty is cavitation, where low pressure behind a turning blade causes air bubbles.
Main ClauseOne technical difficulty is cavitation.
Subordinate / Relative'where low pressure behind a turning blade causes air bubbles' = relative clause defining the term.
Grammar Note'where' is used here to define a process, not a place — a common academic use. Q25 and Q26 both come from this sentence.
Simple MeaningOne problem is cavitation: low pressure behind the spinning blade creates air bubbles.
中文一个技术难题是空化现象，即转动叶片后方的低压会产生气泡。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Claim → evidence → qualification: the passage asserts tidal power's promise, supplies named sites and figures as evidence, then closes with the technical difficulties. Q18–22 all sit in the claim-and-evidence half; Q23–26 sit in the qualification half.

Comparison as the organising device: almost every advantage is stated against wind power — predictable vs unpredictable, one third the size vs three times the power, unlikely environmental objections vs wind's known ones. Track 'unlike', 'similar to' and 'compared with'.

Cause & effect: tidal currents turn blades (cause) → constant power input (effect) → Britain could close fossil and nuclear plants (effect) → and export renewable power (effect). Options D, E and F in Q18–22 all sit on this chain.

Definition inside a relative clause: 'cavitation, where low pressure behind a turning blade causes air bubbles'. Technical terms in this passage are always defined immediately — the diagram labels come straight from those definitions.

Quotation as evidence: Dr Bahaj is quoted twice, once for optimism ('the prospects… are far better than from wind') and once for caution ('There are a few technical difficulties'). The writer uses one expert to carry both sides.

Problem → solution → residual problem: saline environment (problem) → North Sea oil technology already exists (solution) → cavitation and submerged debris remain (residual problems). Paragraph F is entirely this last stage.

Author's attitude — optimistic but measured: 'all the signs are that they will play a significant role', 'all the signs that we can do it are good'. Positive, yet the writer keeps hedging with 'could', 'unlikely to be' and 'we do not know how much of a problem it might be'.
