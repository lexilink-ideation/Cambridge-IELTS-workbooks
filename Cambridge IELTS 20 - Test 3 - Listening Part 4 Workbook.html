Listening Part 4

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 3 — Listening Part 4

Inclusive Design · Notes Completion (Q31–40)

📋 Quick Review

A design and technology lecturer explains what inclusive design means: making products that as many different kinds of people as possible can use without any changes being made to the original design. The talk has three clear stages — a definition (and how it differs from universal design), everyday examples such as adjustable desks, sensor taps and interfaces for older users, and finally the harm caused by non-inclusive design in access, safety and workplace comfort. You must complete a set of notes with ONE WORD ONLY for each answer.

Who is speaking? A single speaker: a university design and technology lecturer giving a formal talk. The register is academic but the examples are everyday — desks, taps, seat belts, air conditioning.

The situation 情境: 一位设计与技术专业的讲师介绍"包容性设计"（inclusive design）：先给出定义，并与"通用设计"（universal design）作区分；接着列举成功案例（可调节的桌子、感应水龙头、为老年人优化的界面）；最后讲不包容的设计在"出行、安全、办公舒适度"三方面造成的后果。笔记的三个标题就是录音的三个部分，跟着标题走就不会跟丢。

Task type: Notes completion, ONE WORD ONLY for each answer, Questions 31–40. Answers come in the order of the talk. Two of them (desks, taps) are plural because the note has ‘which are’ after the gap.

💡 Listening strategy tip: This is a classic Part 4 lecture: one speaker, no interruptions, no second chance. Your safety net is the headings printed on the note page — Definition / Examples of inclusive design / Impact of non-inclusive designs, with the sub-headings Access — Safety — Comfort in the workplace. The lecturer signposts every one of these before the answer comes (“Today, examples…”, “Safety is another issue”, “Another problem is related to comfort at work”). Track the headings, not individual words, and you will always know which gap you are on. Because the instruction is ONE WORD ONLY, never write ‘voice access’, ‘sensor taps’ or ‘police officers’ — two words scores zero even when the meaning is right.
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • adaptation, not adapt or adaptations — the lecturer says ‘without any type of adaptation having to be made’. The note says ‘without the need for any’, so a singular uncountable-style noun fits (Q31).
  • universal design is the distractor, not the answer — Q32 is about the thing universal design adds: people with cognitive difficulties. The audio says ‘difficulties’, the note says ‘problems’, so only the adjective goes in the gap.
  • Q33 and Q34 must be plural — the note reads ‘(33) which are adjustable’ and ‘(34) in public toilets which are easier to use’: desks, taps.
  • Q35 is a colour, not a design word — the sentence is long (‘vision declines… we become worse at distinguishing between similar colors, in particular shades of blue’) and the answer arrives before the words ‘interfaces’ and ‘designers’ that are printed on your page.
  • Q36: ONE WORD ONLY — the audio says ‘voice access is now a routine way of making commands’. Write voice only; ‘voice access’ is two words and scores 0.
  • Q38 shoulders is plural; Q39 is police, not emergency — the audio ends with ‘worst in the emergency services, particularly the police’, and the note already prints ‘officers’, so the last word you hear is the one you need.

accessibleadj.able to be reached, entered or used, especially by disabled people可使用的；无障碍的“It means making sure products are accessible…”Noun access is the first sub-heading in the ‘Impact’ section. Listen for ‘wheelchair friendly’ as its paraphrase.

universaladj.involving or intended for absolutely everyone通用的；普适的“Universal design aims to make products that work for everyone.”The note says ‘Not the same as universal design’ — a warning that a contrast is coming. Q32 sits inside that contrast.
cognitiveadj.connected with thinking, learning, understanding and memory认知的“…the needs of people who have cognitive difficulties.”Answer to Q32. Audio ‘difficulties’ = note ‘problems’, so only the adjective goes in the gap. Pronounced COG-ni-tiv.
adjustableadj.able to be changed in height, size or position可调节的“…desks which can be adjusted to suit people of different heights.”The note prints ‘which are adjustable’; the audio uses the passive verb ‘can be adjusted’. Same idea, different word class (Q33).
deskn.a table you sit at to work in an office or at school书桌；办公桌“In workplaces, it is common to see desks which can be adjusted.”Answer to Q33 — PLURAL. The note says ‘(33) which are adjustable’.
wheelchairn.a chair with wheels for someone who cannot walk轮椅“…to suit people of different heights or for wheelchair users.”Spelling trap: whee-l-chair, one word. It appears twice (adjustable desks, and public transport that is not ‘wheelchair friendly’).
tapn.the device you turn or touch to get water from a pipe (US: faucet)水龙头“Taps that you activate by sensor require no pressing or twisting movements.”Answer to Q34 — PLURAL. Do not write ‘sensor taps’: ONE WORD ONLY.
sensorn.a device that detects movement, heat or light and reacts to it传感器；感应器“Taps that you activate by sensor…”A strong distractor for Q34 — it is the how, not the what. The gap needs the object in the toilet.
hygienicadj.clean, so that germs do not spread卫生的“These are not only more hygienic, they’re also easier for people with dexterity issues.”Spelling: h-y-g-i-e-n-i-c. Noun hygiene. ‘Not only… they’re also…’ signals two benefits — the second one is the inclusive-design point.
dexterityn.skill in using your hands手的灵巧度；手部灵活性“…easier for people with dexterity or mobility issues.”Paired with mobility. Both are polite academic ways of describing physical difficulty.
mobilityn.the ability to move around行动能力“…people with dexterity or mobility issues.”Connects to the ‘Access’ section later: buses and trains that are not wheelchair friendly reduce mobility.
declinev.to become gradually weaker, smaller or worse衰退；下降“It’s well known that vision declines with age.”Said twice: vision declines, then ‘motor skills also decline with age’. ‘Also’ is your signal that a new gap (Q36) is starting.
distinguishv.to see or hear the difference between two similar things区分；分辨“We also become worse at distinguishing between similar colors.”The full phrase is distinguish between A and B. This sentence is where Q35 hides.
shaden.a particular version of a colour, lighter or darker色调；深浅“…similar colors, in particular shades of blue.”The answer is the colour itself (blue), not ‘shades’ — the note asks what designers avoid using.
interfacen.the screen and controls you use to operate a computer or app（人机）界面“…software designers rarely create interfaces with this color.”Printed on the question paper (‘avoid using (35) in interfaces’), so it is a locator word, not an answer.
commandn.an instruction given to a computer or device（计算机）指令“Voice access is now a routine way of making commands.”The note says ‘make commands using a mouse, keyboard or their (36)’ — mouse and keyboard are printed, so the missing third option is voice.
dummyn.a model of a human body used in tests（碰撞测试）假人“Most cars are still crash tested using a dummy based on an average-sized male.”Sets up Q37: the dummy is male, so the seat belt is not adapted for women — especially pregnant ones.
accommodatev.to change something so that it fits or suits someone容纳；照顾到（某人的需要）“…the seat belts worn by the dummy are not adapted to accommodate them.”A high-value Writing verb too: accommodate the needs of…. Double c, double m.
pregnantadj.expecting a baby怀孕的“This has safety implications for all women, particularly those who are pregnant.”Answer to Q37. The note reverses the word order — ‘especially problematic for (37) women’ = ‘particularly those who are pregnant’.
protectiveadj.made to keep someone safe from harm防护的“Employers are legally required to provide personal protective equipment or PPE.”PPE = personal protective equipment. The lecturer gives the full phrase once, then uses the abbreviation, which is what the note uses.
shouldern.the part of the body where the arm joins the neck肩膀“Women can be tall and still have much smaller shoulders than the average man.”Answer to Q38 — PLURAL. The note says ‘the size of women’s (38)’.
hamperv.to make it harder for someone to do something妨碍；阻碍“95% of women said that their PPE often hampered their work.”Synonym family: hinder / restrict / get in the way of. A useful Task 2 verb as well.
emergency servicesn.the police, fire and ambulance services紧急救援部门（警察、消防、急救）“This problem was worst in the emergency services, particularly the police.”The note says ‘officers dealing with emergencies’, so ‘emergency’ is already given — the answer must be the more specific word, police (Q39).
metabolic raten.the speed at which your body turns food into energy and heat新陈代谢率“This is due to differences in metabolic rates for men and women.”The scientific reason behind Q40 — women feel colder at the same setting.
temperaturen.how hot or cold something is温度“There is a standard setting for air conditioning to be at a temperature of 21 degrees.”Answer to Q40. Spelling trap: tem-per-a-ture. The number 21 is a distractor — the gap needs the noun, not the figure.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening the audio never uses the exact words printed on the question paper. Recognising paraphrase is the whole skill. 雅思听力中，录音几乎不会重复题干原词——识别同义替换才是关键。
Paraphrase Example 1
Question wording“…without the need for any (31)”
What you hear“…so that as many different types of people as possible can use them without any type of adaptation having to be made to the original design.”
Key paraphrasewithout the need for any X = without any type of X having to be made
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question wording“catering for people with (32) problems”
What you hear“…considering the needs of people who have cognitive difficulties, which can present quite a challenge.”
Key paraphrasecatering for the needs of = considering the needs of; problems = difficulties
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question wording“(33) which are adjustable, avoiding back or neck problems”
What you hear“It is common to see desks which can be adjusted to suit people of different heights… one reason why office workers often suffer from back or neck problems.”
Key paraphrasewhich are adjustable = which can be adjusted (adjective ↔ passive verb). Plural because of ‘are’.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question wording“(34) in public toilets which are easier to use”
What you hear“Taps that you activate by sensor require no pressing or twisting movements… they are also easier for people with dexterity or mobility issues.”
Key paraphraseeasier to use = require no pressing or twisting / easier for people with dexterity issues
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 5
Question wording“designers avoid using (35) in interfaces”
What you hear“…we become worse at distinguishing between similar colors, in particular shades of blue, which is why software designers rarely create interfaces with this color.”
Key paraphraseavoid using X = rarely create… with X. ‘This color’ refers back to blue.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 6
Question wording“Seatbelts are especially problematic for (37) women”
What you hear“This has safety implications for all women, particularly those who are pregnant, as the seat belts… are not adapted to accommodate them.”
Key paraphraseespecially problematic for = has safety implications… particularly for
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 7
Question wording“PPE for female (39) officers dealing with emergencies is the worst”
What you hear“…this problem was worst in the emergency services, particularly the police.”
Key paraphraseofficers dealing with emergencies = the emergency services; the specific answer is the LAST word said.
Your practice

Review Quiz

Q1
B. Designing products that as many people as possible can use with no changes made.
‘Making sure products are accessible so that as many different types of people as possible can use them without any type of adaptation.’ A is the opposite of inclusive design.
Q2
B. It aims at products for everyone, including people with cognitive difficulties.
‘Universal design aims to make products that work for everyone and that includes considering the needs of people who have cognitive difficulties.’ That is the source of Q32.
Q3
A. To explain why some offices still do not have adjustable desks.
Adjustable desks ‘still isn’t always the case… and is one reason why office workers often suffer from back or neck problems’. The problem is evidence of missing inclusive design.
Q4
B. Sensor is the method of activation, not the object in the toilet.
‘Taps that you activate by sensor’ — the noun after the gap-phrase is taps, and ‘which are’ forces the plural.
Q5
B. They are cleaner AND a second benefit is coming.
‘Not only… they’re also…’ always signals a second point — here, easier for people with dexterity or mobility issues.
Q6
B. Older users find similar shades of blue hard to tell apart.
‘We become worse at distinguishing between similar colors, in particular shades of blue, which is why software designers rarely create interfaces with this color.’
Q7
B. voice
The audio is ‘voice access is now a routine way of making commands’, but the instruction is ONE WORD ONLY, so voice is the only safe answer.
Q8
B. Testing is based on an average-sized male, so belts do not suit all women.
‘Most cars are still crash tested using a dummy based on an average-sized male… the seat belts… are not adapted to accommodate them’ — especially pregnant women (Q37).
Q9
B. Employers buy jackets designed for a small man, but women have smaller shoulders.
‘All they need to do… is buy jackets designed for a small man… women can be tall and still have much smaller shoulders than the average man.’
Q10
C. In the emergency services, particularly the police.
The final words of that sentence give the answer to Q39: ‘worst in the emergency services, particularly the police’.
