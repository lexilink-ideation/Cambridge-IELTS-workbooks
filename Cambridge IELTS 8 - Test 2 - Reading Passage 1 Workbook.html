📋 Passage Overview

This passage explains how flat sheet glass is made, focusing on the float process invented by Alistair Pilkington. It compares earlier methods (spinning and the ribbon process) with the float process, in which molten glass is floated on a bed of molten tin to give perfectly flat surfaces. It then covers the difficult early years of production and how modern float plants achieve near-optical quality with automated inspection.

💡 Reading strategy tip: For table and diagram completion, follow the passage's own words and stay within the word limit (NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS). For True/False/Not Given, judge only what the text says — 'Not Given' means you cannot find it confirmed or denied, even if it sounds likely.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"Glass, which has been made since the time of the Mesopotamians and Egyptians, is little more than a mixture of sand, soda ash and lime."
Main ClauseGlass … is little more than a mixture of sand, soda ash and lime
Subordinate / Relativewhich has been made since the time of the Mesopotamians and Egyptians (non-defining relative clause)
Grammar NoteThe 'which' clause adds extra info between commas; 'little more than' = basically just.
Simple MeaningGlass, made since ancient times, is basically just sand, soda ash and lime.
中文玻璃自美索不达米亚人和埃及人时代起就已被制造，其实只不过是沙子、纯碱和石灰的混合物。
"This allowed glass of virtually any thickness to be made non-stop, but the rollers would leave both sides of the glass marked."
Main ClauseThis allowed glass … to be made non-stop … but the rollers would leave both sides … marked
Subordinate / Relative(no subordinate clause) — two independent clauses joined by 'but' (contrast)
Grammar Note'allowed X to be made' = made X possible; 'but' introduces the drawback.
Simple MeaningThe ribbon method could make any thickness continuously, but it left marks on both sides.
中文这使几乎任何厚度的玻璃都能不停地生产，但滚轴会在玻璃两面留下痕迹。
"If the glass were kept hot enough, it would flow over the molten tin until the top surface was also flat, horizontal and perfectly parallel to the bottom surface."
Main Clauseit would flow over the molten tin until the top surface was … flat … parallel to the bottom surface
Subordinate / RelativeIf the glass were kept hot enough (second conditional) / until … (time clause)
Grammar Note'If … were … , it would …' is a conditional; 'until' shows the end point.
Simple MeaningKept hot, the glass spreads over the tin until its top is as flat as its bottom.
中文只要玻璃保持足够高温，它就会在熔锡上流动，直到上表面也变得平整、水平并与下表面完全平行。
"Inspection technology allows more than 100 million measurements a second to be made across the ribbon, locating flaws the unaided eye would be unable to see."
Main ClauseInspection technology allows more than 100 million measurements a second to be made
Subordinate / Relativelocating flaws (that) the unaided eye would be unable to see (participle + reduced relative clause)
Grammar Note'-ing' participle 'locating' shows a result; the relative pronoun 'that' is omitted.
Simple MeaningThe technology takes millions of measurements per second and finds faults people cannot see.
中文检测技术每秒可在玻璃带上进行超过一亿次测量，找出肉眼无法看见的瑕疵。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect — Pouring glass onto flat molten tin results in a flat underside; keeping it hot makes the top flat too. Signal: consequently, because, so that.

Problem / Solution — Problem: rollers marked the glass and wasted ~20%. Solution: float it on molten tin — no rollers, no marking.

Chronology — 1952 idea → 1953 pilot plant → 1955 full-scale plant → 14 months of loss → 1959 success → today's plants. Dates anchor the True/False/Not Given items.

Comparison / Contrast — Spinning gave an unblemished 'fire finish' but was slow; the ribbon process was continuous but wasteful; the float process is both continuous and clean.
