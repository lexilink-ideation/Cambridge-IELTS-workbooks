📋 Passage Overview

'The Story of Silk' traces silk from the legend of Lei Tzu in about 3000 BC, through its use as a symbol of status and even as currency, along the Silk Road, into Byzantium, Spain and Italy, and finally to its nineteenth-century decline and China's return as the world's largest producer.

题型:笔记填空 Q1–9(ONE WORD ONLY)+ TRUE/FALSE/NOT GIVEN Q10–13。

💡 Reading tip: The notes in Q1–9 follow the passage in order and are grouped under two headings ('Early silk production in China' and 'Silk reaches rest of world'). Use the headings to jump to the right half of the text instead of re-reading from the top.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

It just so happened that while she was sipping some tea, one of the cocoons that she had collected landed in the hot tea and started to unravel into a fine thread.
Main Clauseone of the cocoons … landed in the hot tea and started to unravel into a fine thread
Subordinate / Relative'It just so happened that…' = introductory device; 'while she was sipping some tea' = time clause; 'that she had collected' = defining relative clause
Grammar NoteThree layers: an empty 'it' opening, a past-continuous time clause, and a past-perfect relative clause.
Simple MeaningBy chance, as she drank tea, one cocoon fell in and began to come apart into thread.
中文恰巧在她啜饮茶水时,她采集的一只茧掉进热茶里,开始散开成一根细丝。
She also devised a special reel to draw the fibres from the cocoon into a single thread so that they would be strong enough to be woven into fabric.
Main ClauseShe also devised a special reel
Subordinate / Relative'to draw the fibres … into a single thread' = purpose infinitive; 'so that they would be strong enough to be woven into fabric' = purpose clause
Grammar NoteTwo purposes stacked: an infinitive of purpose plus a 'so that' clause. Q2 comes from the object 'reel'.
Simple MeaningShe invented a reel that pulled the fibres into one thread strong enough to weave.
中文她还发明了一种特制的缫丝器,把茧上的纤维抽成一根丝线,使其足够结实以织成布料。
Sometime during the Han Dynasty (206 BC–220 AD), silk was so prized that it was also used as a unit of currency.
Main Clausesilk was so prized that it was also used as a unit of currency
Subordinate / Relative'so + adjective + that + clause' = result structure; '(206 BC–220 AD)' = bracketed dates
Grammar Note'so … that …' expresses degree and result — the classic pattern for showing importance.
Simple MeaningDuring the Han Dynasty silk was valued so highly that it served as money.
中文在汉朝(公元前206年—公元220年)的某个时期,丝绸备受珍视,甚至被用作一种货币单位。
According to another legend, monks working for the Byzantine emperor Justinian smuggled silkworm eggs to Constantinople in 550 AD, concealed inside hollow bamboo walking canes.
Main Clausemonks … smuggled silkworm eggs to Constantinople in 550 AD
Subordinate / Relative'working for the Byzantine emperor Justinian' = reduced relative ('who worked for'); 'concealed inside hollow bamboo walking canes' = reduced passive participle ('which were concealed')
Grammar NoteTwo reduced clauses squeeze a lot of information around one short main clause.
Simple MeaningIn 550 AD, monks serving Emperor Justinian hid silkworm eggs in hollow walking sticks and took them to Constantinople.
中文据另一个传说,为拜占庭皇帝查士丁尼效力的僧侣在公元550年把蚕卵藏在中空的竹制手杖里,偷偷带到君士坦丁堡。
Cheaper Japanese silk, trade in which was greatly facilitated by the opening of the Suez Canal, was one of the many factors driving the trend.
Main ClauseCheaper Japanese silk … was one of the many factors driving the trend
Subordinate / Relative'trade in which was greatly facilitated by the opening of the Suez Canal' = non-defining relative clause with a preposition ('trade in which' = 'trade in this silk')
Grammar Note'preposition + which' is formal academic English. Note the long gap between subject and verb.
Simple MeaningCheap Japanese silk, easier to trade after the Suez Canal opened, was one cause of the decline.
中文更廉价的日本生丝(其贸易因苏伊士运河的开通而大为便利)是推动这一趋势的众多因素之一。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Chronology. The passage runs 3000 BC → Han Dynasty → Byzantine Empire (330–1453 AD) → 550 AD → 7th century Arabs → 10th century Spain → 13th century Italy → 19th century decline → 20th century nylon → 1970s Japan → today China. Dates are the fastest way to locate any answer.

Legend vs. fact. The writer carefully separates story from evidence: 'Legend has it that…', 'One account of the story goes that…', 'According to another legend…', 'While it is unknown just how much of this is true, it is certainly known that…'. This hedging matters for T/F/NG.

Cause and effect. Demand for silk → created the Silk Road → brought gold, silver and wool east. Later: industrialisation + cheaper Japanese silk + the Suez Canal + manmade fibres + two world wars → the downfall of European silk.

Definition and example. 'Fishing lines, bowstrings, musical instruments and paper were all made using silk' — a list of examples supporting the general point that silk had many uses (Q6 = paper).

Comparison of value. The Silk Road was named after 'its most precious commodity, which was considered to be worth more than gold'. Q10 turns exactly on this comparison.
