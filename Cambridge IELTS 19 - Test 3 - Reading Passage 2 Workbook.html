📋 Passage Overview

The global importance of wetlands. This passage explains why wetlands matter — for livelihoods, culture and climate — and how they are being lost, especially peatlands drained for palm oil and pulpwood. Several named experts (McCartney, van Eijk, Silvius, Tickner) give views on the damage and possible solutions. Question types: paragraph matching, sentence completion and matching features (experts).

💡 Reading tip: For 'matching experts' (Q23–26), note what each named person argues as you read, and mark their paragraph. Then match each statement to the view — the wording will be paraphrased, so match the idea, not the exact words.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

But according to the World Wide Fund for Nature, half of the world's wetlands have disappeared since 1990 — converted or destroyed for commercial development, drainage schemes and the extraction of minerals and peat.
Main ClauseHalf of the world's wetlands have disappeared since 1990.
Subordinate / Relative'converted or destroyed for commercial development…' (reduced relative explaining how).
Grammar NoteThe dash + past participles ('converted or destroyed') give the reasons.
Simple MeaningSince 1990, half the world's wetlands have been lost to development and extraction.
中文但据世界自然基金会称，自1990年以来，全球一半的湿地已经消失——被改造或摧毁，用于商业开发、排水工程以及矿产和泥炭开采。
When peatlands are drained for cultivation, they become net carbon emitters instead of active carbon stores.
Main ClauseThey become net carbon emitters instead of active carbon stores.
Subordinate / Relative'When peatlands are drained for cultivation' (time/condition clause).
Grammar Note'instead of' contrasts the new state with the former one.
Simple MeaningDrained peatlands give off carbon rather than storing it.
中文当泥炭地被排水用于耕种时，它们便从活跃的碳储存体变成了净碳排放源。
And while these industries affect wetlands in ways that can easily be documented, Dr Dave Tickner believes that more subtle impacts can be even more devastating.
Main ClauseDr Dave Tickner believes that more subtle impacts can be even more devastating.
Subordinate / Relative'while these industries affect wetlands in ways that can easily be documented' (concession clause) + 'that can easily be documented' (relative clause).
Grammar Note'while' concedes the obvious damage before naming the hidden damage.
Simple MeaningObvious industrial damage is easy to see, but Tickner says hidden harm can be worse.
中文尽管这些行业对湿地的影响很容易被记录下来，但戴夫·蒂克纳博士认为，更隐蔽的影响可能更具破坏性。
The idea is that people in a developing country will only protect wetlands if they value and profit from them.
Main ClauseThe idea is that people will only protect wetlands.
Subordinate / Relative'if they value and profit from them' (conditional clause).
Grammar Note'only… if' makes protection conditional on benefit.
Simple MeaningPeople will protect wetlands only when they gain from them.
中文其理念是：发展中国家的人们只有在重视湿地并从中获益时，才会去保护它们。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Problem → cause → solution: wetlands are disappearing (problem) → drainage for palm oil/pulpwood (cause) → involve and reward local people (solution). This structure maps the whole passage.

Cause & effect chain (peat): logging companies dig ditches → water drains → bacteria break down carbon → CO2 + methane released + subsidence → land floods. Follow each step.

Matching viewpoints: track each expert — McCartney (livelihoods, involvement), van Eijk (simple, cheap solutions), Silvius (peat catastrophe, income), Tickner (invisible, unintentional harm).

Concession/optimism: McCartney is 'cautiously optimistic' — degradation continues 'but… things are slowly changing.' A qualified positive.
