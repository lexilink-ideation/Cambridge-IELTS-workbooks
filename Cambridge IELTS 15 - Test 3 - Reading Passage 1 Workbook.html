📋 Passage Overview

Henry Moore (1898–1986). A biography of the British sculptor: his early training at Leeds and the Royal College of Art, the influence of ancient (Mayan) and modern (Picasso) art, the hostile press reaction to his 1931 exhibition, his wartime drawings of Londoners and miners, the 1944 family commission that widened his appeal to collectors and boosted his income, and his final international fame. Question types: TRUE/FALSE/NOT GIVEN (Q1–7) and note completion (Q8–13).

💡 Reading tip: Biographies run in time order, so scan for dates to locate answers fast. For T/F/NG, FALSE needs a clear contradiction; NOT GIVEN means the passage never says (e.g. public reaction that is not reported).

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

His work was enthusiastically welcomed by fellow sculptors, but the reviews in the press were extremely negative and turned Moore into a notorious figure.
Main ClauseHis work was … welcomed by fellow sculptors, but the reviews … were extremely negative and turned Moore into a notorious figure
Subordinate / Relativetwo independent clauses joined by 'but'; the second has a compound predicate ('were … and turned…')
Grammar NoteContrast with 'but'; parallel verbs 'were … and turned …' share the subject 'the reviews'.
Simple MeaningOther sculptors loved his work, but the newspapers attacked it and made him infamous.
中文他的作品受到同行雕塑家的热烈欢迎,但报刊评论极为负面,把摩尔变成了一个声名狼藉的人物。
He became interested in the paintings of the Spanish artist Pablo Picasso, whose work inspired him to distort the human body in a radical way.
Main ClauseHe became interested in the paintings of the Spanish artist Pablo Picasso
Subordinate / Relative'whose work inspired him to distort the human body in a radical way' = non-defining relative clause (possessive 'whose')
Grammar NoteMain clause + a 'whose' relative clause showing the effect of Picasso's work.
Simple MeaningHe grew interested in Picasso, whose paintings led him to reshape the human body dramatically.
中文他开始对西班牙艺术家毕加索的绘画产生兴趣,毕加索的作品激励他以激进的方式扭曲人体。
The resulting work signifies a dramatic change in Moore's style, away from the experimentation of the 1930s towards a more natural and humanistic subject matter.
Main ClauseThe resulting work signifies a dramatic change in Moore's style
Subordinate / Relative'away from the experimentation of the 1930s towards a more natural and humanistic subject matter' = paired 'away from … towards …' phrase describing the change
Grammar NoteMain clause + a balanced 'away from X towards Y' structure.
Simple MeaningThat sculpture marked a big change in his style — from 1930s experiments to more natural, human subjects.
中文这件作品标志着摩尔风格的巨大转变,从1930年代的实验转向更自然、更具人文色彩的题材。
Critics who had begun to think that Moore had become less revolutionary were proven wrong by the appearance, in 1950, of the first of Moore's series of standing figures in bronze.
Main ClauseCritics … were proven wrong by the appearance … of the first of Moore's series of standing figures in bronze
Subordinate / Relative'who had begun to think that Moore had become less revolutionary' = relative clause with an embedded that-clause
Grammar NotePassive main verb 'were proven wrong'; the subject 'Critics' is modified by a relative clause.
Simple MeaningCritics who thought Moore had grown tame were proved wrong when his bronze standing figures appeared in 1950.
中文那些开始认为摩尔变得不再具有革命性的评论家,在1950年他首批青铜站立人像问世时被证明看错了。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Chronology. The passage follows dates: 1920s training → 1931 exhibition → 1930s Picasso influence → 1940 move + wartime drawings → 1944 family commission → 1950 bronze figures → final years. Dates anchor both the T/F/NG and the note answers.

Cause and effect. Hostile reviews → calls for his resignation; shortage of materials → he turned to drawing; bronze editions → work reached collectors → income rose → he could take on bigger projects.

Contrast of reputation. The writer keeps contrasting how sculptors valued Moore with how the press attacked him, and how critics later changed their minds. Watch 'but', 'however', 'proven wrong'.
