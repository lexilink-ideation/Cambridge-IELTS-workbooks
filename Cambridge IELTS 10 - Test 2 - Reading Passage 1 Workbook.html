Reading Passage 1

Cambridge IELTS 10 — Test 2 — Reading Passage 1: Tea and the Industrial Revolution

Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

Cambridge professor Alan Macfarlane argues that the missing pieces in the puzzle of Britain's Industrial Revolution were tea and beer. Because both are made with boiled water and contain antiseptic substances (tannin and hops), they let crowded towns grow without deadly water-borne disease — which gave Britain the population it needed for industry.

这是一篇“历史悬案 + 意外答案”类文章。结构:提出谜题 → 组合锁比喻 → 提出答案 → 人口数据 → 啤酒 → 日本与茶 → 日本为何没成功。

💡 Reading tip: The seven headings map onto seven very distinct paragraph topics, so topic sentences will get you most of the marks. Watch for the two 'Japan' paragraphs (F and G): F uses Japan to find the answer; G uses Japan to explain why it did not industrialise. Different headings, same country.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

For industry to take off, there needs to be the technology and power to drive factories, large urban populations to provide cheap labour, easy transport to move goods around, an affluent middle-class willing to buy mass-produced objects, a market-driven economy and a political system that allows this to happen.
Main Clausethere needs to be the technology and power … , populations … , transport … , a middle-class … , an economy and a political system
Subordinate / Relative'For industry to take off' = purpose infinitive with its own subject; each listed item carries its own modifier ('to drive factories', 'to provide cheap labour', 'willing to buy…', 'that allows this to happen')
Grammar Note'For + noun + to + verb' is a compressed purpose clause ('In order for industry to take off'). Then a six-item list — read the head nouns only on a first pass.
Simple MeaningIndustry needs six things at once: technology, workers, transport, rich buyers, a free market and the right politics.
中文要让工业起飞,必须具备:驱动工厂的技术和动力、提供廉价劳动力的大量城市人口、便捷的货物运输、愿意购买大规模生产商品的富裕中产阶级、市场驱动的经济,以及允许这一切发生的政治制度。
The antiseptic properties of tannin, the active ingredient in tea, and of hops in beer — plus the fact that both are made with boiled water — allowed urban communities to flourish at close quarters without succumbing to water-borne diseases such as dysentery.
Main ClauseThe antiseptic properties … allowed urban communities to flourish
Subordinate / Relative'the active ingredient in tea' = appositive defining tannin; 'plus the fact that both are made with boiled water' = dash-bracketed addition; 'without succumbing to water-borne diseases such as dysentery' = prepositional phrase of result avoided
Grammar NoteThe subject ('The antiseptic properties') is separated from its verb ('allowed') by 30 words. Find the verb first. This sentence alone answers Q9 (TRUE).
Simple MeaningTannin in tea and hops in beer kill germs, and both drinks use boiled water, so crowded towns could grow without dying of dysentery.
中文茶中的有效成分单宁,以及啤酒中的啤酒花所具有的抗菌特性——再加上两者都是用煮沸的水制成——使城市社区能够在密集居住的条件下繁荣发展,而不致死于痢疾等水传疾病。
The infant mortality rate halved in the space of 20 years, and this happened in both rural areas and cities, and across all classes.
Main ClauseThe infant mortality rate halved … and this happened in both rural areas and cities, and across all classes
Subordinate / Relativetwo coordinated main clauses; 'in both … and …' and 'across all classes' are adverbial phrases of scope
Grammar Note'Halve' as an intransitive verb ('halved' = became half). Note how the scope phrases make the mystery harder — a local cause cannot explain a universal drop.
Simple MeaningBaby deaths fell by half within 20 years, everywhere and in every social class.
中文婴儿死亡率在二十年间减半,而且这在农村和城市、在所有阶层中都同样发生。
But in the late 17th century a tax was introduced on malt, the basic ingredient of beer.
Main Clausea tax was introduced on malt
Subordinate / Relative'the basic ingredient of beer' = appositive explaining malt
Grammar NoteShort passive + appositive. This is the first link in the causal chain that makes Q13 TRUE: tax → poor drink water and gin → mortality rises.
Simple MeaningIn the late 1600s the government taxed malt, which is what beer is made from.
中文但在17世纪晚期,政府对啤酒的基本原料麦芽征税。
Macfarlane guessed that the fact that water had to be boiled, together with the stomach-purifying properties of tea, meant that the breast milk provided by mothers was healthier than it had ever been.
Main ClauseMacfarlane guessed that … the fact … meant that the breast milk … was healthier than it had ever been
Subordinate / Relative'that water had to be boiled' = noun clause in apposition to 'the fact'; 'together with the stomach-purifying properties of tea' = additive phrase; 'provided by mothers' = reduced relative
Grammar NoteThree levels of embedding: guessed that → the fact that → meant that. Note 'together with' does NOT change the verb number — the subject is still 'the fact'.
Simple MeaningHe thought boiled water plus tea's cleansing effect made mothers' milk healthier than ever before.
中文麦克法兰推测,水必须煮沸这一事实,再加上茶具有净化肠胃的特性,意味着母亲提供的母乳比以往任何时候都更健康。
Macfarlane notes that even though 17th-century Japan had large cities, high literacy rates, even a futures market, it had turned its back on the essence of any work-based revolution by giving up labour-saving devices such as animals, afraid that they would put people out of work.
Main ClauseMacfarlane notes that … it had turned its back on the essence of any work-based revolution
Subordinate / Relative'even though 17th-century Japan had large cities, high literacy rates, even a futures market' = concession clause; 'by giving up labour-saving devices such as animals' = phrase of means; 'afraid that they would put people out of work' = reduced clause of reason ('because they were afraid')
Grammar NoteConcession + means + reason, all hanging off one that-clause. The final reduced clause 'afraid that…' is the evidence for heading ii (fear of unemployment).
Simple MeaningEven though Japan had cities, readers and a futures market, it gave up labour-saving animals because it feared unemployment — so it lost its chance at revolution.
中文麦克法兰指出,尽管17世纪的日本拥有大城市、很高的识字率,甚至还有期货市场,但它因担心省力工具(如役畜)会使人失业而放弃了它们,从而背弃了任何以劳动为基础的革命的本质。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

1. Problem & solution (问题与方案). This is the spine of the passage. Problem (A): why Britain, why the late 18th century? Framework (B): a combination lock with about 20 factors, all necessary but not sufficient. Proposed solution (C): the missing factors are tea and beer. Evidence (D–F). Loose end tied up (G): why Japan, which also drank tea, did not industrialise.

2. Cause & effect (因果链). Two chains run through the text. Chain 1 (beer): hops preserve beer → beer protects the English → malt tax → the poor drink water and gin → mortality rises in the 1720s. Chain 2 (tea): clipper trade with China → tea becomes cheap by the 1740s → water is boiled + tea purifies the stomach → healthier breast milk → infant mortality halves → population burst → cheap urban labour → Industrial Revolution. Being able to say these two chains aloud is worth more than re-reading the passage.

3. Elimination reasoning (排除法). Paragraph D is a model of academic elimination: four candidate causes are raised and rejected in turn — viruses and bacteria ('Unlikely'), medical science ('a century before Lister's revolution'), environmental conditions ('these were small gains… sanitation did not become widespread until the 19th century'), and food ('the height and weight statistics show a decline'). The rhetorical questions ('Was there…? Was there…?') are a signal that elimination is happening.

4. Comparison (比较). Britain is compared with Holland ('had everything except coal'), China ('also had many of these factors'), France, the Netherlands — and above all Japan, which appears twice for opposite purposes. Paragraph F: Japan is the clue (large cities, no sanitation, but less water-borne disease → tea). Paragraph G: Japan is the counter-example (it had tea but rejected labour-saving devices).

5. Definition & metaphor (定义与比喻). Difficult terms are glossed on the spot: 'tannin, the active ingredient in tea'; 'malt, the basic ingredient of beer'; the footnote on Lister. The controlling metaphor is the combination lock — 20 numbers must all be right, and 'most historians are convinced there are one or two missing factors that you need to open the lock'. Notice also 'Big Bang' for the birth of industry and 'kitchen cupboard' for the everyday answer.

6. Author's attitude (作者态度). The reporter (Anjana Ahuja) is initially doubtful and ends up persuaded: 'The theory sounds eccentric but … the scepticism gives way to wary admiration.' She supports this by citing a third party ('Roy Porter … wrote a favourable appraisal'). The hedging verbs matter for TRUE/FALSE: Macfarlane proposes, deduced, guessed, notes — this is a hypothesis, not a proven fact.
