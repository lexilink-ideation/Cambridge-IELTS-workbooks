📋 Passage Overview

An ideal city. The passage describes Leonardo da Vinci's plan for an 'ideal city' along the Ticino River — a compact, multi-level city with outdoor staircases, artificial canals and streets matched to building height. It argues his vision was centuries ahead of its time and still relevant to today's climate challenges. Question types: True/False/Not Given and summary completion (ONE WORD).

💡 Reading tip: Distinguish 'ideal' from 'built'. Leonardo's city was planned but never constructed — Q30 is FALSE because the project 'would remain unfulfilled'. Watch dates and comparisons closely.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Around 1486 — after a pestilence that killed half the population in Milan, Italy — Leonardo turned his thoughts to urban planning problems.
Main ClauseLeonardo turned his thoughts to urban planning problems.
Subordinate / Relative'after a pestilence that killed half the population in Milan' (time phrase + relative clause).
Grammar NoteDashes insert the cause/time; the relative modifies 'pestilence'.
Simple MeaningAbout 1486, after a plague killed half of Milan, Leonardo began thinking about city planning.
中文大约在1486年——一场夺去米兰半数人口的瘟疫之后——列奥纳多开始思考城市规划问题。
Following a typical Renaissance trend, he began to work on an 'ideal city' project, which — due to its excessive costs — would remain unfulfilled.
Main ClauseHe began to work on an 'ideal city' project.
Subordinate / Relative'Following a typical Renaissance trend' (participial phrase) + 'which… would remain unfulfilled' (relative).
Grammar NoteThe relative clause tells us the project was never realised.
Simple MeaningLike others in the Renaissance, he planned an ideal city, but it was too costly to build.
中文顺应文艺复兴时期的典型潮流，他开始着手一个"理想城市"项目，但由于费用过高，该项目最终未能实现。
Leonardo also thought that the width of the streets ought to match the average height of the adjacent houses: a rule still followed in many contemporary cities across Italy, to allow access to sun and reduce the risk of damage from earthquakes.
Main ClauseLeonardo thought the width of the streets ought to match the height of the houses.
Subordinate / Relative'a rule still followed in many contemporary cities…' (appositive) + 'to allow access to sun and reduce the risk…' (purpose).
Grammar NoteA colon introduces an appositive; the infinitives state the purpose.
Simple MeaningHe said street width should match house height — a rule still used in Italy for sunlight and earthquake safety.
中文列奥纳多还认为街道宽度应与相邻房屋的平均高度相匹配：这一规则至今仍为意大利许多城市所遵循，以便采光并降低地震损害的风险。
Many scholars think that the compact city, built upwards instead of outwards, integrated with nature, with efficient transport infrastructure, could help modern cities become more efficient and sustainable.
Main ClauseMany scholars think that the compact city could help modern cities become more efficient and sustainable.
Subordinate / Relative'built upwards instead of outwards' + 'integrated with nature' + 'with efficient transport infrastructure' (stacked modifiers of 'city').
Grammar NoteSeveral descriptive phrases pile up before the verb 'could help'.
Simple MeaningScholars say a compact city — built up, close to nature, well-connected — could be more efficient and sustainable.
中文许多学者认为，向上而非向外扩张、与自然（尤其是水系）融合、并配有高效交通设施的紧凑型城市，能帮助现代城市变得更高效、更可持续。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Comparison (then vs now): the writer draws a parallel between the 14th-century plague that prompted the Renaissance and today's climate crisis — both 'called for radical solutions'.

Contrast (planned vs built): Leonardo's ideas were 'unconventional at the time' and largely not implemented until the 1920s–30s (staircases) or the 19th century (Haussmann's Paris) — key to Q35, Q39.

Cause & effect: unsustainable urban models are 'a key cause of global climate change', which is why Leonardo's compact-city idea is offered as a solution (Q31, Q40).

Attribution of certainty: the passage separates fact ('he recommended high, strong walls') from what is 'not easy to identify' — helping you judge NOT GIVEN (Q32).
