📋 Quick Review

A lecturer introduces business cultures — the unwritten ideas, beliefs and values of an organisation. One writer classified them into four types; the lecture covers three: Power Culture, Role Culture and Task Culture, each with its characteristics, advantages, disadvantages and the type of employee who suits it. Note completion — ONE WORD ONLY.

💡 Listening tip: The notes are built on a repeating frame — Characteristics → Advantage → Disadvantage → Suitable employee. Once you spot that pattern you can predict what is coming next even if you miss a word. ONE WORD ONLY is strict: for Q31 write central, not 'central source'.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

The notes strip long academic sentences down to one word. Practise that reduction.
Q31 — Power source
Note line
______ power source
What you hear
'It's the type of culture that needs a central source of power to be effective'
Key paraphrase
'a central source of power' → the ONE WORD needed is central (word order is reversed in the notes)
Your turn
Q33 — The disadvantage
Note line
might not act ______
What you hear
'this type of organisation doesn't always act effectively, because it depends too much on one or two people'
Key paraphrase
'doesn't always act effectively' = might not act effectively → effectively
Your turn
Q37 — Role Culture strength
Note line
successful when ______ ability is important
What you hear
'it is particularly successful in business markets where technical expertise is important'
Key paraphrase
'expertise' = 'ability' in the notes, so the adjective technical fills the gap
Your turn
Q39 — Suitable employee
Note line
doesn't want ______
What you hear
'it suits employees who value security, and who don't particularly want to have responsibility'
Key paraphrase
'don't particularly want to have responsibility' → responsibility
Your turn
