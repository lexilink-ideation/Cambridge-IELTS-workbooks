📋 Quick Review

This passage looks at the transport problems of Dar es Salaam, one of Africa’s fastest-growing cities. It describes the traffic and informal settlements, explains why the city chose a bus rapid transit (BRT) system called DART instead of a metro, reports the problems it still faces, and outlines the plans to expand it.

💡 Reading strategy tip: Passage 2 mixes True/False/Not Given with note completion. For T/F/NG, watch for comparisons and claims the text does not actually make ("more rapidly than predicted", "consulted"). For notes, the answer is ONE exact word from the passage.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"Today, four out of five of its people live in single-storey informal settlements on the spreading edges of the city, where the journey to and from the centre regularly takes over two hours."
Main Clausefour out of five of its people live in single-storey informal settlements
Subordinate / Relative Clausewhere the journey to and from the centre regularly takes over two hours (relative clause)
Grammar Note"where" introduces a relative clause about the settlements. "four out of five" = 80%.
Simple Meaning80% of people live in unplanned single-storey housing on the city’s edges, where commuting takes over two hours.
中文如今，五分之四的居民住在城市不断扩张的边缘的单层非正规住区，往返市中心通常要花两个多小时。
"Dar es Salaam’s reliance on four arterial roads into the city is a legacy of the colonial government that planned the city at the start of the 20th century to cater for a population of 35,000."
Main ClauseDar es Salaam’s reliance on four arterial roads … is a legacy of the colonial government
Subordinate / Relative Clausethat planned the city … to cater for a population of 35,000 (relative + infinitive of purpose)
Grammar Note"that planned the city" describes the government. "to cater for" states the purpose.
Simple MeaningThe city depends on just four main roads — a leftover from colonial planners who designed it for only 35,000 people.
中文达累斯萨拉姆对四条进城主干道的依赖，是20世纪初为容纳3.5万人口而规划城市的殖民政府所留下的遗产。
"It has chosen a less exciting but cheaper and more achievable method: the bus."
Main ClauseIt has chosen a … method: the bus
Subordinate / Relative Clause(the colon introduces the specific method)
Grammar NoteA three-part adjective contrast: "less exciting but cheaper and more achievable". The colon names the choice.
Simple MeaningThe city picked a duller but cheaper and more realistic option — buses.
中文它选择了一种不那么令人兴奋、却更便宜、更可行的方法：公共汽车。
"A shortage of buses after a serious flood at the main depot during the rainy season means the system is carrying 200,000 people a day — half the expected capacity."
Main ClauseA shortage of buses … means the system is carrying 200,000 people a day
Subordinate / Relative Clauseafter a serious flood at the main depot during the rainy season (time/cause phrase) + appositive "half the expected capacity"
Grammar NoteLong noun-phrase subject. "means" links cause to effect. The dash adds an appositive figure.
Simple MeaningBecause a flood damaged the depot, there are too few buses, so only 200,000 people a day travel — half of what was planned.
中文雨季主车场遭遇严重洪水后车辆短缺，意味着该系统每天只运送20万人——仅为预期运力的一半。
"Bus rapid transit allows existing stakeholders to get involved."
Main ClauseBus rapid transit allows existing stakeholders to get involved
Subordinate / Relative Clause(simple sentence — "allow somebody to do")
Grammar Note"allow somebody to do something" = make it possible for them. Contrast with a metro firm that "parachutes in".
Simple MeaningA BRT system lets local people and companies already there take part.
中文快速公交让当地已有的利益相关者能够参与进来。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Follow the problem–solution structure of the passage.

Problem & Solution

Problem: huge growth, traffic, informal sprawl, four overloaded roads. Solution: the DART bus rapid transit system. Signal: "But Dar es Salaam is pinning its hopes on a solution…".

Cause & Effect

Growth (young people arriving) → sprawl on the periphery → long commutes. Flood at the depot → bus shortage → half capacity → long queues. Trace each chain.

Comparison

The passage contrasts BRT with a metro: cheaper, more achievable, and lets local stakeholders take part rather than an international firm "parachuting in". Comparisons often power the T/F/NG answers.

Writer’s Stance

The tone is broadly optimistic about BRT ("transformational", "best hope of ever being connected") while honestly reporting its current problems.
