📋 Passage Overview

Insight or evolution? Two scientists argue that discovery is not the work of lone genius and sudden insight, but a gradual, evolutionary process of trial and error — often driven by mistakes, necessity or luck (Nicholson, the 'acey-deucy' stirrup, the Post-It note). They propose Thorndike's Law of Effect as a better model. Question types: multiple choice (Q27–31), YES / NO / NOT GIVEN (Q32–36) and summary from a word list (Q37–40).

💡 Reading tip: This is an argument passage. Keep asking 'what do the writers believe?' YES/NO/NG tests their view: NO = they would disagree; NOT GIVEN = they never say. Signal words: 'we believe', 'we propose', 'we suggest'.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Conventional wisdom also places great weight on insight in promoting breakthrough scientific achievements, as if ideas spontaneously pop into someone's head – fully formed and functional.
Main ClauseConventional wisdom also places great weight on insight
Subordinate / Relativeas if ideas spontaneously pop into someone's head (manner clause of comparison)
Grammar Note'as if' introduces an imagined comparison; the dash adds a descriptive phrase.
Simple MeaningPeople wrongly think big ideas just appear, complete, in a genius's mind.
中文传统观念也非常看重“灵感”在推动重大科学突破中的作用,仿佛想法会完整成形地突然冒出来。
These successes are all the more noteworthy given the fact that Nicholson was wrong about the presence of proto-elements: they do not actually exist.
Main ClauseThese successes are all the more noteworthy
Subordinate / Relativegiven the fact that Nicholson was wrong… (reason phrase); that Nicholson was wrong about the presence of proto-elements (noun clause)
Grammar Note'given (the fact) that' = 'considering that'; the colon introduces an explanation.
Simple MeaningHis successes are even more impressive because his proto-elements do not really exist.
中文考虑到尼科尔森关于“原元素”存在的看法是错的——它们其实并不存在——这些成就就更加值得注意。
If mutations prove beneficial, then the animal or the scientific theory will continue to thrive and perhaps reproduce.
Main Clausethe animal or the scientific theory will continue to thrive and perhaps reproduce
Subordinate / RelativeIf mutations prove beneficial (conditional clause)
Grammar NoteFirst conditional 'If + present, will + base'; 'prove' = 'turn out to be'.
Simple MeaningIf a change turns out useful, the animal — or the theory — survives and spreads.
中文如果突变被证明有益,那么这种动物或这一科学理论就会继续繁荣,甚至可能繁衍传播。
Just like Darwin's Law of Natural Selection, the Law of Effect involves an entirely mechanical process of variation and selection, without any end objective in sight.
Main Clausethe Law of Effect involves an entirely mechanical process of variation and selection
Subordinate / RelativeJust like Darwin's Law of Natural Selection (comparison phrase); without any end objective in sight (prepositional phrase)
Grammar NoteFront comparison phrase sets up the parallel; 'without … in sight' = 'with no goal'.
Simple MeaningLike natural selection, the Law of Effect is a blind, goal-free process of variation and selection.
中文与达尔文的自然选择法则一样,效果律是一个完全机械的变异与选择过程,并无任何最终目标。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Thesis vs common belief. Paragraph 1 sets out the popular assumption (genius + insight); the writers spend the rest of the passage arguing against it. Recognising this frame answers Q27 (outline a common assumption) and Q28.

Examples as evidence. Nicholson (mistake), Westrope's stirrup (necessity), the Post-It note (luck) each illustrate a different non-genius source of innovation — the basis of the Q37–40 summary.

Analogy. The writers repeatedly compare scientific progress to biological evolution (variation and selection). Spotting this analogy clarifies the Law of Effect questions (Q34–36).
