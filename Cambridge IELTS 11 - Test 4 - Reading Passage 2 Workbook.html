📋 Passage Overview

'An Introduction to Film Sound' argues that a film's sound track is as complex as its images. It covers the three ingredients — the human voice, sound effects and music — and examines dialogue, synchronous and asynchronous sound effects, and background music.

题型:四选一 Q14–18 + T/F/NG Q19–23 + 句子配对 Q24–26。

💡 Reading tip: The five multiple-choice questions each name a paragraph or an example ('In the first paragraph…', 'the writer refers to Humphrey Bogart…', 'the "click" of a door'). Go straight to that spot — you do not need to read the whole passage first.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

A meaningful sound track is often as complicated as the image on the screen, and is ultimately just as much the responsibility of the director.
Main ClauseA meaningful sound track is often as complicated as the image … and is … just as much the responsibility of the director
Subordinate / Relativetwo parallel comparative structures: 'as complicated as' and 'just as much … as'
Grammar NoteTwo 'as … as' comparisons share one subject. This sentence alone answers Q14.
Simple MeaningThe sound is as complex as the picture, and the director is equally responsible for it.
中文一条有意义的声轨往往与银幕上的画面同样复杂,而且归根结底同样是导演的责任。
When voice textures fit the performer's physiognomy and gestures, a whole and very realistic persona emerges.
Main Clausea whole and very realistic persona emerges
Subordinate / Relative'When voice textures fit the performer's physiognomy and gestures' = time/condition clause
Grammar Note'When' here means 'if / whenever'. Q26 is a direct paraphrase of this sentence.
Simple MeaningIf the voice matches the face and the movements, the character feels completely real.
中文当嗓音质感与演员的相貌和举止相契合时,一个完整而极为真实的人物形象便浮现出来。
In this way the film-maker was able to portray what Thomas Sobchack and Vivian Sobchack call the 'inadequacy of human responses when compared with the magnificent technology created by man'.
Main Clausethe film-maker was able to portray what … Sobchack and … Sobchack call the 'inadequacy of human responses…'
Subordinate / Relative'what … call …' = nominal relative clause functioning as the object; 'when compared with…' = reduced passive clause
Grammar Note'what X calls Y' packages a quotation as an object. Note the reduced clause 'when compared with'.
Simple MeaningBy using so little dialogue, the film showed how weak human reactions seem beside great technology.
中文通过这种方式,电影制作者得以呈现索博查克夫妇所称的“与人类创造的宏伟技术相比,人类反应的不足”。
However, if the 'click' of an opening door is part of an ominous action such as a burglary, the sound mixer may call attention to the 'click' with an increase in volume; this helps to engage the audience in a moment of suspense.
Main Clausethe sound mixer may call attention to the 'click' with an increase in volume; this helps to engage the audience
Subordinate / Relative'if the click … is part of an ominous action such as a burglary' = conditional clause
Grammar NoteConditional + semicolon joining a consequence. Directly answers Q18.
Simple MeaningIf the click belongs to something threatening, the sound mixer makes it louder to build suspense.
中文然而,如果开门的“咔嗒”声属于入室行窃这类不祥的动作,混音师可能会提高音量以引起注意,这有助于让观众进入悬念时刻。
We need to foster an awareness of film sound as well as film space so as to truly appreciate an art form that sprang to life during the twentieth century — the modern film.
Main ClauseWe need to foster an awareness of film sound as well as film space
Subordinate / Relative'so as to truly appreciate an art form' = purpose; 'that sprang to life during the twentieth century' = relative clause; '— the modern film' = dash apposition
Grammar Note'so as to' = 'in order to'. The dash names the art form at the very end for emphasis.
Simple MeaningTo appreciate modern film properly, we must notice its sound as well as its images.
中文我们需要培养对电影声音以及电影空间的认识,才能真正欣赏这一在二十世纪诞生的艺术形式——现代电影。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Classification. The passage announces its own structure in paragraph 1: three ingredients, then four topics (dialogue, synchronous effects, asynchronous effects, music). Every later paragraph fills one slot.

Definition → example → effect. Each type of sound is defined, illustrated ('For example, if the film portrays a character playing the piano…'), and then explained in terms of its effect on the audience.

Contrast between two films. 2001 (almost no dialogue) is set against Bringing Up Baby (non-stop dialogue). Both are used to make the same larger point: the amount of dialogue is a deliberate choice.

Conscious vs. subconscious. A recurring thread: 'the audience may only subconsciously note the expected sound'; 'Usually not meant to be noticeable'; 'noted by only our subconscious minds'. Q23 is FALSE because of this.

Convention vs. innovation. The last paragraph balances the expected ('acceleration of music during car chases', 'creaky doors in horror films') against the inventive ('sound is often brilliantly conceived').
