Practice Workbook — "Australia's Sporting Success"

📋 Passage Overview

This passage explains why Australian sports teams are so successful internationally. The answer lies in a scientific, well-funded system: institutions like the Australian Institute of Sport (AIS) use precise data measurement, biomechanical analysis, and medical testing to squeeze small improvements out of every athlete.

The article gives concrete examples — a 3D swimming analysis tool, immune-system saliva tests, and "competition models" that predict winning times — then notes that although rivals can copy individual technologies (like coolant jackets or altitude tents), no country has yet replicated Australia's whole system.

💡 Reading strategy tip: This passage has six lettered paragraphs (A–F) and mixes a paragraph-matching task with a classification task. Skim each paragraph once to note its single main idea before you start answering — that "one-line summary" approach makes paragraph-matching much faster.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"A big part of the secret is an extensive and expensive network of sporting academies underpinned by science and medicine."
Main ClauseA big part of the secret is an extensive and expensive network of sporting academies
Reduced Clauseunderpinned by science and medicine (= which is underpinned by science and medicine)
Grammar NotePast participle clause reducing a relative clause ("which is underpinned")
Simple MeaningAustralia's success partly comes from a costly system of academies built on science and medicine.
中文秘诀的很大一部分在于一个由科学和医学支撑的庞大且昂贵的体育学院网络。
"So why did he finish 35 hundredths of a second down? 'His turn times were 44 hundredths of a second behind the other guy,' says Mason."
Main Clausewhy did he finish 35 hundredths of a second down?
Supporting Clause"His turn times were 44 hundredths of a second behind" (direct quotation, explaining cause)
Grammar NoteRhetorical question followed by a quoted explanation — a common structure in this passage
Simple MeaningThe swimmer lost time mainly because of slow turns, even though he swam faster overall.
中文那么他为什么慢了百分之三十五秒？梅森说，他的转身时间比对手慢了百分之四十四秒。
"But Australia's success story is about more than easily copied technological fixes, and up to now no nation has replicated its all-encompassing system."
Main Clause 1Australia's success story is about more than easily copied technological fixes
Main Clause 2no nation has replicated its all-encompassing system
Grammar NoteTwo independent clauses joined by "and" — present perfect tense ("has replicated") shows an unfinished timeframe up to now
Simple MeaningTechnology alone doesn't explain Australia's success — the whole system is what's hard to copy.
中文但澳大利亚的成功不仅仅在于容易被模仿的技术改进，迄今为止没有一个国家复制出其全方位的体系。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect: "IgA levels suddenly fall below a certain level → training is eased or dropped altogether → soon IgA levels start rising again." This is a clear cause-effect chain used to prevent illness in athletes.

Comparison: The passage compares the swimmer who finished third (but swam faster overall) with the swimmer who finished second, isolating "turn times" as the deciding factor.

Problem → Solution: Problem — athletes get 11–12 colds a year, disrupting training. Solution — AIS and University of Newcastle developed a saliva protein test to catch illness early.

Example-giving: The coolant-lined jackets (1996 Atlanta Olympics) and the "altitude tent" are both given as concrete examples of technology that other countries later copied.

Author's Attitude: The tone is admiring but balanced — the writer credits Australia's system as unique ("all-encompassing"), while acknowledging that individual technologies are copiable.
