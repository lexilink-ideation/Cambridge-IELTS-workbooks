📋 Passage Overview

'The Benefits of Being Bilingual' — a survey of research showing that speaking two languages changes the brain for the better. It covers language co-activation and eye-tracking, the Stroop Task and conflict management, brain-stem responses to sound in noise, easier third-language learning, protection against Alzheimer's symptoms, and an experiment with seven-month-old babies. Question types: table completion (Q27–31), YES / NO / NOT GIVEN (Q32–36) and matching information (Q37–40).

💡 Reading tip: YES / NO / NOT GIVEN here tests precision of scope. Watch for words like 'consistently', 'in all situations', 'fewer… suffer'. The passage says bilinguals do better in noise and show symptoms later — not that they are better everywhere or get the disease less often.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

In the past, such children were considered to be at a disadvantage compared with their monolingual peers.
Main Clausesuch children were considered to be at a disadvantage
Subordinate / Relative'compared with their monolingual peers' = reduced comparison clause; 'In the past' = time adverbial that sets up an implied contrast with now.
Grammar NotePassive reporting structure 'were considered to be'. The time phrase 'In the past' plus the later 'however' creates the change of attitude tested in Q32.
Simple MeaningPeople used to think bilingual children were worse off than children who spoke only one language.
中文过去,这类儿童被认为与只讲一种语言的同龄人相比处于劣势。
Over the past few decades, however, technological advances have allowed researchers to look more deeply at how bilingualism interacts with and changes the cognitive and neurological systems, thereby identifying several clear benefits of being bilingual.
Main Clausetechnological advances have allowed researchers to look more deeply
Subordinate / Relative'at how bilingualism interacts with and changes the cognitive and neurological systems' = wh- noun clause; 'thereby identifying several clear benefits' = result participle clause.
Grammar Note'allow somebody to do'; two verbs share one object ('interacts with and changes … the systems'); 'thereby + -ing' = and as a result.
Simple MeaningIn recent decades, better technology has let researchers study how bilingualism changes the brain, and they have found clear benefits.
中文然而在过去几十年里,技术进步使研究者得以更深入地考察双语如何与认知和神经系统互动并改变它们,从而识别出双语的若干明显益处。
A Russian-English bilingual asked to 'pick up a marker' from a set of objects would look more at a stamp than someone who doesn't know Russian, because the Russian word for 'stamp', marka, sounds like the English word he or she heard, 'marker'.
Main ClauseA Russian-English bilingual … would look more at a stamp than someone who doesn't know Russian
Subordinate / Relative'asked to "pick up a marker" from a set of objects' = reduced passive relative clause (= 'who was asked to…'); 'because the Russian word for "stamp", marka, sounds like the English word' = reason clause containing an appositive ('marka').
Grammar NoteReduced relative + comparative 'more … than' + reason clause. 'would' here describes a typical result, not the past.
Simple MeaningA Russian-English speaker told to pick up a marker looks at a stamp more than a non-Russian speaker does, because marka (Russian for stamp) sounds like marker.
中文一位俄英双语者在被要求从一组物品中“拿起马克笔”时,会比不懂俄语的人更多地看向邮票,因为俄语中“邮票”一词marka听起来像他/她听到的英语词marker。
Bilinguals are also better at switching between two tasks; for example, when bilinguals have to switch from categorizing objects by colour to categorizing them by shape, they do so more quickly than monolingual people, reflecting better cognitive control when having to make rapid changes of strategy.
Main ClauseBilinguals are also better at switching between two tasks
Subordinate / Relative'when bilinguals have to switch from… to…' = time clause; 'reflecting better cognitive control' = result participle; 'when having to make rapid changes of strategy' = reduced time clause.
Grammar NoteSemicolon + 'for example' introducing the illustration; 'from … to …' with parallel gerunds.
Simple MeaningBilinguals switch tasks faster — for instance from sorting by colour to sorting by shape — which shows better mental control.
中文双语者在两项任务之间切换也更出色;例如,当他们必须从按颜色分类物品转为按形状分类时,速度比单语者更快,这反映出在需要快速改变策略时更好的认知控制能力。
When researchers play the same sound to both groups in the presence of background noise, however, the bilingual listeners' neural response is considerably larger, reflecting better encoding of the sound's fundamental frequency, a feature of sound closely related to pitch perception.
Main Clausethe bilingual listeners' neural response is considerably larger
Subordinate / Relative'When researchers play the same sound to both groups in the presence of background noise' = time/condition clause; 'reflecting better encoding of the sound's fundamental frequency' = result participle; 'a feature of sound closely related to pitch perception' = appositive defining that term.
Grammar NoteThe condition 'in the presence of background noise' is essential — without noise the two groups are the same. This single phrase decides Q35.
Simple MeaningWith background noise, bilingual listeners' brains respond much more strongly, showing they process the sound's base pitch better.
中文然而,当研究者在有背景噪音的情况下向两组播放同一声音时,双语听者的神经反应明显更强,这反映出他们对声音基频——一种与音高感知密切相关的声音特征——的编码更好。
Surprisingly, the bilinguals' brains had more physical signs of disease than their monolingual counterparts, even though their outward behaviour and abilities were the same.
Main Clausethe bilinguals' brains had more physical signs of disease than their monolingual counterparts
Subordinate / Relative'even though their outward behaviour and abilities were the same' = concession clause.
Grammar NoteComparative 'more … than' + 'even though' concession. The whole point is a contrast between inside (worse) and outside (the same).
Simple MeaningBilingual patients' brains were physically more damaged, yet they behaved and functioned just as well.
中文令人惊讶的是,双语者的大脑比单语者显示出更多的病理体征,尽管他们的外在行为和能力是相同的。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Old view → new evidence. Paragraph A is built on a reversal: 'In the past, such children were considered to be at a disadvantage… Over the past few decades, however…'. That single 'however' is the answer to Q32 (attitudes have changed).

Mechanism → evidence → implication. Paragraph B explains how word recognition works (sounds arrive in order, the brain guesses), then gives the eye-tracking evidence, then names the phenomenon (language co-activation). Scientific paragraphs usually run in this order, and the table in Q27–31 follows it exactly.

Cost before benefit. Paragraph C opens with the downside — slower picture naming, more tip-of-the-tongue states — and only then turns it into an advantage ('As a result, the constant juggling… creates a need to control…'). Q40 asks for the negative consequences, so the answer is C, not A.

Conditional advantage. Paragraph D is a controlled comparison: no noise → same response; with noise → bigger bilingual response. Whenever a paragraph sets up two conditions, expect a YES/NO/NOT GIVEN question that removes the condition.

Age range as structure. The last two paragraphs bookend the lifespan: F on older adults with Alzheimer's, G on seven-month-old babies. The final sentence ties them together — 'for very young children, as well as for older people'. That is how you locate Q38 (benefits before we learn to speak) in G.
