Cambridge IELTS 7 — Test 3 — Reading Passage 3

"Plans to Protect the Forests of Europe" — Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

This passage describes the 1990 Strasbourg Ministerial Conference, where 31 European countries met to coordinate policy on protecting Europe's declining forests. It explains the biological, economic, and recreational roles forests play, dispels the myth of untouched "natural" forest (all European forests are described as artificial/man-shaped), and summarises six detailed resolutions covering monitoring, genetic diversity, forest fires, mountain forests, tree-disease research, and ecosystem research networks.

The passage closes by stressing that any forest policy must be flexible enough to adapt to future, unforeseen changes.

💡 Reading strategy tip: This passage is dense with policy detail organised as six numbered resolutions. When you see a "matching" question referring to numbered items (Resolution 1, 2, 3...), scan for ordinal signposts ("The first proposes...", "The second resolution concentrates on...", "Finally...") — they mark exactly where each answer lives.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"European countries are becoming increasingly concerned by major threats to European forests, threats which know no frontiers other than those of geography or climate: air pollution, soil deterioration, the increasing number of forest fires and sometimes even the mismanagement of our woodland and forest heritage."
Main Clause"European countries are becoming increasingly concerned by major threats..."
Subordinate/Relative Clause"threats which know no frontiers other than..." (relative clause + appositive list after the colon)
Grammar NoteThe colon introduces a list that specifies exactly what "threats" means — a common academic technique for adding detail.
Simple MeaningEuropean countries worry about forest threats that cross borders freely: pollution, soil damage, fires, and poor management.
中文欧洲各国日益担忧森林面临的重大威胁，这些威胁除了受地理或气候限制外，不受国界约束：空气污染、土壤退化、森林火灾增多，有时甚至是林地和森林资源管理不善。
"Their initial task was to decide which of the many forest problems of concern to Europe involved the largest number of countries and might be the subject of joint action."
Main Clause"Their initial task was to decide..."
Subordinate Clause"which of the many forest problems... involved the largest number of countries and might be the subject of joint action" (indirect question, noun clause)
Grammar NoteTwo verbs ("involved" and "might be") share the same subject "which... problems" — a compound predicate inside the noun clause.
Simple MeaningExperts first had to choose which forest problems affected the most countries and were suitable for joint action.
中文他们最初的任务是确定，在众多欧洲关注的森林问题中，哪些问题涉及的国家数量最多，可作为共同行动的议题。
"All European forests are artificial, having been adapted and exploited by man for thousands of years."
Main Clause"All European forests are artificial"
Subordinate Element"having been adapted and exploited by man for thousands of years" (perfect participle clause of reason)
Grammar NoteThe perfect participle ("having been...") shows an action completed before/leading up to the main statement — explains WHY forests are "artificial."
Simple MeaningNo European forest is truly untouched — humans have shaped and used all of them for millennia.
中文所有欧洲森林都是人工的，因为几千年来一直被人类改造和利用。
"This means that a forest policy is vital, that it must transcend national frontiers and generations of people, and that it must allow for the inevitable changes that take place in the forests, in needs, and hence in policy."
Main Clause"This means that..." (three parallel "that" clauses follow)
Subordinate Clause"that a forest policy is vital" / "that it must transcend..." / "that it must allow for..." — three coordinated noun clauses; "that take place in..." is a further relative clause
Grammar NoteParallel structure: three "that" clauses of equal grammatical weight, all objects of "means," build a cumulative argument.
Simple MeaningTherefore forest policy must be essential, cross borders and generations, and remain flexible as needs change.
中文这意味着森林政策至关重要，它必须超越国界和世代更替，并且必须能够适应森林、需求乃至政策本身不可避免的变化。
"Forest decline is still poorly understood but leads to the loss of a high proportion of a tree's needles or leaves."
Main Clause"Forest decline is still poorly understood but leads to..."
Subordinate ElementCoordinated verb phrase: "is... understood" + "leads to..." (joined by "but," showing contrast between limited understanding and known effect)
Grammar Note"But" here contrasts a limitation (poor understanding) with a known fact (the visible effect) — common structure for hedged scientific claims.
Simple MeaningScientists don't fully understand why forests decline, but they know it causes trees to lose a lot of leaves/needles.
中文森林衰退目前仍未被充分理解，但它会导致树木大量落叶或落针。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect

Atmospheric pollutants (nitrogen and sulphur dioxide compounds), worsened by drought and soil acidification, are identified as the principal cause of forest decline, whose effect is major loss of tree leaves/needles across 30–50% of trees.

Contrast

The "myth" of untouched natural forest is contrasted with the reality that all European forests are artificial — shaped by human use for thousands of years.

Comparison

The passage compares forests' three functions — biological ("green lung"), economic (renewed wood supply), and recreational (leisure activities) — noting that the economic role has been valued since "the dawn of man," while the other two were recognised only in recent centuries.

Examples

Specific leisure activities are given as examples of the recreational function: "hunting, riding and hiking." Specific threats are listed as examples under "major threats": air pollution, soil deterioration, forest fires, mismanagement.

Problem & Solution

Problem: serious imbalances threaten Europe's forests. Solution: coordinated international policy — the Strasbourg conference and its six resolutions (surveillance, genetic diversity, databank, mountain forest research, Eurosilva, ecosystem research network).

Chronology

The passage references a sequence: forest decline "over the last decade and a half" → two preparatory expert meetings → the December 1990 Strasbourg conference → six resulting resolutions → ongoing future discussion.

Author's Attitude

The tone is formal, advocacy-driven, and forward-looking — emphasising urgency ("real concern throughout Europe") while framing the conference positively as a landmark achievement in international cooperation ("one of the first events on such a scale to reach this conclusion").
