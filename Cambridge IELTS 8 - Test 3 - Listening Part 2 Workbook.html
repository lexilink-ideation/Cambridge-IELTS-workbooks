📋 Quick Review

On the local radio show Focus on the Arts, host Dave Green describes The National Arts Centre — what it is famous for, the facilities in the complex, its history (bombed in 1940, planned and built later, opened 1983), who manages it, and its opening days. He then lists star attractions for the week in a table (day, time, event, venue, price). It is a Part 2 monologue with notes and a table to complete.

💡 Listening strategy tip: Follow the notes and table headings in order. Listen for reworded facts (‘famous throughout the world as one of the major venues for classical music’ → ‘well known for: classical music’). Prices and dates are read quickly — write the exact number and watch the reduced price (‘£4.50, a reduction on the usual £5.50’).

🔄 Paraphrase Training

In IELTS the question words are almost never the words you hear or read. Recognising paraphrase is the key skill. Study each example, then write your own sentence.
Paraphrase Example 1
Question / promptQ11: The Centre is well known for …
What you hear / read"It’s famous throughout the world as one of the major venues for classical music."
Key paraphrase‘well known for’ = famous as → classical music (concerts).
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question / promptQ13–14: Historical background …
What you hear / read"it was planned in the 60s, built in the 70s and eventually opened … in 1983."
Key paraphraseTwo answers: planned (Q13) and 1983 (Q14).
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question / promptQ15: Managed by …
What you hear / read"It is not privately owned … it’s run by the City Council."
Key paraphrase‘managed by’ = run by → City Council (not private).
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question / promptQ19: Ticket price of the film …
What you hear / read"tickets cost just £4.50, which is a reduction on the usual price of £5.50."
Key paraphraseWrite the reduced price (£4.50), not the usual price (£5.50).
