"Let's Go Bats" — Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

"Let's Go Bats" explains why bats hunt at night and how they solve the "engineering problem" of finding their way and their prey in complete darkness. The passage compares bats to other creatures that cannot rely on sight (deep-sea fish, blind humans), and traces how the discovery of "facial vision" in blind people led to sonar, radar, and finally the scientific understanding of bat echolocation.

The passage has five paragraphs (A–E) and moves from evolutionary background, to an engineering problem, to possible solutions (light vs. echoes), and finally to the history of the word "echolocation."

💡 Reading strategy tip: This passage is organised like a science-writing argument: problem → possible solutions → best solution → naming of the phenomenon. When you skim before reading questions, try to label each paragraph with one of these roles — it makes Matching Information (Q1–5) much faster.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"Given that there is a living to be made at night, and given that alternative daytime trades are thoroughly occupied, natural selection has favoured bats that make a go of the night-hunting trade."
Main Clausenatural selection has favoured bats...
Subordinate / Relative ClauseTwo parallel "Given that..." clauses (reason); "that make a go of the night-hunting trade" (relative clause modifying "bats")
Grammar Note"Given that" = a formal way of saying "because/considering that." Two parallel "given that" clauses build the reasoning before the main clause.
Simple MeaningBecause night offered food and daytime jobs were already taken by other animals, evolution favoured bats that became successful night hunters.
中文既然夜间有生存机会，白天的生态位又已被占满，自然选择便偏爱那些成功进行夜间捕猎的蝙蝠。
"Fish and dolphins that live in extremely muddy water cannot see because, although there is light, it is obstructed and scattered by the dirt in the water."
Main ClauseFish and dolphins... cannot see
Subordinate / Relative Clause"that live in extremely muddy water" (relative clause); "because... it is obstructed and scattered by the dirt" (reason clause, containing a concession "although there is light")
Grammar NoteA relative clause identifies which fish/dolphins are meant; a "because" clause gives the reason, itself containing an "although" concession clause.
Simple MeaningFish and dolphins living in very muddy water can't see, because even though light exists, the dirt blocks and scatters it.
中文生活在极浑浊水域中的鱼类和海豚看不见东西，因为尽管有光线，但泥沙会阻挡并散射光线。
"One report tells of a totally blind boy who could ride his tricycle at good speed round the block near his home, using facial vision."
Main ClauseOne report tells of a totally blind boy
Subordinate / Relative Clause"who could ride his tricycle... using facial vision" (relative clause describing the boy, containing a participle phrase "using facial vision")
Grammar Note"who" introduces a relative clause; "using facial vision" is a present participle phrase showing manner/method.
Simple MeaningAccording to one report, a completely blind boy could ride his tricycle quickly around his block by using facial vision.
中文有一份报告提到一个完全失明的男孩，他能利用"面部视觉"以相当快的速度骑三轮车绕过家附近的街区。
"Blind people, without even being aware of the fact, are actually using echoes of their own footsteps and of other sounds, to sense the presence of obstacles."
Main ClauseBlind people... are actually using echoes... to sense the presence of obstacles
Subordinate / Relative Clause"without even being aware of the fact" (concessive prepositional phrase, functions like a subordinate clause)
Grammar Note"without + -ing" expresses an action done unconsciously — a compact way of writing "even though they don't realise it."
Simple MeaningBlind people use the echoes of their footsteps and other sounds to detect obstacles, even though they don't realise they're doing it.
中文盲人在自己都没有意识到的情况下，实际上是利用自己脚步声及其他声音的回声来感知障碍物的存在。
"It is technically incorrect to talk about bat 'radar', since they do not use radio waves."
Main ClauseIt is technically incorrect to talk about bat 'radar'
Subordinate / Relative Clause"since they do not use radio waves" (reason clause)
Grammar Note"It is + adjective + to + verb" is a common formal structure for stating a judgement; "since" here means "because," not "from the time that."
Simple MeaningCalling bats' system "radar" is technically wrong, because bats do not actually use radio waves — they use sound.
中文从技术上讲，把蝙蝠的系统称为"雷达"是不准确的，因为它们并不使用无线电波。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect

Cause: The daytime economy was already fully exploited by other animals like birds. → Effect: Natural selection favoured bats that specialised in night-hunting.

Contrast

The passage contrasts manufactured light (used by fireflies, humans) with echo-based navigation (used by bats, blind people, submarines) — the text argues echo-based systems are far more energy-efficient for "seeing" one's own path than light is.

Comparison

Bats' biological sonar is compared to human-made radar and sonar technology — the passage states "the underlying mathematical theories of radar and sonar are very similar," and that engineers learned from applying radar theory to bats.

Examples

The passage gives examples of other animals with limited/no vision: night-flying insects, deep-sea fish, whales, fish and dolphins in muddy water — all used to show that "seeing" in the dark is a general biological problem, not unique to bats.

Definition

"Echolocation" is defined in the final paragraph as a term coined by zoologist Donald Griffin "to cover both sonar and radar, whether used by animals or by human instruments."

Problem / Solution

Problem: How can a creature move and hunt without light? Solution path traced by the passage: manufactured light (energy-expensive) → facial vision in blind humans (echo-based) → sonar/radar instruments → bat echolocation (the most refined natural solution).

Chronology

The passage moves through time: dinosaur era (mammals forced into night) → mass extinction 65 million years ago → modern understanding of facial vision → WWII sonar/radar devices → Donald Griffin's coining of "echolocation."

Author's Attitude

The tone is admiring toward bats' evolutionary "engineering": the author says their system would "strike an engineer dumb with admiration," showing genuine scientific appreciation rather than neutral reporting.
