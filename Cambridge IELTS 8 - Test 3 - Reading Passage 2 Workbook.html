📋 Passage Overview

This passage, ‘The Nature of Genius’, examines how we think about geniuses and gifted people. It traces the word ‘genius’ from Roman times, lists common myths about giftedness, questions 19th-century studies for lacking objectivity, and argues that geniuses stand at the top of a continuum of human ability — superior in degree but not fundamentally different from the rest of us — and often pay a high personal price for their achievements.

💡 Reading strategy tip: Keep separating reported popular beliefs from the writer’s own opinion. For ‘which beliefs are reported’, scan the myth list in paragraph 2. For True/False/Not Given, base every answer on the text — choose Not Given when the writer expresses no view (e.g. whether being a genius is ‘worth’ it).

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"In the mythology of giftedness, it is popularly believed that if people are talented in one area, they must be defective in another."
Main Clauseit is popularly believed that …
Subordinate / Relativeif people are talented in one area, they must be defective in another (conditional noun clause)
Grammar Note‘it is believed that …’ reports an opinion; the ‘if …’ clause is the belief’s content.
Simple MeaningA common myth says that a talent in one area means a weakness in another.
中文在关于天赋的迷思中，人们普遍认为：如果一个人在某方面有才华，就一定在另一方面有缺陷。
"Two of the most significant aspects of these studies are the frequency with which early teaching had beneficial effects but caused great difficulties of adjustment later in their lives."
Main ClauseTwo of the most significant aspects … are the frequency with which early teaching had beneficial effects but caused great difficulties
Subordinate / Relativewith which early teaching had … but caused … (relative clause with ‘but’ contrast)
Grammar Note‘the frequency with which …’ = how often …; ‘but’ links a good and a bad effect.
Simple MeaningOften early teaching helped the children develop but later made it hard for them to adjust.
中文这些研究最重要的两个方面是：早期教育常常带来益处，却也在他们后来的生活中造成很大的适应困难。
"Genius is a term we apply to those whom we recognise for their outstanding achievements and who stand near the end of the continuum of human abilities."
Main ClauseGenius is a term we apply to those
Subordinate / Relativewhom we recognise for their outstanding achievements / who stand near the end of the continuum (two relative clauses)
Grammar Note‘whom’ (object) and ‘who’ (subject) both describe ‘those’; parallel relative clauses.
Simple MeaningWe call people ‘geniuses’ when they achieve outstandingly and sit at the top of human ability.
中文“天才”是我们用来称呼那些因杰出成就而被认可、并处于人类能力连续统一体末端的人的词。
"We may envy their achievements and fame, but we should also recognise the price they may have paid in terms of perseverance, single-mindedness and dedication."
Main ClauseWe may envy their achievements and fame, but we should also recognise the price they may have paid
Subordinate / Relative(that) they may have paid in terms of … (relative clause, ‘that’ omitted)
Grammar Note‘may envy … but should also recognise …’ balances two ideas; ‘in terms of’ = measured by.
Simple MeaningWe might be jealous of geniuses, yet we should see the sacrifices they made to succeed.
中文我们也许羡慕他们的成就与名声，但也应认识到他们在毅力、专注和奉献方面所付出的代价。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Reported beliefs vs the writer’s view — Paragraph 2 lists popular myths about giftedness; the writer does not endorse them. Distinguishing report from opinion answers Q14–18 and several T/F/NG items.

Cause & Effect — Early encouragement helped intellectual development but caused difficulties of adjustment later. Note the double effect.

Comparison — Geniuses are ‘the peaks’ near the end of a continuum of human ability; they are superior in degree, not fundamentally different in kind.

Concession — The writer concedes we may envy geniuses’ fame, but stresses the price they pay — perseverance, dedication, restrictions on personal life.
