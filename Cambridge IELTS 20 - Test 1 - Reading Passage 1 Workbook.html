Reading Passage 1

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 1 — Reading Passage 1

The kākāpō · True / False / Not Given + Note Completion (Q1–13)

📋 Passage Overview

This passage describes the kākāpō, the world’s only flightless parrot, which lives in New Zealand and is critically endangered. The first half explains what the bird looks like, what it eats and how it breeds; the second half tells the story of its decline after humans arrived and of the long conservation effort to save it, from Richard Henry in 1894 to the Recovery Plan that still guides management today. It is a factual, chronological passage, so once you find the right decade you are almost always standing on the answer.

What the passage argues: That the kākāpō was pushed to the edge of extinction by introduced predators rather than by anything wrong with the bird itself, and that recovery only became possible once those predators were removed and the remaining birds were intensively managed.

段落结构 Structure: 第1–3段：外形、习性与繁殖（不会飞、独居、素食、雌鸟独自孵蛋）。第4段：人类到来后的衰退——波利尼西亚人、欧洲殖民者及其带来的狗、鼠、猫、鼬。第5–6段：保护尝试的时间线——1894年 Richard Henry、1949–73年考察、1977–80年 Rakiura 岛发现雌鸟。第7–8段：1980–97年迁往三个岛屿保护区、1996年恢复计划与成效。第9段：当前三大目标。

Task type: Q1–6 True / False / Not Given (statements follow the order of the passage) and Q7–13 Note completion — ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER from the passage. Writing two words where one is asked for scores zero.

💡 Reading strategy tip: Two very different tasks sit on one passage. For True / False / Not Given (Q1–6) test the statement word by word: FALSE needs the passage to say the opposite; NOT GIVEN means the passage never makes that comparison or claim at all. For the note completion (Q7–13) first decide the word type each gap needs (a plant part? a place? a year? a group of people?), then use the headings Arrival of Polynesian settlers / Arrival of European colonisers / Protecting kākāpō as signposts — they follow the passage in order. Copy the word exactly as printed: one word and/or a number, no extra adjectives.
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • Q1 “other parrots” — the passage says the kākāpō is the world’s only flightless parrot. ‘Only’ is the single word that makes this FALSE, not NOT GIVEN.
  • Q4 is a comparison — the passage says both the settlers and the Polynesian rat killed kākāpō, but it never says which was the greater threat. A comparison the writer never makes is always NOT GIVEN.
  • Q7 diet list — the question already gives you ‘fern fronds’ and ‘various parts of a tree’ (leaves, roots, bark), so the only item left in the sentence is bulbs. Cross off what the question has already used.
  • Q11 has two dates close together — males were spotted on Rakiura in 1977, but females were confirmed in 1980. The note says ‘a definite sighting of female kākāpō’.
  • Dates that are not answers — 1894, 1949, 1973, 1974, 1980 (cats eradicated), 1986, 1995, 1996, 2000 and June 2020 all appear. Match the date to its event, never to the nearest number.
  • Plural discipline — Q9 feathers and Q13 stakeholders are plural in the passage; copy the plural. Q8 soil is uncountable — do not add an ‘s’.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Academic Reading sentences are long because they stack clauses, not because the words are hard. Find the main verb first, then peel off the extra layers. 先找主句谓语动词，再逐层剥离修饰成分。
“The female kākāpō has to spend long periods away from the nest searching for food, which leaves the unattended eggs and chicks particularly vulnerable to predators.”
Main ClauseThe female kākāpō has to spend long periods away from the nest.
Subordinate / Relative‘searching for food’ = a participle phrase of purpose; ‘which leaves…’ = a non-defining relative clause commenting on the whole first idea.
Grammar NoteA comma + which does not refer to one noun here — it refers to the entire situation (her being away). Notice the pattern leave + object + adjective (‘leaves the eggs vulnerable’).
Simple MeaningBecause the mother must leave the nest to find food, nothing protects the eggs and chicks from predators.
中文雌性鸮鹦鹉必须长时间离巢觅食，这使得无人看护的鸟蛋和雏鸟特别容易遭到捕食者的攻击。
“By the time European colonisers arrived in the early 1800s, kākāpō had become confined to the central North Island and forested parts of the South Island.”
Main ClauseKākāpō had become confined to the central North Island and forested parts of the South Island.
Subordinate / Relative‘By the time European colonisers arrived in the early 1800s’ = a time clause fixing the deadline.
Grammar NoteBy the time + past simple, + past perfect. The past perfect tells you the shrinking had already happened before the Europeans came — so the earlier damage was Polynesian.
Simple MeaningBefore Europeans even arrived, the birds were already limited to two regions.
中文到19世纪初欧洲殖民者到达时，鸮鹦鹉已经被局限在北岛中部和南岛的森林地区。
“A great deal of habitat was lost through forest clearance, and introduced species such as deer depleted the remaining forests of food.”
Main ClauseTwo coordinated main clauses joined by ‘and’: habitat was lost… / introduced species… depleted…
Subordinate / Relative‘such as deer’ = an exemplifying phrase inside the subject.
Grammar NoteThe first half is passive (was lost — the agent is unimportant), the second is active (deer did it). Pattern: deplete something of something.
Simple MeaningTrees were cut down, and animals brought in from elsewhere — deer, for example — ate the food that was left.
中文大量栖息地因森林砍伐而丧失，而鹿等引入物种又把残存森林中的食物消耗殆尽。
“Six were caught, but there were no females amongst them and all but one died within a few months of captivity.”
Main ClauseSix were caught. / All but one died within a few months of captivity.
Subordinate / Relative‘but there were no females amongst them’ = a contrasting clause explaining why the capture was useless.
Grammar Noteall but one = every one except one, i.e. five of the six died. Learners often read this as ‘all of them’ — it does not mean that.
Simple MeaningThey caught six birds; none was female, and five of the six soon died in captivity.
中文共捕获六只，但其中没有雌鸟，而且除一只外全都在被圈养的几个月内死亡。
“Rats were found to be a major predator of kākāpō chicks and an insufficient number of chicks survived to offset adult mortality.”
Main ClauseRats were found to be a major predator… / An insufficient number of chicks survived…
Subordinate / Relative‘to offset adult mortality’ = an infinitive of result attached to ‘survived’.
Grammar Notebe found to be = research showed that it was. ‘Insufficient’ is a negative quantity word: too few chicks lived, so the population still fell.
Simple MeaningRats ate many chicks, and too few young birds survived to replace the adults that died.
中文研究发现老鼠是鸮鹦鹉雏鸟的主要捕食者，存活的雏鸟数量不足以抵消成鸟的死亡。
“However, the population did not start to increase until rats were removed from all three islands, and the birds were more intensively managed.”
Main ClauseThe population did not start to increase.
Subordinate / Relative‘until rats were removed… and the birds were more intensively managed’ = two passive time clauses sharing one ‘until’.
Grammar Notenot…until marks a turning point: nothing improved before this moment. This structure is a favourite source of TRUE/FALSE items about causes.
Simple MeaningNumbers only began to rise once rats had gone from all three islands and humans managed the birds much more closely.
中文然而，直到三个岛屿上的老鼠被清除、并对这些鸟进行更为密集的管理之后，种群数量才开始回升。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

IELTS Reading tests whether you follow the relationship between ideas: cause, contrast, example, definition, sequence and the writer’s own attitude. 抓住句子之间的逻辑关系，比逐词翻译更重要。

Cause & effect — the chain that nearly ended the species

Humans arrive → the flightless bird is ‘easy prey’ → dogs and rats come with them → Europeans clear forest and bring deer, cats, stoats and more rats → numbers collapse. Notice the verb that carries the causation: “The fall in kākāpō numbers was accelerated by European colonisation.” The decline had already started; colonisation only sped it up — a distinction True/False items love to test.

Contrast — what ‘However’ and ‘Unfortunately’ are doing

Every hopeful step in this passage is followed by a reversal word. Henry moves birds to a ‘predator-free’ island → Unfortunately, stoats arrive within six years. Eighteen birds are found in Fiordland → However, there were still no females. When you see these signals, expect the next sentence to contain the problem, and often the answer.

Comparison — the trap in Question 4

The passage states that settlers ate kākāpō and that the Polynesian rat preyed on them. It never says which did more damage. A statement that ranks two things (‘a greater threat than’, ‘more important than’) is NOT GIVEN unless the passage itself makes the comparison. Do not build the comparison yourself.

Examples — ‘such as’ introduces a sample, not a complete list

“…introduced species such as deer depleted the remaining forests of food” and “Other predators such as cats, stoats and two more species of rat…”. For Q10 the note asks for an animal which ate the birds’ food, and the only example given is deer — cats and stoats are hunters, not plant-eaters.

Definition & apposition — information inside commas and dashes

“The kākāpō, also known as the owl parrot, is a large, forest-dwelling bird” and “Rakiura — a large island free from stoats, ferrets and weasels”. Commas and dashes often carry a free definition. Reading them tells you what a strange name means without a dictionary.

Problem → solution — the shape of the second half

Problem: rats eat chicks and too few survive. Solution: remove rats from all three islands, move birds between islands, feed adults, hand-raise weak chicks. Result: “the population was on target… a total of 210 birds was recorded”. Q6 sits exactly in the solution list, so scan for the actions, not for the word ‘increase’.

Chronology — dates are your fastest locating tool

700 years ago (Polynesians) → early 1800s (Europeans) → 1894 (Henry, Resolution Island) → 1949–73 (60+ expeditions) → 1974–77 (18 birds, still no females) → 1977 (males on Rakiura) → 1980 (females confirmed) → 1980–97 (evacuation to three islands) → 1995 (51 birds) → 1996 (Recovery Plan) → 2000 (62 birds) → June 2020 (210 birds). Question 11 needs the date attached to females, not to the first sighting.

The writer’s attitude — factual, but quietly hopeful

The tone is neutral reporting until the end, when it becomes measured optimism: “For the first time, there was cautious optimism for the future of kākāpō”. The adjective cautious matters: the writer is not claiming the bird is safe. In a Yes/No/Not Given task, an option saying ‘the species is now secure’ would be wrong for exactly this reason.

🎯 Question-Type Skill Practice

Skill 1 — TRUE / FALSE / NOT GIVEN (Q1–6)

Decide first, then read the reason. The golden rule: FALSE = the passage says the opposite; NOT GIVEN = the passage never says.

  • 1. Other parrots also cannot fly. → FALSE — the passage says the kākāpō is the world’s only flightless parrot, which directly contradicts the statement.
  • 2. Adults produce chicks every year. → FALSE — they breed ‘only in years when food is plentiful’. ‘Only in years when’ is the opposite of ‘every year’.
  • 3. Males bring food to nesting females. → FALSE — ‘Males play no part in incubation or chick-rearing’; the female feeds the chicks alone and leaves the nest to find her own food.
  • 4. The Polynesian rat was a greater threat than the settlers. → NOT GIVEN — both are mentioned as threats, but no comparison of size is ever made.
  • 5. Birds were moved off Rakiura because of feral cats. → TRUE — ‘predation by feral cats… led to a rapid decline… As a result… the surviving population was evacuated’. ‘As a result’ gives you the cause the statement claims.
  • 6. Caring for struggling young birds helped numbers grow. → TRUE — ‘rescuing and hand-raising any failing chicks’ is listed among the management steps that came before the population increase.

Habit to build: underline the one word that carries the risk — other, every, greater, because. That word, not the topic, decides the answer.

Skill 2 — Note Completion (Q7–13): obey the word limit

ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER. Every answer must be copied from the passage, spelled exactly, in the grammatical form the note needs.

  • Q7 bulbs — the note already lists fern fronds and ‘various parts of a tree’, so eliminate leaves, roots and bark. Only bulbs is left.
  • Q8 soil — ‘The 1–4 eggs are laid in soil’. The note says ‘nests are created in… where eggs are laid’, so the gap needs a material or place.
  • Q9 feathers — ‘used its feathers to make soft cloaks’. Cloaks = clothes; the gap needs the body part, and the verb ‘were used’ tells you it is plural.
  • Q10 deer — the note says ‘an animal which they introduced that ate the kākāpō’s food sources’. Deer is the only plant-eater in the introduced list.
  • Q11 1980 — a number is expected after ‘in the year’. 1977 is the year males were spotted; 1980 is when females were confirmed.
  • Q12 funding — ‘an increase in’ needs an uncountable noun; the passage says ‘a higher amount of funding’.
  • Q13 stakeholders — ‘the involvement of…’ needs a group of people. Passage: ‘ensure stakeholders continue to be fully engaged’.

Check before you move on: read the completed note aloud. If it is not grammatical English, your word is in the wrong form.

Skill 3 — Locating information fast

Which paragraph would you scan for each idea? Train yourself to jump straight to the right block instead of re-reading from the top.

  • How long the bird can live → paragraph 1 (physical description, ‘up to 100 years’).
  • What the bird eats → paragraph 2 (‘entirely vegetarian…’).
  • Who looks after the eggs → paragraph 3 (breeding).
  • The effect of dogs and rats → paragraph 4 (arrival of humans).
  • Resolution Island and stoats → paragraph 5 (1894, Richard Henry).
  • The first confirmed females → paragraph 6 (Rakiura, 1977–80).
  • Why breeding on the sanctuary islands failed → paragraph 7 (rats, chick survival).
  • The three current goals → the final paragraph.

Tip: in a chronological passage, write the decade beside each paragraph as you skim. Dates are the cheapest index you will ever build.

Skill 5 — Matching headings practice (extra)

Match each paragraph of this passage to the heading that fits it best. Headings summarise the whole paragraph, never one detail.

  • Paragraph 3 (breeding) → Raising young without help — not ‘A dangerous diet’, which uses a detail from a different paragraph.
  • Paragraph 4 (humans arrive) → How newcomers changed the bird’s fortunes.
  • Paragraph 5 (1894–mid 1900s) → An early rescue attempt that failed.
  • Paragraph 7 (1980–95) → Safe islands, but still no recovery.
  • Paragraph 8 (1996 onwards) → A plan that finally worked.

Watch out: paragraphs 5 and 7 both describe moving birds to islands. The difference is the outcome — total failure in 1894, partial success after 1980. Headings are usually distinguished by result, not by topic.

Skill 6 — Yes / No / Not Given thinking (extra)

These statements are about the writer’s claims rather than plain facts. Decide before reading the reason.

  • Removing rats alone was enough to make numbers rise. → NO — the passage pairs rat removal with more intensive management.
  • The kākāpō is now out of danger. → NO — the writer only reports ‘cautious optimism’ and the bird is still described as critically endangered.
  • Kākāpō are easier to breed in captivity than in the wild. → NOT GIVEN — captivity is mentioned only as something the six caught birds did not survive.
  • Loss of genetic diversity is a concern for the future. → YES — it is the first goal listed in the final paragraph.

Review Quiz

Q1
B. It is the only parrot that cannot fly.
‘It is the world’s only flightless parrot.’ This single sentence answers Question 1 (FALSE).
Q2
C. Because they breed only when food is plentiful.
‘Kākāpō breed in summer and autumn, but only in years when food is plentiful.’ This is why Question 2 is FALSE.
Q3
D. They take no part at all.
‘Males play no part in incubation or chick-rearing — females alone incubate eggs and feed the chicks.’ Question 3 is therefore FALSE.
Q4
C. Because the passage mentions both but never compares them.
Both threats appear in the same paragraph, but no ranking is given. A comparison the writer never makes is always NOT GIVEN.
Q5
B. feral
‘Predation by feral cats on Rakiura Island led to a rapid decline’ — the cause behind the evacuation in Question 5.
Q6
C. bulbs — because the note already covers fronds and tree parts
The note gives you ‘fern fronds’ and ‘various parts of a tree’ (leaves, roots, bark), so the only remaining food in the sentence is bulbs.
Q7
B. Because the note asks for an animal that ate the birds’ food sources.
Deer ‘depleted the remaining forests of food’. Cats and stoats hunted the birds themselves, which is a different idea.
Q8
B. 1980, because that is when females were confirmed present.
The note asks for a definite sighting of female kākāpō. 1977 refers to males; 1980 (Little Barrier cats) is a different island entirely.
Q9
C. Five of the six birds died.
‘All but one’ = everyone except one. Six were caught, so five died and one survived.
Q10
C. Only after rats were removed from all three islands and management became more intensive.
The ‘not…until’ structure marks the turning point: ‘the population did not start to increase until rats were removed from all three islands, and the birds were more intensively managed.’
Q11
B. Rescuing and hand-raising failing chicks.
‘Caring for struggling young birds’ in the question = ‘rescuing and hand-raising any failing chicks’ in the passage.
Q12
C. Carefully hopeful, but still aware of the risks.
The exact phrase is ‘cautious optimism’, and the final paragraph still lists three unfinished goals. Any option claiming the bird is now secure would be wrong.
