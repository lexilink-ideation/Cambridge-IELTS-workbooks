📋 Passage Overview

Cutty Sark: the fastest sailing ship of all time. A narrative history of the last surviving tea clipper, built in 1869. The passage covers her odd name from a Robert Burns poem, the contract that ruined her builders, the lost race against Thermopylae, the threat from steam ships and the Suez Canal, her great years in the Australian wool trade under Captain Woodget, and her rescue and preservation at Greenwich.

💡 Reading tip: Q1–8 are TRUE/FALSE/NOT GIVEN and follow the passage in order — a great chance to work sequentially. Q9–13 are sentence completion, ONE WORD ONLY, from the second half of the passage. Dates are your anchors here: 1869, 1870, 1872, 1878, 1880, 1922, 1923, 1954, 2007, 2014.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

From the 1840s until 1869, when the Suez Canal opened and steam propulsion was replacing sail, clippers dominated world trade.
Main Clauseclippers dominated world trade
Subordinate / Relativewhen the Suez Canal opened and steam propulsion was replacing sail — a non-defining relative clause of time modifying '1869'
Grammar NoteA long time adverbial fronted before the main clause, with a relative clause embedded inside it. Find the comma after 'sail' and the main clause begins.
Simple MeaningClippers ruled world trade from the 1840s to 1869, the year the Suez Canal opened.
中文从19世纪40年代直到1869年——那一年苏伊士运河开通、蒸汽动力正取代风帆——飞剪船主导了世界贸易。
To carry out construction, Willis chose a new shipbuilding firm, Scott & Linton, and ensured that the contract with them put him in a very strong position.
Main ClauseWillis chose a new shipbuilding firm … and ensured that the contract … put him in a very strong position
Subordinate / RelativeTo carry out construction (purpose infinitive) + that the contract with them put him in a very strong position (noun clause)
Grammar NoteFronted infinitive of purpose. Note the appositive 'a new shipbuilding firm, Scott & Linton' — the comma pair marks it as extra identifying information.
Simple MeaningWillis picked a new shipbuilder and made sure the contract favoured him.
中文为进行建造，威利斯选择了一家新的造船公司斯科特与林顿，并确保与他们签订的合同使自己处于非常有利的地位。
Crossing the Indian Ocean, Cutty Sark gained a lead of over 400 miles, but then her rudder was severely damaged in stormy seas, making her impossible to steer.
Main ClauseCutty Sark gained a lead of over 400 miles, but … her rudder was severely damaged
Subordinate / RelativeCrossing the Indian Ocean (fronted participle) + making her impossible to steer (result participle)
Grammar NoteTwo participle clauses framing the sentence — one giving the setting, one giving the consequence. The passive 'was damaged' hides the agent (the storm).
Simple MeaningShe led by 400 miles across the Indian Ocean until a storm broke her rudder and left her unsteerable.
中文横渡印度洋时，卡蒂萨克号领先了400多英里，但随后其船舵在风暴海域严重受损，使其无法操舵。
While steam ships could make use of the quick, direct route between the Mediterranean and the Red Sea, the canal was of no use to sailing ships, which needed the much stronger winds of the oceans, and so had to sail a far greater distance.
Main Clausethe canal was of no use to sailing ships
Subordinate / RelativeWhile steam ships could make use of the quick, direct route (contrast clause) + which needed the much stronger winds… and so had to sail a far greater distance (relative)
Grammar NoteWhile = 'whereas'. be of no use to is a formal alternative to 'be useless for'. This sentence explains Q6 and Q7 together.
Simple MeaningSteam ships used the canal; sailing ships needed ocean winds and had to go the long way round.
中文蒸汽船能利用地中海与红海之间快捷的直达航线，而运河对帆船毫无用处——帆船需要海洋上强得多的风力，因此不得不航行远得多的距离。
In 1880, violence aboard the ship led ultimately to the replacement of the captain with an incompetent drunkard who stole the crew's wages.
Main Clauseviolence aboard the ship led ultimately to the replacement of the captain
Subordinate / Relativewho stole the crew's wages — a defining relative clause
Grammar Notelead to + noun phrase is the standard cause-and-effect pattern. Notice the heavy nominalisation: 'the replacement of the captain with…' rather than 'the captain was replaced by…'.
Simple MeaningAfter violence on board in 1880, a useless, drunken captain took over and stole the crew's pay.
中文1880年，船上的暴力事件最终导致船长被一个偷走船员工资的无能酒鬼所取代。
As a sailing ship, Cutty Sark depended on the strong trade winds of the southern hemisphere, and Woodget took her further south than any previous captain, bringing her dangerously close to icebergs off the southern tip of South America.
Main ClauseCutty Sark depended on the strong trade winds … and Woodget took her further south than any previous captain
Subordinate / RelativeAs a sailing ship (reason phrase) + bringing her dangerously close to icebergs (result participle)
Grammar NoteAs here means 'being' / 'in her capacity as'. The comparative further south than any previous captain is a superlative in disguise. The result participle supplies the answer to Q8.
Simple MeaningBecause she was a sailing ship she needed southern winds, so Woodget sailed dangerously far south, near icebergs.
中文作为一艘帆船，卡蒂萨克号依赖南半球强劲的信风，伍德盖特把她驶得比以往任何船长都更靠南，使其危险地接近南美洲南端附近的冰山。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Chronological narrative (时间叙事): The whole passage runs on dates. 1869 built and Suez opens → 1870 maiden voyage → 1872 the lost race → 1878 dropped by tea traders → 1880 violence and a new captain → 1890s decline and sale → 1922 gale → 1923 returned to Falmouth → 1954 Greenwich → 2007 and 2014 fires. Marking these on your first read makes the sentence-completion questions almost automatic.

Cause and effect (因果): Several tight chains. Steam ships gain speed and capacity + the Suez Canal opens → clippers lose the tea trade → Cutty Sark takes low-status cargo work. And: Woodget sails further south → stronger winds → fastest wool ship for ten years, but also → dangerous proximity to icebergs. Signal words: as a result of, had a serious impact, led ultimately to, and so, though.

Expectation versus outcome (预期与结果): The passage repeatedly sets up a hope and then defeats it. 'Cutty Sark was designed to make the journey more quickly than any other ship' → 'However, Cutty Sark never lived up to the high expectations of her owner'. She gains a 400-mile lead → arrives a week after Thermopylae. This is exactly the trap in Q5, which many students mark TRUE.

Contrast between technologies (技术对比): Sail versus steam is the passage's underlying opposition. Steam: quick direct canal route, growing cargo capacity, two months faster. Sail: needs 'the much stronger winds of the oceans', so must travel 'a far greater distance'. Note that the passage says the canal was of no use to sailing ships — it never says steam ships stopped using the ocean route, which is why Q7 is NOT GIVEN.

Reversal of fortune (命运转折): 'This marked a turnaround and the beginning of the most successful period in Cutty Sark's working life.' The word turnaround is the hinge of the passage — everything before it is decline, everything after it is (briefly) triumph. Q9's answer, wool, sits immediately after this hinge.

Author's stance (作者态度): Affectionate but factual, and quietly amused in places — 'In legend, and in Burns's poem, witches cannot cross water, so this was a rather strange choice of name for a ship.' The evaluative words are mostly reserved for people: excellent navigator, incompetent drunkard, determined. Facts about the ship itself are given plainly, which is why nearly all of Q1–8 can be settled from a single sentence.
