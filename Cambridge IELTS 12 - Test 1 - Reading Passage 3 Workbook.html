Cambridge IELTS 12 — Test 1 — Reading Passage 3

Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

'What's the purpose of gaining knowledge?' — a philosophy lecturer reflects on a course at his university called 'Arson for Profit'. He uses Kant's distinction between means and ends to argue that a field of knowledge is defined by its purpose as well as its techniques. Question types: matching headings (Q27–32), summary completion (Q33–36) and YES / NO / NOT GIVEN (Q37–40).

💡 Reading tip: For matching headings, read the first and last sentence of each section — an argumentative text states its point at the edges. For YES / NO / NOT GIVEN you are judging the writer's opinion, so hunt for evaluative language ('I kid you not', 'highly welcome', 'which seems right') rather than facts.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

'I would found an institution where any person can find instruction in any subject.' That was the founder's motto for Cornell University, and it seems an apt characterization of the different university, also in the USA, where I currently teach philosophy.
Main ClauseThat was the founder's motto for Cornell University / it seems an apt characterization of the different university
Subordinate / Relative'where any person can find instruction in any subject' = relative clause of place inside the quotation; 'where I currently teach philosophy' = relative clause of place; 'also in the USA' = parenthetical.
Grammar NoteTwo 'where' relative clauses of place, and a compound sentence joined by 'and'.
Simple MeaningCornell's founder promised any subject for anyone. The writer says that also describes the different American university where he teaches.
中文“我要创办一所任何人都能学到任何学科的学校。”这是康奈尔大学创办人的格言,而这似乎也恰当地描述了我目前教授哲学的那所——同样位于美国的——另一所大学。
Naturally, the course is intended for prospective arson investigators, who can learn all the tricks of the trade for detecting whether a fire was deliberately set, discovering who did it, and establishing a chain of evidence for effective prosecution in a court of law.
Main Clausethe course is intended for prospective arson investigators
Subordinate / Relative'who can learn all the tricks of the trade' = non-defining relative clause; inside it three parallel gerunds: 'detecting whether…', 'discovering who…', 'establishing a chain of evidence…'.
Grammar NoteNon-defining relative clause + three parallel '-ing' objects of the preposition 'for'.
Simple MeaningThe course is for future arson investigators, who learn to work out if a fire was deliberate, who started it, and how to build a case for court.
中文当然,这门课程是为未来的纵火案调查员开设的,他们可以学到全部专业技巧:判断火灾是否为人为纵火、查明是谁所为,以及建立证据链以便在法庭上有效起诉。
This example suggests how dishonest and illegal behavior, with the help of higher education, can creep into every aspect of public and business life.
Main ClauseThis example suggests …
Subordinate / Relative'how dishonest and illegal behavior … can creep into every aspect of public and business life' = noun clause (the object of 'suggests'); 'with the help of higher education' = inserted prepositional phrase.
Grammar NoteReporting verb + 'how' noun clause; the inserted phrase separates subject from verb, so read past it.
Simple MeaningThe example shows that university training can accidentally help dishonest behaviour spread everywhere.
中文这个例子说明,在高等教育的“帮助”下,不诚实与违法的行为如何能渗入公共生活和商业生活的方方面面。
After all, a subject matter can have principles in the sense of being codified, having rules, as with football or chess, without being principled in the sense of being ethical.
Main Clausea subject matter can have principles
Subordinate / Relative'in the sense of being codified, having rules' = defining phrase; 'as with football or chess' = comparison; 'without being principled in the sense of being ethical' = contrasting participle phrase.
Grammar NoteThe whole sentence turns on a pun: 'principles' (= rules) vs 'principled' (= ethical). 'in the sense of' signals which meaning is active.
Simple MeaningA subject can have rules, like football or chess, without being morally good.
中文毕竟,一门学科可以像足球或国际象棋那样“有原则”(即有成文规则),却并不“有原则”(即合乎道德)。
A field of knowledge or a professional endeavor is defined by both the means and the end; hence both deserve scrutiny.
Main ClauseA field of knowledge or a professional endeavor is defined by both the means and the end / both deserve scrutiny
Subordinate / RelativeTwo independent clauses joined by a semicolon and the linker 'hence'.
Grammar Note'both … and …' correlative pair; 'hence' = therefore (formal).
Simple MeaningA subject is defined by how it is done and by what it is for, so we must examine both.
中文一个知识领域或职业活动是由手段和目的共同界定的;因此两者都值得仔细审视。
Kant gives the example of a doctor and a poisoner, who use the identical knowledge to achieve their divergent ends.
Main ClauseKant gives the example of a doctor and a poisoner
Subordinate / Relative'who use the identical knowledge to achieve their divergent ends' = non-defining relative clause referring to both people.
Grammar NoteNon-defining relative clause after a comma + infinitive of purpose ('to achieve').
Simple MeaningKant's example: a doctor and a poisoner know the same things but want opposite results.
中文康德举了医生与投毒者的例子:他们运用完全相同的知识,却追求截然相反的目的。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Anecdote → argument. The writer opens with a surprising fact (a course called 'Arson for Profit') and only later turns it into a philosophical claim. Sections A–B are the hook; D–F carry the argument. This shape tells you the headings for A and B will be about the course title and the risk of the wrong students, not about Kant.

Concession before criticism. Section B contains a classic hedge: 'My point is not to criticize academic programs in fire science: they are highly welcome…' followed by 'However, it's not unknown for a firefighter to torch a building.' The 'However' is where the real claim lives — and where Q38 (YES) is answered.

Definition and application. Section D introduces Kant's means/end distinction abstractly; Section E applies it to marketing; Section F applies it back to arson. Recognising the abstract-then-applied pattern is what separates heading iv ('Applying a theory in an unexpected context') from heading iii.

Contrast pairs. The passage runs on paired opposites: investigator / arsonist, firefighting / arson, marketing / fraud, medicine / murder, doctor / poisoner. In each pair the means are identical and only the end differs. Spotting the pattern makes Section F predictable.

Author's attitude markers. 'I kid you not', 'downright crazy in light of the evidence', 'which seems right', 'we would not call that firefighting'. These are opinion signals — exactly what Q37–40 (YES / NO / NOT GIVEN) test. Where the writer states no opinion at all (e.g. about how hard it is to recruit students to non-career courses), the answer is NOT GIVEN.
