📋 Quick Review

This is a book review of Ulbe Bosma’s The World of Sugar, a global history of sugar. It discusses cane and beet sugar, changing trade in Britain, the labour used to grow sugar, the influence of sugar corporations on politics, the industrial processes involved, and the modern rise in consumption and its harm to health.

💡 Reading strategy tip: Passage 3 is the hardest, with abstract argument and YES/NO/NOT GIVEN about the writer’s views. Distinguish the reviewer’s opinion from what the book reports. For MCQ about a paragraph, read the whole paragraph before choosing.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"Following the abolition of slavery in the British Empire, cane sugar was imported to Britain from places which retained the practice, such as Cuba and Brazil."
Main Clausecane sugar was imported to Britain
Subordinate / Relative Clausewhich retained the practice, such as Cuba and Brazil (relative clause) + "Following the abolition …" (participial time phrase)
Grammar Note"Following …" = after. "which retained the practice" describes "places". Passive: "was imported".
Simple MeaningAfter Britain ended slavery, it still bought cane sugar from countries like Cuba and Brazil that had NOT ended it.
中文大英帝国废除奴隶制后，英国仍从古巴、巴西等仍保留该做法的地方进口蔗糖。
"Contrary to popular belief, cane sugar production was never just restricted to large, dedicated plantations owned by rich men."
Main Clausecane sugar production was never just restricted to large, dedicated plantations
Subordinate / Relative Clauseowned by rich men (reduced relative = which were owned by rich men)
Grammar Note"Contrary to popular belief" flags a misconception. "owned by …" is a reduced relative clause.
Simple MeaningUnlike what many think, cane sugar was not only grown on big plantations belonging to wealthy men.
中文与普遍看法相反，蔗糖生产从来不只限于富人拥有的大型专用种植园。
"In the face of appalling obstruction from the sugar industry to attempts to reduce consumption, some countries have been forced to tax sugar in order to bring that about."
Main Clausesome countries have been forced to tax sugar
Subordinate / Relative ClauseIn the face of appalling obstruction … (adverbial) + in order to bring that about (purpose)
Grammar Note"In the face of" = despite. "in order to bring that about" states the purpose (= to reduce consumption).
Simple MeaningEven though the sugar industry fought hard against cutting consumption, some countries had to tax sugar to achieve it.
中文面对糖业对减少消费的强烈阻挠，一些国家不得不对糖征税以实现这一目标。
"High-fructose corn syrup made from maize using an enzymatic process invented in Japan in the 1960s has a similar number of calories to table sugar but is far cheaper to produce."
Main ClauseHigh-fructose corn syrup … has a similar number of calories to table sugar but is far cheaper to produce
Subordinate / Relative Clausemade from maize using an enzymatic process invented in Japan in the 1960s (two reduced relatives)
Grammar NoteLong subject with stacked reduced relatives ("made …", "invented …"). "but" contrasts calories vs. cost.
Simple MeaningCorn syrup, made from maize by a Japanese method from the 1960s, has about the same calories as sugar but costs much less to make.
中文高果糖玉米糖浆用玉米经日本1960年代发明的酶法制成，热量与食糖相近，但生产成本低得多。
"Bosma avoids outbursts of emotion, celebratory or critical, even if they might have made his analysis of the multiple tragedies involving sugar all the more powerful."
Main ClauseBosma avoids outbursts of emotion
Subordinate / Relative Clauseeven if they might have made his analysis … all the more powerful (concession)
Grammar Note"even if" = although possibly. "all the more" = even more. "celebratory or critical" describes the emotions.
Simple MeaningBosma stays calm and unemotional, although showing feeling might have made his account of sugar’s tragedies even stronger.
中文博斯玛避免情绪化的表达（无论褒贬），尽管那样也许会让他对糖的种种悲剧的分析更有力。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Passage 3 is built from argument and evaluation — follow the reasoning.

Contrast & Correction

“Contrary to popular belief …” signals the writer correcting a common myth (cane sugar was NOT only from big plantations). Watch for these to answer “what is the writer doing?” questions.

Cause & Effect

Beet sugar is subsidised → sold at artificially low prices → threatens the livelihood of cane producers. And rising consumption → more harm to teeth and weight. Trace each chain.

Chronology

Britain’s sugar sources change over time: own colonies → Cuba/Brazil after abolition → beet from Europe (late 1800s) → national beet industry (1900s). Time order underlies the Q28 answer.

Writer’s Stance

The review is warm about the book (“wonderfully rich”, “a model of global history”) while its overall message is that sugar is harmful and unnecessary. Separate praise of the book from claims about sugar for YES/NO/NOT GIVEN.
