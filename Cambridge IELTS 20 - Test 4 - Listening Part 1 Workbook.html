Listening Part 1

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 4 — Listening Part 1

Advice on a Family Visit · Notes Completion (Q1–10)

📋 Quick Review

A man is expecting his cousin and her two children to visit next month, and because he has no children of his own he asks his colleague Sandra for advice. Sandra has just had her own brother’s family to stay, so she recommends a hotel, two trips, a museum day, a market, a cheap-ticket website and two free things to do. You must complete a set of notes under six headings with ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER for each answer.

Who is speaking? Two colleagues or friends: a man who is asking for help, and Sandra, a woman who has recent experience of hosting visiting family. Sandra gives almost all of the answers; the man mostly asks questions and reacts (“Sounds good”, “That’s a shame”).

The situation 情境: 男士的表姐一家（带两个孩子，12岁和9岁）下个月要来玩，他自己没有孩子，不知道该带他们去哪里，于是向同事 Sandra 请教。Sandra 依次给出建议：住哪家酒店、家庭房多少钱、市中心怎么逛、去古堡的交通方式、科学博物馆哪天去、哪个展览快开幕、去哪儿吃饭、几点前吃午饭、剧院票怎么打折、有哪些免费活动。答案顺序与笔记标题顺序完全一致。

Task type: Notes completion under six headings. Instruction: ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER for each answer. That means walking is fine but a walking is not, and 2.30 counts as a number, not as two words. The £ sign and the % sign are already printed, so do not write them.

💡 Listening strategy tip: The notes are printed under six bold headings — Accommodation, Recommended trips, Science Museum, Food, Theatre tickets, Free activities. Those headings are your map: the speakers move through them in exactly that order, so the moment you hear a new topic word you know which gap you are on. Use the 30 seconds of preparation time to decide the word type of each gap (name / number / adjective / noun) and to notice the words already printed next to the gap — Hotel, tour, p.m., %. Those printed words stop you writing a two-word answer. Above all, this recording works by rejection: the man suggests something, Sandra says “I think it’s better to…” and gives the real answer. 第一个被提到的选项几乎从来不是答案——要等对方修正之后再写。
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • Q2 — two prices, one gap. Sandra gives £125 for a family room and £95 for each of two double rooms. The note says “cost of family room per night”, so 125 is the answer and 95 is the distractor. Match the number to its label, not to its position.
  • Q3 — the man’s idea is wrong. He says “I was thinking of doing a bus tour”. Sandra replies “those bus tours are quite expensive… it’s better to do a walking tour”. The word printed on the paper (tour) appears with both options.
  • Q5 — three days are named. Monday (the museum is closed), Saturday (they arrive, and it is terrible / far too crowded) and Tuesday (“Tuesday would be best”). Only one of the three is a recommendation.
  • Q6 — two exhibitions. The old-computers exhibition is finished; the note says “which opens soon”, so the answer is the new one on space. Watch the tense: was / it’s finished vs starting soon.
  • Q8 — 2.30, not 2. “Most of the stalls stop serving lunch at two thirty”. Write 2.30; p.m. is already printed, so adding it changes nothing but writing “half past two” risks the word limit.
  • Q10 — view of what? Sandra never says the word “view” here. She says “you’ll be able to look right down on the port”. The word “views” comes one line later, from the man, after the answer has gone.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening the audio never uses the exact words printed on the question paper. Recognising paraphrase is the whole skill. 雅思听力中，录音几乎不会重复题干原词——识别同义替换才是关键。
Paraphrase Example 1
Question wording“(1) Hotel on George Street”
What you hear“I always recommend people stay at the King’s Hotel… It’s about five minutes’ walk from Murray Station, so nice and central. It’s actually on George Street.”
Key paraphraseThe street name comes after the hotel name, and it confirms you wrote the right one. Street names are the exam’s way of saying “yes, this one”.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question wording“cost of family room per night: £(2) (approx.)”
What you hear“If you book a family room, it’s about £125 per night. My brother paid for two double rooms in the end, and I think that was around £95 for each room.”
Key paraphraseapprox. = about / around / roughly. Both prices are hedged, so hedging alone does not identify the answer — the label (family room) does.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question wording“a (3) tour of the city centre”
What you hearMan: “I was thinking of doing a bus tour…” Sandra: “Those bus tours are quite expensive. I think it’s better to do a walking tour. It gives you a much better feel for the city.”
Key paraphraseSuggestion → objection → better idea. In Part 1 the answer is almost always the better idea, not the first suggestion.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question wording“best day to visit: (5)”
What you hear“Don’t forget it’s closed on Mondays.” – “They’re here from Saturday for four nights, so Tuesday would be best.” – “And it won’t be so crowded then.”
Key paraphrasebest = would be best + a supporting reason (won’t be so crowded). Closed / terrible / too crowded are all rejection signals.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 5
Question wording“the exhibition about (6), which opens soon”
What you hear“I took my kids to the exhibition on old computers… I wanted to go back, but it’s finished now.” – “There’s another one starting soon on space, which looks really good.”
Key paraphraseopens soon = starting soon. The tense does the work: it’s finished eliminates the first exhibition completely.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 6
Question wording“need to have lunch before (8) p.m.”
What you hear“You need to get there quite early, though. At the weekend, most of the stalls stop serving lunch at 2.30.”
Key paraphrasehave lunch before X = they stop serving lunch at X. Before/after and start/stop flips are everywhere in Listening.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 7
Question wording“a view of the (10)”
What you hear“If you’re in Blakewell Gardens, climb Telegraph Hill. You’ll be able to look right down on the port. Everyone’s always really impressed because it’s so huge.”
Key paraphrasea view of X = you can look (right) down on X. The word ‘views’ is said later, but by then the answer has already passed.

Review Quiz

Q1
B. His cousin and her family are visiting and he has no children himself.
‘I’ve got my cousin and her family visiting next month. And as I don’t have kids, I’d have no idea where to take them.’ C describes Sandra’s brother, not his.
Q2
B. There would not be enough room.
‘Thankfully, there wouldn’t be room.’ ‘Room’ here means space, not a bedroom — a nice double meaning just before the Accommodation answers.
Q3
C. £125, because the notes say ‘family room’.
Match each number to its label: £125 = family room; £95 = each of two double rooms, which is what her brother actually chose.
Q4
A. They are quite expensive.
‘Those bus tours are quite expensive.’ C and D describe the walking tour, which is the answer to Q3.
Q5
C. by boat
‘You could visit the old fort, but you could get there by boat. The whole trip takes half a day.’ Note the condition: only if the weather is nice.
Q6
A. Monday
‘Don’t forget it’s closed on Mondays.’ The family arrive on Saturday for four nights, so Tuesday is chosen — and it is quieter then.
Q7
B. It has already finished.
The notes say ‘which opens soon’. Sandra says ‘I wanted to go back, but it’s finished now’; the new one ‘starting soon’ is on space.
Q8
C. ‘My cousin’s vegetarian… it’s one of the best places for that kind of food.’
