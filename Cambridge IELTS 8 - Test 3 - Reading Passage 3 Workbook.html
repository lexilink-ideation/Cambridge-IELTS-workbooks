📋 Passage Overview

This passage, ‘How Does the Biological Clock Tick?’, asks why living things age and die. It contrasts the wearing-out of lifeless objects with living organisms, which are open systems that renew themselves, argues that a limited life span is useful for evolution, and links life span to metabolic rate: the more energy an organism uses, the shorter it lives. It concludes that a sparing use of energy — through rest, calm and moderate food — tends to lengthen life.

💡 Reading strategy tip: For heading matching, pin down each paragraph’s single main idea (contrast, cause, benefit, stability). For Yes/No/Not Given, remember these test the writer’s views — a claim can sound plausible yet be Not Given if the writer never states it (e.g. the ‘90% in seven years’ figure).

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"At least as long as a biological system has the ability to renew itself it could actually become older without ageing."
Main Clauseit could actually become older without ageing
Subordinate / RelativeAt least as long as a biological system has the ability to renew itself (conditional/time clause)
Grammar Note‘As long as …’ = provided that …; contrasts ‘older’ (in time) with ‘ageing’ (wearing out).
Simple MeaningIf a living thing can keep renewing itself, it can grow older in time without wearing out.
中文至少只要一个生物系统具有自我更新的能力，它就可以在不衰老的情况下变得更“老”。
"Because of changes in the genetic material these have new characteristics and in the course of their individual lives they are tested for optimal adaptation to the environmental conditions."
Main Clausethese have new characteristics … they are tested for optimal adaptation
Subordinate / RelativeBecause of changes in the genetic material (adverbial of cause) / in the course of their individual lives (time phrase)
Grammar Note‘Because of …’ gives the cause; passive ‘are tested’ = nature checks their fitness.
Simple MeaningMutations give new organisms new traits, and life tests how well these suit the environment.
中文由于遗传物质的变化，这些新个体具有新的特征，并在其个体生命过程中接受对环境条件最优适应性的检验。
"Although more and more people attain an advanced age as a result of developments in medical care and better nutrition, the characteristic upper limit for most remains 80 years."
Main Clausethe characteristic upper limit for most remains 80 years
Subordinate / RelativeAlthough more and more people attain an advanced age … (concessive clause)
Grammar Note‘Although …’ concedes a point; the main clause states the limit still holds.
Simple MeaningEven though better medicine and food let more people live long, the usual maximum is still about 80.
中文尽管由于医疗和营养的改善，越来越多的人达到高龄，但大多数人特有的寿命上限仍是80岁左右。
"If you examine the metabolic rates of the two sexes you establish that the higher male metabolic rate roughly accounts for the lower male life span."
Main Clauseyou establish that the higher male metabolic rate roughly accounts for the lower male life span
Subordinate / RelativeIf you examine the metabolic rates of the two sexes (conditional clause) / that … (noun clause)
Grammar Note‘If …, you establish that …’ presents a finding; ‘accounts for’ = explains.
Simple MeaningMen’s higher metabolic rate roughly explains why men live shorter lives than women.
中文如果你考察两性的代谢率，就会发现男性较高的代谢率大致解释了男性较短的寿命。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Contrast (objects vs organisms) — Dead products are ‘static, closed systems’ that simply wear down; a living organism is an ‘open, dynamic system’ that renews itself. This contrast underlies Para B (heading ix).

Cause & Effect — A higher metabolic rate → a shorter life; saving energy (hibernation, low food, calm living) → a longer life. Signal: the larger … the lower …, thus, it follows that.

Why dying is beneficial — Immortality would leave no ‘room for new and better life’, disturbing evolution — so a limited life span is useful (Para C, heading ii).

Stability despite change — Although medicine and nutrition improve, the human upper limit stays about 80 years — a ‘stable life span despite improvements’ (Para D, heading vii).
