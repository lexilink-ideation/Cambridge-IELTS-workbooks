📋 Passage Overview

Conquering Earth's space junk problem. Satellites, rocket parts and collision debris are crowding low Earth orbit and creating real crash risks. The passage describes the scale of the problem, international guidelines, and researchers' efforts to track and catalogue debris — a field called space traffic management. Question types: section matching, summary completion and matching statements to people.

💡 Reading tip: For 'match the statement to the person', find each quoted name in the passage and read what they actually claim. Two statements (Q37 and Q39) both belong to Moriba Jah — a letter can be used more than once.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Just a few uncontrolled space crashes could generate enough debris to set off a runaway cascade of fragments, rendering near-Earth space unusable.
Main ClauseA few uncontrolled crashes could generate enough debris to set off a cascade.
Subordinate / Relative'to set off a runaway cascade of fragments' (infinitive of result) + 'rendering near-Earth space unusable' (participial result).
Grammar NoteTwo results are stacked: an infinitive and an '-ing' clause.
Simple MeaningA small number of crashes could trigger a chain reaction that makes near-Earth space unusable.
中文仅仅几次失控的太空碰撞就可能产生足够的碎片，引发碎片的失控连锁反应，使近地空间变得无法使用。
Those include inactivating satellites at the end of their useful life by venting pressurised materials or leftover fuel that might lead to explosions.
Main ClauseThose include inactivating satellites at the end of their useful life.
Subordinate / Relative'by venting pressurised materials or leftover fuel' (means) + 'that might lead to explosions' (relative).
Grammar Note'by + -ing' shows the method; the relative modifies 'materials or fuel'.
Simple MeaningOne guideline is to switch off old satellites and release fuel that could explode.
中文这些做法包括在卫星使用寿命结束时，通过排放可能导致爆炸的加压物质或剩余燃料，使其失效。
In theory, given the vastness of space, satellite operators should have plenty of room for all these missions to fly safely without ever nearing another object.
Main ClauseSatellite operators should have plenty of room to fly safely.
Subordinate / Relative'given the vastness of space' (concessive/causal phrase) + 'without ever nearing another object' (adverbial).
Grammar Note'given…' means 'considering'; 'without + -ing' expresses a negative condition.
Simple MeaningSpace is so big that, in theory, satellites should never come near each other.
中文理论上，鉴于太空的浩瀚，卫星运营方应有充足空间让所有任务安全飞行，而永远不会靠近其他物体。
When he types in an identifier for a particular space object, the database draws a purple line to designate its orbit.
Main ClauseThe database draws a purple line to designate its orbit.
Subordinate / Relative'When he types in an identifier for a particular space object' (time clause).
Grammar Note'When…' sets the condition; 'to designate' is an infinitive of purpose.
Simple MeaningType in an object's ID and the database shows its orbit as a purple line.
中文当他输入某个太空物体的标识符时，数据库会画出一条紫色线来标示其轨道。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & effect: more satellites launched (cause) → more traffic and crash risk (effect). The Iridium–Cosmos collision (section A) is the concrete example of that risk (Q29).

Problem & solution: the passage moves from the problem (crowding, no authoritative catalogue) to solutions (better tracking, shared standards, a single database).

Comparison: section E compares space traffic to a busy airport, where controllers know each plane 'down to one metre' — unlike space debris (Q30).

Attribution: each claim is tied to a named expert (Frueh, Krag, Sorge, Jah). Tracking who says what is exactly what Q36–40 test.
