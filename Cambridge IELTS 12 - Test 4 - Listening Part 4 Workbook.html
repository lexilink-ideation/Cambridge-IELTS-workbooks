📋 Quick Review

A lecture on noise in cities by an acoustic engineer. It covers the limits of decibel-based noise 'maps', the problems noise causes, the fact that some sounds are pleasant, what people actually want from an urban soundscape, the training gap among architects and planners, and the case for treating sound as an art form. Note completion, ONE WORD ONLY.

💡 Listening tip: This lecture argues by criticising current practice. Almost every gap sits just after a negative: 'these aren't a lot of use', 'they can't capture', 'it doesn't differentiate', 'these people receive very little training', 'but these programs are silent'. Follow the criticism and you will find the answer.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

Lecture notes compress a whole clause into a single word. Practise the compression.
Q32 — what the maps achieved
Question note
have made people realize that the noise is a ___ issue that must be dealt with
What you hear
'they've been useful in providing information and raising awareness that noise matters, we need to deal with it and so it's a political matter'
Key paraphrase
a ___ issue = a ___ matter; 'we need rules and regulations' confirms it → political
Your turn
Q33 — the effect on schoolchildren
Question note
effect on the ___ of schoolchildren
What you hear
'if schoolchildren don't have a quiet place to study, their work will suffer'
Key paraphrase
effect on the ___ = their ___ will suffer → work (or study)
Your turn
Q36 — what people want
Question note
Plenty of activity in urban environments which are ___, but also allow people to relax
What you hear
'a city needs to have a sense of activity, so it needs to be lively… but these mustn't be too intrusive, because at the same time we need to be able to relax'
Key paraphrase
plenty of activity = a sense of activity → lively
Your turn
Q40 — the drawback of VR
Question note

What you hear

Key paraphrase
'but' introduces the disadvantage → silent
