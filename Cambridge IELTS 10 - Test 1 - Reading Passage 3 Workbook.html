Reading Passage 3

Cambridge IELTS 10 — Test 1 — Reading Passage 3: The Psychology of Innovation

Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

Why are so few companies truly innovative? Psychologist Robert Cialdini argues that innovation starts with recruitment — the fit between an employee's values and the company's values. He then discusses teamwork, social proof, the power of writing things down, and the damage done by overbearing leaders ('captainitis').

这是一篇“心理学 + 管理”类文章。每段一个观点,并用一个具体例子支撑(猫王照片、DNA、麦片盒比赛、飞行员、孟菲斯设计团体)。例子是用来证明观点的,题目考的是观点。

💡 Reading tip: Q31–35 is sentence endings matching. Do it as a grammar exercise first: every stem ends in a verb-friendly position, so each ending must be a bare infinitive phrase ('take chances', 'share their ideas'…). Then match by meaning. Q36–40 is YES / NO / NOT GIVEN — you are testing the writer's claims, not facts about the world.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

There are, nevertheless, people working in luxurious, state-of-the-art centres designed to stimulate innovation who find that their environment doesn't make them feel at all creative.
Main ClauseThere are … people … who find that their environment doesn't make them feel at all creative
Subordinate / Relative'working in luxurious, state-of-the-art centres' = reduced relative ('who work in'); 'designed to stimulate innovation' = second reduced relative describing the centres; 'who find that…' = the real relative clause of 'people'
Grammar NoteTwo reduced relatives sit between 'people' and its relative pronoun 'who' — you must jump over them. This sentence is the evidence for Q36 = NO.
Simple MeaningSome people work in beautiful modern innovation centres and still don't feel creative.
中文然而,有些人在为激发创新而设计的豪华、最先进的中心里工作,却发现自己所处的环境丝毫没有让他们感到有创造力。
Research shows that the fit between an employee's values and a company's values makes a difference to what contribution they make and whether, two years after they join, they're still at the company.
Main ClauseResearch shows that the fit … makes a difference
Subordinate / Relative'to what contribution they make' + 'and whether … they're still at the company' = two parallel noun clauses after 'a difference to'; 'two years after they join' = interrupting time clause
Grammar Note'Makes a difference to A and B' where A and B are both noun clauses. The interrupter between commas can be skipped on a first read.
Simple MeaningWhen an employee's values match the company's, they contribute more and are more likely to still be there two years later.
中文研究表明,员工价值观与公司价值观之间的契合度,会影响他们做出的贡献,以及入职两年后他们是否还留在公司。
Studies at Harvard Business School show that, although some individuals may be more creative than others, almost every individual can be creative in the right circumstances.
Main ClauseStudies … show that almost every individual can be creative in the right circumstances
Subordinate / Relative'although some individuals may be more creative than others' = concession clause embedded inside the that-clause
Grammar NoteA concession clause nested inside a reported-speech that-clause. The point is always in the main clause of the reported idea — hence Q37 = YES.
Simple MeaningHarvard research says nearly anyone can be creative if the conditions are right, even though some people are naturally more creative.
中文哈佛商学院的研究表明,尽管有些人可能比其他人更有创造力,但几乎每个人在合适的条件下都能富有创造力。
Managers should therefore adopt an approach that appears counter-intuitive — they should explain what stands to be lost if the company fails to seize a particular opportunity.
Main ClauseManagers should adopt an approach
Subordinate / Relative'that appears counter-intuitive' = defining relative clause; after the dash, 'they should explain what stands to be lost' = restatement; 'if the company fails to seize a particular opportunity' = conditional clause
Grammar NoteThe dash introduces an explanation of the vague noun 'approach'. Whenever an abstract noun is followed by a dash, the definition follows.
Simple MeaningManagers should do the opposite of what seems natural: tell people what the company will lose if it misses the chance.
中文因此,管理者应当采取一种看似反直觉的做法——他们应当说明,如果公司未能抓住某个机会,将会失去什么。
He said he and Crick had succeeded because they were aware that they weren't the most intelligent of the scientists pursuing the answer.
Main ClauseHe said he and Crick had succeeded
Subordinate / Relative'because they were aware that they weren't the most intelligent of the scientists' = reason clause containing a that-clause; 'pursuing the answer' = reduced relative describing 'the scientists'
Grammar NoteReported speech + past perfect + embedded that-clause. 'Were aware that they weren't the most intelligent' = 'were conscious of their own limitations' — the exact paraphrase tested in Q28.
Simple MeaningWatson said they won because they knew they were not the cleverest people working on the problem.
中文他说,他和克里克之所以成功,是因为他们清楚自己并非追寻答案的科学家中最聪明的。
He calls it captainitis because, he says, 'crew members of multipilot aircraft exhibit a sometimes deadly passivity when the flight captain makes a clearly wrong-headed decision'.
Main ClauseHe calls it captainitis
Subordinate / Relative'because … crew members … exhibit a sometimes deadly passivity' = reason clause; 'when the flight captain makes a clearly wrong-headed decision' = time clause inside the quotation; 'he says' = interrupting reporting clause
Grammar NoteReason clause interrupted by 'he says', then a quotation containing its own time clause. Note the coined word 'captainitis' (-itis = a disease).
Simple MeaningHe calls it 'captainitis' because aircraft crews sometimes stay dangerously silent when the captain makes an obviously bad decision.
中文他称之为“机长症”,因为他说:“多驾驶员飞机的机组人员,在机长做出明显错误的决定时,有时会表现出致命的消极被动。”

🧠 Reading Logic Training

1. Claim + example (观点 + 例证). This is the engine of the whole passage. Each paragraph makes an abstract claim and then proves it with a concrete story: value fit → the million-dollar quartet; teamwork beats the lone genius → Watson, Crick and Rosalind Franklin; writing deepens commitment → Kellogg's Corn Flakes competitions; bad authority → airline 'captainitis'; no rules → the Memphis design collective. Questions test the claim, not the story. Q27 asks what the quartet underlines, not who was in the photograph.

2. Cause & effect (因果). Value fit causes greater contribution and longer retention; change causes risk-avoidance; fear of loss causes risk-taking; overbearing leadership causes passivity; a rule-free environment causes free interchange of ideas. Q31–35 is simply this causal chain turned into a matching exercise.

3. Contrast (对比). Two strong contrasts. (a) Loss vs reward: 'we invariably take more gambles when threatened with a loss than when offered a reward' — counter-intuitive, and directly tested. (b) Horizontal vs vertical power: 'peer power, used horizontally not vertically, is much more powerful than any boss's speech' — this single clause decides Q40 (NO). Also note the structural contrast signalled by 'At the other end of the scale' when the writer moves from captainitis to the Memphis collective.

4. Definition (定义). The writer coins or borrows terms and defines them immediately: 'follow-the-leader syndrome… because it encourages bosses to go it alone'; 'the principle of social proof… if your project is being resisted… ask another old-timer'; 'captainitis… because crew members exhibit a sometimes deadly passivity'. When you meet an invented word, the definition is in the same sentence.

5. Author's attitude and hedging (作者态度与模糊限定). Most claims are attributed to Cialdini or to research, not asserted by the writer directly: Cialdini believes, says Cialdini, research shows, studies show, many theorists believe. This matters for YES/NO/NOT GIVEN: the writer's own voice appears mainly in the last paragraph — 'The frustrating thing about innovation is that there are many approaches, but no magic formula.'

6. What is NOT there (无信息). Two of the last five questions are NOT GIVEN, and both concern topics the passage never raises: whether equally matched intelligence helps a team (Q38 — the passage says three beat one, which is about numbers, not equality) and whether company size affects innovation (Q39 — size is never mentioned). Train yourself to notice the absent topic rather than hunting for a matching sentence.
