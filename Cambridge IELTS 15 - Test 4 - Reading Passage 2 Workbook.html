📋 Passage Overview

Silbo Gomero — the whistle 'language' of the Canary Islands. On the mountainous island of La Gomera, people communicate across valleys using a whistled form of Spanish. The passage explains how words are recoded into whistles, how a finger and cupped hand shape the sound, brain-activity research on silbadores, the language's uncertain origins, and how modern technology now threatens it — prompting efforts to preserve it, including a UNESCO award. Question types: TRUE/FALSE/NOT GIVEN (Q14–19) and summary completion (Q20–26).

💡 Reading tip: For T/F/NG, a strong word like 'only', 'most' or 'always' in the statement is a warning — check whether the passage really makes that strong claim. For the summary, follow the passage order and predict the part of speech.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Silbo is a substitute for Spanish, with individual words recoded into whistles which have high- and low-frequency tones.
Main ClauseSilbo is a substitute for Spanish
Subordinate / Relative'with individual words recoded into whistles' = 'with + noun + past participle' phrase; 'which have high- and low-frequency tones' = relative clause defining 'whistles'
Grammar NoteLinking verb 'is' + a 'with…' absolute phrase containing a relative clause.
Simple MeaningSilbo replaces Spanish by turning each word into a high- or low-pitched whistle.
中文“西尔博”是西班牙语的替代形式,把单个词语重新编码为具有高低频音调的口哨声。
A whistler — or silbador — puts a finger in his or her mouth to increase the whistle's pitch, while the other hand can be cupped to adjust the direction of the sound.
Main ClauseA whistler … puts a finger in his or her mouth to increase the whistle's pitch
Subordinate / Relative'— or silbador —' = inserted synonym; 'to increase the whistle's pitch' = purpose; 'while the other hand can be cupped to adjust the direction of the sound' = simultaneous clause
Grammar NoteMain clause + infinitive of purpose + a 'while' clause showing a second action.
Simple MeaningA whistler puts a finger in the mouth to raise the pitch, and cups the other hand to steer the sound.
中文吹哨者(silbador)把一根手指放进嘴里以提高哨声的音高,同时用另一只手做成杯状来调整声音的方向。
Because whistled 'words' can be hard to distinguish, silbadores rely on repetition, as well as on the context of the conversation.
Main Clausesilbadores rely on repetition, as well as on the context of the conversation
Subordinate / Relative'Because whistled words can be hard to distinguish' = reason clause; 'as well as on the context…' = additional element
Grammar NoteFront reason clause ('Because…') + main clause with 'rely on X as well as on Y'.
Simple MeaningBecause whistled words are hard to tell apart, silbadores use repetition and the context.
中文由于哨语“词”很难分辨,吹哨者依靠重复,以及对话的语境。
But with modern communication technology now widely available, researchers say whistled languages like Silbo are threatened with extinction.
Main Clauseresearchers say whistled languages like Silbo are threatened with extinction
Subordinate / Relative'with modern communication technology now widely available' = 'with…' absolute phrase giving the cause; 'whistled languages … are threatened with extinction' = reported clause
Grammar Note'With…' cause phrase + reporting verb 'say' + a that-clause (that omitted).
Simple MeaningNow that modern technology is everywhere, researchers say whistled languages like Silbo may die out.
中文但随着现代通信技术如今普及,研究者称像西尔博这样的哨语正面临消亡的威胁。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

How-it-works description. The middle of the passage explains the mechanics of whistling (words → whistles, finger for pitch, hand for direction). The summary (Q20–26) follows this order closely.

Claim strength for T/F/NG. Statements with 'most', 'only' or 'always' are tested against what the passage actually says — e.g. 'only for short messages' is contradicted by 'any Spanish sentence could be whistled'.

Threat and response. Modern technology → whistled languages threatened → teaching in schools and seeking a UNESCO award. This cause-and-response chain covers Q25–26.
