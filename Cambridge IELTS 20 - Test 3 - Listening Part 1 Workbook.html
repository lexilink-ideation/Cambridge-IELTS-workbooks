Listening Part 1

Cambridge IELTS 20 — Test 3 — Listening Part 1

Furniture Rental Companies · Table Completion (Q1–10)

📋 Quick Review

Shelley Meyer has rented a house on Archwood Avenue and is moving in next week. She phones the house owner, who has promised to tell her about furniture rental companies in the city. He describes four companies in turn — Peak Rentals, Aaron and Oliver, Larch Furniture and Space Rentals — giving prices, extra charges and one or two conditions for each. You complete a table with ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER per gap.

Who is speaking? Two speakers: Shelley Meyer, a woman about to move into a rented house, and the house owner (male), who gives all the information. She mostly asks short questions and reacts, so almost every answer is in his turns.

The situation 情境: Shelley 下周就要搬进 Archwood Avenue 的租住房，因为工作合同只是临时的，她打算先租家具而不是买。房东按顺序介绍了四家家具租赁公司：Peak Rentals（价格 105–239 美元／房间／月，家具最新，1–2 天送达，租客厅整套家具送台灯）、Aaron and Oliver（中等价位，每月多收 12% 的损坏费，另提供房屋清洁服务）、Larch Furniture（家具和电子设备价格最低，但必须自己买保险，最短合同六个月）、Space Rentals（离房子很近，价格要看 App，送达后七天内可要求更换）。答案按公司顺序出现。

Task type: Table completion. Instruction: ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER for each answer. Two words = 0 marks, so ‘electronic equipment’ or ‘a lamp’ would both be wrong — write electronic and lamp.

💡 Listening strategy tip: The table has one row per company and two information columns (Information about costs / Additional notes). Because the speakers work through the companies one at a time, the ten answers arrive strictly in order — but the two columns of a single row can be filled in either order, so keep your eyes on the whole row. Before the audio starts, look at what is already printed: ‘$’, ‘12% monthly fee’, ‘1–2 days’, ‘Six months’, ‘within 7 days’. Those printed numbers are not answers; they are anchors that tell you which sentence you have reached. Then predict the word type: Q1 is a price, Q2 and Q6 are adjectives, Q3, Q5, Q7, Q9 and Q10 are nouns, Q4 and Q8 are company names that will be spelled out or clearly stressed.
⚠️ Traps in this section 本节陷阱:
  • $105 vs $239 (Q1). Both numbers are said in one breath: ‘starts at $105 and goes up to $239’. The table already prints ‘from $105 to $___’, so you need the upper figure. Write 239, not 105.
  • ‘more modern than…’ vs ‘is very ___’ (Q2). The table wants a plain adjective but the audio uses a comparative: ‘the furniture from Peak Rentals is more modern than any of the other companies’. Strip the comparative and write modern.
  • Cleaning: furniture or house? (Q5 area). ‘They also do cleaning for customers.’ — ‘For the furniture?’ — ‘For the house.’ The cleaning service is already printed on the paper; the gap in that row is the reason for the 12% charge, which is damage. Do not let the correction distract you.
  • Six months vs twelve months (Q7 area). Two contract lengths are mentioned seconds apart: the minimum rental contract (six months, already printed) and the house lease (twelve months). Neither is an answer — the answer in that row is insurance.
  • ‘a week’ = ‘7 days’ (Q10). The table prints ‘within 7 days of delivery’ but the speaker says ‘within a week of receiving it’. Recognise the equivalence, and note the grammar: ‘___ are allowed’ forces the plural exchanges.
  • Names must be captured letter by letter (Q4, Q8). Shelley mishears the first company (‘Sorry. What? And Oliver?’) and the owner spells it out. Aaron has a double A; Space Rentals is said only once, so write it immediately.

chargev. / n.to ask someone to pay an amount of money收费；索价“they charge an extra 12% every month”The 12% is already printed, so do not write it. What you need is what the charge is for (Q5).
damagen.harm that makes something less useful or attractive损坏；损害“they charge an extra 12% every month in case of damage”Answer to Q5. Uncountable here — never write ‘damages’, which means financial compensation.
electronicadj.working by means of electric circuits and microchips电子的“That’s for both furniture and also electronic equipment.”Answer to Q6. The paper says ‘___ items’, so you need the adjective only — not ‘electronic equipment’ (two words = 0 marks).
equipmentn. (uncountable)the machines and tools needed for a particular purpose设备；器材“both furniture and also electronic equipment”On the question paper this is paraphrased as ‘items’. Both are ways of avoiding a repeated word.
take out insurancephr.to buy an insurance policy投保；买保险“you have to take out insurance on the furniture”Answer to Q7. The paper says ‘Must have own ___’, which matches ‘you need to organize that yourself’.
organizev.to arrange something yourself安排；自行办理“you need to organize that yourself”Here it means ‘arrange’, not ‘tidy’. ‘Yourself’ is the clue that becomes ‘own’ on the paper.
contractn.a formal written agreement合同；合约“you can’t take out a contract for less than 6 months”Two contracts appear in this recording — her work contract and the rental contract. Keep them apart.
minimumn. / adj.the smallest amount or level allowed最低限度；最少“you can’t take out a contract for less than 6 months” = a minimum of six monthsThe paper prints ‘Minimum contract length: Six months’. The audio never says ‘minimum’ — it says ‘can’t… for less than’.
locatedadj.situated in a particular place位于“It’s called Space Rentals, and it’s located very near to the house.”Q8 comes immediately after this sentence, so listen through the location detail to catch the name.
appn.a computer program you use on a phone or tablet应用程序“it’s best to use their app to find out what it would cost you”Answer to Q9. Only three letters, easy to miss. It answers ‘See the ___ for the most up-to-date prices’.
up-to-dateadj.containing the newest information最新的The table asks you to check the app for the ‘most up-to-date prices’.Paraphrase in the audio: ‘I don’t have any information about their charges, so it’s best to…’ — the owner’s information is out of date.
exchangen. / v.the act of giving something back and receiving a different item更换；调换“you can request exchanges as long as you do that within a week”Answer to Q10. The paper says ‘___ are allowed’, so the PLURAL is required.
requestv.to ask for something formally or politely请求；申请“you can request exchanges”On the paper this becomes ‘are allowed’. Active in the audio, passive on the question paper — an extremely common Listening shift.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening the audio never uses the exact words printed on the question paper. Recognising paraphrase is the whole skill. 雅思听力中，录音几乎不会重复题干原词——识别同义替换才是关键。
Paraphrase Example 1
Question wording“Prices range from $105 to $(1) per room per month”
What you hear“It says the monthly price per room starts at $105 and goes up to $239.”
Key paraphraserange from X to Y = start at X and go up to Y. The gap is on the RIGHT of ‘to’, so you need the higher number.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question wording“The furniture is very (2)”
What you hear“People have mentioned that the furniture from Peak Rentals is more modern than any of the other companies.”
Key paraphraseA comparative in the audio (more modern than any other) becomes a plain adjective on the paper (very modern).
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question wording“Special offer: Free (3) with every living room set”
What you hear“If you rent living room furniture, I believe that’s a set of chairs and a TV table. You’ll also get a lamp at no extra cost.”
Key paraphrasefree = at no extra cost; a living room set = a set of chairs and a TV table.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question wording“12% monthly fee for (5)”
What you hear“they charge an extra 12% every month in case of damage”
Key paraphrasea fee for X = they charge… in case of X. The number is printed, so the answer is the reason.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 5
Question wording“Offers cheapest prices for renting furniture and (6) items”
What you hear“it has the lowest prices in town. That’s for both furniture and also electronic equipment.”
Key paraphrasecheapest = the lowest prices; items = equipment. Only the adjective goes in the gap.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 6
Question wording“Must have own (7)”
What you hear“you have to take out insurance on the furniture, and you need to organize that yourself”
Key paraphrasemust have your own X = you need to organize X yourself. ‘Take out’ is the standard verb with insurance.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 7
Question wording“(10) are allowed within 7 days of delivery”
What you hear“if you don’t like the furniture once it’s delivered, you can request exchanges as long as you do that within a week of receiving it.”
Key paraphraseyou can request = are allowed; a week = 7 days; delivery = receiving it. Note the plural forced by ‘are’.
Your practice

Review Quiz

Q1
B. Her work contract may only be temporary.
‘At least until I know whether my temporary work contract is going to be made permanent.’ D confuses her 12-month house lease with Larch Furniture’s six-month minimum.
Q2
B. 239, because the table already prints ‘from $105 to $___’.
Always read what is already printed. The gap sits after ‘to’, so it needs the upper figure of the range.
Q3
C. ‘more modern than any of the other companies’
The table wants a plain adjective, so you must remove the comparative structure: more modern than… → very modern.
Q4
C. a lamp
The chairs and the TV table are what a living room set is; the free extra is the lamp, given ‘at no extra cost’.
Q5
B. He spells it out letter by letter.
Shelley mishears (‘Sorry. What? And Oliver?’), so he spells the name. Note the double A: A-A-R-O-N.
Q6
B. possible damage
‘They charge an extra 12% every month in case of damage.’ Insurance belongs to Larch Furniture, and the cleaning is a separate free-standing service.
Q7
B. the house
Shelley guesses ‘For the furniture?’ and is corrected: ‘For the house.’ This correction pattern — wrong guess, then correction — is a favourite IELTS distractor.
Q8
B. Because the instruction allows only ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER.
The word ‘items’ is already printed and does the job of ‘equipment’. Writing two words scores zero even though the meaning is right.
Q9
B. your own insurance and a minimum six-month contract
‘You have to take out insurance… you need to organize that yourself’ and ‘you can’t take out a contract for less than 6 months’. The 12% belongs to Aaron and Oliver.
Q10
B. Because he does not know their charges.
‘I don’t have any information about their charges, so it’s best to use their app to find out what it would cost you.’ That is why the table says ‘most up-to-date prices’.
Q11
B. exchanges
The verb ‘are’ requires a plural subject. Grammar on the question paper is free information — use it before the audio starts.
Q12
B. Use the printed numbers (12%, 1–2 days, six months, 7 days) as signposts and predict the word type of each gap.
The printed details are not answers — they are landmarks that tell you which company you are on. Combined with word-type prediction, they stop you writing a grammatically impossible word.
