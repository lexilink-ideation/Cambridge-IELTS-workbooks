📋 Quick Review

A university lecturer gives a talk on the social history of the East End of London. She traces its development from Roman times (1st–4th centuries) through Saxon settlement, Norman rule, dock construction, and marshland drainage, up to overcrowded and unsanitary 19th- and 20th-century housing conditions.

This is a classic IELTS Listening Part 4 academic lecture — one speaker delivering continuous, information-dense content in chronological order, tested with table completion and multiple-selection questions.

💡 Listening strategy tip: Part 4 moves through time periods in strict chronological order. Use the century/date headings in the question table as checkpoints — once you hear the next date mentioned, you know the previous answer has already passed.

🔄 Paraphrase Training

In IELTS Listening, the audio never uses the exact words in the question. Recognising paraphrases is the key skill. Study each example below, then try your own.
Paraphrase Example 1 — Feeding the Population
Question wordingProduce from the area was used to ___ the people of London
Audio meaning"...consisted of farmland with crops and livestock which helped to feed that population."
Key paraphrase"helped to feed" = used to feed

Paraphrase Example 2 — Fewer Restrictions

Audio meaning"because there were fewer restrictions there than in the city itself, plenty of newcomers settled there"
Key paraphrase"fewer restrictions" = lack of restrictions
