Doctoring Sales · Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

This passage looks at the pharmaceutical industry's sales and marketing practices in North America. It describes how sales representatives use gifts, free meals, drug samples, and cash payments to build relationships with doctors, and it asks whether this kind of marketing goes too far. The passage weighs the benefits — useful drug information reaching busy doctors — against the ethical concerns of bribery and the higher prescription prices that patients ultimately pay.

💡 Reading strategy tip: This passage's paragraphs (A–G) each carry a distinct idea — an anecdote, a list of gifts, a debate, an argument for reps, an argument against, evidence from research, and a conclusion. Label each paragraph with a 3–5 word "tag" before answering the heading-matching questions.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"I tend to think I'm not influenced by what they give me."
Main ClauseI tend to think...
Subordinate Clause"(that) I'm not influenced by what they give me" (noun clause, object of "think"); "what they give me" is a nested relative clause functioning as the object of "by"
Grammar Note"tend to think" is a hedging phrase that softens a personal claim — common in reported opinion; the nested clause avoids repeating "the gifts"
Simple MeaningThis doctor believes company gifts do not affect his prescribing decisions.
中文我倾向于认为，他们送给我的东西并不会影响我。
"Selling pharmaceuticals is a daily exercise in ethical judgement, as reps must decide where a free lunch ends and an improper inducement begins."
Main ClauseSelling pharmaceuticals is a daily exercise in ethical judgement
Subordinate Clause"as reps must decide where a free lunch ends and an improper inducement begins" (reason clause containing an embedded question)
Grammar Note"as" introduces a reason clause; "where...begins" functions as the direct object of "decide" — an embedded wh-question
Simple MeaningReps face ethical choices every day, judging when hospitality crosses into bribery.
中文药品销售是一项每天都需要做出道德判断的工作，因为销售代表必须判断免费午餐在哪里结束，不当诱导又从哪里开始。
"Although drug samples are often praised as a way to help poorer patients, research suggests that they also nudge doctors toward prescribing medicines they would not normally choose."
Main Clauseresearch suggests that they also nudge doctors toward prescribing medicines...
Subordinate Clause"Although drug samples are often praised..." (concessive clause); "they would not normally choose" (zero relative clause modifying "medicines")
Grammar Note"Although" signals concession; the relative pronoun before "they would not normally choose" is omitted (zero relative clause) — common in written academic English
Simple MeaningEven though free samples seem helpful, studies show they can change what doctors prescribe.
中文尽管药品样本常被称赞为帮助较贫困患者的方式，但研究表明，它们也会促使医生开出他们本来不会首选的药物。
"Because marketing budgets are folded into a drug's final price, it is ultimately the patient who pays for every free ticket and expensive dinner."
Main Clauseit is ultimately the patient who pays for every free ticket and expensive dinner
Subordinate Clause"Because marketing budgets are folded into a drug's final price" (reason clause); "who pays for every free ticket and expensive dinner" (relative clause inside a cleft sentence)
Grammar Note"it is X who..." is a cleft structure used to emphasise "the patient" as the true payer
Simple MeaningPatients indirectly cover marketing costs through higher drug prices.
中文由于营销预算被计入药品的最终价格，最终买单的其实是患者，他们为每一张免费门票和昂贵的晚餐付了钱。
"The rapid growth in the number of sales representatives, together with the size of the budgets behind them, has made it necessary to examine closely how reps and doctors interact."
Main ClauseThe rapid growth...has made it necessary to examine closely how reps and doctors interact
Subordinate Clause"together with the size of the budgets behind them" (appositive addition); "how reps and doctors interact" (embedded question, object of "examine")
Grammar Note"make it + adjective + to-infinitive" is a common formal structure; the wh-clause functions as the object of the infinitive "to examine"
Simple MeaningMore reps and larger budgets mean people must now look carefully at the doctor-rep relationship.
中文销售代表数量的迅速增长，加上他们背后预算规模的扩大，使得人们有必要仔细审视销售代表与医生之间的互动关系。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Cause & Effect

Cause: doctors are given free samples of an expensive new drug. → Effect: a University of Washington study of 131 doctors found that sample availability led them to prescribe or dispense drugs different from their usual choice.

Contrast

The passage contrasts doctors who insist gifts do not affect them ("I tend to think I'm not influenced by what they give me") with research evidence showing that free samples do change prescribing behaviour.

Comparison

A small branded gift like a golf ball is compared with a $1,000 honorarium for attending a lecture — both are marketing tools, but they differ enormously in scale and potential ethical weight.

Examples

The passage gives concrete examples of promotional gadgets: pens, mugs, shirts, umbrellas, and golf balls, all printed with a drug's name — illustrating the everyday scale of pharmaceutical marketing.

Definition

An "honorarium" is implicitly defined as a payment (around $1,000) given to a doctor simply for attending a company's educational lecture — distinct from payment for actual work performed.

Problem / Solution

Problem: marketing spending has grown to extravagant levels, raising ethical concerns about influence on prescribing. Implied solution: continued heavy scrutiny of the industry's sales and marketing practices.

Chronology

The passage traces a sequence: a rep's office visit with gifts → meals and trips → free samples building long-term prescribing habits → higher prices that patients eventually pay. Tracking this sequence clarifies the writer's final argument.

Author's Attitude

The writer's tone is balanced but ultimately critical — acknowledging that "companies have a right to profit" while insisting they "must continue to be heavily scrutinised for their sales and marketing practices."
