📋 Passage Overview

Is there anybody out there? This passage explains SETI — the search for extra-terrestrial intelligence. Five lettered paragraphs cover, in turn: why we search (A), the two ground rules scientists adopt (B), how likely life elsewhere is (C), why radio waves are used and how the searches are organised (D), and how we should respond if a signal arrives (E). Question types: matching headings (Q14–17), short answer (Q18–20) and Yes / No / Not Given (Q21–26).

💡 Reading tip: For matching headings, read the first and last sentences of each paragraph first — the topic sentence usually names the paragraph's function. Note the difference between Yes/No/Not Given (about the writer's views) and True/False/Not Given (about facts). Q21–26 ask what the writer thinks, so hunt for opinion language: 'it seems inconceivable', 'everybody agrees', 'it's not important, then'.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Although groups around the world have been searching intermittently for three decades, it is only now that we have reached the level of technology where we can make a determined attempt to search all nearby stars for any sign of life.
Main ClauseIt is only now that we have reached the level of technology… (it-cleft)
Subordinate / Relative'Although groups… have been searching intermittently for three decades' (concession) + 'where we can make a determined attempt…' (relative clause of place/situation).
Grammar Note'Although' concedes past effort; the it-cleft emphasises now. Present perfect continuous shows an ongoing thirty-year action.
Simple MeaningPeople have searched on and off for thirty years, but only today is the technology good enough for a serious full search.
中文尽管世界各地的研究团队断断续续地搜寻了三十年，但直到现在，我们的技术水平才足以对所有邻近恒星展开认真的生命迹象搜索。
We want to know whether life evolves naturally if given the right conditions, or whether there is something very special about the Earth to have fostered the variety of life forms that we see around us on the planet.
Main ClauseWe want to know…
Subordinate / RelativeTwo parallel 'whether' clauses joined by 'or'; inside the first, 'if given the right conditions' is a reduced conditional (= if it is given); inside the second, 'that we see around us' is a defining relative.
Grammar Note'whether A or whether B' presents two competing explanations — a very common academic structure.
Simple MeaningWe want to know if life appears naturally when conditions are right, or if Earth is unusually special.
中文我们想知道：生命在条件合适时是否会自然演化，还是地球有某种特殊之处，才孕育出我们周围所见的各种生命形式。
Second, we make a very conservative assumption that we are looking for a life form that is pretty well like us, since if it differs radically from us we may well not recognise it as a life form.
Main ClauseWe make a very conservative assumption.
Subordinate / Relative'that we are looking for a life form' (appositive that-clause) + 'that is pretty well like us' (defining relative) + 'since if it differs radically from us…' (reason clause containing a conditional).
Grammar NoteThree layers: appositive → relative → reason + condition. 'may well' = is quite likely to.
Simple MeaningWe cautiously assume the aliens resemble us, because if they were completely different we probably wouldn't even recognise them as alive.
中文其次，我们做了一个非常稳妥的假设：我们寻找的是与我们相当相似的生命形式，因为如果它与我们截然不同，我们很可能根本认不出那是生命。
However, when we look at the 100 billion stars in our galaxy and 100 billion galaxies in the observable Universe, it seems inconceivable that at least one of these planets does not have a life form on it.
Main ClauseIt seems inconceivable that at least one of these planets does not have a life form on it.
Subordinate / Relative'when we look at the 100 billion stars…' (time/condition clause).
Grammar Note'it seems + adj + that-clause' expresses the writer's judgement. The double negative ('inconceivable… does not have') means: surely some planet DOES have life.
Simple MeaningGiven the huge numbers of stars and galaxies, it is almost impossible to believe that no planet has life.
中文然而，当我们看到银河系中的一千亿颗恒星以及可观测宇宙中的一千亿个星系时，若说这些行星中连一颗都没有生命，实在令人难以想象。
An alien civilisation could choose many different ways of sending information across the galaxy, but many of these either require too much energy, or else are severely attenuated while traversing the vast distances across the galaxy.
Main ClauseAn alien civilisation could choose many different ways… but many of these require too much energy or are attenuated.
Subordinate / Relative'while traversing the vast distances' = reduced time clause (= while they traverse).
Grammar Note'either… or else…' presents two disqualifying problems. This sentence sets up the answer to Q19 (radio waves).
Simple MeaningAliens could send messages in many ways, but most methods use too much energy or fade too much over huge distances.
中文外星文明可以选择许多方式在星系间传递信息，但其中许多要么耗能过大，要么在穿越星系的巨大距离时严重衰减。
Quite apart from the impracticality of sending a reply over such large distances at short notice, it raises a host of ethical questions that would have to be addressed by the global community before any reply could be sent.
Main ClauseIt raises a host of ethical questions.
Subordinate / Relative'Quite apart from the impracticality of…' (prepositional phrase adding a second reason) + 'that would have to be addressed… before any reply could be sent' (relative + time clause).
Grammar Note'Quite apart from X, Y' = 'X is one problem, and Y is another'. Note the modal past ('would have to', 'could be') for hypothetical situations.
Simple MeaningBesides being physically impractical, a quick reply would create many ethical problems the world would need to settle first.
中文且不说在如此遥远的距离上仓促回复根本不切实际，此举还会引发大量伦理问题，必须由国际社会先行讨论，才能发出任何回复。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Structure by function: this passage is organised by purpose of each section, not chronology — why (A), rules (B), probability (C), method (D), response (E). Matching-headings tasks almost always test exactly this kind of functional structure.

Enumeration signals: 'First, UFOs are generally ignored… Second, we make a very conservative assumption…' Number words are the fastest route to paragraph B's heading (assumptions).

Contrast: 'One part is a targeted search… The other part is an undirected search…' Two matched clauses set up two contrasting methods — Q20 (1000 stars) belongs only to the targeted half.

Concession within an argument: 'although it is also important to keep an open mind in case any really convincing evidence emerges'. The writer dismisses UFOs but softens the dismissal — this hedging is what makes Y/N/NG hard.

Cause & effect: vast distances (cause) → signals attenuate (effect) → only radio waves in one frequency band survive (effect) → all searches concentrate there (effect). Q19's answer sits at the end of this chain.

Author's attitude — hopeful but cautious: 'gives us some cause for optimism', 'Luckily, there is no urgency about this'. He is positive about SETI and relaxed about the timing of any reply, which is exactly why Q26 ('respond promptly') is NO.

Problem / solution: paragraph E states the problem (should we reply?), rejects the quick answer (don't reply immediately), and offers the resolution (there is plenty of time to debate).
