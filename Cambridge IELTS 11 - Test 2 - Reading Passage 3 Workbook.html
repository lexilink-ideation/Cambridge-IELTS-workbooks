📋 Passage Overview

'Neuroaesthetics' is a new field that uses brain science to study art. The passage reports six studies — Hawley-Dolan (famous artists vs. animal doodles), Pepperell (ambiguous images), Mondrian eye-tracking, Vartanian (altered compositions), Forsythe (visual intricacy and fractals) and mirror neurons and Pollock — before warning that art cannot be reduced to scientific laws.

题型:四选一 Q27–30 + 词库摘要 Q31–33 + YES/NO/NOT GIVEN Q34–39 + 副标题 Q40。

💡 Reading tip: YES / NO / NOT GIVEN tests the writer's views, not facts. Q36 and Q37 are NO because the writer explicitly argues the opposite ('it would be foolish to reduce art appreciation to a set of scientific laws'). Look for the writer's own evaluative language: 'foolish', 'We shouldn't underestimate'.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

When asked to make simple perceptual decisions such as matching a shape to its rotated image, for example, people often choose a definitively wrong answer if they see others doing the same.
Main Clausepeople often choose a definitively wrong answer
Subordinate / Relative'When asked to make simple perceptual decisions…' = reduced passive time clause ('When they are asked'); 'if they see others doing the same' = conditional clause
Grammar NoteA reduced passive clause opens the sentence; note the subject 'people' only appears in the middle.
Simple MeaningIn simple shape tests, people copy others even when the answer is clearly wrong.
中文例如,在被要求做出简单的知觉判断(如把一个形状与其旋转后的图像配对)时,如果看到别人这样选,人们往往会选择一个明显错误的答案。
In each set of trials, volunteers generally preferred the work of renowned artists, even when they believed it was by an animal or a child.
Main Clausevolunteers generally preferred the work of renowned artists
Subordinate / Relative'even when they believed it was by an animal or a child' = concessive time clause
Grammar Note'even when' strengthens the concession — this single sentence answers Q28.
Simple MeaningPeople still liked the famous artists' work, even when told an animal or child had painted it.
中文在每一组试验中,志愿者普遍更喜欢知名艺术家的作品,即使他们以为那是动物或儿童画的。
The longer they took to answer these questions, the more highly they rated the piece under scrutiny, and the greater their neural activity.
Main Clause(triple correlative comparison)
Subordinate / Relative'The + comparative …, the + comparative …' structure, extended to three parts
Grammar NoteCorrelative comparatives express proportional change: as one rises, so does the other.
Simple MeaningThe more time people needed, the more they liked the picture and the more brain activity they showed.
中文他们回答这些问题所花的时间越长,对被审视作品的评价就越高,神经活动也越强。
Mondrian's works are deceptively simple, but eye-tracking studies confirm that they are meticulously composed, and that simply rotating a piece radically changes the way we view it.
Main Clauseeye-tracking studies confirm that they are meticulously composed, and that … rotating a piece radically changes the way we view it
Subordinate / Relative'Mondrian's works are deceptively simple, but…' = contrast; two parallel that-clauses
Grammar Note'deceptively simple' = they look simple but are not. Two 'that'-clauses share one reporting verb.
Simple MeaningMondrian's paintings look simple but are carefully arranged; turning one round changes how we see it.
中文蒙德里安的作品看似简单,但眼动研究证实它们经过精心构图,而仅仅旋转一幅画就会彻底改变我们观看它的方式。
While the fashions of the time might shape what is currently popular, works that are best adapted to our visual system may be the most likely to linger once the trends of previous generations have been forgotten.
Main Clauseworks … may be the most likely to linger
Subordinate / Relative'While the fashions of the time might shape what is currently popular' = concession; 'that are best adapted to our visual system' = defining relative; 'once the trends … have been forgotten' = time clause
Grammar NoteConcession + relative + time clause around one short main clause. Directly relevant to Q36.
Simple MeaningFashion decides what is popular now, but works that suit our eyes may last longest.
中文尽管时代风尚可能决定当下什么流行,但最适应我们视觉系统的作品,在前几代人的潮流被遗忘之后,或许最有可能长久留存。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Debate frame. The passage opens with a sceptical claim ('people claim to like such works simply because they are famous') and then tests it study by study. Every study is evidence against the sceptics.

Study → finding → implication. Each paragraph follows the same shape: who did it, what they did, what they found, what it suggests ('It seems that…', 'It would seem that…', 'It is possible that…'). Hedged verbs mark the implication, which is usually the MCQ answer.

Comparison as method. Four studies work by comparison: famous art vs. animal doodles; originals vs. rotated versions; originals vs. rearranged versions; too little vs. too much detail. Recognising the comparison tells you the finding instantly.

Analogy with nature. Fractals appear 'in the shapes of mountain peaks or the branches of trees' — the writer suggests our visual system evolved outdoors, so it processes such patterns easily. This is a hypothesis, not a proven fact (note 'It is possible that').

Final qualification. The last paragraph pulls back: neuroaesthetics is 'still early days', reducing art to laws would be 'foolish', and style, history and artistic environment still matter. Q37 and Q38 both come from here.
