📋 Quick Review

Michael Alexander phones Total Insurance to make a claim for belongings damaged when they were shipped from overseas to Australia. The agent Judy takes down his details on a form, then records the damage to four items — a television, a bathroom cabinet, a dining-room table and a set of china — with the cost to repair or replace each. It is a typical Part 1 phone call with a form and a table to complete.

💡 Listening strategy tip: Answers come in order and follow the form/table layout. Listen for spelling ('M-I-L-P-E-R-R-A'), dates and prices, and watch out for correction ('$1285 … but the one here is $140'). Mind the word limit — here parts change between THREE words and ONE word AND/OR A NUMBER.

🔄 Paraphrase Training

In IELTS the question words are almost never the words you hear or read. Recognising paraphrase is the key skill. Study each example, then write your own sentence.
Paraphrase Example 1
Question / promptQ1: the suburb of the address …
What you hear / read"It's 24 Manly Street, Milperra near Sydney … M-I-L-P-E-R-R-A."
Key paraphraseThe spelt-out word is the answer — write letters exactly: Milperra.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 2
Question / promptQ4: item damaged on the television …
What you hear / read"Our insurance doesn't cover electrical problems." — "It isn't electrical. The screen has a huge crack."
Key paraphraseBeware the 'electrical' distractor: the damaged part is the screen.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 3
Question / promptQ7: cost to replace the cabinet …
What you hear / read"When I bought it I paid $1285 … but the one I've seen here is a bit more expensive, it's $140."
Key paraphraseIgnore the old price ($1285); the replacement cost recorded is $140.
Your practice
Paraphrase Example 4
Question / promptQ10: total replacement value of the china …
What you hear / read"up to $10 each …" — "so that would be around $60 altogether?"
Key paraphraseSix plates × up to $10 = around $60 total.
