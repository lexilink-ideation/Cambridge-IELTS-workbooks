Cambridge IELTS 12 — Test 1 — Listening Part 3

Practice Workbook

📋 Quick Review

Two students, Trudie and Stewart, plan a paper on public libraries. First they narrow the topic and argue about digitalised books and the future of libraries (Q21–23, multiple choice). Then they list questions they could ask about their own local library (Q24–30, note completion, ONE WORD ONLY).

💡 Listening tip: In a two-speaker discussion, watch for agreement markers — 'That's an important point', 'Right', 'Yes, but…'. Q21–23 ask what they agree or what Stewart expects, so an opinion stated by only one person and then rejected is a distractor.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

Note completion here rewrites the students' spoken questions into formal note language. Learn the swap.
Q24 — the library's money
Question note
whether it has a ___ of its own
What you hear
'find out whether the library has its own budget, or if that's controlled by the local council'
Key paraphrase
of its own = its own → budget
Your turn
Q25 — the law
Question note
how it's affected by laws regarding all aspects of ___
What you hear
'how the library is affected by employment laws… working hours, facilities for staff'
Key paraphrase
laws regarding all aspects of ___ = ___ laws → employment
Your turn
Q26 — the building design
Question note
how the design needs to take the ___ of customers into account
What you hear
'what measures does the library take to ensure their safety? floor coverings that aren't slippery, and emergency exits'
Key paraphrase
take ___ into account = take measures to ensure ___ → safety
Your turn
Q30 — a comparison
Question note
how it's different from a library in a ___
What you hear
'the differences… between a library that's open to the public and one that's part of a museum'
Key paraphrase
a library in a ___ = one that's part of a ___ → museum
Your turn

🧠 Mini Dictation

Academic discussion chunks. Notice the hedging language ('probably', 'I expect', 'I'm not so sure').
1.try and relate the changes in libraries to external developments
2.it's probably best if we don't go into funding in any detail
3.the digitalised books available online for free are mostly out of copyright
4.copyright in this country lasts for seventy years after the author dies
5.some have already become centres where community activities take place
6.they'll still be serving their traditional function
7.what measures does the library take to ensure their safety
8.I wonder if they keep a database, or whether they just look online
