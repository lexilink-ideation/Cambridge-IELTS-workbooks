📋 Passage Overview

The life and work of Marie Curie. A biography of the first woman to win a Nobel Prize and the only person of her era to win two. It follows her childhood in Poland, her sister's bargain that paid for her Sorbonne studies, her marriage to Pierre, the discovery of polonium and radium, her professorship after Pierre's death, the 'Little Curies' of World War I, and her death from leukaemia. Question types: True/False/Not Given (Q1–6) and note completion (Q7–13, ONE WORD only).

💡 Reading tip: Biographies run in date order, so the answers appear in question order — but the traps are in the details. Q1 turns on which prize Pierre shared (1903 only, not 1911). Q4 turns on 'failed to interrupt her scientific work'. Q6 turns on what Bronia actually did (she became a director, but her field of study is never specified beyond 'medical studies').

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

From her earnings she was able to finance her sister Bronia's medical studies in Paris, on the understanding that Bronia would, in turn, later help her to get an education.
Main ClauseShe was able to finance her sister Bronia's medical studies in Paris.
Subordinate / Relative'on the understanding that Bronia would… later help her to get an education' = condition clause.
Grammar Note'on the understanding that' introduces a mutual agreement. Note the direction: Marie paid FIRST, Bronia repaid LATER — this is what Q3 tests.
Simple MeaningMarie used her teaching pay to fund Bronia's medical course in Paris, on condition that Bronia would later fund Marie's education.
中文她用自己的收入资助姐姐布罗尼娅在巴黎学医，条件是布罗尼娅日后要反过来帮助她接受教育。
Following Henri Becquerel's discovery in 1896 of a new phenomenon, which Marie later called 'radioactivity', Marie Curie decided to find out if the radioactivity discovered in uranium was to be found in other elements.
Main ClauseMarie Curie decided to find out if the radioactivity was to be found in other elements.
Subordinate / Relative'Following Henri Becquerel's discovery… of a new phenomenon' (time phrase) + 'which Marie later called radioactivity' (non-defining relative) + 'discovered in uranium' (reduced relative).
Grammar NoteThree layers before the main clause even begins. Note that Becquerel discovered the phenomenon; Marie named it.
Simple MeaningAfter Becquerel found the effect in 1896 — which Marie later named radioactivity — she set out to see whether other elements had it too.
中文在亨利·贝克勒尔于 1896 年发现一种新现象（玛丽后来称之为「放射性」）之后，居里夫人决定查明在铀中发现的放射性是否也存在于其他元素中。
Turning her attention to minerals, she found her interest drawn to pitchblende, a mineral whose radioactivity, superior to that of pure uranium, could be explained only by the presence in the ore of small quantities of an unknown substance of very high activity.
Main ClauseShe found her interest drawn to pitchblende.
Subordinate / Relative'Turning her attention to minerals' (participial opener) + 'a mineral whose radioactivity… could be explained only by…' (relative with 'whose') + 'superior to that of pure uranium' (comparison insert).
Grammar Note'that of' = 'the radioactivity of'. The logic is deductive: the radioactivity was too strong for uranium alone, so something unknown must be present.
Simple MeaningStudying minerals, she focused on pitchblende, which was more radioactive than pure uranium — explicable only if it contained a tiny amount of an unknown, highly active substance.
中文她把注意力转向矿物后，对沥青铀矿产生了兴趣：这种矿物的放射性强于纯铀，唯一的解释是矿石中含有极少量活性极高的未知物质。
The births of Marie's two daughters, Irene and Eve, in 1897 and 1904 failed to interrupt her scientific work.
Main ClauseThe births of Marie's two daughters failed to interrupt her scientific work.
Subordinate / Relative'Irene and Eve' and 'in 1897 and 1904' are appositives supplying names and dates.
Grammar Note'failed to + verb' is a formal negative: the births did NOT interrupt her work. Q4 claims she stopped research for several years — the opposite.
Simple MeaningHaving her daughters Irene (1897) and Eve (1904) did not stop her scientific work.
中文玛丽的两个女儿伊雷娜和艾芙分别于 1897 年和 1904 年出生，但这并未中断她的科学工作。
The sudden death of her husband in 1906 was a bitter blow to Marie Curie, but was also a turning point in her career: henceforth she was to devote all her energy to completing alone the scientific work that they had undertaken.
Main ClauseThe death was a bitter blow, but was also a turning point in her career.
Subordinate / RelativeThe colon introduces the consequence + 'that they had undertaken' (defining relative).
Grammar Note'was to devote' is the future-in-the-past, used in biography for what someone would go on to do.
Simple MeaningPierre's sudden death in 1906 devastated her but changed her career: from then on she alone completed the work they had begun.
中文1906 年丈夫的猝然离世对居里夫人是沉重打击，但也是她事业的转折点：此后她将全部精力投入到独自完成两人共同开创的科学工作中。
One of Marie Curie's outstanding achievements was to have understood the need to accumulate intense radioactive sources, not only to treat illness but also to maintain an abundant supply for research.
Main ClauseOne of Marie Curie's outstanding achievements was to have understood the need…
Subordinate / Relative'not only to treat illness but also to maintain an abundant supply for research' = correlative pair of purposes.
Grammar Note'not only… but also…' balances two reasons. Q11 asks for the first of them, expressed as a single word: illness.
Simple MeaningOne of her great achievements was realising that large radioactive stocks were needed both to treat disease and to supply research.
中文居里夫人的杰出成就之一，是理解了积累强放射源的必要性——既为治疗疾病，也为研究维持充足的供应。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Chronology as structure: the passage is strictly date-ordered from 1867 to 1934, so Q1–6 and Q7–13 both run in passage order. When you lose your place, find the next date.

Cause & effect: her father lost his savings (cause) → she had to teach (effect) → she financed Bronia (effect) → Bronia later financed her (effect) → she reached the Sorbonne in 1891 (effect). Q3 sits at the end of this chain.

Deductive reasoning: pitchblende was more radioactive than pure uranium → therefore an unknown highly active substance must be present → therefore polonium and radium were found. The passage models scientific inference in a single sentence.

Contrast (setback vs opportunity): 'The sudden death of her husband… was a bitter blow… but was also a turning point in her career.' The 'but' reframes tragedy as a professional pivot.

Concession through negation: 'The births of Marie's two daughters… failed to interrupt her scientific work.' The writer anticipates the assumption that motherhood would stop her, then denies it. Q4 tests exactly this.

Purpose pairs: 'not only to treat illness but also to maintain an abundant supply for research'. Whenever you see 'not only… but also…', expect a question on one half of the pair.

Author's attitude — admiring, and quietly poignant: 'probably the most famous woman scientist who has ever lived', 'Her contribution to physics had been immense'. The detail of the pretty blue-green light in her pocket is left without comment, letting the irony speak for itself.
