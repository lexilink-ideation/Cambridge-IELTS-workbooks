📋 Passage Overview

Obtaining Linguistic Data is a methods passage about how linguists collect data about a language. It covers using informants (native speakers), how factors like age and setting affect language, the value and limits of tape and video recording, structured elicitation techniques, and the use of a corpus. Seven paragraphs (A–G) with matching tasks.

💡 Reading tip: For table and summary completion, the answer is copied word-for-word from the passage. Find the paragraph on that method, then take the exact phrase that fits the gap and the word limit.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Often, when studying their mother tongue, linguists act as their own informants, judging the ambiguity, acceptability, or other properties of utterances against their own intuitions.
Main Clauselinguists act as their own informants
Subordinate / Relativewhen studying their mother tongue (time clause); judging the ambiguity… against their own intuitions (participle phrase)
Grammar Notethe 'judging…' phrase explains how they act as informants.
Simple MeaningWhen studying their own language, linguists often use themselves as informants, judging utterances by their own instinct.
中文研究母语时，语言学家常常充当自己的语料提供者，凭借自己的直觉来判断话语的歧义性、可接受性等特性。
But a linguist's personal judgements are often uncertain, or disagree with the judgements of other linguists, at which point recourse is needed to more objective methods of enquiry.
Main Clausea linguist's personal judgements are often uncertain, or disagree with the judgements of other linguists
Subordinate / Relativeat which point recourse is needed to more objective methods (relative clause of consequence)
Grammar Note'at which point' links to the whole preceding idea; 'recourse is needed to' = one must turn to.
Simple MeaningBut a linguist's own judgements can be unsure or clash with others', so more objective methods are then needed.
中文但语言学家的个人判断往往不确定，或与其他语言学家的判断相左，此时就需要求助于更客观的研究方法。
A variety of tape-recording procedures have thus been devised to minimise the 'observer's paradox' — how to observe the way people behave when they are not being observed.
Main ClauseA variety of tape-recording procedures have thus been devised
Subordinate / Relativeto minimise the 'observer's paradox' (purpose); how to observe the way people behave when they are not being observed (appositive)
Grammar Notethe dash introduces a definition of 'observer's paradox'.
Simple MeaningSo several recording methods have been created to reduce the 'observer's paradox' — seeing how people act when they think no one is watching.
中文因此，人们设计了各种录音方法，以尽量减小'观察者悖论'——即如何观察人们在不被观察时的行为方式。
An important principle is that all corpora, whatever their size, are inevitably limited in their coverage, and always need to be supplemented by data derived from the intuitions of native speakers.
Main Clauseall corpora … are inevitably limited in their coverage, and always need to be supplemented …
Subordinate / Relativethat all corpora … (noun clause); whatever their size (concession); derived from the intuitions of native speakers (participle)
Grammar Note'whatever their size' = no matter how big; 'supplemented by' = added to with.
Simple MeaningA key point is that every corpus, however large, covers only part of a language and must be topped up with native speakers' intuitions.
中文一条重要原则是：无论规模多大，所有语料库的覆盖范围都不可避免地有限，因此总需要用来自母语者直觉的数据加以补充。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Method spectrum: the passage ranges from armchair introspection to intensive fieldwork — a scale of techniques.

Advantage vs limitation: each method (self as informant, recording, video, corpus) is weighed for what it gains and what it misses.

Problem → solution: the 'observer's paradox' is the problem; hidden recorders, radio mics and engaging topics are the solutions.

Qualification: even a corpus is 'inevitably limited' and must be supplemented by native-speaker intuition (matches Q40).
