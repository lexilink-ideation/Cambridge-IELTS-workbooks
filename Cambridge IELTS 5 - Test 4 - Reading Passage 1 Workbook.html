📋 Passage Overview

This passage, 'The Impact of Wilderness Tourism', examines how tourism to remote 'wilderness' regions affects local people and environments. Regions across the world now promote their wild areas, but rapid growth in visitor numbers can damage fragile ecosystems and disrupt traditional ways of life. The writer discusses problems such as water shortages, waste and cultural change, and argues that careful management can make tourism sustainable and beneficial.

💡 Reading strategy tip: 'Which opinion does the writer hold?' (YES/NO/NOT GIVEN) tests the writer's view. Look for evaluative words (should, must, can be, risks) that show what the writer thinks, not just factual statements.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

"Regions across the world are actively promoting their ‘wilderness’ areas, hoping to attract visitors and the money they bring."
Main ClauseRegions across the world are actively promoting their 'wilderness' areas
Subordinate / Relativehoping to attract visitors and the money they bring (participle phrase of purpose)
Grammar Note'hoping to …' shows purpose; 'the money (that) they bring' is a reduced relative.
Simple MeaningPlaces everywhere advertise their wild areas to bring in tourists and their spending.
中文世界各地都在积极推广自己的“荒野”地区，希望吸引游客以及他们带来的收入。
"Although tourism brings income, a sudden influx of visitors can put great pressure on fragile ecosystems."
Main Clausea sudden influx of visitors can put great pressure on fragile ecosystems
Subordinate / RelativeAlthough tourism brings income (concessive clause)
Grammar Note'Although' concedes the benefit before stating the risk.
Simple MeaningTourism earns money, but too many visitors at once can badly strain delicate nature.
中文尽管旅游业带来收入，但游客的突然涌入会给脆弱的生态系统带来巨大压力。
"In desert areas, where water is scarce, the needs of hotels and visitors may compete with those of local people."
Main Clausethe needs of hotels and visitors may compete with those of local people
Subordinate / RelativeIn desert areas, where water is scarce (place + non-defining relative)
Grammar Note'where …' describes the desert areas; 'those' replaces 'the needs'.
Simple MeaningIn dry deserts, hotels and tourists may use water that local people also need.
中文在缺水的沙漠地区，酒店和游客的需求可能与当地居民的需求相冲突。
"If it is carefully managed, however, wilderness tourism can be sustainable and can even help protect the environment."
Main Clausewilderness tourism can be sustainable and can even help protect the environment
Subordinate / RelativeIf it is carefully managed (conditional clause); however (contrast connector)
Grammar Note'If … , however, …' balances the earlier warnings with a positive condition.
Simple MeaningWith good management, though, wilderness tourism can last and even help nature.
中文然而，如果管理得当，荒野旅游可以是可持续的，甚至有助于保护环境。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Benefit vs Cost — Tourism brings revenue but risks degradation and cultural disruption. The whole passage weighs the two sides.

Cause & Effect — Sudden influx → pressure on water, waste, ecosystems → harm to locals and nature. Trace the pressures.

Condition — 'If carefully managed', tourism can be sustainable. The writer's solution is conditional management.

Author's stance — Balanced but hopeful: problems are real, yet good management can 'strike a balance'. Note evaluative language.
