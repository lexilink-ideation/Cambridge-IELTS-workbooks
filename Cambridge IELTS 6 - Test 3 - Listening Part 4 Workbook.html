Ireland in the Neolithic Period · Practice Workbook

📋 Quick Review

A university lecturer gives a talk about Ireland during the Neolithic period (roughly 4,000–6,000 years ago), when farming was introduced. The lecture covers where the first farmers may have come from, how they transported and bred livestock, evidence for early cereal crops, stone tool and axe-making technology, and the origins of pottery making.

This is a classic Part 4 academic lecture: one speaker, dense factual content, and a mix of multiple-choice and note/sentence-completion questions covering archaeology and history.

💡 Part 4 strategy: This is the hardest section — the talk is continuous with no break, and vocabulary is specialised (archaeological terms). Before listening, read ALL the questions so you know the topic order (hypotheses → animals → crops → stone tools → pottery) and can anticipate what's coming next.

Unit at a glance

Task breakdown

Q31–34: Theories about the first Neolithic settlers, animal breeding, transportation of livestock, and evidence for cereal crops.

Q35–37 (Stone Tools): Ploughing methods, axe-making stages, and axe exports.

Q38–40 (Pottery Making): How pots were finished, the source of clay, and early decoration.

🔄 Paraphrase Training

Academic lectures often state a claim, then immediately give the supporting reason. The reason is usually where the paraphrase — and the answer — is hiding.
Q31 — Uncertainty About Origins
Question wordingAccording to the speaker, it is not clear… (choose A, B or C)
Exact script line
LECTURER: There are several hypotheses about the origins of the first Neolithic settlers in Ireland, but most of these contain problems.
Key paraphrase"hypotheses about the origins...contain problems" = it isn't clear where the farmers came from → Answer C
Your practice
Q32 — Minimum Breeding Numbers
Question wordingWhat point does the speaker make about breeding animals? (choose A, B or C)
Exact script line
LECTURER: If the level of breeding stock falls below about three hundred females, the future of the species locally is at risk. So...the number of breeding sheep would have considerably exceeded three hundred.
Key paraphrase"must have exceeded 300" = "numbers must have been above a certain level" → Answer A
Your practice
Q33 — Transportation Limits
Question wordingWhat does the speaker say about the transportation of animals? (choose A, B or C)
Exact script line
LECTURER: Neolithic settlers used skin-covered boats to transport livestock. But this method would have severely restricted the range of the colonising fleets. ...it's unlikely that this livestock could have been brought from anywhere further than England.
Key paraphrase"severely restricted the range" = "limited the distance the farmers could sail" → Answer A
Your practice
Q34 — Evidence for Cereal Crops
Question wordingWhat is the main evidence for cereal crops? (choose A, B or C)
Exact script line
LECTURER: The main evidence for their presence consists of impressions on pottery, where a cereal grain accidentally became embedded in the surface of a pot before it was fired.
Key paraphrase"impressions on pottery" = "marks left on pots by grains" → Answer B (not "burnt grain," which is a distractor detail)
