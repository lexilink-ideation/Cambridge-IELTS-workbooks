Reading Passage 3

Cambridge IELTS 10 — Test 3 — Reading Passage 3: Beyond the Blue Horizon

Practice Workbook

📋 Passage Overview

A 3,000-year-old cemetery on the island of Efate in Vanuatu has revealed the Lapita — the ancestors of today's Polynesians. The passage describes the discovery, then presents two competing explanations for how they crossed the Pacific: Geoff Irwin's theory of deliberate skilled sailing against the trade winds, and Atholl Anderson's theory of accidental drift during El Niño events.

这是一篇考古学文章,包含两位学者的对立观点。做YES/NO/NOT GIVEN时要分清:哪些是已证实的,哪些只是假设。

💡 Reading tip: For the summary (Q27–31), the last two gaps are the trickiest: gap 30 is the object found ('a Lapita burial urn'), and gap 31 is what was inside it ('the human remains sealed inside' = bones). Notice that 'proof' and 'archaeological discovery' are both in the options list — one fits gap 28, the other does not fit gap 30 because a thing was found, not an abstraction.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

An agricultural worker, digging in the grounds of a derelict plantation, scraped open a grave — the first of dozens in a burial ground some 3,000 years old.
Main ClauseAn agricultural worker … scraped open a grave
Subordinate / Relative'digging in the grounds of a derelict plantation' = participle clause between commas; '— the first of dozens in a burial ground some 3,000 years old' = appositive after a dash
Grammar NoteSubject – interrupting participle – verb – appositive. Note 'some 3,000 years old' where 'some' = 'approximately'.
Simple MeaningA farm worker digging on an abandoned plantation uncovered the first of dozens of graves in a 3,000-year-old cemetery.
中文一名农业工人在一座废弃种植园的地里挖掘时,刨开了一座坟墓——那是一处约三千年历史的墓地中数十座坟墓中的第一座。
Those sailing skills, he says, were developed and passed down over thousands of years by earlier mariners who worked their way through the archipelagoes of the western Pacific, making short crossings to nearby islands.
Main ClauseThose sailing skills were developed and passed down over thousands of years by earlier mariners
Subordinate / Relative'he says' = interrupting reporting clause; 'who worked their way through the archipelagoes of the western Pacific' = relative clause; 'making short crossings to nearby islands' = participle clause
Grammar NotePassive with 'by' + agent, interrupted by 'he says'. The two trailing clauses both describe the earlier mariners.
Simple MeaningIrwin says earlier sailors built up these skills over thousands of years by island-hopping across the western Pacific.
中文他说,这些航海技能是由更早的水手在数千年间发展并世代相传的;这些水手一路穿行于西太平洋的群岛之间,进行短距离的岛屿间航行。
They could sail out for days into the unknown and assess the area, secure in the knowledge that if they didn't find anything, they could turn about and catch a swift ride back on the trade winds.
Main ClauseThey could sail out for days into the unknown and assess the area
Subordinate / Relative'secure in the knowledge that…' = adjective phrase; inside it, 'if they didn't find anything' = conditional clause, and 'they could turn about and catch a swift ride back' = the result
Grammar NoteAn adjective phrase ('secure in the knowledge that…') carries a full conditional sentence inside it. This whole idea is what 'This' refers to in Q34.
Simple MeaningThey could explore for days knowing that if they found nothing, the trade winds would blow them quickly home.
中文他们可以向未知海域航行数日并勘察那片区域,因为他们心里有底:如果什么也没找到,就可以掉头,借助信风迅速返航。
Without this to go by, overshooting their home ports, getting lost and sailing off into eternity would have been all too easy.

Grammar NoteThree gerund phrases form a compound subject. 'Would have been' = third conditional, referring to a past unreal situation.

By reversing the regular east-to-west flow of the trade winds for weeks at a time, these 'super El Niños' might have taken the Lapita on long unplanned voyages.

Subordinate / Relative'By reversing the regular east-to-west flow of the trade winds for weeks at a time' = prepositional phrase of means
Grammar Note'Might have + past participle' = speculation about the past. This hedge is why Q37 is YES ('may have played a role') but Q36 is NO.

However they did it, the Lapita spread themselves a third of the way across the Pacific, then called it quits for reasons known only to them.
Main Clausethe Lapita spread themselves a third of the way across the Pacific, then called it quits

🧠 Reading Logic Training

1. Discovery narrative (发现的叙事). The opening three paragraphs follow a standard science-journalism shape: an important find → how it was found (by chance) → what was found (62 individuals, six complete pots, a burial urn) → an expert quotation confirming its importance → what it might answer (the DNA question). Summary questions almost always target this section, because the facts are concrete.

2. The unanswered question (悬而未决的问题). 'There is one stubborn question for which archaeology has yet to provide any answers: how did the Lapita accomplish the ancient equivalent of a moon landing?' Everything after this sentence is attempted explanation, not established fact. Marking this turning point in the text with your pencil makes the last five questions much easier.

3. Two competing explanations (两种对立解释). Irwin (Auckland): the Lapita were skilled; sailing against the prevailing trade winds was deliberate, because it guaranteed a fast ride home; their own archipelagoes acted as a safety net. Anderson (ANU): 'All this presupposes one essential detail … the Lapita had mastered the advanced art of sailing against the wind. And there's no proof they could do any such thing.' Instead he 'invokes the winds of chance' — super El Niños. Skill versus luck is the axis of the whole second half.

4. Cause & effect (因果). Irwin's chain: headwinds → safe exploration → confidence to sail beyond sight of land → discovery of leads (seabirds, coconuts, twigs, afternoon clouds) → successful settlement. Anderson's chain: frequent El Niños → trade winds reverse for weeks → unplanned eastward voyages → accidental scattering. Note that both chains explain the same outcome — a classic feature of unresolved scientific debate.

5. Evidence and its absence (证据与证据的缺失). Positive evidence: 62 skeletons, six complete pots, a decorated burial urn, coral climate data. Absent evidence: no canoes, no rigging, no useful oral histories, no proof of windward sailing, no idea what the canoes looked like. IELTS loves this contrast — anything in the 'absent' column is a strong candidate for a NO or NOT GIVEN answer.

6. Author's stance (作者立场). The writer stays neutral and reports both experts fairly, using attribution throughout ('Irwin notes', 'he argues', 'Anderson suggests', 'He points out'). The writer's own voice appears only in the framing sentences — 'However they did it…', 'for reasons known only to them'. Because the writer takes no side, statements claiming that one theory is proven will be NO.
