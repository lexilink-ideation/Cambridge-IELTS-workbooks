Listening Part 3

Cambridge IELTS 11 — Test 1 — Listening Part 3

Practice Workbook

📋 Quick Review

Two students, Lisa and Greg, discuss an article about Akira Miyake's study on gender in physics. They summarise what the study did and found, criticise what the article left out, and then plan their own project. Ten multiple-choice questions (A/B/C).

学术讨论型 Part 3:两人一边复述研究,一边互相纠正——正确答案常出现在“纠正”之后。

💡 Listening tip: In two-speaker MCQs, the answer is what the pair agree on, not the first thing either says. Listen for 'Actually…', 'But they were wrong…', 'OK, maybe let's just go with your idea' — these signal the final answer.

✏️ Paraphrase Training

MCQ options are always paraphrases. Match the meaning, not the words.
Q22 — the aim
Option B
a way of improving women's performance in physics
What you hear
'what the researchers wanted to find out was basically what they could do about the relatively low level of the women's results'
Key paraphrase
'what they could do about the low level of results' = 'a way of improving performance'
Your turn
Q24 — the writing task
Option C
something that was important to them personally
What you hear
'write an essay focusing on things that were significant to them … like music or people who mattered to them'
Key paraphrase
'significant to them' + 'people who mattered to them' = 'important to them personally'
Your turn
Q25 — the purpose
Option A
to reduce stress
What you hear
'putting these thoughts into words can relax them and help them overcome the psychological factors that lead to poor performance'
Key paraphrase
'relax them / overcome psychological factors' = 'reduce stress'
Your turn
Q29 — Smolinsky
Option B
had no effect on the performance of men or women
What you hear
'the men and the women got the same results whether they were working in teams or on their own'
Key paraphrase
'same results in teams or alone' = 'teamwork had no effect'
Your turn
Q30 — next step
Option A
talk to a professor
What you hear
'perhaps we should just make an appointment to see one of the science professors. That'd be better.'
Key paraphrase
'make an appointment to see' = 'talk to'; 'that'd be better' rejects the timetable idea
Your turn

🧠 Mini Dictation

Academic discussion chunks. Notice the contrast markers, which usually come just before the answer.
1.They were majoring in what's known as the STEM disciplines.
2.Women are under-represented in introductory physics courses.
3.But actually they were wrong. No one was making any assumptions.
4.They had to write an essay focusing on things that were significant to them.
5.Putting these thoughts into words can relax them.
6.What the researchers hadn't expected was that this one activity raised the women's grades.
7.Had they been told why they were doing the writing?
8.Perhaps we should just make an appointment to see one of the science professors.
