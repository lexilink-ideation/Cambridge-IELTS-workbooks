📋 Passage Overview

The Desolenator: producing clean water. William Janssen developed a portable, solar-powered device that purifies water from the sea, rivers, boreholes or rain — with no need for a generated power supply. The passage explains the inspiration, how the machine works, the scale of global water scarcity, and the plans and funding behind it. Question types: matching headings to sections (Q14–20) and note/summary completion (Q21–26).

💡 Reading tip: For 'matching headings', read the first and last sentence of each section — the heading captures the main idea, not one detail. For the machine notes (Q21–26), follow the description of the water's journey in order.

🔍 Complex Sentence Analysis

Two decades later Janssen developed that basic idea he saw in Southeast Asia into a portable device that uses the power from the sun to purify water.
Main ClauseJanssen developed that basic idea … into a portable device
Subordinate / Relative'he saw in Southeast Asia' = contact (reduced) relative clause defining 'idea'; 'that uses the power from the sun to purify water' = relative clause defining 'device'
Grammar Note'develop X into Y' pattern with two relative clauses.
Simple MeaningTwenty years on, he turned that idea from Southeast Asia into a portable, solar-powered water purifier.
中文二十年后,詹森把他在东南亚看到的这个基本想法,发展成了一种利用太阳能净化水的便携装置。
It is particularly valuable in regions where natural groundwater reserves have been polluted, or where seawater is the only water source available.
Main ClauseIt is particularly valuable in regions
Subordinate / Relative'where natural groundwater reserves have been polluted' and 'where seawater is the only water source available' = two relative clauses describing 'regions'
Grammar NoteMain clause + two parallel 'where' relative clauses joined by 'or'.
Simple MeaningIt is most useful where groundwater is polluted, or where the only water is seawater.
中文在天然地下水资源已受污染、或海水是唯一可用水源的地区,它尤其有价值。
Its main selling point is that unlike standard desalination techniques, it doesn't require a generated power supply: just sunlight.
Main ClauseIts main selling point is that … it doesn't require a generated power supply
Subordinate / Relative'that … it doesn't require a generated power supply' = noun clause; 'unlike standard desalination techniques' = comparison phrase; ': just sunlight' = added detail
Grammar NoteLinking verb 'is' + that-clause; a colon adds the key contrast ('just sunlight').
Simple MeaningIts big advantage is that, unlike normal desalination, it needs no generated power — only sunlight.
中文它的主要卖点在于,与标准的海水淡化技术不同,它不需要发电供电——只需阳光。
Janssen says that by 2030 half of the world's population will be living with water stress, where the demand exceeds the supply over a certain period of time.
Main ClauseJanssen says that … half of the world's population will be living with water stress
Subordinate / Relative'that … will be living with water stress' = noun clause; 'where the demand exceeds the supply over a certain period of time' = relative clause defining 'water stress'
Grammar NoteReporting verb 'says' + that-clause + a 'where' relative clause giving a definition.
Simple MeaningJanssen says that by 2030 half the world will face water stress — when demand is greater than supply for a while.
中文詹森说,到2030年,全球一半人口将面临水资源紧张——即在一段时间内需求超过供给。

🧠 Reading Logic Training

Problem → solution. The passage sets up the problem (water scarcity, high carbon cost of normal desalination) and presents the Desolenator as the solution. Headings for early sections often capture inspiration or need; later ones capture the device or its future.

Process order. The machine notes (Q21–26) follow the water's path: enters as a thin film → heated → boiled to steam → cooled to distilled water → waste removed → performance monitored → serviced. Track the sequence.

Cause and effect. Poor countries lack money for plants → they can't build/run/fuel them → a cheap, solar, low-maintenance device is needed. These links help place the section headings.
